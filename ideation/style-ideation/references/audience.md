## 1. The viewer, in this niche

A woman past fifty (the pool's center of gravity is likely 60+) who feels she has not changed but suspects her mirror, her closet and her hairdresser have quietly aged her; who fears reading as "given up", frumpy or invisible far more than she fears any dollar figure; who was sold a rulebook at fifty ("no long hair after 50", "cover the arms", "age-appropriate") and half-believes it; who admires the well-dressed woman — the rich one, the Parisienne — as a trusted voice rather than a resented one, provided that voice speaks to her as a friend and never as a judge. She is not shopping for a makeover; she is being offered, in the feed, a look at her own aging tells through an expert's eyes and one concrete thing to change tonight — a color near the face, a length, a third piece, a lip.

**Mode:** browse. The click is won on self-recognition of the tell ("things that make you look older", "the haircut that ages you", "why you look frumpy") plus the insider's authority, then closed with a visible, tonight-sized fix.

## 6. Real mechanisms — the honest floor under every big title (verify specifics at scripting)

- **Color near the face:** as hair grays and skin loses contrast and warmth, the strongest colors and stark black next to the face can deepen shadows and lines; softer, muted mid-tones, the right undertone (warm / cool / neutral — the wrist-vein and jewelry tests as the folk methods, seasonal color analysis as the formal system, hedged) and a colored scarf or collar between black and the face are the defensible fixes. "Black ages you" runs as "can", with the scarf as the exception.
- **Proportion and line:** the rule of thirds (a 1:2 or 2:1 split beats half-and-half); hem at the narrowest point of the leg; the monochrome column for length; a defined waist or a clean shoulder line does more than hiding; the "third piece" (a blazer, a cardigan, a vest, a scarf) finishes an outfit; fabric weight and drape; the right bra changes the whole silhouette (the "most women wear the wrong size" line is widely quoted — hedge it); shapewear as smoothing, never as a promise; the $15–25 tailor (hem, sleeve, waist) as the highest-value spend in the closet.
- **The pieces:** dark straight or slim-straight jeans with a mid-to-high rise; the well-cut blazer; the trench; the white shirt; the knit that skims; the pointed or almond flat, the loafer, the low block heel; the structured bag; the silk scarf; gold or pearl earrings that catch light near the face; glasses frames that lift at the outer corner (drooping round frames drag the face down); the coat that fits at the shoulder.
- **Hair:** face-framing layers soften; one-length long hair on fine or thinning hair can pull the face down (the common stylist advice — hedge); the chin-to-collarbone bob lifts; bangs soften the forehead; volume at the crown counters the downward pull of the face; color one to two shades lighter than the pre-gray natural shade, warm glosses over flat black or brassy blonde; the set-and-sprayed helmet, the tight perm and the over-teased crown read as the "old lady" look the viewer fears; going gray on purpose — the grow-out with highlights and lowlights, the silver gloss, purple shampoo for brass, a sharp cut so gray reads deliberate; thinning hair — blunt cuts, root lift, dry shampoo for texture, the part change; never a diagnosis or a cure claim.
- **Makeup:** cream textures over powder (powder settles into lines); less foundation, more skincare; a lighter concealer, never a heavy one under the eye; blush placed higher on the cheekbone; defined, slightly fuller brows; skip shimmer on crepey lids; tightline and curl the lashes; a lip liner to stop feathering; a berry or red lip on a bare face (the French floor); SPF daily as the one cosmetic-skincare claim that needs no hedge.
- **The French documented practices** are in `steering.md` §2 (truth doctrine); the honest floor there is the capsule, the uniform, buy-less-buy-better, the trench and the Breton stripe, the scarf, low-maintenance hair, gray on purpose, the bare face and the red lip, the pharmacy culture, comfort in one's age. Hedge every "every Frenchwoman" to "most women I know in Paris".
- **Quality:** natural fibers and fabric weight, seam and stitch density, lining, the pull test on knits, buttons and buttonholes as the tells of a well-made garment; fast-fashion garments that lose shape after a few washes — the honest version of "the fast-fashion lie".

## 7. Safety, policy and integrity boundaries (flag inside briefs; these are niche hard rules)

- **Never shame the woman.** Adjectives attach to garments, cuts and habits; the viewer is never the problem. No "trashy", "fat", "ugly", "slutty", "mutton dressed as lamb"; "old lady" / "grandma" only in quotes as the feared look.
- **No diet, weight-loss, fasting, supplement or exercise-program content** (core wellbeing rules and the operator's niche rule). "Slimmer" is a line and a silhouette, never a weight.
- **No medical or procedure content:** skincare stays cosmetic and hedged; hair thinning is a styling topic; no Botox, filler, surgery, hormone or treatment advice; "younger" is a styling effect.
- **No sexual or suggestive framing** in titles or thumbnails.
- **Brands in text only**, no logos or reproduced designs; no implied sponsorship; no celebrity names by default.
- **No fake statistics and no checkable personal history** for either persona (§8).
- **Synthetic-media disclosure** stays on if a presenter is generated.
