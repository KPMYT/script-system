## 1. The spine — look younger, on four surfaces

The operator's launch brief: how to look good, look smart (polished), look younger — the hairstyles, the fashion and the colors that take years off. The four surfaces every batch covers: **hair** · **color** · **outfits** · **makeup**. Ch. 2 covers the same four through the French lens; its native additions are the capsule, the scarf and the trench, gray on purpose, the bare face, the pharmacy.

## 1b. The operator's angle grid and topic matrix (endorsed 2026-09-14)

**Angles** — every topic runs through one of three angle families, each mapping onto banked formats: **tricks** (to turn heads · to look younger / shave off the years · to solve X problem · on a budget / without spending a dime → F3, F12, F18 free rung, F25, F13) · **mistakes** (causing X problem · making you have X problem · that age you → F9, F33, F1) · **styles** (style mistakes · styles causing X problem · styles solving X problem · styles to use · trending styles of X month / year → F3-positive, F28, F25, F17, F5, plus the recency modifier).

## 4. Problem selection inside a proven template

Once a template proves out on a channel, the work is picking the tell: **universality of the tell** (gray hair, thinning, the black cardigan, the jeans — nearly every viewer owns the problem) × **the age stake** (how many years the fix visibly takes off) × **thumbnail visibility** (a before / after the b-roll can honor — hair and color beat "posture") × **how honestly the persona owns it** (the wealthy insider's verdict on ch. 1; a documented French practice on ch. 2 — no practice, no flip). Universality sets the view ceiling; the age stake sets the click. **Intake lesson (S1, 2026-09-14):** accessories are the apex of this formula — glasses (572k), earrings (199k) and necklaces (90k) beat color (62k / 42k), lipstick (66k), hair (57k / 30k / ≤30k) and the scarf (43k) on the same channel; nearly every viewer wears them, the before / after is instant on a face, and the fix costs under $100.
