# Audience in woodworking — how Profile A shows up here

Profile A (50+ American DIY homeowner) is defined in core `audience-profiles.md`; the operator confirmed woodworking shares it (2026-09-06). This file is the niche layer: mode, lever ranking, dead levers, money and time language, adversaries, honest mechanisms, safety.

## 1. The viewer, in this niche

A DIY-inclined hobbyist woodworker skewing **beginner-to-intermediate** (confirmed by the own winner "…Every Beginner Should Use"). Wants to build things that look professional, avoid ruining projects and wasting expensive wood, buy the right tools without overspending, and feel competent. Takes pride in craft and self-reliance; drawn to lost-wisdom and built-to-last narratives. Watches faceless how-to / tips heavily plus the adjacent trim / finish-carpentry and DIY-project pools.

**Mode:** browse. **Difference from plumbing:** the dominant emotions are competence / aspiration, craft mastery, tool-buying anxiety and fear of ruining a project — not water-damage fear or money bleeding from a home. Disgust barely applies. Money still matters (wood and tools are expensive) but the aspirational and mastery levers are stronger.

## 2. Lever ranking (best titles pull two or more)

1. **Competence / avoiding amateur shame** — I BET, Most Beginners Get This Wrong, skills that separate amateurs from pros. Confrontational (F6) and aspirational (F37) both work; the beginner skew makes this the top lever.
2. **Fear of ruining the project / wasting wood** — mistakes, NEVER, FATAL, "ruining your furniture", blotchy stain, split boards. A ruined hardwood board is real money.
3. **Money — save it and make it** — save THOUSANDS, cheap-beats-expensive, tools you actually need; plus the make-money angle (projects that SELL) and the look-expensive angle. Both proven on scrap topics.
4. **Lost wisdom / nostalgia** — old-school tricks, before power tools, the way it has always been done. Run honestly; **Sam's core lever**.
5. **Mastery / precision** — dead-flat, razor-sharp, no nails, the secret the West never learned. **Kenji's core lever**.
6. **Quality-decline / built-to-last** — modern furniture is disposable, why old pieces outlast new ones, the workaround. **Sam's second lever**.
7. **Resourcefulness** — use what you would throw away (scrap, offcuts, sawdust), one cheap board into many projects. The biggest validated demand cluster.
8. **Ease** — the easiest way possible, in 60 seconds, no special tools; friendly trigger for jobs perceived as hard (miters, finishing, joinery).
9. **Curiosity / contrarian** — "I don't get why anyone still…", pour glue in sawdust and watch. The F36 contrarian take was the single biggest outlier.
10. **Certainty and permanence** — GUARANTEED, FOREVER, never again.

## 3. Dead or weak levers here

Disgust (F20) and family emergency (F31) — no raw material. Water damage, sewage, the plumber's invoice — plumbing levers that do not exist here. Generic "N tips" without a specific topic tops out around 18k on Dale.

## 4. Money and time language (persona-locked)

- **Dale:** FREE → $1 / $5 micro-cost ("One $5 Board From Home Depot") → "Save $400" exact figures → THOUSANDS; SELL for Stupid Money; Look Like You Spent THOUSANDS.
- **Kenji:** "worth every penny", the tool worth buying once; never micro-cost or bargain framing.
- **Sam:** "built to last generations" vs "disposable"; never cheap, never fast.
- **Time:** "in 60 Seconds" and "Under 5 Hours" on Dale; time framing on Kenji only where it does not undercut the craft; Sam's time runs in generations.
- **Briefs carry real dollar anchors:** hardwood ~$6–15/board-foot, tool prices, what the pro / commercial equivalent costs.

## 5. Adversaries

Big-box furniture makers ("particleboard", "flat-pack"), tool marketers, "the woodworking stores", brand manufacturers, the modern disposable-furniture industry. The persona is the honest exception. Adversary appetite is gentler than plumbing's — use it for quality-decline and tool-marketing stories, not crusades.

## 6. Real mechanisms that support big titles

End-grain glue joints are weak (wick adhesive); wood moves across the grain seasonally (floating panels, elongated screw holes); mineral oil is non-curing — a temporary conditioner, not a finish, while cured film finishes are inert once cured; coped inside corners beat mitered ones as houses move; a coped / burnished trick hides small miter gaps while a tuned saw is the real fix; biscuits add alignment more than strength; exotic wood is rarely necessary.

## 7. Safety boundaries (flag inside briefs where relevant)

Table-saw kickback is the apex hazard — never the rip fence as a crosscut stop; riving knife / splitter; push sticks; never reach over the blade; no gloves or loose sleeves near spinning tools. Oily rags (linseed / tung) can spontaneously combust — dry flat or store in water / sealed metal. Never burn, compost, smoke-cook with, or use as animal bedding any pressure-treated, MDF, plywood or finished sawdust (formaldehyde, resins, arsenic in old CCA lumber); fine dust is a respiratory and explosion hazard. Untreated, food-safe materials only for anything that contacts food or animals.

## 8. Persona-claim discipline (core `rules-and-specs.md` §2.2)

Dale's "40 years at the bench" is a standing voiceover claim; Kenji's and Sam's traditions are real and documented — lean into them and never invent lineage, dates, districts or "banned" stories. Cultural authenticity is a hard floor on Kenji and Sam.

## 9. The money-motivated slice — Dale (post-pivot) and Hank Hardwood — labeled hypotheses

Inherited from Profile A as facts; the for-profit specifics are **hypotheses** from ecosystem data (74 titles, observed 2026-06-22) until ~20 own datapoints exist.

- **The viewer:** the same 50+ DIY homeowner, but arriving with a question — "could I make money from this?" — and a deeper doubt underneath it — "would anything I make actually sell?" Beginner-to-intermediate skew holds (the beginner-profit titles were among the largest). Side-hustle income, retirement supplement and "turn the hobby into a business" are the stated motives.
- **Lever ranking (hypothesis):** 1) certainty of sale — demand-overload proof (F51); 2) money earned — the earned-income scale, arbitrage multiples (F52), margin figures; 3) zero-cost input — free and waste material (F26 money-key, F30); 4) ease and low barrier — beginner projects, one board, five minutes (F41 / F42 / F12); 5) resourcefulness and yield — "30 products from one 2x4"; 6) competence and avoiding amateur shame — mistakes that kill the sale (F9 money-key); 7) curiosity / contrarian — the selling myth (F36); 8) lost wisdom as territory — Japanese / Amish / Viking tests. Disgust and family emergency stay dead; quality-decline reads here as a selling opportunity ("people are paying to escape flat-pack").
- **Money and time language:** the earned-income scale (core §5) — `$X each` → `$X in under an hour` → `$X/month` → `N% margin` → `THOUSANDS in profit` → `replace your salary`; one rung per title. Dale keeps the big-box anchor and the tenure money-key; Hank has neither. Hourly-wage framing killed by the operator. Time framing that works: five-minute, fifteen-minute, one-hour builds and "a week's income in six weeks" (craft-fair season).
- **Adversaries (gentle):** big-box stores (price and quality — "Big Box Hates Me"), boutiques and retailers charging the markup the maker could capture, stores that throw out pallets, "people who think it's hard". The persona is the honest seller who shows the real numbers.
- **Real mechanisms that support the big titles:** construction 2x4s cost $4–5 and yield dozens of smalls; HT-stamped pallets are free and usable (MB pallets are not); sawdust has real buyers (fire starters, smoker chips — hardwood only, bedding — untreated pine only) and costs nothing; end-grain construction, thickness and finish drive cutting-board prices more than species; dovetails and visible joinery lift furniture prices because buyers read them as quality; rough-sawn hardwood from a mill runs a third of big-box S4S; Etsy fees stack to roughly 10–20% of a sale; craft-fair booths cost $50–500 and sell impulse smalls in volume; Q4 carries about a third of a maker's annual sales; retail anchors (Boos, Williams Sonoma, West Elm) set the price ceiling for handmade.
- **Persona-claim discipline on the money channels:** income and order figures are persona narrative, never audited fact — no checkable counts, no named customers; every dollar figure in a title must have its cost-and-price math in the brief.
- **Safety and integrity boundaries specific to selling:** no treated, MDF, plywood, painted or finished sawdust in any product; hardwood and food-safe glue and finish on anything for food; no small parts on toys for under-threes; no treated wood on food-growing beds; lead-paint testing on pre-1978 furniture flips; oily rags dried flat; never a filmed proof-of-work claim.
