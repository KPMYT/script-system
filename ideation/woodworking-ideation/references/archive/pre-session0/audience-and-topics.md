# Audience Psychology, Per-Channel Personas, Topic Demand Map & Seasonal Calendar

## 1. The viewer

DIY-inclined hobbyist woodworker, skewing **beginner-to-intermediate** (confirmed by the operator's own winner "…Every Beginner Should Use"). Wants to build things that look professional, avoid ruining projects and wasting expensive wood, buy the right tools without overspending, and feel competent. Takes pride in craft and self-reliance; drawn to lost-wisdom and "built to last" narratives. Watches faceless how-to/tips content heavily, and the adjacent trim/finish-carpentry and DIY-project pools.

Note the difference from the plumbing audience: woodworking's dominant emotions are **competence/aspiration, craft-mastery, tool-buying anxiety, and fear of ruining a project/wasting wood** — not water-damage fear or money bleeding from a home. Disgust barely applies. Money still matters (wood and tools are expensive), but the aspirational and mastery levers are stronger.

## 2. Psychology levers (best titles pull two or more)

- **Competence / avoiding amateur shame** — I BET, Most Beginners Get This Wrong, skills that separate amateurs from pros. Confrontational (F6) and aspirational (F37) both work; the beginner skew makes this the top lever.
- **Fear of ruining the project / wasting wood** — mistakes, NEVER, FATAL, "ruining your furniture", blotchy stain, split boards. Hardwood is expensive, so a ruined board is real money.
- **Money — save it and make it** — save THOUSANDS, cheap-beats-expensive, tools you actually need; AND the make-money angle (projects that SELL) and the look-expensive angle (looks like you spent THOUSANDS). Both proven on scrap topics.
- **Lost wisdom / nostalgia** — old-school tricks, before power tools, the way it's always been done. Run honestly; **Sawdust Sam's core lever**.
- **Mastery / precision** — dead-flat, razor-sharp, no nails, the secret the West never learned. **Master Kenji's core lever**.
- **Quality-decline / built-to-last** — modern furniture is disposable, why old pieces outlast new ones, the workaround. Deeply resonant; **Sawdust Sam's second lever**.
- **Resourcefulness** — use what you'd throw away (scrap, offcuts, sawdust), one cheap board into many projects. The scrap/sawdust cluster is the biggest validated demand.
- **Ease** — the easiest way possible, in 60 seconds, no special tools. Friendly trigger for jobs perceived as hard (miters, finishing, joinery).
- **Curiosity / contrarian** — "I don't get why anyone still…", pour-glue-in-sawdust-and-watch. The F36 contrarian take was the single biggest outlier.
- **Certainty & permanence** — GUARANTEED, FOREVER, never again; removes the fear of wasted effort on a chronic problem.

## 3. Per-channel persona guide (the steering layer)

All three are faceless; the persona lives in voiceover. Match topic + format to the persona so titles read native. When the operator doesn't name a channel, default to **Dale**.

**Dale Oakfield's Workshop — standard/Western woodworking.**
- Persona: veteran American woodworker, ~40 years at the bench, practical, mixes power and hand tools, talks to beginners as a patient mentor.
- Topic fit: the whole demand map — tips listicles, beginner mistakes, tool-buying, jigs, table-saw upgrades, scrap/sawdust, finishes, cheap-lumber builds, seasonal gift builds.
- Format fit: the entire bank. Native to big-box/cheap-lumber anchors ("$5 board from Home Depot"), value verdicts, mistakes.
- Register: friendly, thrifty, practical. This is the volume channel.

**Master Kenji — Asian/Japanese woodworking.**
- Persona: Japanese master craftsman; precision, sharpening, hand tools, joinery that needs no nails or glue, reverence for wood and tool.
- Topic fit: hand-tool technique (chisels, hand planes/kanna, Japanese saws), sharpening and waterstones, traditional Japanese joinery (dovetails, mortise-and-tenon, kumiko, sashimono), wood selection and grain, fine natural finishes, tool maintenance. Less generic "N tips"; more "the one joint", "the sharpening secret".
- Format fit: F19 in an Eastern key ("the method the West forgot"), F36 ("why the West still does X the hard way"), F37 ("amateurs from MASTERS"), F22 (the joint masters always use), precision F5/F18, hand-tool F13/F28, F32 (myth-busting Western habits).
- Register: mastery and precision, NOT thrift. Avoid big-box/$5-hack anchors and "in 60 seconds" cheapness where it undercuts the craft. Money language becomes "worth every penny".

**Sawdust Sam — Amish/traditional woodworking.**
- Persona: old-order traditional craftsman; hand tools, minimal or no electricity, furniture built to last generations, the way it's been done for 200 years, quiet distrust of modern disposable goods.
- Topic fit: hand-tool methods, no-power solutions, old joinery, traditional finishes (milk paint, shellac, boiled/raw oils, wax), restoring and using old hand tools, wood-movement wisdom, furniture that lasts, "why Amish/old furniture lasts". Not tech-tool-heavy.
- Format fit: F19 (lost wisdom — his strongest lane, zero fabrication needed), F2 (veteran authority), F29 (quality-decline/built-to-last), F4 (insider betrayal), F21 (abandoned methods), F26 (waste-nothing resourcefulness).
- Register: heritage, durability, self-reliance. Avoid big-box/disposable/speed anchors. Money language is "built to last" vs "disposable", not micro-cost hacks.

Cross-channel note: the same proven topic can often run on all three by swapping the persona lens — e.g., wood joints as Dale's "7 Joints You Should NEVER Use", Kenji's "The Japanese Joint That Needs No Nails", Sam's "The Joint Furniture Makers Used for 200 Years". Variants across channels are intentional.

## 4. Topic demand map

**Validated (from ecosystem data — the primary source):** scrap/offcut/waste-wood projects (biggest, 3 signals — sell, look-expensive, clever-uses, and gravity/illusion sub-angle), sawdust uses, workshop jigs/devices, table-saw upgrades, wood joints (3.3m ceiling — the strongest single topic), bad-miter/gap fixing, cutting-board and wood finishes (mineral-oil myth, food-safe finishes), tool-buying (expensive-vs-actually-needed, cheap-beats-expensive, pre-purchase gate — highest monetization), beginner mistakes / amateur-vs-pro, seasonal & cheap-lumber builds (Dale-only register), general tips listicles (the operator's own proven format).

**Adjacent, validated (frame craft-side):** trim & finish carpentry — caulking/gaps, sagging doors, baseboard skills, damaged door/jamb repair, trim mistakes. Huge raw demand; keep it woodworker-framed, not handyman.

**Mapped, not yet produced (future batches draw here first):** wood movement & why joints fail, sharpening (Kenji), hand-plane technique (Kenji/Sam), wood selection & grain reading, traditional finishes & milk paint (Sam), old-tool restoration (Sam), Japanese joinery deep-dives (Kenji), staining/blotch control, glue-up and clamping, shop dust collection & safety, plywood vs solid vs MDF, "why modern furniture is disposable" (Sam), workshop setup on a budget (Dale), router/table-saw technique, finishing myths (F32).

**Problem-selection criteria when slotting a topic into a proven template:** how universal the problem is × how much it costs to get wrong (wasted wood/tools) × how aspirational the payoff × how frequent. Universality sets the view ceiling; the beginner skew rewards "avoid this mistake" and "the skill that levels you up".

## 5. Seasonal calendar (publish at the season's front edge)

- **Q4 / holidays:** gift builds, projects that sell at craft fairs, "build these Christmas gifts from one board", ornaments — high-volume season (proven: the Christmas-builds video).
- **Spring:** outdoor projects, garden furniture, planters, workshop setup/spring-clean-the-shop.
- **Summer:** beginner-project season (new hobbyists), deck/outdoor builds, drying-and-finishing (humidity affects finish/movement — a real angle).
- **Winter:** indoor shop projects, tool maintenance and sharpening, jig-building, tool-buying (post-holiday, gift cards) — pairs with the high-monetization tool content.
- Evergreen anchors (joints, finishes, tips listicles, scrap/sawdust, tool-buying) carry the schedule between seasonal spikes.

## 6. Production guardrails worth keeping

- Claims in titles can be exaggerated; claims in briefs/scripts must hold up — the persona's trust is each channel's moat. For Kenji and Sam this includes cultural authenticity: Japanese joinery and Amish craft are real, well-documented traditions with abundant honest ammunition — lean into the real tradition, never invent fake history, dates, or "banned"/"suppressed" stories.
- Real mechanisms that support big titles: end-grain glue joints are weak (wicks adhesive), wood moves across the grain seasonally (floating panels, elongated screw holes), mineral oil is non-curing (temporary conditioner, not a finish; cured film finishes are inert once cured), coped inside corners beat mitered ones as houses move, a coped/burnished trick hides small miter gaps while a tuned saw is the real fix.
- Safety boundaries appear in briefs where relevant: table-saw kickback is the apex hazard — never use the rip fence as a crosscut stop, use a riving knife/splitter and push sticks, don't reach over the blade, no gloves/loose sleeves near spinning tools; oily rags (linseed/tung) can spontaneously combust — dry flat or store in water/sealed metal; never burn, compost, smoke-cook with, or use as animal bedding any pressure-treated, MDF, plywood, or finished sawdust (formaldehyde, resins, arsenic in old CCA lumber), and fine dust is a respiratory + explosion hazard; untreated, food-safe materials only for anything that contacts food or animals.
