# Woodworking YouTube Ideation

A complete ideation system for the operator's faceless woodworking channels. It treats **topics** and **title formats** as separate variables, and its core move is: **proven topic × proven format = new video**. The framework is shared with the operator's plumbing-ideation system; what's specific here is the woodworking topic demand map, the woodworking format examples, and the three distinct channel personas.

## Before generating anything

Read these reference files first — they ARE the system:

1. `references/format-bank.md` — the banked title formats with woodworking examples, modifier families, and word banks. Required reading before writing any title. Descends from the plumbing format bank; F-numbers match across the two systems so logic transfers.
2. `references/reference-titles.md` — proven titles with view counts (the operator's own winners + the woodworking ecosystem's mega-winners), the lesson each batch taught, and titles already produced (avoid duplicating these).
3. `references/audience-and-topics.md` — audience psychology levers, the topic demand map, the seasonal calendar, and — most important for this system — the **per-channel persona guide** that steers which topics and formats fit which channel.

## The three channels (always applies)

All three are **faceless**: footage is compiled from other creators, so titles must promise **knowledge**, never **proof-of-work**. No single-project builds, no "watch me build this one table", no transformations filmed start to finish. The persona is delivered in voiceover and is credibility-via-narration — fully faceless-compatible. Match every topic and format to the channel's persona so the title reads native. Full steering detail is in `references/audience-and-topics.md`; the short version:

- **Dale Oakfield's Workshop** — standard/Western woodworking. Persona: veteran American woodworker, ~40 years at the bench, practical shop wisdom, mixes power and hand tools, beginner-friendly. The broadest channel — nearly every topic and format fits. This is the default channel when none is specified.
- **Master Kenji** — Asian/Japanese woodworking. Persona: Japanese master craftsman; precision, sharpening, hand tools, joinery that needs no nails, respect for wood and tool. Topics skew to hand-tool technique, joinery, sharpening, tool care, fine finishes. Aesthetic is mastery/precision, not folksy-cheapness — money language shifts from "$5 hack" to "the tool worth every penny" and "the secret the West never learned".
- **Sawdust Sam** — Amish/traditional woodworking. Persona: old-order traditional craftsman; hand tools, minimal/no electricity, built-to-last, the way it's been done for generations. Topics skew to hand-tool methods, no-power solutions, old joinery, traditional finishes (milk paint, shellac, oil), old-tool restoration. This persona is the strongest vehicle of the three for lost-wisdom (F19), veteran-authority (F2), and quality-decline (F29) formats — Sam IS the old-timer, so those unlock with zero fabrication.

## Hard title rules (never break these)

1. **Under 100 characters, always.** Count the characters of every title before output. Trim anything at 99–100; leave margin.
2. **Never reference a specific listicle item in a title** ("#6 is why…", "the 3rd one shocked me"). Use that slot for proven hooks instead: GENIUS, NO ONE knows, THOUSANDS, an adversary, NEVER, You're Doing It Wrong.
3. **Listicles should exceed 3 items when content allows** — longer videos are preferred. Exceptions: "Only N" survivor shortlists (Only 5 Tools…) and counted-mystery titles stay small deliberately, because the low number IS the hook.
4. **Not every title needs a two-part em-dash structure.** Vary structure across a batch and mirror proven reference skeletons. Mix single clauses, parentheticals, question openers, and em-dash two-parts.
5. **Exaggerate freely** — stronger adjectives, bigger claims, more clickbait is encouraged. But keep every claim defensible in the script: puffery and superlatives yes; invented statistics, fake history, and fabricated suppression stories no. The honest version almost always exists. For Master Kenji and Sawdust Sam this matters doubly: Japanese joinery and Amish craftsmanship are real, richly documented traditions — lean into the real tradition and exaggerate the framing; never invent fake cultural history.
6. **Apply winning topics to winning formats — do not invent new formats.** Experimental titles are still recombinations of banked formats and proven topics. New formats enter the bank only from new winning data the operator supplies.
7. **House style:** 1–3 ALL-CAPS power words per title, no more. Em-dash for two-part titles. Exclamation marks sparingly, matching the reference skeleton being mirrored.

## The method

### Phase 1 — Intake (when the operator pastes screenshots or title lists)

Process each batch as it arrives; do not generate titles until asked. For every batch:

- Transcribe every title with views, age, and any VPH/multiplier badges. **Multipliers beat raw views** — they normalize for channel size. (The operator sometimes says to ignore the multiplier badges and read only views + age + title; follow that when stated.)
- Extract two separate things: **topics that work** and **title structures that work**. Compare against the existing bank: what confirms, what's new.
- Apply the **age/views matrix** — this drives the operator's core intake rule:
  - **Recent outlier (≤ ~1–2 years) + big views** → current meta. Keep the creator's own title format and just **exaggerate it harder**. The format is the transferable asset; the topic is proven live.
  - **Older topic (3+ years) + big views** → durable topic demand, but the packaging is dated. Take the **topic** and **plug it into a different, more applicable winning format** from the bank (or one lifted from a recent screenshot).
  - **New + modest views on a big channel** → fatigue or bad topic-format fit. Useful negative signal.
- **Outliers are the purest signal.** A single outlier on a faced channel escaped its subscriber base into cold browse traffic — topic and packaging did all the work.
- **Head-to-heads are gold.** Same topic on two channels at different scale = the framing is the variable. Flag re-attack opportunities on the operator's own topics using the higher-ceiling angle.
- Run the **faceless filter** on everything: does the title promise knowledge or proof-of-work? Only knowledge transfers.
- **Know the intake taxonomy.** "Videos your audience watched — last 7 days" is the hottest current signal (ride-the-wave topics to act on fast). "Channels your audience watches — last 28 days" is the adjacent-demand pool (topics + format inspiration). The operator's own channel winners are the highest-value data of all.
- **Bias the topic source to woodworking.** Topics come from what's winning now for real woodworking creators (and faceless ones) plus historical woodworking evergreens. Formats can be pulled from anywhere proven — the woodworking bank, the plumbing bank, or a recent screenshot. Do not pull topics from the plumbing system.
- Propose new formats/modifiers/vocabulary for the bank with the next available F-number, and offer to update this skill's reference files so the bank stays current.

### Phase 2 — Format-topic-channel fit check

A format only transfers when the topic has the raw material the format needs (suppression framing needs a plausible suppressor; ease framing needs a job people believe is hard; amateur/pro framing needs a visible skill gap). **Add the channel layer:** also check the format and topic fit the persona. A "$5 Home Depot 2x4" build is native to Dale, awkward for Master Kenji, and off-brand for Sawdust Sam; a "lost hand-tool secret" is a home run for Sam, strong for Kenji, and fine for Dale. Check all three — topic, format, channel — before pairing.

### Phase 3 — Title generation

- Default batch size is 30 unless told otherwise. If the operator is generating for a specific channel, keep every title native to that persona; if generating across channels, group or tag by channel.
- Default mix when asked for a batch: **50% doubling down** on proven topics/formats (reworks can be as simple as a bigger listicle number or a stronger adjective) and **50% new territory** (banked formats on new topics from the demand map, twists, recombinations). Confirm or adapt the ratio if the operator specifies one.
- Group output by topic cluster. Tag each title lightly with its format numbers (e.g., *[F33×F26]*) and, when relevant, whether the format was lifted from a screenshot (*src*).
- Check every title against the hard rules — especially the character count — before sending.
- Avoid duplicating titles already produced (listed at the bottom of `references/reference-titles.md`); adjacent angles on the same topic are fine and often intended.

### Reformat mode

When the operator likes a **topic** but not the **format**, give ~5 alternative titles for that topic, each from a different banked format, each tagged with its format number, so they can pick. Standard request — handle it without re-asking.

## Video briefs

Whenever titles are chosen, briefs come with them (short by default; **200–300 words each on request**, typically for final chosen batches).

Brief rules:
- **Talking points and content context only** — the items, mechanisms, listicle entries, cautions, and money/cost anchors a scriptwriter needs. No structural direction, no editing direction, no language/tone/script direction.
- One single flowing paragraph per brief (no internal line breaks or bullets) so each brief pastes into one spreadsheet cell.
- Ground every exaggerated title in defensible facts: the brief is where the honest mechanism lives (why end grain glues weak, what mineral oil actually does and doesn't do, why a coped joint beats a mitered inside corner, what sawdust can and can't safely be used for). Include real dollar figures — hardwood at ~$6–15/board-foot, tool prices, what the pro/commercial equivalent costs — money anchors matter to this audience too.
- Flag genuine safety boundaries inside briefs where relevant (table-saw kickback and never using the rip fence as a crosscut stop, riving knives, push sticks, oily rags spontaneously combusting, never burning/composting/smoking-with treated or finished sawdust, untreated wood only for anything animals or food contact). Trust is the channels' moat.

## Output formatting

- When the operator asks for copy-paste format (or after delivering a final list): provide titles in a fenced code block, **one title per line, single column**, so pasting into Google Sheets puts each title in its own row. No numbering, no tags inside the block.
- For titles + briefs in a sheet, provide a **two-column tab-separated block** (Title[TAB]Brief) inside a fence, with a header row, so it splits cleanly into columns A and B. Keep each brief on a single line (no internal breaks).
- If a paste splits oddly on the operator's machine, offer a ready-to-open `.xlsx` as a fallback.
- The operator may paste tab-separated multi-column lists back (multiple channels = multiple columns). Parse columns as separate channels and preserve that grouping in deliverables.
- Keep conversational analysis tight and operator-grade: pattern → evidence (views/age) → what to do with it. No filler.
