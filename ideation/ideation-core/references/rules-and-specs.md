## 1. Universal hard rules

Never break these. Overlays may add niche rules; they may not relax these.

1. **Under 100 characters, always.** Count the characters of every title before output. Anything at 99–100 gets trimmed — leave margin.
2. **Never reference a specific listicle item in a title** ("#6 is why…", "the 3rd one shocked me"). Use that slot for a proven hook instead — GENIUS, NO ONE knows, THOUSANDS, an adversary, NEVER, You're Doing It Wrong, GUARANTEED.
3. **Listicles exceed 3 items when the content allows** — longer videos are preferred. Exceptions: "Only N" survivor shortlists (F28) and counted-mystery titles (F23) stay small deliberately, because the low number IS the hook; those videos still run long by covering everything that fails.
4. **Vary structure across a batch.** Not every title needs a two-part em-dash structure; sometimes a single clean clause is stronger. Mix single clauses, parentheticals, question openers, em-dash two-parts and caps-the-skill openers, and mirror the overlay's proven skeletons closely.
5. **Exaggerate freely; keep every claim defensible.** Stronger adjectives, bigger claims, more clickbait are encouraged. But every claim must hold up in the script: puffery and superlatives yes; invented statistics, fake dates, fake history and fabricated suppression stories no. The honest version almost always exists ("BURIED since 1947" → "the trick plumbers used before drain cleaner existed"). Where a persona draws on a real tradition (Japanese joinery, Amish craft), lean into the real, documented tradition and exaggerate the framing — never invent cultural history.
6. **Banked formats only.** Apply winning topics to winning formats; do not invent formats. New formats enter the master only from new winning data or an explicit, tagged operator endorsement.
7. **No fabricated proof-of-work.** "I tested" survives only as a persona verdict, never as a filmed experiment the channel did not run; rankings are research-based guides, never fake hands-on claims (`framework.md` §7).
8. **Produced-title dedup.** Never re-issue a title in the overlay's `produced-log.md`; adjacent angles on the same topic are fine and often intended.

## 2. Conditional rules keyed to dials

Each rule switches on for channels whose registry / steering line carries the named dial value. They are not optional for those channels.

**Search dial (demand shape = search, or the search slots of a mixed channel)**
- **Keep the search keyword intact.** "How to crochet a granny square" must survive the exaggeration — search titles are search plays first, clickbait second. Browse titles may exaggerate freely.
- The caps budget may go on the technique itself (F40) instead of a hype word, because the searched-for skill is the power word.
- Tutorial briefs spec the teachable content (§4).
- Direct one-to-one transformations of proven tutorial titles are a legitimate default batch mode; tag each with its source views (*← 13M*).

**Face / persona dial (presence = face, or a named voiceover persona)**
- **Persona-claim discipline.** Only warm, soft, uncheckable backstory ("I've made one for every grandchild", "in 45 years of plumbing"). No fabricated checkable facts, no awards, no counts of students, no named districts or first-hand dates, and no heritage claim attached to a checkout button. Cultural personas hedge community-universal claims ("the way I was taught", "in many communities").
- **Register lock.** A channel has one register and never mixes it: register mixing muddies browse identity. The register is recorded in steering; vocabulary, money language and format choice follow it.
- **Territories, not identities.** A region, era or tradition (Appalachian, homestead, Depression-era make-do) is a series territory any persona can visit — a theme, never a claimed identity. Living named communities and ethnic identities are never claimed as persona identity; topic-level coverage only, respectful.
- **Generated visuals never demonstrate technique.** The presenter talks; the owned hands library and diagrams demonstrate. Synthetic-media disclosure stays on for realistic presenters.
- Formats that claim tenure (F2) or an in-group (F22 first-person) are available only where a persona exists to claim them. A faceless-neutral brand-voice channel with no persona cannot run F2.

**Presence filter (applies per dial, never assumed faceless)**
- Faceless channels promise **knowledge**, never proof-of-work: no single-project transformations, no "watch me build this one table", no filmed builds. Persona claims in voiceover are fine — credibility is narrative, not visual.
- A faceless channel with an owned hands library may additionally run follow-along tutorials at whatever share its steering block allows.
- Face channels may run follow-along tutorials and persona specials; proof-of-work still needs a real filmed build, which no current channel has.

**Profile-keyed tone (from `audience-profiles.md`)**
- Confrontational formats (F6) run at full strength for profiles with high adversary appetite and are softened — challenge wrapped in rescue — for profiles with low adversary appetite.
- Stake vocabulary follows the profile: money-loss for the homeowner profile, time-loss ("months of work") first for the crafter profiles.

## 3. House style

- 1–3 ALL-CAPS power words per title, no more (on search titles the technique may take the caps budget — F40).
- Em-dash for two-part titles. Exclamation marks sparingly, matching the reference skeleton being mirrored.
- Title in the viewer's sensory vocabulary, not the technical cause.
- One money/time scale per title (`format-bank-master.md` §6).
- US terms are the default where a craft has regional term divergence (US crochet terms; flag when a title could confuse).
- Multi-channel variants of one concept are intentional; label which channel each belongs to.

## 4. Brief spec

Whenever titles are chosen, briefs come with them: short by default; **200–300 words each on request**, typically for final chosen batches.

- **Facts only.** Talking points and content context: the items, mechanisms, listicle entries, materials, specs, cautions and money/time anchors a scriptwriter needs. No structural direction, no editing direction, no language/tone/script direction.
- **One flowing paragraph per brief** — no internal line breaks or bullets — so each brief pastes into a single spreadsheet cell.
- **The honest mechanism lives in the brief.** Ground every exaggerated title in defensible facts; the overlay's `audience.md` lists the real mechanisms that support big titles in that niche.
- **Anchors.** Include real dollar figures (service-call costs, part and material prices, what the pro or commercial equivalent costs); where the profile's sharpest stake is time, include hours as well as dollars.
- **Safety and integrity boundaries** appear in briefs where relevant; each overlay lists its own (gas lines, table-saw kickback, baby-item hazards, pattern IP, and so on). The channel's trust is its moat.
- **Tutorial briefs (search dial)** also spec the teachable content: materials and sizes, construction sequence, counts or multiples, finished size, the 2–3 failure points, and total cost.

## 5. Output formatting

- **Generation layout — always (operator standing order, 2026-09-25).** Every generation output — a batch, a sift, a reformat request — is laid out by topic, never as a flat list:
  - **Topic header.** `Topic N — [the topic in plain words]. Sources:` then one line per source video: the full title verbatim (emoji kept) · views · age, then its age/views verdict in plain words (`framework.md` §6) — `Recent, so exaggerate.` · `Recent, but exaggerate only on the honest parts.` (name the part that fails the defensibility rule: "Replace", "10X", an unnamed "they") · `Old, so re-package.` · `Old, but a strong escape — exaggerate once, re-package the rest.` · `Footage-bound — topic only.` (the presence filter, `framework.md` §7). Add the channel's letter or name when the intake has one.
  - **Titles** under their header, numbered continuously across the whole output (numbers never restart per topic, so picks by number stay unambiguous). Under each title, one italic tag line: `Exaggerated · topic + format ← [views]` when one source's own format is pushed harder; `Reformat · topic ← [views] · format F[n] ← [views]` when the topic goes into another banked format — the format's views are the evidence the title's skeleton actually mirrors (the niche's own intake example if there is one, else the master's founding or strongest evidence); `New · format F[n] ← [views]` for new territory with no intake topic. Stacked formats cite the one carrying the hook, plus a modifier only when the modifier is the hook (expected-answer negation, authority tag). Candidates say so (`product villain (candidate) ← 568k`); a format lifted from a fresh screenshot rather than the bank is tagged `format src ← [views]`. After a `·`, a few words name what the title withholds or the honest mechanism it rests on, where that isn't obvious.
  - **New territory** runs as the last topic block, headed as evergreen with no intake source.
  - **Format key** at the end: every format cited, with the full source title + views + where it came from (own / niche ecosystem / adjacent niche), so briefs and the `thumbnails` skill can find each source by name (the full-title convention, `filing-rule.md` §1).
  - **Close:** pick guidance where it helps (near-twins, lens caps, seasonal timing, candidate tests) and, on a sift, a suggested shortlist by number.
- **Copy-paste titles:** on request, or after delivering a final list, provide titles in a fenced code block, **one title per line, single column** — no numbering, no tags inside the block — so pasting into Google Sheets puts each title in its own row.
- **Titles + briefs for a sheet:** a **two-column tab-separated block** (Title[TAB]Brief) inside a fence, with a header row, each brief on one line, so it splits cleanly into columns A and B.
- **Three-column variant (operator request 2026-06-22, adopted 2026-09-09):** Title[TAB]Reference[TAB]Brief — the Reference column carries the full source video title(s) + views the title was built from — used for the woodworking for-profit batches; use it whenever the operator asks for sources alongside briefs.
- **Large batches:** default straight to a ready-to-open `.xlsx` for 30+ briefs; offer `.xlsx` as the fallback whenever a paste splits oddly on the operator's machine.
- **Multi-column input:** the operator may paste tab-separated multi-column lists back (one channel per column). Parse columns as separate channels and preserve that grouping in deliverables.
- **Multi-channel output:** group or tag by channel.
- **Conversational analysis** stays tight and operator-grade: pattern → evidence (views / age / channel normal) → what to do with it. No filler.
- **Thumbnail seed** for greenlit titles only: one line — proof moment + layout hint + reference pointer (`filing-rule.md` §4).
