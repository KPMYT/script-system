## Strengths

1. **Research and accuracy.** Checked on 2026-10-22: the Xtava recall (2018, about 235,000 units, 18 burns) and the Vornado SRTH recall (June 2026, about 255,000) are right. NEW 15's opening ("where fires turn deadly … it's the bedroom") stretches NFPA's 2016-2020 numbers (bedroom 25% of home fire deaths, living room 26%).
2. **Safe, with the line drawn:** "Finding it is your job. Opening the wall isn't." Each item ends on a free fix for tonight, a sign to watch for and a rule for when to throw it out.
3. **Named enemies and callbacks:** trapped heat, "the drift", "the second storm", "the stand-ins", "dead air", "the recall gap", "the head start".
4. **Good hooks:** NEW 5 "The hurricane isn't the thing most likely to burn your house down. The week after it is."; NEW 19 "Find the outlet closest to wherever you're sitting right now… count what's plugged into it."; NEW 15 "A fire in the kitchen gets noticed. A fire in the bedroom gets a head start."; NEW 3's split-house burn at IBHS's lab in South Carolina.
5. **More "you" than plumbing run 02:** about 27 a 1,000 against 21 (Roger's 453x hit: 41).

## Gaps

1. **The hook takes back the title's feeling.** NEW 1 "Firefighters don't unplug everything before bed, and you don't have to either."; NEW 7 "On almost any night, that battery is fine by breakfast."; NEW 19 "Almost everything you just counted is fine."; NEW 23 "Most of it is perfectly fine." The Notion profile asks for it ("never alarmist", "reassure that most of what's plugged in is fine"); it runs against the plumbing decision to reaffirm the title's emotion throughout and against the Fireman steering in the home-security ideation skill ("beg you", "please").
2. **Little push:** "but" about 1 a 1,000 (Silver Cymbal about 20); 0-5 questions a script, 12 of 23 with one or none; no villain beyond objects; the family-safety fear always played soft.
3. **Name intro in all 23** ("I'm Frederick Barnes, and this one's for whoever…"), against the locked no-intro rule; usually 150-300 words before item one (NEW 2's answer at about word 430).
4. **Teases:** "by the end" in about six scripts, "Stay with me to the end" (NEW 17), long-range promises ("Hold onto that. We'll get there.") in nearly all.
5. **CTAs:** mid 148-193 words (Roger's hit template 60-80; woodworking winners 45-65); the banned "no rush" family in 18 of 23 ("There's no deadline, so whenever suits you is fine", "It'll keep", "Nobody's timing you", "Tonight, next week, or never. No clock on it from me"); step-by-step camera instructions; "None of this started with me" style disclaimers in at least 7.
6. **Invented first-person stories** (allowed by the profile, against the library's claims policy): "I pulled the tray out of my own toaster once and found enough crumbs to feed every bird on the block"; "I've seen a pickup parked square on top of a shelter lid"; "I've watched a neighbor run a generator cord through a kitchen window and prop the window up with a paperback"; "[chuckles] I've caught myself nudging a heater closer with my heel"; NEW 15 "I walk houses with the people responsible for them".
7. **Format:** voice tags in 16 of 23 (from NEW 7); 906 curly quotes and 65 em dashes (NEW 13-23); "e-book" twice; early scripts end on an extra line promising a future video.
8. **Sameness across the channel:** space heater in 17 of 23, electrician hand-off 21, parent's house 17, palm on the plate 14, the 10-year alarm 14, e-bike 12, closed bedroom door 11; the three storm videos (NEW 5, 8, 10) share about six items; NEW 3 and NEW 12 share five items and the same lab.
9. **Titles not fully delivered:** NEW 4 names no alarm; NEW 21's number one (the oxygen concentrator) is something the script says to leave on; NEW 18 says space heaters cause "only about a third" of heating fires.
10. **Fast narration:** about 195 words a minute (Roger's hits 134-161).
