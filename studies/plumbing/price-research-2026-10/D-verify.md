# Group D verification: damage costs, licensing, DIY shelf

Checked 2026-10-21 (US) by a second, independent checker against sources the researcher (D-research.md) did not use. Method: WebFetch where the site allowed it (Target, Bob Vila, Fixr, This Old House, Florida Legislature, Manatee County opened; Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart, Ace, Kroger, Amazon, HomeGuide, Forbes, Bankrate blocked fetches), otherwise WebSearch result snippets restricted to named retailer or publisher domains. About 45 searches used.

**Caveats that apply to the whole table**
- Retail prices are snapshots. Lowe's, Ace and Menards prices mostly come from search snippets of product pages. Several of those pages are flagged as cached months or years ago (noted where the search said so). Menards prices often carry an 11% mail-in rebate; the figures below are before the rebate.
- Target prices were read directly from target.com search pages today, so they are the freshest figures here.
- HomeGuide and Forbes figures come from search snippets only (both block fetches); Forbes pages show no date in the snippet.
- Fixr was a researcher source for several damage items. Where I cite a Fixr figure the researcher did not use, it is marked "same publisher" and not counted as fully independent.

## Notes

**Changes a viewer could easily dispute**
1. **Heinz vinegar.** The researcher's "$10-$20" for a gallon of Heinz comes from Walmart listings that look like third-party sellers. Grocery prices are $5-$8 (H-E-B $5.12, Albertsons $5.29). A viewer who buys Heinz would catch this right away. Use "about $5 to $8".
2. **Name-brand peroxide.** Swan 32 oz is $2.59-$3.99 in hardware and grocery stores, not "up to about $10". The viewer's "close to 5.00" fits a pharmacy or name-brand bottle, so the safe line is "about a dollar for the store brand, a few dollars for a name brand".
3. **Florida $5,000 fine.** Florida Statute 455.228 lets the state fine the unlicensed *practitioner* up to $5,000 per incident. It does not fine the consumer. The Manatee County page words it as if the hirer gets fined, but the statute doesn't say so. Don't tell viewers they can be fined $5,000 for hiring someone.
4. **Adjustable wrench.** "$8-$15" is store-brand money. At Lowe's a Kobalt 10" is about $20 and a Crescent about $30. Say "$10 to $20 for a basic one".
5. **Wet-dry vac.** "$25" is a sale price. Regular prices at Lowe's, Ace and Menards are $40-$50. Say "around $50".
6. **Outdoor faucet cover.** A single foam cover is about $3-$5 at Menards and Lowe's. "$7-$12" is the price of a 2-pack or a fabric sock.
7. **Faucet supply lines.** Single 20" braided lines are $6-$8 each at Lowe's and Menards, so a pair is $10-$15, not $7.
8. **Closet auger.** A basic Cobra is $10 at Menards, so "$22" is not the floor. Say "$10 to $25".
9. **Borax and citric acid.** Target now has borax at $7.19, so say "$6 to $7". Target's 1 lb citric acid is $14.95 on sale ($18.95 regular), so say "$10 to $15".

**Damage costs**
- **Subfloor (DISPUTED).** Fixr (Jan 2025) gives much lower numbers for the subfloor work alone: $150-$200 around a toilet and $500-$750 for a bathroom. Its $25-$45/hr labor rate looks low, though, and the figures may leave out pulling the toilet and redoing the floor. The researcher's $400-$2,500 comes from Block Renovation and Angi. Fixr's overall typical range ($600-$2,500) overlaps it. The safest line describes it in words: a few hundred for a small patch, $1,000-$2,500 once the floor and toilet come up.
- **Mold average (ADJUST).** Bob Vila (Apr 2024: average $2,254, typical $1,125-$3,439) backs This Old House, not Fixr's $3,500 average and $9,000 top. Don't call $9,000 "typical".
- **Sink base cabinet.** HomeGuide fills the gap the researcher left: one stock cabinet box is about $250-$600. The figure is from a snippet only, since the page blocks fetches. Lowe's sells a 36" unfinished sink base for $164, close to the researcher's Home Depot $149-$189.
- **Insurance claim.** $15,400 stands as the Triple-I figure. Be careful with "frozen pipe" wording: State Farm says its freeze-related claims (Jan 2024-Jun 2025) averaged over $30,000. If a script talks about burst or frozen pipes in particular, "$15,000" may sound low to an adjuster in the audience. Policygenius's $11,098 is from 2023; don't use it.
- **Flooded basement.** The other sources put the top of the typical range a bit higher (up to $10,000 in 2026 cost guides). $2,000-$7,000 is still fine for "most".

**Old sources (pre-2024) found while checking.** Bob Vila ceiling repair (Nov 2022), Bob Vila plumber cost and permit figures (Jun 2023), Policygenius water-damage statistics (2023). Several Lowe's and Ace product pages were flagged as cached 3-8 years ago (Kobalt wrench, GE silicone at Ace, Frost King FC1 at Ace, Channellock at Lowe's). Treat those as support only. The Target prices (fetched today) and Menards prices (current rebate dates) are the freshest.

**Store brand vs name brand to watch in scripts.** Peroxide (up&up/Equate $1 vs Swan $3-$4), vinegar (store $4.59 vs Heinz $5-$8), plungers (Dealworthy $4.99 vs Korky $12-$21), wrenches (store $8-$13 vs Crescent ~$30), supply lines (RELIABILT/ProLine $6-$8 vs Fluidmaster $9.68, or $9.99 each at Ace). Wherever a brand is named on screen or in the script, quote that brand's price, not the store-brand floor.

**Not checked independently.** Store-brand peroxide: Kroger and Walgreens pages blocked, so only Target (also the researcher's source) re-confirmed $0.99 today. The license-check method rests on low-weight third-party pages plus the researcher's state-board pages; it's procedure, not a price, so it's low risk.
