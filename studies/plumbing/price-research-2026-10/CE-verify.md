# Groups C and E price check (second, independent checker)

Checked 2026-10-21 against `C-research.md` (13 jobs) and `E-research.md` (24 jobs). National US figures, used only as rounded on-screen estimates.

## How this check was done

- For each job I counted only publishers the researcher did **not** use for that job. The main ones were **Modernize** (pages updated Aug 12, 2026), **Thumbtack** (2025-2026), **Today's Homeowner** (2025), **Homewyse**, **Family Handyman**, **USGS**, the **Water Research Foundation** study, **IAPMO** and **UpCodes** for code text, **ARS/Rescue Rooter** (a national franchise page), and **True Value / Do it Best** for parts.
- **Read live** means I downloaded the page and read the figure on it myself. **Via search** means the figure came from the search engine's summary. HomeGuide, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Forbes, Lowe's, Ace, SupplyHouse, HomeServe and Walmart all blocked downloads (403 or bot walls). Home Depot, Thumbtack, Modernize, Today's Homeowner, TOH, True Value and Do it Best could be read.
- **Thumbtack has two kinds of figure.** Its headline ranges ("according to Thumbtack data") are national data. Its bulleted flat rates ("a Thumbtack pro in Poway charges...") are single-pro examples. I mark the second kind as **pro example**: these are weaker than a national figure, though better than nothing.
- **Searches used: 29**, well under the roughly 2-per-job allowance. Most of the work was done by reading pages directly.
- Rows: E17 (sump) and E18 (alarm and smart valve) are each split into two rows, because their parts got different verdicts. The table therefore has 40 rows: 13 for C and 27 for E (24 jobs, plus 8a, 17b and 18b).

## Notes

### Sources that are not independent of each other
- **This Old House and Today's Homeowner print the same plumbing cost table**, word for word: faucet replacement $150-$600, burst pipes $1,000-$4,000, leaking pipes $150-$450, plumbing reroute $700-$1,500 and so on. Count them as one source. E's "TOH much higher" burst figure is that shared table.
- **Modernize repeats some Fixr figures.** Its sump install table (submersible $90-$1,000, pedestal $80-$800) and its frozen-pipe "in-wall $150 to $500" match Fixr. I did not count those lines as independent. Modernize's sump *replacement* ($800-$1,600), slab, burst and repipe figures are its own.
- **Cost to Renovate** says it bases its national ranges on Fixr and Homewyse, so it is not independent of C's main shutoff sources.
- **True Value and Do it Best** list identical items at near-identical prices (they are now under one owner). Count them as one retailer.
- **HomeServe's sink-drain $100-$350** matches Angi's exactly and may be borrowed.

### Pre-2024 or old material still in the research (flag before quoting)
- Fixr burst-pipe page (updated Aug 2022): used in E3, E4 and E16.
- Bob Vila repipe page (Apr 2023), which only repeats Angi: E6.
- This Old House leaky-faucet article ($10-$15 cartridge), first written in 2002: C6.
- Home Depot HOMEWERKS supply line (price valid in 2022), Lowe's snapshots "possibly several years old", and Ace "very old listing" entries: C4, E10, E13.
- State Farm's $10,200 average washer-hose claim is 2020 data and still unverified (the page returns 503 again).
- Consumer Reports' "expect your cost to almost double" (an older article): E18.
- My own WRF figure (C13) comes from a 2016 study. It is cited only to show that EPA's average is at the high end, not as a script number.

### Figures a viewer could easily dispute
1. **"The national average repipe is $7,500"** (E6). Thumbtack's national average is $3,364 ($1,743-$6,490). Use a range, not an average.
2. **"A drip a second wastes 3,000 gallons a year"** (C11). USGS's drop size gives about 2,000. Say "2,000 to 3,000", or attribute the figure to EPA. Drop "a slow drip can waste 20 gallons a day": at the USGS drop size, even one drip per second is under 6 gal/day.
3. **"A battery backup costs $600 to $2,000 installed"** (E17b). Modernize says $200-$500, and Today's Homeowner says $1,000-$3,000. Say it in words.
4. **"A smart shutoff is $600 installed"** (E18b). That is a floor. Plumbers' own pages quote $900 to several thousand.
5. **"Burst pipe: $400 to $1,500"** (E3). Thumbtack's national figure is $232-$764, so a viewer with a $300 bill will object. Start at $200.
6. **"Angle stop: $50 to $150"** (C2). Most plumbers' minimums are $100 or more.
7. **Slab reroute "$4,000 to $15,000"** (Fixr, E2). Modernize says $1,500-$5,000+. Keep $15,000 for whole-house work only.
8. **"Sump pump replaced for $350"** (E17a). Thumbtack's lowest national figure is about $400, and Modernize starts at $800.
9. **Washer-hose interval.** ARS says 3-5 years, against GE, Whirlpool and CT's 5 years. "Every five years at the latest" is safe.
10. **Healthy pressure.** Some sources say 60-70 psi is ideal (one line on the Today's Homeowner page). "Around 50 to 60" stays inside every source, and the 80 psi code limit is solid (UPC 608.2 read live on IAPMO; IPC 604.8 via the ICC page summary).

### Code facts (80 psi PRV rule)
- **UPC 608.2** (IAPMO Code Spotlight, read live): "Where static water pressure in the water supply piping is exceeding 80 psi (552 kPa), an approved-type pressure regulator preceded by an adequate strainer shall be installed and the static pressure reduced to 80 psi (552 kPa) or less." Regulators of 1-1/2 in and larger need no strainer. UpCodes (read live) has the same text.
- **IPC 604.8** (ICC; read via search summary because the ICC page renders by script): "Where water pressure within a building exceeds 80 psi (552 kPa) static, an approved water pressure-reducing valve conforming to ASSE 1003 or CSA B356 with strainer shall be installed to reduce the pressure in the building water distribution piping to 80 psi (552 kPa) static or less." Some state versions (e.g., Pennsylvania) exempt lines to sillcocks and outside hydrants. The researcher's quote is correct.
- Script line "Code says anything over 80 needs a pressure regulator" is accurate under both model codes. Local adoption can vary, so "most plumbing codes" is the safer wording.
