### How often to test (exercise) the main shutoff

**Figure:** once or twice a year. Open and close the valve fully, then back it off about a quarter turn from fully open. If it is stiff, do not force it.

Sources:
- City of Decatur, Indiana, "Meter and Main Valve" (PDF), https://cityofdecatur.in.gov/documents/Meter%20and%20Main%20Valve.pdf: "It is also a good idea to open and shut off the valve a couple times a year. This exercise could keep the valve from becoming stuck when you need it most." Not dated. [read on page]
- Balkan Plumbing, "How To Close A Water Shut Off Valve And Avoid Costly Repairs", https://balkanplumbing.com/how-close-water-main-line-shut-off-valve/: "it is advisable to exercise all water valves in your home about once a year." Modified 2025-10-01. [read on page] (A plumbing-company blog.)
- Milwaukee Valve, "Move It or Lose It" (PDF), https://milwaukeevalve.com/wp-content/uploads/02-19-25-move-it-or-lose-it.pdf: "a single home's water main valve can be checked less frequently. But even at a minimum, you'll want to exercise valves at least every few years, if not more often." 2025. [read on page] (The manufacturer's minimum.)

**Agreement:** agree that once or twice a year is common advice, but the sourcing is thin: a city sheet plus a plumber blog. The manufacturer's minimum is looser ("at least every few years").

### Water meter leak check (method; no price)

**Method:** turn off every faucet, appliance and the irrigation system. Read the meter, or watch the low-flow (leak) indicator, which is a small red or blue triangle, star, gear or snowflake on the dial. EPA says to wait 2 hours without using water; Denver Water says 30 minutes or more. If the meter moves at all, there is a leak. Then close the house's main shutoff. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house; if it keeps moving, the leak is in the service line between the meter and the house.

Sources:
- EPA WaterSense, "Fix a Leak Week", https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week: "Check your water meter before and after a two-hour period when no water is being used. If the meter changes at all, you probably have a leak." [read on page]
- Portland Water Bureau, "How to check for leaks", https://www.portland.gov/water/water-efficiency-programs/find-leak: "The leak detection dial may be a red or blue triangular-shaped dial or a blue snowflake-shaped dial. It may spin rapidly when water is running. If water is dripping, the leak detection dial may move slowly." [read on page]
- Denver Water, "Leaks", https://www.denverwater.org/residential/services-and-information/troubleshooting-and-repairs/leaks: "After 30 minutes or more, take another look at the dial. If the hand or the number wheels have moved, you have a leak ... Now, close the main shut-off valve. If the indicator stops, your leak is inside the building." [read on page]
- Seattle Public Utilities, "Detect Leaks", https://seattle.gov/utilities/your-services/water/metering/detect-leaks: if the indicator keeps moving after the building's shutoff is closed, the leak is probably underground between the meter and the valve. [via search summary]

**Agreement:** agree.

**Bill guidance:**
- EPA, "Fix a Leak Week" (above): "Take a look at your water usage during a colder month, such as January or February. If a family of four exceeds 12,000 gallons per month, there could be serious leaks." [read on page]
- Austin Water, "Water Leaks", https://www.austintexas.gov/water/water-leaks: "compare current water use to the same months of previous years. Unusually high use may indicate a leak." [read on page]

**Agreement:** waste per year agrees (EPA 9,300-9,400 gallons now, nearly 10,000 in 2022-2024; LADWP up to 10,000). **A percentage increase that signals a leak was not found:** no utility gives a number such as "25% higher". The defensible lines are the EPA winter test (more than 12,000 gallons a month for a family of four) and comparing the bill with the same month last year. "Leaks can add as much as 10% to a bill" is EPA's figure for the leaks' share of a bill, not a warning threshold.
