# Group B price research: water heaters, gas, temperature and energy

Researched 2026-10-21. US national figures. Every price below is a range or average that a named source states. Script figures are rounded the way the brief asks.

## How the figures were collected (read first)

- **Fetched and read directly:** Fixr, This Old House (TOH), Bob Vila, Modernize, Home Depot (search pages, prices read on 2026-10-21), ENERGY STAR, A.O. Smith (hotwater.com), an American Water Heater manual PDF, and a CPSC PDF. Quotes from these pages are exact.
- **Read through search-result summaries only:** HomeGuide, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Forbes Home and energy.gov blocked direct fetching (HTTP 403/404). Their figures come from domain-restricted web search summaries, so they may be paraphrased, and a few may be attributed to the wrong page. Check them on the live page before a script quotes one as "according to".
- **Not independent:** Angi and HomeAdvisor are the same company (Angi Inc.). Bob Vila's cost guides say their figures come from Angi/HomeAdvisor. None of the three counts as independent of the others. Each job below names at least one source from a different company, or says it is thin.
- **Search cap reached:** the session's web-search limit (200 searches) ran out during the job. The last jobs (recirculation pump, mixing valve) were researched by fetching known publisher pages instead, and those are the thinnest.
- **Lowe's** blocked fetching, so store part prices are Home Depot only, sometimes checked against a cost guide's part figure.
- Store prices change often. Treat them as of October 2026.

### 7. How often to check an anode rod

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Inspect | First check after 6 months, then at least once a year (manufacturers) |
| Replace | Typically every 3-5 years (cost guides); sooner with softened water |

**Sources**
- A.O. Smith, "Water Heater Maintenance Guide", https://www.hotwater.com/info-center/water-heater-maintenance.html: "To extend the life of your water heater, we recommend inspecting the anode rod once a year." Replace "If the sacrificial anode rod shows signs of deterioration or has several inches of exposed core wire". (fetched 2026-10-21)
- American Water Heater, Residential Gas Water Heater Use and Care Guide (PDF), https://assets.americanwaterheater.com/damroot/Original/10017/100400765.pdf: "remove and inspect anode rod after first six months of operation and at least annually thereafter." (fetched 2026-10-21)
- HomeGuide repair guide, https://homeguide.com/costs/water-heater-repair-cost: "Plumbers recommend replacing the anode rod every 3 to 5 years." (search summary)
- Bob Vila, https://www.bobvila.com/articles/water-heater-repair-cost/: "an anode rod should be replaced at least once every 5 years." (2023)
- Fixr gas page, https://www.fixr.com/costs/gas-water-heater: "Inspect and replace anode rods every 2–3 years." (2026)
- Softened water: the American Water Heater manual (search summary) says a water softener makes the rod "deplete faster than normal". A Rheem bulletin, as quoted on Money Pit (secondary), recommends annual inspection with a softener.

**Agreement:** agree. Manufacturers say check yearly. Replacement every 3-5 years is the common guidance (Fixr says 2-3). Script-safe: "check it once a year; most last about 3 to 5 years."

### 19. Gas leak: what to do, and typical repair cost

**What to do (non-price, two or more sources agree):**
1. Leave the house right away. Get everyone out, and go upwind if outside.
2. Do not create sparks: no light switches, matches, appliances, flashlights, or starting a car.
3. Call 911 or the gas utility **from outside, at a safe distance**. AGA says not to use a phone inside.
4. Do not go back in or turn the gas back on until the utility or a pro says it is safe.

| DIY part | Pro range | Average |
|---|---|---|
| n/a | Reachable leak about $120-$800; buried or in-wall leak $1,500-$5,000 | $400 (Fixr, gas line repair) |

**What drives the price:** where the leak is (an exposed fitting vs buried or in a wall), digging or drywall, pipe replacement, emergency or after-hours fees, and the pressure test. Leaks on the utility's side of the meter are usually fixed by the utility at no charge.

**Sources (what to do)**
- PG&E, "Gas Safety", https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/gas-safety.html: leave the area; avoid "cell phones, flashlights, light switches, matches" until at a safe distance; "call 9-1-1 first, then" PG&E (search summary).
- American Gas Association, "Using Natural Gas Safely", https://www.aga.org/natural-gas/safety/using-natural-gas-safely/: do not light matches, change switches, use a flashlight or start a car; do not use a telephone inside; "find a phone away from the area and call 911 or your natural gas utility" (search summary).
