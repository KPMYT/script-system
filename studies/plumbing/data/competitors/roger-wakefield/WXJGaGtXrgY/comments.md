13. (1 likes) There's one thing you didn't mention regarding tank vs tankless - SPACE. Probably 4-5 years ago, I replaced my old tank water heater when it started having issues and I had learned about the anode rod and found that it had been gone for who knows how long. I replaced it myself as my stepfather taught me real world skills, including plumbing and sweating pipes. We've been happy with our tankless. But with regards to space. Our 'mechanical room' that includes our washer, dryer, water heater and furnace is cramped. With the tank water heater, it was REALLY hard to get the blower out of our furnace when it failed. The water heater made it cramped to get clothes in and out of the dryer. The tankless, mounted up on the wall, freed up a LOT of space. As far as gas requirements, it actually was pretty easy with no re-piping - the gas co came out and upgraded our meter to a 2psi unit, and they put a regulator right where it goes into the house to step it down to the proper 8" WC or so. From that regulator, it's about 12" to the first tee that goes to our dryer (22K BTU), then another 18" or so to the tee for the tankless (199.9KBTU), and then after that is the last device, our furnace (56k/80k BTU). The ONLY issue I've had with our tankless has been on some REALLY hot humid summer days were the hot air being sucked in from outside doesn't shed its moisture in the inline condensation trap and ends up condensing on the much cooler components in the water heater, triggering a moisture detected shutdown. I called Rheem and they weren't much help. It's installed the way they say to install it with a condensate trap on the air intake, but it can only do so much. The 'fix' is to take the cover off and run it without the cover and drawing air from inside during these really hot & humid days. Otherwise, it's been great. Hot water for days, and lots of freed up real estate in our 'mechanical room'.
