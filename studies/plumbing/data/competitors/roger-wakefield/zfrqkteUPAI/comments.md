1. (0 likes) Wrong! Water heaters are intentionally engineered to fail upon draining. The drain valves are made narrow, and do not open full bore. What will often happen is they get stuck open and now the tank is standing in a garage waiting to flood it. Here’s the bullet proof method. Turn off the water and power. Be sure to measure voltage is zero across the upper element. Remove the anode. You need a new one anyway. Insert a garden hose into the tank through the open andode access. Syphon out all water. Remove the entire valve assembly and replace it with a 8 in nipple. Use a rubber boot to connect a wet and dry vacuum to the nipple. Connect a sprayer water wand to the end of a gardenhose and stick it through the anode hole. Make sure the max water pressure out of the wand is not higher than the house water pressure. Turn on the vacuum and then start using the wand targeting the bottom and the area of the lower element. Run about 20 gl of water through the wand while moving it around. Remove the wand. Insert a new anode. Remove the vacuum attachment. Install a brass lever action ball valve at the end of the nipple. Throw away the old valve. 😇 Turn on the water until the house hot water runs evenly. Turn on the power.
