8. (0 likes) For a tankless water heater the biggest concern is the water intake temperature vs the outlet temperature at the required flow rate.  If your ground water is cold (ours is 57F) any heater has to work harder to get it to the recommended 120F.  This results in plumbers always recommending the biggest tankless heater they can sell you.  Here's what we did ... We only have electricity at out location.  No gas.  We replaced the old 50 gallon water heater with a 10 gallon heater set to minimum temperature around 86F.  I installed a small tankless that took the 86F water and boosted it to 120F easily.  The rated 1.3 gpm tankless heater with 57F input water temp easily delivers 3.8 gpm with an intake temp of 86F.  A 10 gallon tank costs <$200.  A 13kW 240V tankless heater costs <$200.  Add sundry plumbing hardware (valves, flexible pipes, sealant, etc.) and electrical hardware (50A breaker and possibly heavier gauge wire if the old heater wire is inadequate) and you're looking at a cost of $500 - $600.  Our actual cost, after buying the tank and heater on sale totaled less than $400!  If you have to get an electrician to install 8 gauge wiring and a new breaker you can add another $150 - $450 depending on the difficulty and distance for the cable runs. It's all about the water intake temperature when planning a tankless system.  A few years ago I built a new home with a well.  We pumped the water into a RO (Reverse Osmosis) tank which fed a 30 gallon cistern located in the insulated attic.  That gravity fed two small electric tankless units.  The water stored in the unheated cistern warmed from the internal building space to a typical 70F - 75F enabling the use of smaller tankless heaters that would be necessary with the cold ground water temps.  The smallest tankless system was located in the guest bathroom and only used when we had visitors.  The other was located on the kitchen wall that backed onto our main bathroom.  Perfect.
