# 05 Emotions: what these videos make viewers feel, and what landed

Study date: 2026-10-20. Covers all 80 videos: the operator's three channels (Roger the Pipe Guy, Mark Silverbridge, The Vintage Pipefitter) and five competitors (Silver Cymbal, Roger Wakefield, Got2Learn, Arrow Pest Service, TheCheapFix).

Sources: `README.md` and `01`-`03` in this folder, all 80 notes, the eight per-channel transcript files (every own hit and every TheCheapFix hit read in full; the openings and emotional peaks of every other video read closely), and every `comments.md`. Nothing in the library or the study data was changed.

## How to read this

- **Measured** means counted from the data. **Inferred** means my reading. Each section says which is which.
- Script quotes are verbatim auto-captions (a few words may be misheard), with the timestamp of the caption line where the quote starts. Comment quotes are verbatim; like counts are in brackets; no usernames.
- Tags read: channel, video ID, [time], **hit** or **recent**, views, views/normal. "Hit" means the channel's most-viewed group (Feb-Aug 2026 for own channels; Got2Learn's are 2017-2021). "Recent" means the newest five (own: Oct 2026, 1-8 days old).
- "Illustrative" lines were written for this report. None copies 8 or more words from any transcript (checked by script).

## The limits (read first)

1. **Hits and recent uploads are from different months and a different template.** Own recent videos were days old and have 4 real comments between them. So comments describe the hits only, and any hit-versus-recent difference may be the script, the season, the algorithm or audience fatigue.
2. **About 40 comments per video**, top plus newest. Rough counts below are hand counts or keyword counts on that sample.
3. **Keyword counts are blunt.** A word like "honest" or "yourself" can appear without the emotion. Where a count matters I checked the lines by hand.
4. **No thumbnails, retention or click-through.** A hook's emotional pull can't be separated from the thumbnail's.
5. **Face and faceless differ.** Arrow, Silver Cymbal and Roger Wakefield sell a real person; some of their emotional pull (family warmth, trust) can't be copied by an AI voice.

## The short version

1. **The hits run on household pain plus money, not on anger.** Every own hit puts a small fix against a big bill. The emotions that drew the warmest comments were relief ("it worked", "first time in months"), pride ("72 years old and looking forward to seeing what else I can do"), nostalgia ("my grandfather gave me these pointers") and the satisfaction of a clear drain. Comments almost never echo anger at plumbers.
2. **The enemy frame drew the single most-liked pushback on the operator's biggest Roger hit:** "How would I ever know if my plumber has clog problems in his drains at his own home? 🙄" [1.3K]. Viewers love a *friendly* plumber's secret ("A plumber I knew long ago... told me to run the hot water" [155]); they doubt a *hiding* plumber.
3. **Frustration with failed attempts is the strongest hook engine.** "This morning, the sink drains exactly as slow" (Mark's top hit), "$47... fighting one ring in one toilet" (Vintage), "tried everything" (Roger's best recent). It names the viewer's own wasted money and effort, then promises the end of it.
4. **Distrust of the store shelf works better than distrust of the trade.** Bleach tablets, chemical drain cleaners and "15-minute" jugs are safe villains: the persona can attack them without insulting anyone the viewer likes.
5. **Safety fear lands when it is concrete and backfires when it is vague.** "It will put you on the floor" (chlorine gas) drew no complaints; sewer-gas danger on the S-trap video drew "I don't remember seeing anything in the news talking about all the deaths from sewer gasses" [81], and a water-heater list was called "nothing but a scare tactic video".
6. **Viewers protect each other.** The most-liked comments on several videos are safety corrections ("Borax laundry soap is not the same thing as boric acid" [4.3K]; "make sure your tank only has water inside" [96]). When a script gets safety wrong (boiling water, pouring washing soda dry), the backlash is emotional and repeated (about 18 boiling-water objections and about 17 hardening reports on own hits).
7. **Myth-busting a cherished habit cuts both ways.** "The foam looks like work. It is theater." is memorable, but about 23 comments across the study defend baking soda and vinegar, one with 289 likes. Honour the habit, then upgrade it.
8. **Nostalgia and lineage are under-used in the recent template.** Uncle Ray sits in 4 of 5 Vintage hits and 0 of 15 recent uploads; first-person "my own house" lines sit in 4 of 15 hits and 0 of 15 recent. Lineage is the theme commenters volunteer most on competitor hits (about 46 comments in 20 videos).
9. **The recent template leans harder on urgency.** "Tonight", "right now", midnight and 2 a.m. scenes appear in the first minute of 7 of 15 recent uploads against 2 of 15 hits, and the "not a scam, just economics" aside in 9 of 15 recent against 1 of 15 hits. Neither is proven to hurt; neither is linked to the hits.
10. **The length and the AI voice are emotional too.** Impatience ("Why do we suffer through a 20 minute video that could be done in 5 minutes?" [428]) and AI fatigue ("Thanks for being real and not AI" [1K] on Arrow) are the two feelings that most often turn a viewer against an otherwise useful video.

## 1. Emotion catalogue (18 triggers found)

Each entry: what it is and the pain under it; where it is used; verbatim lines; comment evidence; how strongly it goes with the hits; illustrative lines.

### E1. Fear of the big bill ("this could cost you thousands")

**What and why.** The dread of a repair bill far bigger than the problem looks. For older homeowners on fixed or planned budgets, a four-figure surprise is the pain itself. It almost always comes as a contrast: a cheap fix now against an expensive repair later.

**Where.** Titles ("Costing You THOUSANDS!"), hooks (a dollar figure in the first minute in 8 of 15 own hits and 11 of 15 own recent; measured), and as the closing line of nearly every list item.

**Verbatim.**
- "We are talking about $2,000 to $5,000 or more in repair costs. All because of a product that costs about $8 a bottle." (Roger, WfAwg1l0xCE [5:23], hit, 1.22M, 453x)
- "50 cents versus 20 bucks versus 300. That's the whole game." (Roger, kHT6ZL8At0w [8:00], hit, 451k, 168x)
- "300 for the fixture, a couple hundred more for the labor. $500 to get rid of a stain." (Vintage, hOz49worQJg [1:02], hit, 167k, 275x)
- "By then, you're looking at hundreds or even thousands of dollars in repairs you could have avoided." (Mark, VZo8y6AplVQ [0:04], hit, 184k, 335x)
- "That's not a $500 problem. Replacing a drain field runs deep into the thousands." (Vintage, uWXIUmWdPeY [11:09], hit, 240k, 395x)
- "And yet the average homeowner pays a plumber $300 or more every single year for problems this $1 bottle could have fixed in 15 minutes." (TheCheapFix, ls7BwoRhM9Q [0:04], hit, 2.18M, 285x)
- "Right now, somebody is about to lose 20, 30,000 dollars on a water damage claim and they have no idea that is coming." (Roger Wakefield, Wav7A7YhduY [0:15], recent, 3.2k, 0.61x)

**Comments.** Viewers repeat the sums back: the 1.5K-like recap on WfAwg1l0xCE ends "A couple of bucks spent on one is better than hundreds spent on having a plumber clean your drain". "Saved me a $300+ plumbing bill!!" (ls7BwoRhM9Q). But exact prices are disputed when they look dated: "This guy prices are from 1924, Amazon - BIO-CLEAN $79.95"; "3-4 dollars,it's 22$ for 55oz on amazon"; "Every bottle of anything in these AI videos is $1 a bottle. Maybe 30 years ago!" [48]. About 6 price disputes on own hits, 7 on competitor hits (kw, hand-checked).

**Association.** Measured: a money comparison is in 15 of 15 own hits and 15 of 15 own recent; competitors use it on every plumbing channel. It is universal, so it does not separate hits from recent. Inferred: it is the entry ticket, not the differentiator.

**Illustrative (written for this report).**
- "A drip you shrug at in October can be a ceiling you pay for in March."
- "The part costs about what a sandwich costs. Skipping it can cost what a used car costs."
- "Nobody budgets for a flooded hallway, and that's exactly why it hurts."

### E2. Resentment of the service call ("$200 for ten minutes")

**What and why.** The sting of paying a stranger a lot for a short visit. It rides on the call-out fee and the feeling of being at someone's mercy.

**Where.** Mostly hooks and item endings. Own hits voice it sharply; own recent uploads voice it and then soften it.

**Comments.** Landed when aimed at labour cost, not at people: "Is not the cost of the parts is the cost of the LABOR that gets expensive." [97]; "Plumbers on emergency hours are super expensive and the kit is cheap insurance." [14] (Got2Learn). Backfired when aimed at the plumber's honesty: the 1.3K and 108-like "how would I know" comments on YjPAVZAffh0; "lousy framing" (newest); on TheCheapFix's HVAC hit, "No HVAC TECHNICIAN GIVES A CRAP IF YOU 'KNOW' this 'TRICK'" [25] and "I don't believe that they are actually trying to keep things from us." [21]. Hand count: about 5 frame-disputing comments on own hits, about 7 on TheCheapFix; essentially no comments agreeing that plumbers hide things.

**Association.** Measured: call-out words in 8 of 15 own hits and 11 of 15 own recent (kw). The "scam / economics" aside: 1 of 15 hits, 9 of 15 recent. Inferred: resentment is fine as a sting about *price*; as an accusation about *people* it costs trust and adds nothing measurable.

## 5. What backfired

Measured from comments unless marked; counts are rough (about 40 comments a video).

1. **Insulting or accusing plumbers.** The "plumber hides it to pay his mortgage" opener (YjPAVZAffh0) drew the most-liked doubt in the own data ([1.3K], [108], [41], plus "lousy framing", "Inane"). On TheCheapFix's HVAC hit, about 5 technicians mocked the trade-as-villain frame and warned the advice would damage equipment ("This AI garbage will destroy your AC unit."). No comment in the sample agreed that tradespeople hide fixes. Inferred: this audience has a plumber they like, and the frame asks them to distrust him.

2. **Fearmongering without proportion.** "This is nothing but a scare tactic video." (VZo8y6AplVQ); the S-trap danger framing ([81], [27] "I am 78 years of age and I have never found either a P or S trap without sufficient water"); "you act like washing soda is going to eat you alive" (Rb0aS97YnSA). Got2Learn's sewer-gas danger drew corrections too ("Methane is not toxic", noted in `02`). The difference with the hits that worked: a named chemical reaction and a specific step ("Bleach plus acid makes chlorine gas") drew thanks; vague dread drew eye-rolls.

3. **Unsafe advice, which turns trust into anger.** Boiling water (about 18 objections on own hits: "This advice is disturbing.") and pouring washing soda dry (about 17 hardening or failure reports: "Thanks to this video I had to hire a plumber!"; "Hey Rodger, be nice... It's going to cost me thousands of dollars now"). These are the angriest comments in the own data. Inferred: an emotional promise ("never touches your pipes") broken by the method costs more trust than any hook earns.

4. **Clickbait promises the script walks back.** "Forever" then "Nothing in plumbing is permanent"; "ANY Drain Clog" then "What it will not do is dissolve a wad of hair"; "SURPRISE" with predictable results. Comments: "AI junk could of been said in less than a minute, thumbs down!" [68]; "I finish it with zero information". Inferred: the honesty inside the script is good; the title's overclaim is what gets punished.

5. **Myth-busting a loved habit.** About 23 comments defend baking soda and vinegar against Mark's, Vintage's and TheCheapFix's "theater" lines; the top one has 289 likes. Mild, not hostile, but it moves the comment section from "thank you" to "you're wrong".

6. **Overstated disgust and fake demonstrations.** "Horror movie" was mocked; "Where is the footage of him doing the tests?"; "Stolen content user. Blocked!" (sXlzedri_Hg). Inferred: claimed first-hand drama in a faceless video invites a credibility check the video can't pass.

7. **Condescension and padding.** "We don't need all that explanation of what causes the clog. Just tell us how to unclog it." (the only comment on AiUUNM24kCo); "Why do we suffer through a 20 minute video that could be done in 5 minutes?" [428]; "To make this watchable, increase the speed to 150%" [70]. ## 6. Ranked shortlist: the emotions most worth building scripts around

Ranked by evidence strength (hits plus comments), then by fit with a faceless veteran-plumber persona and the claims policy (no false facts, no health claims). Each says how to use it without overclaiming.

**1. Frustration of failed attempts, resolved (E6 into E8).**
Evidence: opens Mark's top hit, two Vintage hits and the best recent video on Roger and on Vintage; viewers answer with their own long "I tried everything" stories (about 21 across the study) and "it worked" reports ([346], [304]).
Use: open on the exact thing the viewer already tried and how it felt ("still slow the next morning"), explain *why* it failed in one plain sentence, then deliver the fix and the sensory payoff. Don't promise "every" or "forever"; say what it clears and what it won't.

**2. Small fix versus big bill, with relief (E1 into E14).**
Evidence: in all 15 hits; the shape of every competitor hit; viewers repeat the sums back.
Use: one dated "about" price range for the fix and one for the call-out, sourced or given as typical. End each item on relief ("nothing is broken", "you're fine on cast iron"). Avoid "$1 a bottle" style prices viewers can check and dispute.

**3. Being let in, with lineage (E11 plus E15).**
Evidence: "my own house" lines in 4 of 15 hits and 0 of 15 recent; Uncle Ray in 4 of 5 Vintage hits and 0 of 15 recent; lineage is the theme viewers volunteer most on competitor hits (about 46 comments) and on own hits ([100], [21], [72]); gratitude for generosity ([81], [1.1K]).
Use: one short personal-routine line and one mentor or "old way" line per video, consistent with the persona file (one tenure number, never contradicted). No claimed demonstrations or tests the footage can't show.

**4. Distrust of the shelf, not the trade (E3, with E10 curiosity).**
Evidence: present in nearly every own video; lands in comments ("They should have a warning label about that! 🙄"; [961], [619] on Silver Cymbal); the trade-as-enemy version drew the biggest pushback.
Use: name the object (the blue tablet, the 15-minute jug) and the mechanism, and cite a maker's own warning when one exists. For curiosity, use "what plumbers do at home" or "the real reason", not "what they hide from you". Present limits plainly ("it won't dissolve an object").

**5. Pride in self-reliance (E13).**
Evidence: the most moving comments in the study come from older viewers who did it themselves ([304], [662], [13], [53], [167]).
Use: end with the viewer able to *judge* as well as *do* ("you'll know which job is yours and which is a call"). Steps concrete enough to try: quantities, times, what it looks like when done. Keep the "call a pro" triggers specific so pride never becomes risk.

**6. Hidden damage, answered quickly (E4 into E14).**
Evidence: in the hook of 6 of 15 hits against 2 of 15 recent (kw); viewers carry the worry into comments (pinhole leaks, artery picture).
Use: describe the slow process in one picture (grease setting like wax), then hand the viewer the simple check. Keep the artery comparison as a picture only; make no health claims and don't imply plumbing affects health.

**7. Relief from household shame (E9).**
Evidence: under-used in scripts, but viewers' comments show relief when a smell turns out not to be their fault ([304], [312], "So it isn't my fault").
Use: "That smell isn't your cleaning; it's a dry trap." Never shame the viewer; take the blame off them, then fix it.

**8. Satisfaction (E8), as the promised ending.**
Evidence: what viewers come back to report ("loud pop", "first time in months").
Use: promise one concrete sensory payoff in the hook (the gulp, the smooth bowl) and pay it in the last minute. Describe it as typical, not guaranteed.

**9. Protectiveness and safety as care (E16 plus E17).**
Evidence: reassurance clauses ("Safe For Pets!") carry Arrow's hits; viewers protect each other with safety corrections ([3K], [4.3K], [234]); proportionate warnings drew no complaints, vague danger drew "scare tactic".
Use: one fixed safety block (3% peroxide only, never mix, hot tap water not boiling, dissolve washing soda first), framed as looking after the household. Use "safe for septic / old pipes" in a title only when the script and sources support it.

**10. Rule-curiosity with a wink (E18).**
Evidence: two of the biggest plumbing hits in the study are "why it's NOT ALLOWED" explainers; viewers value the reason ([1.4K]) and joke about inspectors ([292], [1K]).
Use: explain the reason, say plainly when the viewer doesn't need to change anything, then give options. Use code facts that can be checked; say codes vary by place.

**Use sparingly or not at all.**
- **Resentment of plumbers (E2 as accusation):** no measured lift, clear pushback. Keep the price sting; drop the motive.
- **Urgency hooks (E5):** rose in the recent template (7 of 15 against 2 of 15 hits) without evidence of lift. Better as the disaster the fix prevents than as a fake countdown.
- **Disgust (E7):** a short jolt, not a theme; never inflate it ("horror movie").
- **Myth-busting (E12 against the viewer):** confirm good habits; when correcting a loved one, credit the viewer's thrift first.
