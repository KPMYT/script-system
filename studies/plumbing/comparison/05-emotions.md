# 05 Emotions: what these videos make viewers feel, and what landed

Study date: 2026-10-20. Covers all 80 videos: the operator's three channels (Roger the Pipe Guy, Mark Silverbridge, The Vintage Pipefitter) and five competitors (Silver Cymbal, Roger Wakefield, Got2Learn, Arrow Pest Service, TheCheapFix).

Sources: `README.md` and `01`-`03` in this folder, all 80 notes, the per-channel transcript files (every own hit and every TheCheapFix hit read in full; the openings and emotional peaks of every other video read closely), and every `comments.md`. Nothing in the library or the study data was changed.

## How to read this

- **Measured** = counted from the data. **Inferred** = my reading. Each entry says which.
- Script quotes are verbatim auto-captions (a few words may be misheard), with the timestamp of the caption line where the quote starts. All 170-odd attributed script quotes were checked against the transcripts by script. Comment quotes are verbatim, likes in brackets, no usernames.
- Tags read: channel, video ID, [time], **hit** or **recent**, views, views/normal. "Hit" = the channel's most-viewed group (own: Feb-Aug 2026; Got2Learn: 2017-2021). "Recent" = the newest five (own: Oct 2026, 1-8 days old).
- **(kw)** = keyword count on transcripts or comments (blunt; key ones hand-checked). **(hand)** = counted by reading.
- "Illustrative" lines were written for this report; none copies 8 or more words from any transcript (checked by script).

## The limits (read first)

1. **Hits and recent uploads differ in month and template.** Own recent videos have 4 real comments between them, so comment evidence describes the hits only. Any hit-versus-recent gap may be script, season, algorithm or fatigue.
2. **About 40 comments per video** (top plus newest). Counts are rough.
3. **No thumbnails, retention or click-through.** A hook's pull can't be separated from its thumbnail's.
4. **Face versus faceless.** Arrow's, Silver Cymbal's and Wakefield's warmth partly comes from real people and won't fully transfer to an AI voice.

## The short version

1. **The hits run on household pain plus money, not on anger.** The warmest comments are relief ("first time in months"), pride ("72 years old and looking forward to seeing what else I can do"), lineage ("my grandfather gave me these pointers") and the satisfaction of a clear drain. Comments almost never echo anger at plumbers.
2. **The enemy frame drew the most-liked pushback on Roger's biggest insider hit:** "How would I ever know if my plumber has clog problems in his drains at his own home? 🙄" [1.3K]. Viewers love a *friendly* plumber's tip ("A plumber I knew long ago... told me to run the hot water" [155]); they doubt a *hiding* one.
3. **Frustration with failed attempts is the strongest hook.** It opens Mark's top hit, two Vintage hits and the best recent video on Roger and on Vintage, and viewers answer with their own "I tried everything" stories.
4. **Distrust of the shelf beats distrust of the trade.** Bleach tablets and 15-minute jugs are villains the persona can attack without insulting anyone the viewer likes.
5. **Safety fear lands when concrete, backfires when vague.** "It will put you on the floor" (chlorine gas) drew no complaints; the S-trap danger framing drew "I don't remember seeing anything in the news talking about all the deaths from sewer gasses" [81].
6. **Viewers protect each other.** Safety corrections are among the most-liked comments ("Borax laundry soap is not the same thing as boric acid" [4.3K]); unsafe advice in a script (boiling water, washing soda poured dry) drew about 18 and 17 angry comments on own hits.
7. **Myth-busting a loved habit cuts both ways.** "The foam looks like work. It is theater." is memorable, but about 23 comments in the study defend baking soda and vinegar (top: 289 likes).
8. **Lineage and kinship are missing from the recent template.** Uncle Ray: 4 of 5 Vintage hits, 0 of 15 recent uploads. "My own house" lines: 4 of 15 hits, 0 of 15 recent. Lineage is the theme commenters volunteer most on competitor hits.
9. **The recent template presses urgency and "not a scam" harder.** Time-pressure words in the first minute: 7 of 15 recent against 2 of 15 hits; the "scam / economics" aside: 9 of 15 recent against 1 of 15 hits. Neither is linked to the hits.
10. **Impatience and AI fatigue are the feelings that most often sink a useful video** ("Why do we suffer through a 20 minute video that could be done in 5 minutes?" [428]; "Thanks for being real and not AI" [1K] on Arrow).

## 1. Emotion catalogue (18 triggers)

### E1. Fear of the big bill

**What.** Dread of a repair far bigger than the problem looks; for older homeowners on planned budgets, the four-figure surprise is the pain. Almost always a contrast: cheap fix now, expensive repair later.

**Where.** Titles ("Costing You THOUSANDS!"), hooks (a dollar figure in the first minute of 8 of 15 own hits, 11 of 15 recent; measured), and the closing line of most list items.

- "We are talking about $2,000 to $5,000 or more in repair costs. All because of a product that costs about $8 a bottle." (Roger, WfAwg1l0xCE [5:23], hit, 1.22M, 453x)
- "50 cents versus 20 bucks versus 300. That's the whole game." (Roger, kHT6ZL8At0w [8:00], hit, 451k, 168x)
- "300 for the fixture, a couple hundred more for the labor. $500 to get rid of a stain." (Vintage, hOz49worQJg [1:02], hit, 167k, 275x)
- "That's not a $500 problem. Replacing a drain field runs deep into the thousands." (Vintage, uWXIUmWdPeY [11:09], hit, 240k, 395x)
- "And yet the average homeowner pays a plumber $300 or more every single year for problems this $1 bottle could have fixed in 15 minutes." (TheCheapFix, ls7BwoRhM9Q [0:04], hit, 2.18M, 285x)
- "Right now, somebody is about to lose 20, 30,000 dollars on a water damage claim and they have no idea that is coming." (Roger Wakefield, Wav7A7YhduY [0:15], recent, 3.2k, 0.61x)

**Comments.** Viewers repeat the sums: the 1.5K-like recap on WfAwg1l0xCE ends "A couple of bucks spent on one is better than hundreds spent on having a plumber clean your drain". Dated prices backfire: "This guy prices are from 1924, Amazon - BIO-CLEAN $79.95"; "Every bottle of anything in these AI videos is $1 a bottle. Maybe 30 years ago!" [48]. About 6 price disputes on own hits, 7 on competitor hits (hand).

**Association.** A money comparison is in 15 of 15 own hits and 15 of 15 recent (measured), and on every plumbing competitor. Inferred: the entry ticket, not the differentiator.

**Illustrative (written for this report).** "A drip you shrug at in October can be a ceiling you pay for in March." / "Nobody budgets for a flooded hallway, and that's exactly why it hurts."

### E2. Resentment of the service call

**What.** The sting of paying a stranger a lot for a short visit; rides on the call-out fee and feeling at someone's mercy.

**Where.** Hooks and item endings. Hits say it sharply; recent uploads say it, then soften it.

- "...there are plumbers who will happily charge you 150 just to knock on your door." (Vintage, rGEFZrtOJOI [1:02], hit, 753k, 1,237x)
- "Plumbers love this job. The minimum service call alone is $150, and the actual job takes them about 10 minutes." (TheCheapFix, ls7BwoRhM9Q [3:56], hit)
- "So, when the fix is pocket change, what's the rest of your money buying?" (Roger, n-2hjVGsu-Q [0:33], recent, 2.1k, 0.78x)
- Softened: "The plumber isn't the scam. The price is what looks like one." (Roger, n-2hjVGsu-Q [22:05], recent); "That is not a scam. That is a human being rolling a truck to your house at midnight." (Mark, Fgc-A7FCG44 [1:25], recent, 207, 0.38x)
- Honest version: "That $246 bill is honest. It just covers the last 10 minutes of a problem that took 4 years to grow." (TheCheapFix, SUqzcj5udsk [16:24], hit, 1.51M, 197x)

**Comments.** Lands when aimed at labour cost: "Is not the cost of the parts is the cost of the LABOR that gets expensive." [97]; "Plumbers on emergency hours are super expensive and the kit is cheap insurance." [14] (Got2Learn). Backfires when aimed at honesty: [1.3K] and "How the hell would i know what the drain is like at my plumbers house?" [108] on YjPAVZAffh0; "No HVAC TECHNICIAN GIVES A CRAP IF YOU 'KNOW' this 'TRICK'" [25] on TheCheapFix. About 5 frame-disputing comments on own hits, about 7 on TheCheapFix; none agreeing that plumbers hide fixes (hand).

**Association.** Call-out words: 8 of 15 hits, 11 of 15 recent (kw). "Scam / economics" aside: 1 of 15 hits, 9 of 15 recent (kw). Inferred: fine as a sting about price; costly as an accusation about people.

**Illustrative.** "The trip charge alone can be more than this whole fix. That's not anybody being crooked; it's what a truck and a licence cost." / "Save the service call for the job that truly needs a second pair of hands."

### E3. Distrust of the store shelf

**What.** Suspicion that products are sold to look like they work, or cause the next problem; rides on years of cleaners that "didn't do anything" and a sense that things are made worse now.

**Where.** Hooks (kHT6ZL8At0w, Rb0aS97YnSA, rDLzNLUZnhw, hOz49worQJg) and the "here's what the label won't say" turn.

- "But then fast forward to today and things get really shady." (Silver Cymbal, dVjg_BK3G3s [0:27], hit, 1.33M, 19x)

**Comments.** "I didnt know about how they can damage rubber parts. They should have a warning label about that! 🙄" [15]; "Why do they sell those tablets if they harm the toilets and what should we use"; "Those chemical drain cleaners WILL DEFINITELY destroy your pipes." [15]. Strongest on Silver Cymbal: the creator's pinned "I love my LED bulbs but I hate getting cheated" [961], "It's also ashamed our government allows manufacturers to rip us off like this." [619]. **Association.** Shelf/label words: 13 of 15 hits, 15 of 15 recent (kw); in about 6 of 15 hooks in each group (hand). Inferred: always-on; the hits name an object (the blue tablet, the $20 jug), not "the industry".

**Illustrative.** "The jug says fifteen minutes. It never tells you what's still sitting in the trap at minute sixteen." / "There's a reason the box with the plain label is still on the bottom shelf after a hundred years."

### E4. Dread of silent, hidden damage

**Association.** Hidden-damage words: 13 of 15 hits, 10 of 15 recent; in the hook of 6 of 15 hits against 2 of 15 recent (kw). Inferred: leans toward the hits, and sets up the solution ("you can't see it, but you can stop it").

## 5. What backfired

1. **Accusing plumbers.** The mortgage opener drew the most-liked doubt in the own data ([1.3K], [108], [41], "lousy framing", "Inane."). On TheCheapFix's HVAC hit about 5 technicians mocked the frame ("This AI garbage will destroy your AC unit."). No comment in the sample agreed tradespeople hide fixes. Inferred: the viewer has a plumber they like, and the frame asks them to distrust him.
2. **Fear without proportion.** "This is nothing but a scare tactic video." (VZo8y6AplVQ); S-trap danger ([81]; "I am 78 years of age and I have never found either a P or S trap without sufficient water in it" [27]); "you act like washing soda is going to eat you alive". A named reaction and a specific step drew thanks; vague dread drew eye-rolls.
3. **Unsafe advice turning trust into anger.** About 18 boiling-water objections and 17 hardening or failure reports on own hits: "Thanks to this video I had to hire a plumber!"; "It's going to cost me thousands of dollars now". The angriest comments in the own data. Inferred: a broken emotional promise ("never touches your pipes") costs more trust than any hook earns.
4. **Clickbait the script walks back.** "Forever" then "Nothing in plumbing is permanent"; "ANY Drain Clog" then "will not... dissolve a wad of hair". Comments: "AI junk could of been said in less than a minute, thumbs down!" [68]; "I finish it with zero information".
5. **Myth-busting a loved habit.** About 23 comments defend baking soda and vinegar against "theater" lines (top 289). Mild, but it turns "thank you" into "you're wrong".
