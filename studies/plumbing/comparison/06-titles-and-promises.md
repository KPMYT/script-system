# Titles and promises: the formulas, and how scripts pay them off

Study date: 2026-10-20. This report covers the operator's three plumbing channels (Roger the Pipe Guy, Mark Silverbridge, The Vintage Pipefitter) and the five competitors (Silver Cymbal, Roger Wakefield, Got2Learn, Arrow Pest Service, TheCheapFix). Titles are chosen in a separate ideation step. The script still has to deliver what the title promises, so this report looks at both: the title formulas, and how each script paid (or didn't pay) its title.

**Sources:** `comparison/stats.md`, `README.md`, `01`-`03`; `notes/INDEX.md` and all 80 notes files; the transcripts (`transcripts-combined/` and the per-video `transcript.txt`), searched video by video for where each title's promise is paid; every `comments.md`, searched for title, clickbait, "where is", "didn't work" and similar remarks; and all eight `COLLECTION-LOG.md` files for extra titles.

**How to read this:**
- **Measured** means counted, timed or quoted from the data. **Inference** means my reading of it. Unlabelled claims about cause are inference.
- "V/n" is views divided by that channel's normal (the median of its 15 most recent long-form uploads at least 7 days old). Compare V/n **within a channel only**. Mark's 858× is 471k views, fewer than Roger's 168× (451k).
- Timestamps are from auto-captions and are accurate to within a few seconds. A "~" marks a time read from a 30-second paragraph rather than a single caption line.
- Quotes are verbatim captions, so a few words may be misheard. Commenter names are never given.
- Counts of title parts and payoff grades were tagged by hand. They're a careful reading, not a script.

## 0. Limits: read this first

1. **Hits and recent uploads come from different months.** The 15 own hits were uploaded February to August 2026. The 15 own recent uploads are from October 2026, were 1-8 days old at collection and share a newer script template. Any title-formula difference between the two groups is mixed up with timing, age, the template and the thumbnail. TheCheapFix's recent five were 1-6 days old too.
2. **Small samples.** Five videos per group. Most formulas below have one to five examples.
3. **Thumbnails, click-through rate and retention aren't in the data.** A title's views belong to title and thumbnail together.
4. **Got2Learn's hits are 2017-2021 uploads** set against a normal built from 2021-2025 uploads.
5. **The extra titles were fewer than hoped.** The collection logs list the normal-window videos (about 120) by video ID and view count only, without titles. The only extra titles with views I could recover are five: one skipped Roger upload, one skipped Got2Learn upload, and three Arrow window videos whose titles appear in Arrow's own pinned comments. Nine more titles (three Roger, six Arrow) appear without views and are used as formula evidence only (section 1.5).
6. **Comments:** about 40 per video at most, because the comment tool wouldn't page further. Recent own uploads have 0-2 comments each, so comment evidence is almost all about hits.

### 1.2 Insider and secret family

**F1. "N Things [Trade] Don't Want You to Know About [Product] (#n Saves $X)"**

- **The script must deliver:** exactly N items, each with a method; a plain reason the trade doesn't volunteer it; and the teased #n carrying its dollar arithmetic at that item.
- **How it went (measured):**
  - TheCheapFix names the withholding at once: "They just do not volunteer it." (ls7BwoRhM9Q [0:23]). It pays "#4 Saves $300" at [4:48]: "Number four, it removes the brown ring inside your toilet bowl and saves you $300 on a new fixture". The $300 is an avoided toilet replacement, a stretched framing (inference).
  - yIh1HAlbhc0 pays "#8 Saves $900" at [10:22] ("a $900 job"), then defuses it: "It is not proof your entire duct system needs a $900 cleaning."
  - RA1-fogcslM's title says 11, but its script says "These are 13 things" [0:46].
  - Vintage's rGEFZrtOJOI carries no dollar figure. It pays the insider claim with a jab, "plumbers who will happily charge you 150 just to knock on your door" [1:06]. At #8 it then turns the insider into the good guy: "Why the pros reach for it before the harsh chemicals" [14:22].
  - Comments on yIh1HAlbhc0 challenge the premise, not the content (section 4).

### 3.3 Patterns

**Measured:**
1. **Time to the strongest promise.** By my readings in 3.1-3.2, the median time to pay the title's strongest promise is about **3:13 for own hits** (range 0:28-13:04) and **2:59 for own recent uploads** (range 0:00-12:45). Those are held on purpose, while the other items start by 1:34.
2. **Own hits were not faster than own recent uploads at paying the title** (3:13 vs 2:59). Payoff speed does not separate own hits from own misses in this data.
3. **Limit lines are common in both groups.** A line limiting or contradicting the title's strongest word appears in **7 of 15 own hits** (kHT6ZL8At0w, rDLzNLUZnhw, uWXIUmWdPeY, hOz49worQJg, YjPAVZAffh0, du--FqUoCHI, 9_11vx_L6oM). The recent template has an "honest part" slot, which places these lines more regularly (inference).
4. **Two kinds of limit line:**
   - **Scope lines** keep the title true by naming the method's territory: nxb3nS21zgg, ihZc3VGeG6c, Fgc-A7FCG44, XGWQ3l_zEWA, WxRQIn76yRo, hOz49worQJg, 9_11vx_L6oM.
