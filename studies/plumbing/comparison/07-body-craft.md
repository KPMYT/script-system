# 07: Body craft (how the middle and the end of a video are built)

Plumbing script study, written 2026-10-20. This report covers everything **after the hook**: how list items and steps are built, how they're ordered, the transitions between them, the specifics and proof inside them, money and safety lines, writing for compiled footage, padding, CTAs and endings, and the recurring stock phrases. It follows `README.md` and `01`-`03`, which treated the body only in passing.

Sources: `stats.md`, `README.md`, `01`-`03`, all 80 notes files, all 80 transcripts (read in full: the 15 own hits, 5 TheCheapFix hits, 5 Got2Learn all-time hits, 3 own recent uploads, 7 Silver Cymbal and Wakefield videos; the rest sampled and pattern-searched), and all 80 `comments.md` files (searched for length, padding, "get to the point", summaries and timestamps, footage mismatch, accuracy and prices).

## How to read this

- **Tag format.** Every example carries: channel, video ID, [timestamp], group, views/normal. "Hit" means one of the channel's 5 most-viewed in the last 24 months (Got2Learn: all-time, 2017-2021). Example: (Roger WfAwg1l0xCE [2:37], hit 453×).
- **Measured** = counted or timed from the data. **Inference** = my reading. Unlabelled numbers are measured.
- **Counts in this file are my own pattern counts** over the 80 transcripts (211,449 words). Where they differ from `01`-`03` (hand counts), that's why. Example: "Nine times out of 10" shows up in 12 videos here, not "at least 9", because I also caught the spelled and digit forms.
- **Timing caveat.** All 15 own hits are from February to August 2026. All 15 own recent uploads are from 12-19 October 2026, on a newer template, 1-8 days old, with 0-2 comments each. Vintage's recent ratios are from videos under 7 days old and don't compare with the window. So every hits-against-recent difference is also old template against new template, and season against season. These are **things to test**, not causes.
- **Small samples:** 5 videos per group.
- **Auto-captions:** quotes are verbatim captions, so a few words are misheard ("lie" for lye, "soponification").
- **Comments:** about 40 per video at most. No usernames are quoted. Like counts are as the API returned them.
- **Illustrative lines** (the rewrites, and the CTAs in section 10) were written for this report. They're labelled, and none copies 8 or more words from a transcript.

## The short version

1. **The hits build every item the same way; the recent template builds sections.** Hit items are even units of about 200-300 words with a fixed inner order (Mark VZo8y6AplVQ: 10 items of 197-249 words). Recent uploads run as one long walk-through broken by fixed slots: book pitch, "honest part", stop-and-call block, payoff.
2. **TheCheapFix's item shape is the most transferable:** problem as a scene, then what the pro or product charges, then the method with amounts and wait times, then a one-line payoff. Items run 45-65 seconds. Own hits already do this at their best (Vintage uWXIUmWdPeY #1, Roger WfAwg1l0xCE).
3. **Six of the 15 own hits share one stock transition into a troubleshooting block** ("common problems you might run into…"), and 9 hits reuse "if you're finding this helpful". None of the recent uploads uses them: the template swapped one set of stock phrases for another.
4. **Rehooks that name a concrete payoff read naturally; generic "stick around" doesn't.** "Three tips from now is the one that saves the most money" (TheCheapFix) and the running tally (Roger, Vintage) work. "Stay right here with me… please do not skip out on me now" is padding.
5. **Specificity is high on own channels and uneven.** Own hits carry 2.9-5.4 measurements per 1,000 words, as many as anyone. But they name almost no stores or brands, cite almost no sources, and the numbers disagree between channels (PVC softens at "around 140°" on Roger, "around 175°" on Vintage).
6. **Proof is the weak point, and it's a script problem as much as a camera problem.** Mark's "I took half-inch copper pipe" lab tests and "you will watch me try to rip it off the wall" can't be backed by compiled footage. What does work without a camera: mechanisms, sourced numbers, honest limits, viewer-run tests (fizz test, food-colouring test), and self-correction.
7. **Washing soda: two own channels give opposite instructions for the same powder.** Mark's top hit says "Dissolve it first" (Rb0aS97YnSA [11:17]); Vintage's hit says "Pour it dry. Do not mix it into a slurry" (rDLzNLUZnhw [8:11]). About 10 viewers report hardened traps on the dry-pour videos.
8. **Footage complaints trace back to specific words.** Naming "35%" pulled a 35% jug onto screen while the voice said use 3% (234 likes); "dry powder" pulled a dumping clip in a video that said dissolve first; every dollar figure pulls stacks of $100 bills.
9. **Viewers summarize the hits for each other.** The top comment on Roger's 1.2M-view hit is a numbered recap (1.5K likes); "3:15 is where it starts" has 2K likes. No hit ends on a recap. The recent template doesn't have one either.
10. **The recent template's ending has three parts:** a book pitch repeated (about 80-100 words), a subscribe line with a channel promise, and a "Go… tonight" imperative. Competitors end with one ask and stop. TheCheapFix's two biggest hits have no end CTA at all.
