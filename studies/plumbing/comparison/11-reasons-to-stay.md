# 11: Reasons to stay (what comes right after the point)

Part 11 of the plumbing comparison. Written 2026-10-20 from the study data. The operator asked: "when structuring the scripts, we want to be faster to the point of what we are saying but we want to very quickly give them more reason to stay so we promise more value. watch how competitors do this." `10-opener-teardowns.md` takes the openers apart. This file starts where the opener ends: the moment a script has made its point, and what it promises next so the viewer keeps watching. Nothing in the library was changed.

Sources: the first 4 minutes of all 40 hits read line by line (15 own, 25 competitor), each followed to the end to see whether and when its promises are paid; the per-line originals (`data/*/*/<id>/transcript.txt`) for timestamps; every hit's `comments.md`; `stats.md`; parts `04` §2-3, `07` §2-3 and `09` §4-5 and §24. The 15 own recent uploads and the 5 TheCheapFix recent uploads were read for the whole-video spacing in section 3.

## How to read this

- **Tags.** Script quotes are verbatim and tagged *channel · video ID · [m:ss] · views/normal*. In the section 1 tables the channel and views/normal sit in the table heading and the first column, and each cell quote carries its own [m:ss]. Comment quotes carry their like count and never a username.
- **Timestamps** are the start of the caption line where the quote begins (from the per-line originals). That's more exact than the 30-second blocks used in `04`, `07` and `09`, so a time here can be up to about 30 seconds later than the same line there.
- **"Hit"** = the channel's 5 most-viewed in the last 24 months (Got2Learn: all-time, 2017-2021). **"Recent"** = the 5 newest. Own hits are from February to August 2026; own recent uploads are from 12-19 October 2026, on a newer template, 1-8 days old, with almost no comments. Every hits-against-recent difference is also old template against new and season against season, so read it as a thing to test.
- **Views/normal** ("452.91x") compares a video with its own channel's normal. Compare it within a channel only.
- **Words used in this file.**
  - *First answer:* the first moment the viewer gets something usable: the fix named, the rule stated, or the verdict in plain words. A product described but not named doesn't count until it's named.
  - *Value promise:* a line telling the viewer that specific value is still to come (an item, a number, a fix, a warning). "Stay till the end" with nothing named is a tease, not a value promise.
  - *Concreteness,* scored by me: **C3** names the thing and a stake (a number, a dollar figure, a safety outcome, a time); **C2** names the thing but no stake; **C1** names nothing ("a bonus tip").
  - *Paid:* the time the promised thing is delivered.
- **Measured vs inferred.** Times, counts, view ratios and comment texts are measured. Anything about what "works" is my inference and says so. Counts are one coder's (mine), no second check.
- **Small samples:** 5 hits per channel, about 40 comments per video at most.
- **Operator decisions applied throughout:** no recaps; no name intro; the trade is the villain on every plumbing channel, never a named company; Roger and Mark keep a short mid-video ask and a short outro ask; Vintage has no ask for now; no first-person test claims unless the title or brief calls for one; words per point depend on the point. The scanner's tease bans hold, so every model below promises a concrete payoff instead.
- **Illustrative passages** in section 6 were written for this report and are labelled. None shares 8 or more consecutive words with any transcript, and none trips a scanner hard ban (checked; see the end of the file). Any fact or price in them must be checked against the fact bank before use.

## The short version

1. **The own hits promise first and point second; the competitors point first and promise second.** In 12 of 15 own hits the first forward promise comes before the first answer. The own hits reach their first answer at a median of about 1:46; competitor hits at about 0:33.
2. **The own hits' promises point a long way ahead.** A promise made in the first 4 minutes of an own hit is paid a median **13.2 minutes** later, usually as an end "bonus". Competitor hits pay theirs a median **3.6 minutes** later (Silver Cymbal 1.1, Got2Learn 3.0, Arrow 3.0, Wakefield 5.2, TheCheapFix 5.3).
3. **After the hook, most own hits stop promising.** 8 of 15 own hits make no new value promise between the end of the hook and the end-of-video bonus. The median longest stretch with no promise and no payoff is about 11 minutes in an own hit, about 5 minutes in a TheCheapFix hit and about 6 minutes in an own recent upload.
4. **The competitors' "reason to stay" mostly lives inside each item, not in a teaser.** TheCheapFix labels every item with its benefit and ends it on the price the trade charges for the same job; Got2Learn and Silver Cymbal simply move to the next item every 35-60 seconds; Arrow chains each fix to a stronger one it promises next.
5. **Six devices carry most of the concrete promising:** the numbered item with a dollar figure, the escalation ladder (cheap fix, stronger fix, when to call), the second use for the same product, the safety rule that protects everything, the diagnostic the viewer can run, and the running cost tally. All six can be written without a single banned tease phrase.
6. **The best-performing own hit is also the one that promises most often.** Vintage rGEFZrtOJOI (1,236.97x) makes cross-references every few minutes ("when we get to number eight", [8:48]; "I told you back at number four I would come to this", [14:24]) and pays every one. But Vintage's two weakest hits also promise often, so frequency alone isn't the cause (inferred).
7. **Viewers mark where a video really starts.** The most-liked comment on Roger's YjPAVZAffh0 is "3:15 is where it starts." (2K likes), pointing at the line "If you do nothing else from this entire video, do this." ([3:18]) The point came 3 minutes after the promises.
8. **Payoff discipline is mostly good, with two false callbacks.** Every named own-hit promise was paid, but two hits "pay" a promise that was never made ("And now the part I promised", Mark Rb0aS97YnSA [16:34]; "Now, I said there was one more", Vintage rDLzNLUZnhw [19:42]). The scanner could catch this.
9. **The trade as villain keeps viewers when it's aimed at the price, and draws doubt when it's aimed at motive.** "how he pays his mortgage" (YjPAVZAffh0 [0:33]) drew the most-liked doubt in the own data (1.3K); TheCheapFix's per-item price lines ("You just did it for cents.", ls7BwoRhM9Q [3:39]) draw no pushback in the sample.
10. **The template that fits the operator's instruction** (inferred from the above): answer in the first 20-45 seconds, then within 15-30 seconds promise two or three named, concrete things with their stakes, pay the first of them within about 3-5 minutes, and keep one forward promise alive at all times, paid in turn rather than all at the end.
