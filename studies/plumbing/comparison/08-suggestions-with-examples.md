# Suggestions with examples: medium and weaker evidence

Plumbing script study, written 2026-10-20. The operator asked: "for the rest of medium and weaker evidence please give me examples. more of them." This file takes each open medium- and weaker-evidence suggestion from `README.md` ("Suggestions") and `01-own-channels.md` section 5. For each one it shows the problem and the fix in the operator's own transcripts and the competitors', gives rewrites, and adds draft rule wording, so the operator can see a suggestion working before approving it. **Nothing here has been applied to the library.**

## How to read this

- **Tags.** Every quote is tagged *Channel · video ID · [m:ss] · hit/recent · views/normal*. "Hit" means one of a channel's 5 most-viewed videos from the last 24 months (Got2Learn: all-time). "Recent" means one of its 5 latest uploads. Views/normal is the channel's own ratio from `stats.md`.
- **Timestamps** mark the start of the roughly 30-second caption block that holds the quote, so the spoken line can sit up to about 30 seconds later.
- **Quotes are verbatim auto-captions,** so misspellings and caption slips are left in ("$4 to5", "Ring it", "Sriracha"). Comments are quoted without usernames, with their like counts.
- **Measured vs inferred.** "Measured" means counted or timed from the data. "Inference" is my reading of it. Comment counts are hand counts from samples of about 40 comments per video.
- **"Illustrative, written for this report"** marks a passage I wrote fresh. These passages copy no run of 8 or more words from any transcript (checked by script) and make no first-person test claims. Their prices are typical ranges, flagged as to be checked against the shared fact bank before anything is scripted. Book titles and tenure belong in the persona profile, so they appear as placeholders where they would come up.
- **Draft rule text** is labelled "draft wording, not applied". It has not gone into any rule file.

### The timing caveat (applies to every hit-vs-recent comparison below)

All 15 own hits were uploaded between February and August 2026. All 15 own recent uploads went up between 12 and 19 October 2026, on a newer script template, and were 1-8 days old when collected, with almost no comments. So any difference between hits and recent uploads may come from the script, or from the season, the algorithm, audience fatigue or the thumbnail. None of the examples below proves cause. They show what a suggestion looks like, and the data behind each one is small: 5 videos per group.

## (a) The script pays off the strongest reading of the title, or the title is softened

**What it means.** Take the title's strongest promise word ("every", "any", "forever", "dissolves", "1%", "tested", "vs") and make sure the body delivers it literally. If the body can't honestly deliver it, change the title before scripting, not afterwards. A "vs" or "ranked" title needs a real side-by-side, and a "tested" title needs a test the footage actually shows.

**Evidence.**
- *Measured.* Six own videos walk back their own title in the script. Four are hits (uWXIUmWdPeY, hOz49worQJg, rDLzNLUZnhw, kHT6ZL8At0w) and two are recent (fq7bWC_bRqQ, j_d6TuZKHV4). One hit claims tests that the footage can't show (sXlzedri_Hg, 551×), and one recent upload disowns its own thumbnail (bSrEWmK0g5E [0:00]). The test claim drew "Where is the footage of him doing the tests?"
- *Inference.* The overclaiming hits still won (84× to 1,237×), so this is a rule about trust and repeat viewing, not about reach. There is no evidence in the data that overclaiming cost views.

### As a rule (draft wording, not applied)

> **Title check before scripting.** List the title's strongest words (every, any, forever, dissolves, never, 1%, tested, vs, ranks). Each one must be delivered literally in the body. If it can't be, change the title before writing. A "vs" or "ranks" title needs a one-line verdict for every contender. A "tested" or "I tried" title is allowed only when the footage really shows the test.

### Risks and reasons to reject

- The overclaiming titles were hits (rGEFZrtOJOI's "Plumbers Don't Want You to Know", hOz49worQJg's "Forever"). Softer titles might get fewer clicks, and click-through rate isn't in the data.
- TheCheapFix, the closest format match, overclaims in titles too and still gets 142× to 285× on its hits.
- The benefit is trust and repeat viewing, which this study can't measure.
- Option A (change the answer) means re-researching some topics, which may need a new fact-bank entry.

### Risks and reasons to reject

- It needs the shared fact bank (strong-evidence suggestion 4) to exist and be kept up to date.

> **Compiled-footage beats.** Every beat names at least one physical object a stock or compiled clip can show. Explanations with nothing to film (chemistry, code history) stay short, and the next beat goes back to an object. Where a contrast matters (S-trap against P-trap), add a shot note.

### Risks and reasons to reject

- Mismatch complaints come bundled with AI-voice complaints, and some of the backlash will stay whatever the script does.
- Shot notes add work for the VA and editor.
- The "keep abstract stretches short" part is a judgement call; nothing in the data measures it.

## (d) Cut repetition rather than chase a length; recap every step at the end

**What it means.** There is no word target. Each step, warning and tease is said once. The one planned repetition is a short closing recap of every step, so the viewer doesn't have to write their own in the comments.

**Evidence.**
