# Own channels vs competitors: side by side

Plumbing script study, written 2026-10-20 for the operator. This file puts the operator's three faceless channels (Roger the Pipe Guy, Mark Silverbridge Plumbing, The Vintage Pipefitter) next to the five competitors (Silver Cymbal, Roger Wakefield, Got2Learn, Arrow Pest Service, TheCheapFix) one dimension at a time. It feeds the plumbing script-rule pack; the woodworking pack's headings were used only as a lens.

Each section gives the side-by-side, hits vs recent uploads, the evidence, and **what the gap suggests** (my reading, not a measurement). The ranked list at the end is suggestions for the operator to approve or reject.

---

## How far the numbers go (read this first)

- **Sample:** 80 videos, 10 per channel (5 most recent + 5 most-viewed in the last 24 months; Got2Learn's second group is its all-time top 5 from 2017-2021, made in another era of YouTube).
- **Views:** I only compare views/normal *within* a channel. Normals run from 549 (Mark) to 270,890 (Arrow), so raw views across channels say nothing about scripts.
- **Age:** all 15 own recent uploads and all 5 TheCheapFix recent uploads were 1-8 days old at collection, so their low ratios are partly views still coming in. For the own channels, "hits vs recent" therefore also means "older script template vs newer template". Read it as "what changed", not "what failed".
- **Comments:** own recent uploads have 0-2 comments each. Every own comment finding comes from the 15 own hits.
- **FACE vs FACELESS:** Silver Cymbal, Wakefield and Arrow are on camera; Got2Learn is faceless but films his own hands doing the work; TheCheapFix is the only like-for-like (faceless, AI voice, compiled footage). Where a difference could just be the camera, I say so.
- **How I counted (measured):** simple word-pattern counts over all 80 full transcripts. *Prices* = every dollar figure or "bucks/dollars/cents" phrase (a range counts twice). *First person* = I, I'm, I've, I'd, I'll, my, me. *"You"* = you, your, you're, yourself. Rates are per 1,000 spoken words. Question marks are unreliable where captions have no punctuation (Got2Learn, Wakefield ooWoKmqrooU). Hook, first-value, length and pace figures are from `stats.md`. Comment counts are the helpers' hand counts in the notes files, which I checked against a pattern search and spot-reads. Counts for "teases", "name intros" and "mid-video asks" are my own hand counts from reading the first 2 minutes and last minute of every video.

## 2. Hook

**Side by side (measured).** Own hooks have a median of 62.5 s (range 28-145 s). Competitors' median is 30 s: Arrow 11-19 s, Silver 28 s, Got2Learn 32 s (one video has no hook at all), TheCheapFix 43 s, Wakefield 44 s.

**First 15 seconds.** Own openers are as sharp as anyone's: a second-person scene plus what's at stake.
- "It is 2 in the morning in the middle of January." (Mark 6gl41FmPsCk [0:00])
- "That bleach tablet in your toilet tank is destroying a $5 part that keeps the whole thing working." (Vintage 7oem6cSCg-g [0:00])
- TheCheapFix opens on a dollar contrast plus the trade as the enemy. In 8 of 10 videos a price lands in the first 15 s (own: 10 of 30): "the average homeowner pays a plumber $300 or more every single year for problems this $1 bottle could have fixed in 15 minutes." (ls7BwoRhM9Q [0:00])
- Arrow reads the title's promise aloud, then shows live job clips (9/10): "Safely kill all yellow jackets and wasps up to a half mile without harming any honeybees. Sounds impossible, right?" (2XTSgRtcwh0 [0:00])
- Wakefield's first line is the title in 8/10: "Don't buy a new water heater. Don't buy a new water heater until you watch this." (WXJGaGtXrgY [0:00])
- Got2Learn starts on content: the first trick begins at [0:00] (DHk94YoC95k).

**15 to 60 seconds.** This is where the channels part ways.
- **Name and channel-promise block:** all 15 own recent uploads have one at about 0:16-0:48; none of the 15 own hits do. The wording is nearly the same on all three channels: "homeowners who are tired of paying emergency prices for 10-minute problems" (Mark 6gl41FmPsCk [0:33]); "every homeowner tired of paying service call prices for 5-minute jobs" (Vintage 7oem6cSCg-g [0:16]); "people with older houses who are tired of paying truck roll prices for 10-minute fixes" (Roger ihZc3VGeG6c [0:48]). Silver also names himself early ("Hey guys, I'm Chris…", 4/10), but then his whole video is 4-6 minutes long.
- **"Stay till the end" teases:** 22 of 30 own hooks have one (Roger 10/10, Mark 8/10, Vintage hits 4/5, Vintage recent 0/5). One example: "stick around to the end because I'll show you why those popular drop-in bleach tablets are secretly destroying your toilet" (Rkm2BnFL9FA [0:49]). Wakefield teases in 5/10 and pays the tease off *after* his subscribe ask (RGH2k2G8oaQ promises at [0:00] and pays off at [16:08]). TheCheapFix teases a numbered item with a money figure in 9/10: "number seven by itself can save you $500." (RA1-fogcslM [0:49]). Silver, Got2Learn and Arrow: 0-1 per 10 (Arrow zuiwdNiVylQ [0:15] "the answer is going to shock you").

**Hits vs recent.** Own hits do not have shorter hooks: Vintage hits have a median of 106 s against 58 s for recent uploads; Roger 65 s vs 62 s; Mark 56 s vs 66 s (stats.md). The one hook element that only appears in recent uploads is the name and channel-promise block.

**What the gap suggests.** The first sentence is not the problem. What follows it is: name, channel promise, preview and tease all come before anything useful, and the competitors don't spend those 30-45 s that way. The own data cannot prove that hook length costs views (the hits had long hooks too), so this is a moderate-confidence suggestion: keep the scene, cut the hook to about 30 s, and either drop the channel-promise sentence or move it to the end. If there is a tease, make it a concrete payoff, as TheCheapFix and Wakefield do, rather than "stay till the end".

## 3. Time to first real value

**Side by side (measured).** First real value (the first useful fact, as the helpers recorded it) has a median of 74 s for own videos (range 0:19 to 2:59) and 38 s for competitors. Got2Learn's all-time hits: 15 s. Silver: 26-38 s. TheCheapFix: 30-48 s.

**The method often comes much later in own scripts.** The first useful fact is usually a "why it clogs" explanation. The thing the viewer actually does comes later:
- Roger 8BZZwj93dkQ: the method starts at [7:50] of 16:31.
- Roger kHT6ZL8At0w: product named at [5:25], steps at [8:50].
- Vintage hOz49worQJg: product (citric acid) named at [7:00].
- Vintage AiUUNM24kCo: "It's called an enzyme drain cleaner." at [4:31].
- Mark 6gl41FmPsCk: first hands-on step at [6:07].

Competitors reach the first action much sooner: TheCheapFix 2:02-5:20; Silver names his answer at [1:01] ("This stuff is called duct seal.", sHL4lBAhklA); Got2Learn's first tool comes at about [0:38] (JyVbjDsric4); Arrow's first bait ingredient by about [2:04]-[2:17] in its hits.

**Comments (own hits):** "3:15 is where it starts." (2K likes, YjPAVZAffh0). The only comment on AiUUNM24kCo: "We don't need all that explanation of what causes the clog. Just tell us how to unclog it." Viewers also post answer summaries so others can skip: hOz49worQJg (104 likes), uWXIUmWdPeY (54 likes), WfAwg1l0xCE (top comment, 1.5K likes, lists all five items).

**Hits vs recent.** Own hits are not faster (Roger hits 94 s vs recent 62 s). Two own hits hold back their answer for 5-7 minutes and still won (kHT6ZL8At0w 168×, hOz49worQJg 275×).

**What the gap suggests.** Competitors put the answer near the top and explain afterwards. Own scripts explain first and act second. The comments ask for the opposite order. Because two own hits held their reveal back and still won, the safer rule is: name the answer within the first minute (the curiosity can live in the title and thumbnail), and put the mechanism after the first action.

## 5. Length, words, pace; padding and repetition

**Side by side (measured).** Own: median 18:30, 2,977 words, 156 words/min. Competitors split two ways: short and dense (Silver 5:24 at 224 wpm; Got2Learn 7:48 at 165 wpm), and as long as own (Arrow 18:36; TheCheapFix 16:18). The three own channels are the three slowest speakers in the set (152-163 wpm against 165-224).

**Hits vs recent.** Own hits are not shorter (Roger hits 19:04 vs recent 18:24; Vintage 21:27 vs 16:12; Mark 17:02 vs 18:31).

**Length complaints (helper counts).** Own hits: Roger about 11, Mark about 9, Vintage about 19, so about 39 across 15 videos. TheCheapFix: 1. Arrow: about 1, at the same length. Silver gets about 17 comments *praising* his brevity, Got2Learn about 14 praising its pace. Most-liked examples on own videos:
- "Why do we suffer through a 20 minute video that could be done in 5 minutes?" (428 likes, kHT6ZL8At0w)
- "This should be an 1 to 2 minute video." (96 likes, HsOr7vpQUGg)
- "AI junk could of been said in less than a minute" (68 likes, hOz49worQJg)

**Padding (estimated from the notes' segment word counts).** In own recent uploads the name and promise block (about 40-100 words), the mid-video book pitch (137-230 words) and the closing block (about 120-135 words) together make up roughly 10-15% of each script. Example: Mark fq7bWC_bRqQ has 79 + 137 + 126 = 342 of 3,071 words.

**Repetition (measured).**
- *Within a video:* repeated 8-word runs are rare in own scripts (0-10 per video), so loops aren't a big issue.
- *Across videos:* own verbatim repeats are mostly CTA boilerplate. Roger's Rkm2BnFL9FA and YjPAVZAffh0 share two blocks of about 77 and 102 words, both book pitches ("Before you go, I want to make sure you know about my new book", Rkm2BnFL9FA [22:07], YjPAVZAffh0 [17:43]). TheCheapFix repeats *body* text: "hydrogen peroxide is basically water with one extra oxygen atom bolted on" appears in 4 videos, and RA1-fogcslM and nbBsxB8rFwU share about 354 ten-word runs. Face channels share only sign-offs.
- *Production leftovers read aloud:* 3 of the 5 Vintage hits contain stage cues spoken by the voice: "And subscribe CTA." (hOz49worQJg [18:43]), "Mid-roll subscribe CTA." (rDLzNLUZnhw [8:54]), "Bonus tip stinger." (uWXIUmWdPeY [20:51]). Item numbers are read twice ("Number one. Number one.", hOz49worQJg [1:51], 9 times in that video). TheCheapFix has the same artifact (9 doubled numbers in uJVc7zmq7vs). Only one comment in the whole set complains about repetition ("the ai voice said it was surprising repeatedly", sXlzedri_Hg, 12 likes).

**What the gap suggests.** Arrow and TheCheapFix run as long as own videos without length complaints, so length alone is not the problem. The complaints on own videos come together with AI-voice and footage complaints. That suggests length hurts most when the visuals aren't carrying the video (inferred). Rules worth considering: let length come from the content, with no 15-20 minute target; count the packaging blocks against the length; and have the scanner block stage cues and doubled numbers.

**Measurements and mechanisms.** This is a strength of the own scripts. Examples:
- "You will need a 1 and 1/16 inch socket" (VZo8y6AplVQ [1:17])
- 140°F tank, 120°F delivered (WxRQIn76yRo)
- A 3/8 × 7/8 connector and a 12-inch line (9_11vx_L6oM)

**Words per useful point (inferred from item sizes).** Got2Learn spends about 45 words per trick and Silver 25-40 s per tip; own scripts spend 150-450 words per item, so 3-10 times the words per point.

**Accuracy pushback in comments.** Own washing-soda advice draws reports of it hardening in the trap (about 10: kHT6ZL8At0w about 6, Rb0aS97YnSA 3, rDLzNLUZnhw 1), and boiling-water-on-PVC worries (about 8 on YjPAVZAffh0). Possible rules: name the exact product or spec when it *is* the answer (the plumbing version of woodworking's "where to sell" becomes "where to buy and what to ask for"); give a source for any statistic; and put a known-risk line in the pack for washing soda and for boiling water on PVC.

## 7. Proof: what each channel shows, and what a faceless channel can claim

- **Face channels prove it on camera.** "It's been here for 4 years. Look at that." (Silver sHL4lBAhklA [2:30]). "8 hours of running a fridge. 6% of the battery." (K8F2R1gDSkg [6:43]). Arrow comes back the next day to check the bait boxes: "we had activity in that one box" (Wg9-yFIXlx0 [19:28]). Wakefield works at the bench: "You don't heat the pipe, you heat the fitting." (1kWd9HidNJk [8:25]).
- **Got2Learn (faceless) shows his own hands.** Flux vs no flux demonstrated at [1:35] (JyVbjDsric4). In about 352 comments there are two AI remarks: one about pronunciation, one about his footage being reused by AI channels.
- **TheCheapFix (faceless AI) proves with words:** numbers, named sources, honesty caveats ("we are not here to oversell a dollar bottle", YYLUiZEB_pE [10:10]) and self-correction ("number 12 is one we got wrong ourselves", SUqzcj5udsk [0:16]).
- **Own scripts claim first-hand tests and demonstrations that compiled footage cannot show.**
  - "I put all three connection methods through eight brutal tests" (sXlzedri_Hg [0:17]). A comment replies: "Where is the footage of him doing the tests?"
  - "And you will watch me try to rip it off the wall to prove it." (XGWQ3l_zEWA [1:18])
  - "Look at the toilet on the left… Now, look at the same toilet on the right the very next morning." (n-2hjVGsu-Q [0:00])
  - "There's a stack of receipts on my workbench right now" (hOz49worQJg [0:00])
  - Stories: a customer "2 weeks from calling a remodeler" (hOz49worQJg [0:47]); Uncle Ray (several Vintage videos).
- **Footage-mismatch complaints (helper counts):** Mark about 28 (the top two comments on HsOr7vpQUGg: "really wish the pictures were relevant to what he was talking about...", 235 likes), TheCheapFix about 10, Vintage about 7, Roger about 3. All face channels and Got2Learn: 0.

**What the gap suggests.** This is the clearest difference in the study, and most of it is structural (camera vs no camera), not a script choice. The script makes it worse when it says "I tested", "watch me" or "look at" over footage that can't back it up.
- A faceless AI channel *can* credibly offer mechanisms, published specs and code, prices, a test the viewer runs at home (Vintage's fizz test; Roger's food-colouring leak test, n-2hjVGsu-Q [17:20]), and stories framed as typical cases.
- It *cannot* credibly claim personal tests, on-camera demonstrations or before/after shots unless the footage really shows them.

## 8. Persona and voice

**First person and direct address (measured, per 1,000 words).** First person: Wakefield 25.1, Arrow 19.9, Got2Learn 17.5, Silver 15.2; own 5.3-8.8; TheCheapFix 0.5 (it speaks as "we"). "You": Wakefield 51.6, Silver 49.5; own 28-32; TheCheapFix 32.

**Hits vs recent (measured, consistent on all three own channels).** Own hits use 1.5-2 times as much first person as recent uploads (Roger 11.9 vs 5.9; Mark 6.4 vs 4.3; Vintage 11.1 vs 6.1) and more "you" (39/32/37 vs 25/25/28). The hits carry "what I do" routine lines ("first Saturday of the month while I'm having my coffee", hebGJ43lQ6s [8:05]). Recent uploads lean on crafted metaphor instead. This could reflect the template change rather than cause and effect.

**Tenure claims don't match (measured).**
- Mark says "over 25 years in the trades" (VZo8y6AplVQ [0:10]; sXlzedri_Hg [0:17]) and "35 years" (Rb0aS97YnSA [0:30]; du--FqUoCHI [0:16]), and the title of Rb0aS97YnSA says "A 45-Year Plumber's Verdict".
- Roger says "three decades" (WfAwg1l0xCE [0:13]) and "45 years" (hebGJ43lQ6s [0:30]).
- Vintage says "30 years" (rDLzNLUZnhw, rGEFZrtOJOI, uWXIUmWdPeY), but a title says "45-Year Plumber" (dIe837GgLDs).
- Wakefield, a real person, is consistent: "45 years" (RGH2k2G8oaQ [1:16]), "over four decades" (f8-sd5Zw0E4 [0:15]).
- Commenters notice: "'Ive been a plumber for 35 years'....says an AI plumber reading an AI script" (du--FqUoCHI).

**Humour.** Own humour is written similes: "gravy is just grease wearing its Sunday clothes" (bSrEWmK0g5E [15:30]); "the clog sat there like it had paid rent" (fq7bWC_bRqQ [1:16]). Competitor humour is live banter: "Rat crack, baby." (Wg9-yFIXlx0 [7:04]); "I paint it on like Picasso, man." (1kWd9HidNJk [6:18]).

## The 10 biggest differences, ranked by strength of evidence

**Suggestions for the operator to approve or reject. These are not decisions.** Each item gives the difference, the evidence, and what it could mean for the plumbing script rules.

1. **Competitors prove it on camera; own scripts claim proof they can't show.** Face channels and Got2Learn get 0 footage complaints. Own hits get about 38, with Mark at about 28. Own scripts say "I tested", "watch me" and "look at the toilet on the left" (sXlzedri_Hg, XGWQ3l_zEWA, n-2hjVGsu-Q).
   *Suggests:* ban first-person test, demonstration and before/after claims unless the footage really shows them. Allow "a test you can run tonight", published specs and sourced numbers instead.

2. **The AI backlash lands only on AI-voiced channels, and is worst where the persona over-claims.** Mark: 31 AI remarks (17%), tenure stated as 25, 35 and 45 years. Arrow is praised for being real.
   *Suggests:* fix each persona's tenure and biography in the persona profile, stated identically every time. Keep claims modest. Add a "writing for compiled footage" section, like the woodworking pack's AI-visuals section, so each line can be matched to footage that can actually be found.

3. **Own scripts carry far more packaging.** A channel-promise block in all 15 recent uploads; a mid-video ask in all 30 (book pitch 137-230 words); a three-part ending; packaging is about 10-15% of each recent script. Competitors: one short ask (TheCheapFix 25-105 words; Silver about 15 s).
   *Suggests:* cap the mid-roll pitch at about 60 words, drop or shorten the channel-promise sentence, one book per channel, and end on the payoff.

4. **Own scripts take longer to get useful.** Hook median 62.5 s vs 30 s; first value 74 s vs 38 s; the actual method often comes 4-8 minutes in (8BZZwj93dkQ [7:50], hOz49worQJg [7:00]). Comments ask for the point ("3:15 is where it starts", 2K likes). Caveat: own hits were not faster than own recent uploads.
   *Suggests:* name the answer within 60 s and explain the mechanism after the first action.

5. **More words, slower delivery, and the length complaints to match.** About 3,000 words at the slowest pace in the set (156 vs 174.5 wpm); about 39 length complaints on own hits, while the short competitors get praised for brevity. Arrow and TheCheapFix run as long without complaints, so length alone isn't the cause.
   *Suggests:* let length come from the content (as woodworking does), with no fixed 15-20 minute target. Count packaging against length. Have the scanner flag items over about 250 words and spoken stage cues ("CTA", "stinger") or doubled numbers.

6. **The winning topics are the same on face and faceless channels.** S-traps, tank vs tankless, joining copper, peroxide in drains and toilets, toilet install mistakes. Each wins on two or more channels; Mark's recent uploads drifted to a furnace and a grab bar.
   *Suggests:* the inputs step should check the topic against this proven cross-channel list (a link to ideation, not a script rule as such).

7. **Competitors name the product and the store; own scripts stay generic.** Arrow, Silver and Got2Learn name brands and stores constantly; own scripts rarely do (Arm & Hammer twice). TheCheapFix names its sources; own scripts never do.
   *Suggests:* a "where to buy and what to ask for" block: the exact product type or spec, where it is in the store, and its price, plus a source for any statistic.

8. **Own scripts use the least first person and direct address of any human-voiced channel, and own hits use 1.5-2 times the first person of own recent uploads, consistently on all three channels.**
   *Suggests:* more "here's what I do" routine lines and plain second-person instructions, fewer stacked metaphors. Moderate confidence, because of the template change.

9. **Own scripts tease "stay till the end" (22/30); competitors tease a concrete payoff or don't tease at all.** TheCheapFix: "number seven… can save you $500" (9/10). Silver, Got2Learn and Arrow: almost no teases.
   *Suggests:* replace generic end-teases with one specific promise that names its payoff, or drop the tease.

10. **Own scripts are the most safety- and referral-heavy in the set.** 21 referral phrases across 30 own videos vs 0 for Silver and Arrow; whole when-to-call sections in recent uploads. Nothing in the data shows this costs views, and it protects credibility.
    *Suggests:* keep it, but as one compact "stop and call" block, plus the plumbing hazards commenters actually raise (washing soda hardening, boiling water on PVC, mixing chemicals).

**Not in the top 10:** the adversary. TheCheapFix uses it hardest, but Arrow and Got2Learn win without one, and own hit and recent titles use it equally (3/15 each). The evidence doesn't support making it stronger.
