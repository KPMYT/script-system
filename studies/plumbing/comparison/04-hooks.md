# Hooks: the first minute, in detail

Part 4 of the plumbing comparison. Written 2026-10-20 from the study data, after the operator asked for far more detail on openings than parts 1-3 gave. It covers the opening of all 80 videos (the first ~3 minutes of each transcript were read), the Structure map in every notes file, and every comment that mentions the intro, the pace, a tease or clickbait. Nothing in the library was changed.

Operator decisions already made, which this file respects: get to the point faster; no name or channel intro; no "stay till the end" style teases; TheCheapFix is a format reference, not the main source.

## How to read this file

- **Example tags.** Every quote is verbatim from the transcript and tagged like this: *Vintage rGEFZrtOJOI [0:00], 753,316 views, 1,236.97x normal, hit.* "Hit" means the channel's 5 most-viewed in the last 24 months (Got2Learn: all-time, 2017-2021). "Recent" means the 5 newest uploads.
- **Timestamps** are the start of the roughly 30-second caption block the quote sits in, so a quote tagged [0:30] was spoken somewhere between 0:30 and about 1:00.
- **Views/normal** is how a video did against its own channel's normal. Normals differ hugely (Mark's normal is 549 views, Silver Cymbal's is 70,989), so only compare ratios **within** a channel.
- **Counts are my hand counts** from reading the openings, one coder, no second check. Each video is given **one primary structure** (what its first one or two sentences do). A short greeting ("what's up guys") is skipped and the video is classified by what follows. Most hooks also use two or three other structures as later beats; those are counted separately as "used in the first minute".
- **Measured vs inferred.** Counts, timestamps, view ratios and comment texts are measured. Anything that says a structure "works" or "should" be used is inference, and is labelled as such.
- **The big caveat.** All 15 own hits are from February-August 2026; all 15 own recent uploads are from October 2026, on a newer template, 1-8 days old, with almost no comments. The Vintage recent ratios are from videos under 7 days old and are not comparable with the window. Got2Learn's hits are from 2017-2021. So any hit-versus-recent difference below may be timing, the algorithm or audience fatigue rather than the hook. Samples are 5 per group.
- **Illustrative hooks** in each section are written fresh for this report, for plumbing topics. They are examples of the shape, not tested scripts, and any fact in them must be checked before use.

## The short version

1. **19 distinct opening structures** are in the 80 videos. The operator's channels use 15 of them; competitors use 13.
2. **The own hits and the own recent uploads open differently.** Hits most often open with a **question** (4 of 15) or a **"go look at this in your house"** line (2 of 15). Recent uploads never open with a question (0 of 15); they open with a scene, a price gap or an emergency moment, and then spend 20-40 seconds on a name and channel intro (15 of 15, against 0 of 15 hits).
3. **Question openers lead the biggest hits across the whole study:** 6 of the 40 hits (own and competitor) open with a question, including Wakefield's and Got2Learn's biggest videos. None of the 40 recent uploads does. This is the single cleanest split in the data, but it lines up exactly with the hit/recent timing split, so treat it as a strong lead to test, not proof.
4. **The fastest openings belong to the biggest competitor hits.** Got2Learn's all-time hits reach the first real point in 5-20 seconds; Arrow's hits say the whole promise in one sentence (11-16 s). The own channels take 56-106 s (median hook length by channel and group).
5. **Holding back the product's name is the opening most tied to angry comments.** Four own hits keep the product unnamed for 30 seconds to 7 minutes; their comment sections hold the most-liked length complaints in the study ("Why do we suffer through a 20 minute video that could be done in 5 minutes?", 428 likes).
6. **Teases:** 28 of 30 own openings make some forward promise; 34 of 50 competitor openings make none at all. Only TheCheapFix teases in almost every video (10 of 10), and its teases name a numbered item and a dollar figure. The core already bans the vague ones, but the scanner misses several phrasings the own scripts actually use ("stay to the end", "stay till the end", "stick with me", "hang on for", "do not go clicking away").
7. **Title handoff is mostly fast on the object, slow on the fix.** Nearly every own video names the title's object in its first sentence; the title's promised fix arrives at 0:19 in the best case and 7:00 in the worst. Five videos in the study drift from their own title in the script (examples in section 4).

### H1. The failed-fix scene ("you already tried the obvious thing")

**Definition.** Open on the viewer having already tried the usual fix and watched it fail, then say the usual fix was never going to work.

**Anatomy.**
1. The attempt, in the second person and past tense, with physical detail (10-25 words, 3-8 s): "You did everything right. You bought the plunger."
2. The failure (10-20 words, 3-6 s): the water "just sat there".
3. The cost of giving up, optional (10-20 words): the $150-250 call.
4. The turn (5-10 words): "But the plunger was never the problem."
5. The real cause or the real fix, in one line (15-30 words).

Total about 50-100 words, 20-35 s.

**Examples (verbatim).**
- *Mark Rb0aS97YnSA [0:00], 470,966 views, 857.86x, hit (Mark's biggest).* "Last night, you poured half a box of baking soda down the kitchen sink, chased it with vinegar, and watched the foam climb up out of the drain like something serious was finally happening. This morning, the sink drains exactly as slow as it did the day before. Go back and look at that shelf. 2 ft from the baking soda in a box that looks almost identical, sat a product that would have actually worked."
- *Mark fq7bWC_bRqQ [0:00], 257 views, 0.47x, recent.* "You did everything right. You bought the plunger. You pumped that bathroom sink until your shoulder burned and the water just sat there looking back at you. So now you are staring down a drain call somewhere between $150 and $250 over a clog softer than a bar of soap. But the plunger was never the problem."
- *Vintage dIe837GgLDs [0:00], 1,016 views, 1.67x, recent (Vintage's best recent).* "There's a ring in your toilet bowl that laughs at everything you've thrown at it. You've scrubbed it. You've bleached it. And maybe you've stood in the cleaning aisle wondering if the whole toilet is ruined."
- *TheCheapFix TOurn07rVVQ [0:00], 853 views, 0.11x, recent.* "The smell is back. You grind a lemon, [music] drop in a pod, and a day later that rotten stink is right back in your sink. It was never the food. A $1 bottle of hydrogen peroxide kills it for weeks, [music] not days."
- *TheCheapFix YYLUiZEB_pE [0:00], 1,661 views, 0.22x, recent.* "Your dog had an accident weeks ago. You cleaned it, and now on the first humid day, that corner smells like a kennel again. A cleaner quotes you $300 for the smell, or $500 to rip out the carpet and padding."
- First-person version, *Roger 8BZZwj93dkQ [0:00], 6,599 views, 2.45x, recent (Roger's best recent; classified H15).* "There was a stretch of my life when the cabinet under my kitchen sink looked like the chemical aisle at the hardware store. Red bottles, blue bottles, a gel that swore it could melt an engine block. And the drain still backed up once a month, regular as a utility bill."

**Frequency and performance (measured).** Primary opener in 6 videos (own hits 1, own recent 2, competitors 3, all TheCheapFix recent); in the first minute of 11. Mark's biggest video uses it (857.86x), and the failed-fix idea opens the best recent upload on two of the three own channels (Vintage dIe837GgLDs 1.67x; Roger 8BZZwj93dkQ 2.45x, first-person). TheCheapFix's three failed-fix openers are all recent and all low, like every TheCheapFix recent upload (0.06-0.69x), so the structure doesn't explain that channel's slump either way.

**Faceless adaptation.** Nothing here needs to be faked: the footage is the failed product (foam in a sink, a plunger, a shelf of bottles), which compiled footage covers well. The risk is padding the "what you tried" list; keep it to three attempts and turn within 20 s. The turn line must be true: if the usual fix does work sometimes, say so.

**Illustrative, written for this report.**
- "You ran the shower hot for ten minutes and poured in a capful of bleach, and the water still stands around your ankles. Bleach was never going to reach it. That drain is choked by a rope of hair hooked under the stopper, and a three-dollar plastic barbed strip pulls it out in under a minute."
- "You jiggled the handle and bent the float arm, and the toilet still hisses every twenty minutes through the night. The float was never the problem. A worn rubber flapper is letting the tank leak into the bowl, and a new one costs less than a fast-food lunch."

### H2. The emergency moment + "put the phone down"

**Definition.** A clock-stamped crisis scene (late night, mid-winter), then an order to stop before calling or pouring anything, then reassurance that it's probably simple.

**Anatomy.**
1. Time stamp and situation (8-15 words, 3-5 s): "It is 11:00 at night."
2. Two or three sensory details of the crisis (20-40 words).
3. The stop sign (3-10 words): "Put the phone down." / "Before you reach for your phone…"
4. Reassurance plus the answer or the likely cause (20-40 words).
5. Cost line (5-10 words): "Total cost, about 50 cents."

About 70-120 words, 25-40 s.

**Examples (verbatim).**
- *Mark Fgc-A7FCG44 [0:00], 207 views, 0.38x, recent.* "It is 11:00 at night. The toilet will not go down. The plunger has already put on its little show twice. And now you are standing on the bathroom tile doing math you never wanted to do. Pay a stranger a midnight price or close the lid and hope. Put the phone down. Somewhere under your kitchen sink sits half a cup of plain dish soap."
- *Mark 6gl41FmPsCk [0:00], 746 views, 1.36x, recent (Mark's best recent).* "It is 2 in the morning in the middle of January. The thermostat reads 59 degrees and falling, and the furnace in your basement is sitting in complete silence like it has decided to retire. Before you reach for your phone and pay somebody $200 just to ring your doorbell, hear me out because there is a very good chance nothing in that furnace is actually broken."
- *Mark j_d6TuZKHV4 [0:00], 121 views, 0.22x, recent.* "Put the bottle down for 1 minute. You are standing over a toilet that will not flush. Your phone is telling you hydrogen peroxide will fix it, and that brown bottle from the bathroom cabinet is already in your hand. Maybe it will fix it."

**Frequency and performance.** Primary opener in 3 videos, all Mark's October uploads; in no hit and no competitor video. Ratios 1.36x, 0.38x and 0.22x. Fgc-A7FCG44 is the fastest own opening to name its fix (the soap, about 0:19).

**Faceless adaptation.** Works without faking anything (a dark hallway, a silent furnace, a full bowl are easy stock shots). It suits seasonal topics (frozen pipes, furnace condensate, holiday clogs). Two cautions, both inferred: the reassurance must be honest (the furnace may really be broken), and the stop sign should be followed by the answer straight away, as Fgc-A7FCG44 does, not by the name intro, which all three then do at about 0:30.

**Illustrative, written for this report.**
- "Sunday evening, and the water heater closet is ankle-deep. Before you dial anyone, turn the cold supply valve on top of the tank clockwise until it stops; that alone ends the flood. Then we'll work out whether you're facing a twenty-dollar valve or a new tank."
- "It's nine at night, both kitchen sinks are full of gray water, and company comes tomorrow. Leave the drain-cleaner jug on the shelf. A cup of washing soda dissolved in a pot of hot tap water, and one plunger with a flat cup, open most kitchen lines before bedtime."

### H3. The price gap (the $1 fix against the $300 call)

**Definition.** The first sentence sets a cheap household fix against what a professional charges.

**Anatomy.**
1. The cheap thing and its price (10-20 words, 3-6 s): "That little brown bottle… cost about $1."
2. The pro's price, as big and specific as possible (15-25 words): "$246 for one drain cleaning, plus 59 to 150 just for the plumber to walk in and look."
3. The bridge (8-15 words): the pros know but "just do not volunteer it" (TheCheapFix) or the cost of a wrong fix (own).
4. Count or promise (10-20 words).

About 50-90 words, 18-30 s. TheCheapFix adds one line of chemistry and a numbered tease, reaching item one at about 0:50-1:20.

**Examples (verbatim).**
- *TheCheapFix ls7BwoRhM9Q [0:00], 2,183,304 views, 284.66x, hit (TheCheapFix's biggest).* "That little brown bottle sitting in your medicine cabinet cost about $1 at most drug stores. And yet the average homeowner pays a plumber $300 or more every single year for problems this $1 bottle could have fixed in 15 minutes. Most people only think of hydrogen peroxide as something you pour on a cut. But here's the thing. Plumbers know exactly what this stuff can do. They just do not volunteer it."
- *TheCheapFix SUqzcj5udsk [0:00], 1,513,057 views, 197.27x, hit.* "That little brown bottle in your medicine cabinet costs about a dollar. The average homeowner pays $246 for one drain cleaning, plus 59 to 150 just for the plumber to walk in and look. Plumbers know what this bottle does. They just do not volunteer it, but almost everyone uses it wrong."
- *TheCheapFix RA1-fogcslM [0:00], 1,676,523 views, 218.58x, hit.* "You spill one iced coffee on the passenger seat, let it sit a week, and suddenly a detailer is quoting you $300 to get the stain and the smell back out. Ask him about the musty mildew smell in the carpet while he is at it. And that number climbs right past $500."
- *Roger nxb3nS21zgg [0:00], 1,881 views, 0.70x, recent.* "Tonight, somewhere in your town, a homeowner is going to hand a stranger $200 to clear a slow drain that a $1 cup of powder from the laundry aisle would have cleared before supper."
- *Roger ihZc3VGeG6c [0:00], 2,103 views, 0.78x, recent.* "That brown bottle of hydrogen peroxide in your bathroom cabinet, the one that costs a dollar to $3 at any grocery store, can do the work of three separate plumber visits. The sink that smells like rotten eggs every time you lean over it. The pink ring in the toilet that comes back no matter how hard you scrub."

**Frequency and performance.** Primary opener in 6 videos: 4 of TheCheapFix's 5 hits (128.95-284.66x) and 2 own recent (0.70x, 0.78x). As a beat in the first minute it is the most common device in the study: 5 own hits, 9 own recent, all 10 TheCheapFix videos. Inferred: as a beat it is near-universal, so it can't by itself explain hits; TheCheapFix's version is sharper because the pro's price is specific and comes in the first 10 seconds.

**Illustrative, written for this report.**
- "A plumber will charge around $150 to walk in and swap the fill valve in your toilet tank. The part costs about ten dollars, and it goes in with nothing but your hands and an old towel."
- "A service visit to flush a water heater runs roughly $100 to $200. A garden hose, a bucket and twenty minutes on a Saturday does the same job, and most tank manuals ask you to do it every year."

### H4. "Go look right now" (point at something in the viewer's house)

**Definition.** An instruction or a pointer that sends the viewer's attention to a specific object in their own home, then reveals something about it they never noticed.

**Anatomy.**
1. The directive or pointer (5-12 words, 2-4 s): "Go look in your bathroom cabinet right now."
2. Where exactly, with homely detail (10-25 words): "Behind the aspirin, behind the old cough syrup…"
3. What it is and what they think of it (10-20 words).
4. The reveal (10-25 words): "That ring is not one thing. It's two."
5. Credential or promise (10-20 words).

About 60-110 words, 25-45 s in the hits.

In the first minute of 12 (it doubles as the vehicle for many price-gap openings: "That little brown bottle sitting in your medicine cabinet"). ### H5. The mystery product (name held back)

**Definition.** Describe the cheap product (the box, the aisle, the price, the color) but withhold its name for a while, so the name becomes the reward for watching.

**Anatomy.**
1. Where it is (10-20 words): "three aisles over… down on the bottom shelf".
2. What it looks like and costs (10-20 words): "It comes in a yellow box. It costs about $4."
3. What it beats (10-25 words): "just as well as the $20 jug of chemicals".
4. The reveal of the name: in the data anywhere from 0:52 to 7:00.

### H6. The question open

**Definition.** The first sentence is a direct question to the viewer about their own house or habits (or, on Got2Learn, the title as a question).

**Anatomy.**
1. The question (8-20 words, 3-6 s): "When was the last time you lifted the lid off your toilet tank?"
2. Narrow it or answer for them (10-30 words): "Not the seat, not the bowl lid."
3. The surprising truth (15-30 words).
4. The promise (15-30 words).

**Examples (verbatim).**
