# 10 Opener teardowns: the 43 winning openers, by video type

Part 10 of the plumbing comparison. Written 2026-10-20 from the study data, after the operator asked for the openers to be modelled "aggressively": every winning opener of ours and of the competitors, what type of video each was used for, what it triggers and what it asks. It builds on `04-hooks.md` (structures), `05-emotions.md` (the E1-E18 trigger names used here), `06-titles-and-promises.md` (payoff times) and `09-differences.md`. Nothing in the library or the study data was changed.

## How to read this

- **The 43 openers.** All 15 own hits (5 most-viewed per own channel, February-August 2026), all 25 competitor hits (5 per competitor; Got2Learn's are all-time, 2017-2021) and the single best recent upload of each own channel by views/normal (Roger 8BZZwj93dkQ 2.45x, Mark 6gl41FmPsCk 1.36x, Vintage dIe837GgLDs 1.67x; October 2026).
- **Verbatim.** Every beat in a card is cut by script straight from `data/*/*/<id>/transcript.txt`, so the words, the word counts and the [m:ss] starts are exact. A timestamp is the start of the caption line where the quote begins. Where a beat is long and not part of the hook's job (chemistry paragraphs, footage crosstalk, material lists), the middle is elided with "…" and the number of elided words is shown. Auto-captions sometimes mishear a word ("Silver Symbol", "pale of water"); they are left as captioned.
- **Quotes elsewhere** carry a video ID and a timestamp, and were checked by script against the transcript (or, for comments, `comments.md`). Comment quotes give likes; usernames are never quoted. Section "Verification" at the end gives the result.
- **Measured vs inferred.** Timestamps, word counts, views, ratios, comment text and likes are measured. Video type, beat jobs, triggers, villain strength, question type and anything that says what "works" are my reading, one coder, and are labelled as such where it matters.
- **Views/normal** compares a video with its own channel's normal. Normals differ hugely (Mark 549 views, Arrow 270,890), so ratios are compared **within a channel only**.
- **Timing.** Own hits are February-August 2026; own recent uploads are October 2026, on a newer template, 1-8 days old, with almost no comments. Got2Learn's hits are 2017-2021. A difference between hits and recent uploads may be the script, the month, the algorithm or fatigue. Samples are small: 5 per group, and several video types below rest on one or two videos.
- **Villain strength** (my scale): 0 none; 1 products, the shelf, sellers or makers only; 2 the trade, about price or silence (rGEFZrtOJOI [1:02] “charge you 150 just to knock on your door”); 3 the trade, with a motive (YjPAVZAffh0 [0:33] “how he pays his mortgage”; RA1-fogcslM [0:42] “a very good living not telling you”).
- **Illustrative openers** in the playbook are written fresh for this report, for the operator's three plumbing channels. They follow the operator's decisions: get to the point fast and stack more value early; no name or channel intro; the trade as the villain (never a named company); no first-person test or demo claims unless the title calls for one; Mark's tenure is 45 years and no other tenure is used; no recaps; no vague teases (only concrete payoffs). None copies 8 or more consecutive words from any of the 80 transcripts (checked by script). Prices and facts in them are typical ranges written for the example and must be sourced before use.

## The short version: one winning skeleton per video type

The 43 openers fall into 13 video types. One line each; the full beat-by-beat skeletons, models and examples are in Part 2, section 1.

- **A. Single-product fix** (10): promise with the product named + reassurance clause + disbelief beat answered + jug/visit price (villain) + honest scope + stacked payoffs, first step by 40-60 s.
- **B. N things about one product (insider list)** (6): pointer + $1 price + the trade's price for the same jobs + "they just don't volunteer it" + belief flip + count with a numbered money item and a safety item + one rule, item one by 45-90 s.
- **C. N things / never-do list** (4): habit doing hidden damage + avoidable cost + (trade bills the result) + count + one numbered item with its payoff, item one by 30-45 s.
- **D. Mistakes list** (4): neglect or symptom question + cost in thousands + one credibility line + count + one numbered payoff, mistake one by 15-30 s.
- **E. Head-to-head verdict** (5): failed attempt or choice moment + contenders with prices + noise and the pitch (villain) + "each one gets a job" + one concrete extra, first verdict fact by 45-60 s.
- **F. Product swap** (1): the pro's price + the DIY catch + the popular shortcut judged fairly + the swap named, with price and where not to use it (n=1).
- **G. Banned / code explainer** (2): why-question or discovery + the ban, scoped + the reason and one concrete danger + "you may not have to change it" + the fixes, first value by 20 s.
- **H. How-to / correct way** (5): task + number-one mistake or myth flipped + "you can do this" + step count and the step that decides it + honest limit, step one by 15-45 s.
- **I. Insider routine (the pro's own habit)** (2): the customer's words or a question about the viewer's experience + myth flip + own home with the fixed tenure + the habit's price + one named quick win, habit one by 45-60 s.
- **J. Problem diagnosis / hidden cause** (1): the trusted thing + the betrayal and who profits + the hidden cause + "what does that mean?" answered (n=1).
- **K. Seasonal / emergency** (1): clock, season, crisis + stop sign and the emergency price + "probably nothing broken" + the cause in a line + two fixes with what you own (n=1).
- **L. Story / experiment** (1): scene with three objects + failure like a bill + "you've tried the same" + what failed, what worked and its price + the payoff picture (n=1, plus the receipts story in a type-A hit).
- **M. Expert breakdown / reaction** (1): question pitting a popular claim against the trade + fairness + the claim + verdict promise with safety (n=1).

**Five patterns across the 43 (measured, reading inferred):**
1. **The answer comes within a minute in most competitor hits and few own hits.** The title's answer (product named, first item, verdict, reason or first step, by type) arrives within 60 s in 13 of 25 competitor hits, 4 of 15 own hits and 1 of the 3 best recent uploads.
2. **The fastest openers are the biggest competitor hits:** four of Got2Learn's five all-time hits reach the first real value in 5-20 s, Arrow's hits state the whole promise in 11-16 s, and two TheCheapFix hits pay the bracket in their title ("#4 Saves $300", "#7 Saves $500") word for word at 0:46. Own hits reach the title's answer at a median of 2:42, against 0:45 for competitor hits (my reading of "answer", per type; table in Part 2).
3. **More value, stacked early, is how the long winners hold people.** Counting the distinct payoffs promised in each opener (a price, a count, a numbered item with its payoff, a safety item, a time): own hits median 4 (range 3-7); own best recent median 4 (range 3-4); TheCheapFix median 4 (range 4-5); Got2Learn median 1 (range 0-3); Silver Cymbal median 2 (range 1-4); Wakefield median 2 (range 1-4); Arrow median 3 (range 1-5). Got2Learn's all-time hits promise least and start fastest; their videos are 5-11 minutes.
4. **The question is answerable in the viewer's head and answered within 15 s** in the winners that ask one (Rkm2BnFL9FA [0:02] “When was the last time you lifted the lid off your toilet tank?” → [0:17]). The one that asks about something the viewer can't know (YjPAVZAffh0 [0:00] “Ever wonder why the plumber you called to unclog your drain never seems to have drain problems at his own house?”) drew the most-liked doubt in the own data (1.3K).
5. **Villain lines that land are about price or silence, said as fact; the ones that draw disbelief claim a motive or a private fact the viewer can't check.** Section 4.
