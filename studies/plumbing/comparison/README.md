## Read this first: the limits

- **Hits and recent uploads come from different months.** All 15 own hits are from February to August 2026; all 15 own recent uploads are from October 2026, on a newer script template, and were 1-8 days old with almost no comments. So any hits-versus-recent difference may be the script, or timing, the algorithm or audience fatigue. These are **things to test**, not proven causes.
- **Small samples:** 5 videos per group.
- **Not in the data:** thumbnails, click-through, retention, traffic sources, the footage itself. Your YouTube Studio retention graphs would settle several questions below.
- **Comments:** about 40 per video at most (the tool wouldn't page further).
- **Face against faceless:** on-camera channels can't be compared like for like. TheCheapFix is the only competitor with the same format as yours.

## The main findings

1. **Three things split every own hit from every own recent upload (15 of 15 each way).**
   - The mid-video ask: 76 words or fewer in the hits, often just "subscribe"; a 137-230-word book pitch with QR instructions in the recent uploads.
   - A name and channel intro in the first minute ("I'm the Vintage Pipefitter…"): in no hit, in every recent upload.
   - A tenure number in the narration: in 9 of 15 hits, in no recent upload.
2. **Your scripts are slower to the point than every competitor's.** Hook: median about 62 seconds against about 30. First real value: about 74 seconds against about 38. The actual method often arrives 4-8 minutes in. Your hits were not faster than your recent uploads, though. TheCheapFix runs just as long (about 15 minutes) without length complaints, so length alone isn't the problem; slowness and repetition are.
3. **Proof is the biggest weakness.** Competitors show tests on camera. Your scripts claim tests and demonstrations the footage can't show ("I put all three… through eight brutal tests"), and viewers notice: about 38 footage-mismatch complaints on own hits, none on the on-camera channels.
4. **AI backlash and persona contradictions.** "AI voice" remarks appear only on AI-voiced channels; Mark is worst (about 17% of comments on his hits). Tenure numbers contradict each other on all three channels (Mark: 25, 35 and 45 years), and viewers mock it.
5. **What the hits share:** one cheap fix or one clear decision, priced against the cost of calling a plumber. Household products (peroxide, washing soda, borax) and head-to-head verdicts lead. The same topics win on face and faceless channels: S-traps, tank against tankless, joining copper, peroxide in drains and toilets, toilet-install mistakes.
6. **Script errors that reached upload:** production cues read aloud ("Mid-roll subscribe CTA.", "Bonus tip stinger.", "Continuing with the final sections now"), "the Vintage Plumber" for the channel name, numbers spoken twice, titles walked back in the script ("Peroxide does not dissolve rock" in a limescale-peroxide video).
7. **Safety and consistency.** Roger's hits recommend boiling water, which his later videos ban (about 8 viewer objections). Pouring washing soda in dry drew about 10 reports of it hardening in the pipe. Advice contradicts itself within and across the three channels.
8. **TheCheapFix, the closest format match, wins on one formula:** the title "N Things [Trade] Don't Want You to Know About Hydrogen Peroxide (#n Saves $X)", a five-part hook (a $1 price, the pro's charge, "they just don't volunteer it", the count with one item teased, one line of chemistry), and every item built as scene, what the pro charges, the exact method, the payoff. It also draws the most AI complaints, and its videos repeat and contradict each other.
9. **Titles are being shared in both directions** between your channels and TheCheapFix (dates checked: TheCheapFix's "9 Things Plumbers…" 2026-05-23, then Vintage's 2026-07-15; Roger's toilet-tank peroxide title 2026-07-01, then TheCheapFix's 2026-10-16).
10. **Teases.** Most of your hooks say some form of "stay till the end" (part 3's hand count: 22 of 30; a strict phrase search finds 18). TheCheapFix teases a concrete payoff instead ("number seven… can save you $500"); the others barely tease.
11. **Formats from the competitors that can transfer:** Got2Learn's "Here's Why X Is NOT ALLOWED Anymore (And 3 Ways To Fix It)"; Silver Cymbal's "[X] is Expensive. Use THIS Instead!"; Roger Wakefield's "Don't Buy X Until You Watch This" with an honest verdict and the break-even sum; Arrow's reassurance in the title ("Safe For Pets!", for plumbing "Safe For Septic").
12. **Not a difference:** the "plumbers are the enemy" angle. Arrow and Got2Learn win without any enemy, and your hits and recent uploads use it equally.

**Worth knowing:** a French comment on Got2Learn's channel update says his footage is reused on AI channels and names Roger the Pipe Guy. It's one viewer's claim, unchecked, but that audience can see it.

## Suggestions for the plumbing script rules

Each is a suggestion for the operator to approve or reject; nothing has been applied. Details and evidence are in `01-own-channels.md` section 5 and the top 10 in `03-own-vs-competitors.md`.

**Strong evidence:**
1. One fixed tenure number per persona, stated once, matching the About text, never contradicted in a title.
2. No first-person claims of tests, demonstrations or on-camera actions; every number sourced or given as a typical range.
3. A fixed plumbing safety block: hot tap water (never boiling) in drains, toilets and PVC; washing soda dissolved before pouring; 3% peroxide only; one product per drain; never after a chemical cleaner. Keep the "call a plumber" moments, but in one compact place.
4. One shared plumbing fact bank for all three channels, checked once and reused, so the channels stop contradicting each other.
5. Scanner checks for spoken production cues, wrong channel or book names, doubled numbering, tenure numbers that differ from the persona file, "boiling" near drain or toilet, and "pour it dry".

**Medium evidence (test before making them rules):**

6. Cap the mid-video ask at about 70 words. Check Studio retention at the pitch timestamps on recent videos first.
7. Drop the name and channel intro from the first minute, or move it after the first real value.
8. A faster opening: the answer in one plain sentence within the first minute, a concrete tease instead of "stay till the end", and a recap of all steps at the end. Cut repetition rather than chase a length.
9. The script pays off the strongest reading of the title, or the title is softened; a "vs" or "tested" title needs a real comparison in the body.
10. Keep the fix-cost against call-out-cost comparison in every video; prices as dated "about" ranges; name widely sold products when accurate.
11. Write for compiled footage: name the physical object in each beat; never "look at the one on the left".
12. A candidate banned or limited phrase list from the recent template ("Nine times out of 10", "Here's the part people skip", "Between you and me", "plain economics / not a conspiracy", and others), and rotate the template's fixed slots.

**Weaker evidence:** a "verdict" skeleton (contenders, one-line verdicts, job-by-job assignments); one brief recurring mentor or anecdote per persona (Vintage's Uncle Ray was in 4 of 5 hits and 0 of 5 recent uploads); vary the adversary stance per channel.

**For ideation, not script rules:** the winning topic clusters above, the transferable title formats, and the repeated re-teaching of washing-soda and peroxide facts across the three channels.
