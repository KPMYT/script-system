## Primary docs read directly
- CPSC 516 (Repairing Aluminum Wiring) -> cpsc516.txt: 1965 to mid 1970s; pre-1972 homes 55x more likely to have outlet connections reach "Fire Hazard Conditions" (Franklin Research survey); signs hot cover plates, flickering, burning plastic smell, circuits not working; may fail with no warning signs; cable jacket marked "AL" or "Aluminum" every few feet; "CU-clad" = copper-clad aluminum, not covered; 15A=12 AWG Al, 20A=10 AWG Al; COPALUM recommended, only trained/authorized electricians; AlumiConn next best; twist-on pigtail not recommended (overheated severely in CPSC tests); CO/ALR devices only temporary/incomplete.
- CPSC 513 (Home Electrical Safety Checklist) -> cpsc513.txt: warm outlets/switches -> stop using until electrician checks (some dimmers warm normally); loose plugs -> replace outlet; GFCI test monthly (plug a light, TEST, RESET; if not, replace GFCI); GFCI/AFCI breakers test monthly, handle to middle/off, reset off then on; defective breaker replaced by qualified electrician; AFCIs can fail without signs; fuse correct size, electrician identify/label; exercise breakers off/on 3 times yearly (fridge/freezer/AC off first); 3-prong adapter tab to cover screw (only works if box grounded - my reading), never break ground pin; extension cord 16 AWG handles 1375 W, 14 or 12 for more; cords not under rugs/furniture, not nailed/stapled; heaters 3 ft from combustibles; bulb wattage per fixture, unsure -> 60 W or less.
- CPSC Portable Generator Safety Alert 2017 (5123) -> gen2017.txt: outside only, at least 20 ft from doors/windows/vents; never in homes, garages, basements, crawlspaces, sheds even with fans/doors open; CO can linger hours after shutoff; battery or battery-backup CO alarms (UL 2034), test batteries monthly; can kill in minutes; dizzy/weak -> fresh air right away; dry surface under open canopy, dry hands; heavy-duty outdoor cords, wattage rating exceeds total load, three prongs, no cuts; never backfeed through wall outlet (electrocution risk to utility workers and neighbors on same transformer; bypasses some built-in household circuit protection); turn off and let cool before refueling; fuel stored outside living areas; danger labels on generators made after May 14 2007.
- CPSC Release 26-363 (31 Mar 2026): male-to-male "suicide cords" no legitimate household use (Acting Chairman Feldman); exposed prongs energized; used for backfeeding; short length -> generator too close -> CO.
- CDC CO page: symptoms headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, confusion ("flu-like"); 400+ deaths/yr unintentional non-fire CO, 100,000+ ED visits, 14,000+ hospitalized; generator outdoors more than 20 ft from windows, doors, vents; older adults at higher risk; battery CO detectors near every sleeping area.
- Eversource backfeed page: homes 120/240 V; primary 7,200 V; transformers "don't care which way the power is flowing"; transfer switch by licensed electrician.
- Generac support: bonded = neutral bonded to frame (most open-frame); floating (most inverter); check manual; floating reads "open ground" on tester, normal.

- Shock table (OSHA 3075 table, copied in CPWR "Electrical Safety: Safety & Health for Electrical Trades" student manual on eLCOSH, and OSHAcademy 809): 1 mA faint tingle; 5 mA slight shock, not painful; 6-25 mA women / 9-30 mA men painful, muscular control lost (can't let go); 50-150 mA extremely painful, breathing stops, severe contractions (death possible); 1,000-4,300 mA ventricular fibrillation; 10,000 mA cardiac arrest, severe burns, death probable; 15,000 mA lowest at which typical fuse/breaker opens. CPWR: dry skin 100,000 ohms or more, wet skin as low as 1,000; breathing stopped at voltages as low as 49 V. osha.gov PDF itself 403 / 503 to fetch.
- GFCI trip: IAEI history: Class A trips at 6 mA or more, won't trip below 4 mA. UL 943 trip time is inverse-time (formula), so "1/40 second" is only a nominal product spec: don't use as a rule.
- GFCI history (IAEI 2024 summer history + ESFI article in IAEI 2020): 1971 outdoor, 1975 bathrooms, 1978 garages, 1987 kitchen countertop near sink (basements per IAEI 1987/1990), 1990 crawl spaces, 2005 laundry sinks, 2011 within 6 ft of any sink, 2014 within 6 ft of tub/shower, 2020 125-250 V receptacles. ESFI/CPSC: GFCIs credited with preventing about half of home electrocutions; 81% drop.
- Leviton GFCI sheet (GFNL1): TEST MONTHLY; reset lockout when no power or LINE/LOAD reversed; LOAD protects downstream receptacles, which can be in other rooms; "does NOT protect against circuit overloads, short circuits, or shocks" from touching both bare wires while on a non-conducting surface; self-test models: solid/flashing red = problem, if unable to reset, replace; LINE/LOAD take 10-14 AWG.
- Leviton AC215/AC220 duplex sheet: strip 3/4" side wire, 1/2" back wire; torque 14-16 in-lb; turn off power and test that power is off; aluminum only with devices marked CO/ALR (or CU/AL); break-off fin for two circuits.
- St Paul DSI handout (2023 NEC): 210.12(B) AFCI for new 120 V 15/20 A outlets/devices in kitchens, laundry areas, family, dining, living rooms, parlors, libraries, dens, bedrooms, sunrooms, rec rooms, closets, hallways and similar; extensions/modifications too; 406.4(D)(4) replacement receptacles AFCI via OBC AFCI receptacle, downstream of one, or combination AFCI breaker.
- CPSC 5032 extension cords: temporary only; at least 16 AWG (18 AWG only if fused); not under carpets, through doorways, under furniture; outdoor only if marked; hot cord = overloaded warning; listed (UL, ETL, CSA).
- NFPA (2017 report, 2010-2014): ~45,210 home fires/yr involving electrical failure or malfunction, ~420 deaths, ~1,370 injuries per year.
- Mayo Clinic (search snippet) + UMM Health (WebMD Ignite): don't touch person in contact; turn off source (unplug/breaker/fuse) or push away with dry nonconducting cardboard/plastic/wood; call 911 for severe burns, confusion, breathing difficulty, heart rhythm problems, cardiac arrest, muscle pain/contractions, seizures, loss of consciousness; CPR if no breathing; downed lines stay at least 20 ft away (Mayo). Red Cross pages 403.

- IAEI CMP-18 2023: 406.3(D)(3) push-in only 15 A circuits & 14 AWG solid copper, not Al/CuAl; 406.4(D)(3) GFCI receptacles listed; 406.4(D)(8) GFPE; faceplates with LED/USB <=1 W; 406.9(C) new exception 4 (receptacle within 36 in of tub if GFCI, opposite side of toilet); 406.12 TR expanded.
- Fluke NCV page: false pos/neg when cable shielded, partly buried, inside metal enclosure, battery dead, user isolated from ground, test point touches grounded metal; tool answers "is voltage present", no claim it confirms dead; CAT III/IV ratings.
- ExpertCE 2023 702.5: (A) listed, designed, installed to prevent inadvertent interconnection; (B) meter-mounted transfer switches listed and approved; portable generator temporary connection without transfer equipment only with qualified-person supervision + physical isolation (not homeowner).
- Mike Holt Understanding 2023 NEC Vol 1, 210.8 extract (mh2108.txt): (A) dwelling receptacles 125-250 V: (1) bathrooms (2) garages & accessory buildings (3) outdoors (4) crawl spaces at/below grade (5) basements (6) kitchens (all receptacles incl. range, fridge, disposal, microwave per author) (7) areas with sinks + food/bev prep (8) within 6 ft of sink bowl edge (9) boathouses (10) within 6 ft of tub/shower outside bathroom (11) laundry areas (12) indoor damp and wet; GFCI device readily accessible; GFCI breaker protects whole circuit, GFCI receptacle protects what's plugged in + LOAD side; EVSE receptacles GFCI; (D) appliances incl. dishwashers?, clothes dryers, microwaves, wall ovens, cooktops (hardwired too); (F) outdoor outlets 50 A or less (e.g., AC units) incl. replaced equipment.
- Knoxville summary (search snippet): 2023 removed "serving the countertop"; all basements finished or not; indoor damp/wet added.
- 314.27 (search: Mike Holt forums, ECM): fan sole-support box listed and marked, fan max 70 lb; marked with weight if fan > 35 lb; light boxes support at least 50 lb; heavier luminaire supported independently unless box listed for more; 422.18 points to 314.27.
- 210.11(C)/210.52(B): two or more 20 A small-appliance circuits for kitchen/pantry/breakfast/dining receptacles + fridge; no other outlets (forum quotes).
- 210.21(B): 20 A circuit with 2+ receptacles may use 15 or 20 A receptacles (Table 210.21(B)(3)); single receptacle on individual circuit must equal circuit rating (210.21(B)(1)) (NC OSFM: duplex counts as two). Mike Holt forum says 2023 wording changed to "not less than" -> doubt.
- NM colors: Southwire spec: white 14, yellow 12, orange 10, black 8 & 6; NEMA bulletin: not required by NEC/UL 719, no standard for 8 and larger; read the printed legend. ~2001-2002 origin.
- 334.80 NM limited to 60 C ampacity: 14=15? (240.4(D)), 10 AWG 30 A (ECmag), 8 AWG 40 A, 6 AWG 55 A (forums). 
- 334.30: secured every 4.5 ft and within 12 in of box; fished in finished walls exempt.
- 300.15 box at every splice; 314.29 boxes accessible without removing building finish.
- 314.20: noncombustible walls box set back max 1/4 in; combustible (wood paneling) flush or projecting. 314.21: gaps in drywall/plaster around box max 1/8 in.
- Edison fuses (ECM Q&A, Mike Holt): 240.51 125 V/30 A, replacement only, no tampering/overfusing; 240.53/.54 Type S adapters non-removable, size-rejecting; old holders accept 15/20/30 -> overfusing risk.
- K&T: 394.12 no K&T in hollow spaces where insulation envelops conductors (added 1987 NEC); install era ~ through 1950s (sources conflict: 1880s/1920s start); no ground; California allows insulation with electrician certification (local).
- 110.26: 30 in wide, 36 in deep (0-150 V to ground), 6.5 ft headroom; 240.24(D) not near easily ignitible material such as clothes closets; 240.24(E) not in dwelling bathrooms.
- 230.85 (2020 NEC, NFPA blog + Dallas/Vancouver): outdoor emergency disconnect for one- and two-family dwellings; 230.67 SPD for dwelling services incl. replacements.
- EV: 625.41 OCPD and conductors >=125% of max load; 48 A charger -> 60 A circuit; 14-50 plug-in max 40 A continuous on 50 A circuit; Tesla: 14-50 recommended, 50 A breaker, up to 40 A; consult licensed electrician for load; Tesla doesn't recommend extension cords (forum report). AFDC: Level 1 120 V NEMA 5-15 ~5 mi range per hour (1.9 kW assumed); Level 2 240 V res; most residential up to 30 A / 7.2 kW; ~25 mi/hr.
- Continuous load: Article 100, 3 hours or more; 210.19(A)(1)/210.20(A) 125%.

- NFPA 70E (search, secondary: Graceport, Mike Holt forum): verify tester on known source before and after (110.6(E) / 120.5(7) / 120.6 numbering varies by edition); test each conductor phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground; non-contact tester does NOT satisfy absence-of-voltage test.
- Fluke outlet troubleshooting (search): hot slot shorter, neutral longer; hot-neutral ~120 V; hot-ground similar; neutral-ground near zero (loose); many meters CAT III 600 V.
- Plug-in testers (Wikipedia, JLC, ECM "Failures in Outlet Testing Exposed"): can't detect bootleg ground; can be fooled; never rely on them to confirm safe to work.
- Lutron: dimmer must match bulbs; dimmable LED; low-end trim fixes flicker at low levels; removing side fins when ganging needs derating per instructions.
- LED in enclosed fixtures: only bulbs labelled for enclosed fixtures (retailer sources only -> reading/one source).
- 410.116: non-IC recessed: 1/2 in from combustibles, insulation not above or within 3 in; IC-rated can touch insulation.
- 250.140: 3-wire range/dryer only for existing circuits without EGC; new = 4-wire; 14-30 30 A dryer typical; (1996 date unconfirmed).
- PIRG: CPSC estimated ~70 deaths/yr from portable generator CO (2021) (secondary).
- Downed lines: utilities (BPU Kansas City, GRDA, PSE): assume live, stay at least 35 ft, call 911/utility; Mayo says 20 ft -> use "at least 35 feet" utilities, note difference.
- Space heater: CPSC heaters.pdf: never extension cord or power strip; 3 ft from combustibles; hot plug/outlet/faceplate -> stop, electrician; never leave unattended or while sleeping; CPSC 2026 winter release + ESFI + USFA agree (search).
- NFPA electrical safety checklist (2017): call qualified electrician for frequent trips/blown fuses, tingle when touching appliance, discolored or warm outlets, burning or rubbery smell from appliance, flickering/dimming lights, sparks from outlet; one heat-producing appliance per outlet; bulb max wattage sticker.
- CPSC 518 Guide to Home Wiring Hazards: hot faceplates -> discontinue use immediately, qualified electrician; "any shock, even a mild tingle, may be a warning of danger-electrocution"; shock from metal faceplate -> turn off branch circuit at panel; plugs loose: don't bend blades (causes worse overheating); flicker with wind -> service drop contacts, don't touch, call utility.
- InterNACHI K&T: about 1880 to 1940s; not inherently dangerous; dangers: age, modifications, insulation; no ground; no code requires removal; not permitted in new construction; many insurers refuse; electrician evaluate.
- USFA (search): if fire, get out, stay out, call 911 from outside.
