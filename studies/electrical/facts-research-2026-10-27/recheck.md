## The most important corrections

1. **4.9 GFCI for outdoor AC units:** the row used an air-conditioner disconnect as its example of a required GFCI outlet. The 2023 code exempted listed HVAC equipment (until 1 September 2026), and many states amend it. A script repeating the old row would tell viewers their AC "has to be GFCI". Now: "check with your local inspector".
2. **12.8 someone being shocked:** added "switching off the appliance alone may not stop the current", stand on something dry, never a wet or metal object, and the power-line exception (don't approach; 35 feet; call 911). The old row's "push the source away with wood" read as if it applied to a power line too.
3. **12.12 downed line:** added shuffle away with feet together, never move the line even with a broom or stick (wet wood or cloth conducts), and the only reason to leave a car (fire: jump clear, feet together).
4. **11.7 generator CO deaths:** replaced PIRG's 70 a year with CPSC's own "nearly 100" (2024) / "more than 100".
5. **12.9 when to call 911:** MedlinePlus says call for anyone injured by electricity; the old row made 911 conditional on symptoms.
6. **11.6 CO alarm:** added what to do when it sounds: get outside right away and call 911 (CPSC).
7. **4.18 receptacle ratings:** a duplex counts as two receptacles (so a lone duplex on a 20 A circuit may be 15 A), and the disputed 2023 wording on 20 A receptacles on 15 A circuits is now a never-say.
8. **4.8 hardwired appliances needing GFCI:** the 2023 list adds electric ranges and covers listed appliances up to 150 V to ground and 60 A; dishwashers added.
9. **12.19 electrical fire figures:** 2010-2014 NFPA numbers replaced with 2015-2019 NFPA (via ESFI) and USFA's 2023 count, with a rule never to mix the two definitions.
10. **13.5 meter ownership:** the meter box and the cable down the wall are usually the homeowner's (an electrician fixes them), not the power company's; the old row would send viewers to the utility for a job the utility won't do.

Also worth knowing: 11.16 inlet wattages now the maker's ratings (3,750 / 7,500 / 12,500 W); 11.14 adds the 2023 rule that a portable-generator inlet goes outside, not in the garage; 12.20 adds OSHA's 10 feet from overhead lines; 13.8 notes that Pennsylvania and Illinois license electricians locally; 6.19's code reference moved to 210.12(E); glossary 14.19 (a GFCI breaker has no RESET button) and 14.27 ("cheap outlets" removed).
