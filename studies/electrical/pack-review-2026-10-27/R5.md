### B1. The emergency sequences: the SKILL lists two of the seven, and its burning-smell line merges two different sequences

**Where:** SKILL "Safety", line 299: "Emergency sequences get every step, whatever the brief leaves out … from the facts guide's §12: someone shocked (don't touch them while they're in contact, cut the power first, call 911); a burning smell or smoke from an outlet or panel (power off at the breaker if it's safe, get out, call 911 from outside)." Also the unit plan, line 55: "emergency sequence needed: [none / shock / burning smell: every step from facts-guide §12]".

**Evidence:**
- The facts guide ("How to use it") lists **seven** sequences: "someone being shocked (12.8 to 12.11), a hot outlet or burning smell (12.13 to 12.15), a downed line (12.12), generator carbon monoxide (11.1 to 11.7) and backfeeding (11.8 to 11.12)". The SKILL and the template name only two of them. A writer follows the SKILL and the template, not the guide's preamble.
- Lessons row 68: "every time a script mentions an electrical fire, a person being shocked, a downed line, smoke or a burning smell … it gets the full researched steps". Plumbing run 02 missed gas steps because the summary it worked from was short.
- The briefs bring these situations in. Brief 8 (A31): "every house on the same transformer, where a neighbor can touch a downed wire". Brief 14 (B11): "short length puts generators close to the house, raising carbon monoxide risk". Brief 4 (A12) lists "a faceplate warm to the touch, brown or melted marks … a burning smell". None of them gives the downed-line, CO or hot-outlet steps. Under the SKILL as written, A31 and B11 can ship with no downed-line sequence and only a "CO warning".
- The burning-smell summary merges 12.14 (a smell: "unplug or stop using it, turn off that circuit at the breaker if you can do it safely, and call a qualified electrician") with 12.15 (smoke or flames: "get everyone out … call 911 from outside"). That has two effects. A plain smell becomes "get out, call 911". Smoke at the **panel** becomes "power off at the breaker if it's safe", which sends a 50+ viewer to a smoking panel. Facts 9.5 says a burning smell or buzzing at the panel means "call an electrician and don't touch".
- The shock summary leaves out three steps from 12.8 to 12.11: pushing the source away with something dry, CPR, and "a doctor even if they seem fine".

**Fix:** replace the paragraph at line 299 with:

```
**Emergency sequences get every step, whatever the brief leaves out** (the gas-leak lesson from plumbing run 02), in the script's own words, whenever the script mentions the situation, even in passing:
- **Someone being shocked** (facts guide 12.8 to 12.11): don't touch them while they're in contact, the current can pass into you; cut the power (unplug it, or switch off the breaker or pull the fuse); if you can't, push the source away with something dry that doesn't conduct (wood, plastic, cardboard); call 911 if they have burns, confusion, trouble breathing, an irregular heartbeat, muscle pain or spasms, a seizure, or passed out even briefly; CPR if they aren't breathing or have no pulse; a doctor even when they seem fine.
- **An outlet or switch that's hot, uncomfortable to touch or discolored** (12.13): stop using it now, unplug what's in it, and get a licensed electrician soon.
- **A burning or rubbery smell, sparks, buzzing or crackling** at an outlet, switch or appliance (12.14): unplug it or stop using it, switch that circuit off at the breaker if you can do it safely, and call a licensed electrician. If the smell, heat or buzz is at the panel itself, don't touch the panel (9.5).
- **Smoke or flames** (12.15): get everyone out, stay out, and call 911 from outside. Never water on an electrical fire. Nobody goes to the panel through smoke.
- **A downed power line** (12.12): assume it's live; stay at least thirty-five feet away, and away from anything it touches (a fence, a puddle); call 911 or the utility; never touch a person or a car that's in contact with it; in a car it falls on, stay inside.
- **Carbon monoxide, whenever a generator runs in the script** (11.1 to 11.7): outdoors only, at least twenty feet from doors, windows and vents; never in the house, garage, basement, crawl space or shed, even with the doors open; you can't see or smell it; dizzy, weak or sick near a running generator means fresh air now, then 911; battery or battery-backup CO alarms on every level.
- **Backfeeding, whenever it's named** (11.8 to 11.12): it can electrocute line crews and neighbors on the same transformer; a cord with a plug on both ends has live, bare prongs; the only safe ways to feed the house are in "Generators" below.
Never "pull them off the wire", "throw water on it" or "turn the main off and backfeed carefully".
```

Template line 55 becomes:
`- emergency sequences needed:      [none / shock / hot outlet / burning smell / smoke / downed line / carbon monoxide / backfeed: every step, facts-guide §11-§12]`

### B2. Proving the power off: the rule checks the tester only once and tests only one wire, it trusts a silent non-contact tester, and it never says a dead outlet isn't a dead box

**Where:** SKILL "Safety" rule 1, line 292: "Power off at the breaker, then prove it's off with a tester that's been checked on a known live outlet first, before any cover comes off." Also "The standard kit", line 89, which assumes "a non-contact voltage tester" and nothing else.

**Evidence:**
- Facts 8.2, "confirmed", is the three-point test: "check the tester on an outlet you know is live, test the wires you'll touch, then check the tester on the live outlet again. A dead battery can't fool you that way." The SKILL keeps only the first check.
- Facts 8.3, confirmed: "A non-contact pen tester can stay silent on a live wire: inside a metal box, on shielded or armored cable, with a weak battery … Silence never proves a wire is dead." Facts 8.4 says the workplace standard doesn't accept a non-contact tester at all. Brief 9 (A33) item 4 says the same. The SKILL's standard kit gives the viewer only that tester, and rule 1 lets its silence count as "prove it's off".
- Facts 8.5 ("test every wire in the box … one box can hold more than one circuit"), 4.27 (a split receptacle "possibly from two breakers: verify both halves dead") and 8.6 (a shared neutral can carry current with one side on). Brief 16 (C12) step 2 carries the split-outlet case, but briefs 4, 6, 11 and 15 don't. Under rule 1, an A12 or C15 script can kill one breaker, test one slot, and send the viewer into a box where the other half is still live.
- **The dead-outlet trap, C15 (brief 17):** the viewer's outlet is already dead, so "it's dead" feels proven. But the brief's own cause 4 puts the fault "at the last working outlet or the first dead one", and those boxes have live wires feeding through them. A switched-off light (A14's "switch wired on the neutral … the fixture … stay[s] at 120 V when off") and a GFCI's TEST button (the LINE side stays live, facts 6.10) give the same false "dead".
- Facts 8.11: a plug-in tester "is a check, never permission to touch".

**Fix:** replace rule 1 (line 292) with:

```
1. **Power off at the breaker, then prove it's off,** inside the first step that needs it, not as a disclaimer at the top, and before any cover plate, fixture or device comes off: check the tester on an outlet you know is live, test every wire in the box (both halves of a split outlet and every cable in it, not just the one you're working on), then check the tester on the live outlet again (facts guide 8.2, 8.5, 4.27). An outlet that's already dead, a switch turned off, a tripped GFCI or a GFCI's TEST button never proves the box is dead. A non-contact tester's silence is never proof on its own (8.3): in a metal box or on armored cable, a two-probe tester on the wires (8.4). A plug-in tester is a check, never permission to touch (8.11). Tape over the breaker so nobody flips it back on (8.13).
```

Replace "The standard kit" (line 89) with:

```
**The standard kit:** assume a screwdriver set, pliers, a flashlight, electrical tape and a non-contact voltage tester (or say once that one is worth owning), plus a plug-in outlet tester in any script that opens an outlet. The non-contact tester finds live wires; its silence never proves one dead (facts guide 8.3). Anything beyond that (a two-probe voltage tester or multimeter, wire strippers with a gauge, lever connectors, a fish tape, a stud finder) is named with what it does, never its price.
```

### B5. No standing line on aluminum or old wiring, so receptacle swaps in 1965-1975 houses go out with nothing

**Where:** SKILL "Safety", rules 1-6. Aluminum appears only as an example trigger in lessons row 70. Knob-and-tube appears nowhere. Neither is on the always-explain terms list (line 85).

**Fix:** add as a new Safety rule (after rule 5):

Aluminum repairs are a licensed electrician's job (facts guide 7.5, 7.7, 7.9, 3.16, 3.17). Never an ordinary outlet or switch, a push-in hole, a copper-only lever connector or an ordinary wire nut on aluminum. ### B6. Parts and methods: nothing stops a solder-seal connector in a house box, an upsized breaker or NM cable outdoors

**Where:** SKILL "Safety". "The stakes" mentions "the device that's never been listed" only as a stake, never as a rule. Lessons row 67's default "wire and breaker matched" is not in the SKILL.

**Evidence:**
- **B7, brief 12, "Never Solder Wires Again - The EASY Way to Connect Them".** The idea's thumbnail is "a clear solder-seal connector shrinking onto a wire under a heat gun". The brief says these connectors "carry no listing for building wiring" and suit "automotive, trailer, marine, landscape lighting and 24-volt sprinkler wiring", and that inside boxes you use "listed twist-on wire connectors, push-in connectors, or lever connectors". Facts 3.15 says the same. The title's promise ("the EASY way to connect them") and the thumbnail both point a homeowner at a heat gun inside a one-twenty-volt box. The brief also offers "crimped butt splices … with heat-shrink versions for wet spots" without saying where they may go.
- Brief 9 (A33): "a breaker is never upsized, since it protects the wire". Facts 7.13: "never 'fixed' with a bigger fuse, a penny or foil". Fix-this-problem titles about tripping breakers will come, and the SKILL has no standing line.
- **B10, brief 13:** "NM is not permitted in wet or damp locations … the inside of a raceway installed underground is a wet location". Facts 2.14 agrees, and the guide adds "Never say 'it's fine to bury a splice if you tape it well.'" The brief covers it this time; the SKILL has no default for the next brief that doesn't.
- Brief 2: "Never … splice outside a box". Facts 3.14: "Twisted wires wrapped in tape isn't a splice."

**Fix:** add as a new Safety rule:

```
7. **Parts and methods:** only connectors and devices listed for house wiring go in a house box (facts guide 3.14, 3.15): never a solder-seal or heat-shrink butt connector, never twisted wires and tape, never a splice outside a box. A breaker or fuse is never made bigger to stop it tripping: it protects the wire (1.2 to 1.4, 7.13). Indoor cable (NM, Romex) never goes outdoors, underground or into outdoor conduit (2.14). A box with no ground wire takes only what 4.15 lists; a GFCI there protects people but adds no ground (4.17). A product made for cars, boats or low-voltage lighting is named as that, and never shown or said as the fix for house wiring.
```

Also add to the type table, after the new B4 paragraph:

```
**A title that sells a product or method made for another job** (B7's solder-seal connectors are made for cars, boats and low-voltage lighting): the opening says plainly where it belongs and what goes in a house box instead, inside the first 45 seconds, and the house-wiring answer gets at least as much time as the other.
```

### B7. Panel-mistakes lists: the brief's own faults sit behind the dead front, and the SKILL gives the viewer no safe way to "check yours"

**Where:** SKILL Safety rule 4, line 295. The mistakes-list type row: the opening is "The symptom or the hidden work → the stake → …", and the item shape includes "6. the check that tells them it's right". The terms list (line 85) leaves out "dead front".

- Facts 9.5 gives the homeowner's real part: "with only the panel door open … rust or water stains, scorch marks, buzzing, a hot breaker or a burning smell. Any of the last four means call an electrician and don't touch." The SKILL's rule 4 says only "looks at the outside, resets breakers and reads the labels".

**Fix:** replace rule 4 with:

```
4. **Panels:** the door is the homeowner's; the dead front, the inner metal cover the breaker handles poke through, is not. Any of those last four means don't touch it, don't reset it, call (9.5). Everything behind the dead front stays live even with the main breaker off (9.4), so taking it off, and adding, swapping or upsizing any breaker (a GFCI or AFCI breaker included), is a licensed electrician's job (13.4). The meter and the wires from the pole belong to the utility (13.5, 9.19). In a panel-mistakes list, a fault behind the dead front is said as what an electrician finds when the inner cover comes off, and the item's check is what the viewer can see with the door open or what to ask for. It's never "take the cover off and check yours".
```
