## BLOCKER

### R1-B1. The item length guide can't reach the 12-minute minimum, and plumbing's "the minimum wins" line was dropped

- **Where:** `SKILL.md`, "The video types" > after "The step shape in how-to and fix videos": "**Length per item or step:** about 120-180 words is the guide in lists of 7 to 10; a bit longer is fine when nothing is fluff (operator, after plumbing run 03). Cut repeated warnings and restated points first." Repeated in `unit-plan-template.md` ITEMS OR STEPS ("about 120-180 words each") and in "Before handing over" ("at about 120-180 words").
- **Evidence:**
  - Arithmetic at Frederick's 195 wpm: the opening (about 45 s, ~146 words) and the ending (CTA line plus a 2-3 sentence comment ask, ~70 words) leave about 2,120 words for the items at 12 minutes. A 7-item list at 120-180 words = 840-1,260 item words, so 1,060-1,480 in all: **5.4 to 7.6 minutes**. A 10-item list: 1,420-2,020 words, **7.3 to 10.4 minutes**. A list of 7 to 10 can't pass 12 minutes inside the guide. The 17 first titles include lists of 7 (A29), 9 (A33), 10 (A30) and 3 (B6).
  - The plumbing pack had the tie-break; the electrical pack lost it. Plumbing: "Where the guide and the 12-minute minimum collide (a list of 10 or 11 items), the minimum wins, again with real value, never padding." Electrical has no such sentence.
  - The winners' 12-minute lists don't run 120-180-word items. Beat maps: E24 (12.9M, 11:59) items 264, 356 (+126 check), 388, 412, 290, 432 words; E25 (3.8M, 13:54) "Most run 45 seconds to 1:20", 167 to 364 words; E28 (11:06) tips 258 to 730 words. Only E26 runs 119-189 words, and E26 is 5:12 (6.6 minutes at 195).
  - Lesson 38 asked for more than a list guide: "**Adapt** to how-to and explainer shapes; give a guide per step/section, not a cap." The pack gives no guide for how-to steps, fix rungs, explainer blocks, X vs Y contenders or a single big mistake's parts.
  - Lessons §4.1: plumbing run 03 had "0 of 15 items in the length band", and the judge's "item-length warning" was never built: the plan is the only place length gets checked.
- **Fix (replace the "Length per item or step" bullet in `SKILL.md`):**

  > - **Length per item, step or block: set by the 12-minute minimum, never below it.** At 195 words a minute, about 2,100 words sit inside the items at 12 minutes and about 2,700 at 15 (the opening and the ending take about 220). So the guide per item is:
  >
  > | Items | Words each at 12 min | at 15 min | at 22 min |
  > |---|---|---|---|
  > | 3 | about 700 (split into named parts: see "Small counts") | 900 | 1,350 |
  > | 5 | 420 | 540 | 810 |
  > | 7 | 300 | 390 | 580 |
  > | 9 | 235 | 300 | 450 |
  > | 10 | 210 | 270 | 405 |
  >
  > How-to steps and fix rungs can be shorter (60 to 150 words) when the job has many steps; an explainer block runs about 150 to 250 words (E13, E14, E16, E17). The winners' 12-minute lists ran items of about 170 to 430 words (E24, E25). The minimum never wins over value: a longer item carries more of the brief (the reason, the check, the sign it's a call, the related slip), never a warning said twice or a point restated. Cut those first.

  - `unit-plan-template.md`, ITEMS OR STEPS header: replace "about 120-180 words each" with "words each from SKILL.md 'Length per item, step or block'", and add `| words: N` to the end of the per-item row.
  - "Before handing over", replace "at about 120-180 words, item one by about 30 seconds in lists" with "each at the words the plan's length estimate gave it, item one by about 30 seconds in lists".

### R1-B2. The how-to step shape tells the viewer to do jobs the pack says are a licensed electrician's

- **Where:** `SKILL.md`, "The step shape in how-to and fix videos", step 1: "what to do, as an instruction to the viewer". Type table, How-to row: title shape "How to [do the job] (the Safe Way)". Mistakes-list item shape step 5: "the right way, cut to what the viewer needs".
- **Evidence:**
  - Title A7, "How to Connect a Generator to Your House the Safe, Legal Way (Interlock vs Transfer Switch)", is a how-to by its title. Its brief ends: "This is a permit job for a licensed electrician". Its model E02 is 13 steps inside the main panel (two-pole breaker, neutral and ground bars, hots into the lugs, drilling the cover).
  - The pack's own Safety rules forbid the viewer that work: Safety 4, "the dead front comes off only for a licensed electrician"; Safety 5, "only an interlock with an inlet or a transfer switch, put in with a permit". INDEX on E02: "Panel work: for us, the electrician's job, explained." E02's "Doesn't transfer" also flags the live-panel footage. But the structure rules never say how a how-to becomes "explained". A writer following the step shape will write "Fit the two-pole breaker…" as an instruction to the viewer, so the pack contradicts itself and the script is unsafe.
  - The same gap hits A30 (10 Breaker Box Mistakes; brief: "opening the panel is a licensed electrician's job"), where item step 5, "the right way", and step 6, "the check", assume the viewer can fix and check double taps, neutrals and torque. It also hits B10 (outdoor runs: "Outdoor runs usually need a permit") and the aluminum lines in A2, A29 and A33 ("a licensed electrician's job").
  - Lesson 6 (§2) gives the how-to shape from the research: "…the fix, how to know it's right, when it's a licensed job".
- **Fix (add a new subsection after "The step shape in how-to and fix videos"):**

  > ### When the job is a licensed electrician's
  >
  > When the brief or the facts guide says the work is a licensed electrician's job (anything inside the panel, a generator interlock, inlet or transfer switch, a meter-socket adapter, aluminum wiring repairs, a new outdoor run that needs a permit), no step is an instruction to the viewer. Each step says what is done and why, as what the electrician does ("The electrician fits a two-pole breaker sized to the inlet, so…"). Then it says what the homeowner sees, checks or asks for: the kit listed for that exact panel, the permit and the inspection, the routine on a storm night, the sign it was done right. The opening's scope line says so plainly ("This is the part you hire out. Here's what right looks like, so you know it when you see it."). In a mistakes list about the panel, "the right way" is what the electrician does, and "the check" is what the viewer can see without opening anything: the label and the directory, the filler plates, a breaker that's warm or buzzing, scorch on the cover, the brand on the panel label. A "How to" title is paid as how it's done right and what to ask for, never as panel work the viewer does.

  - Step-shape step 1: replace "what to do, as an instruction to the viewer" with "what to do, as an instruction to the viewer (on a licensed electrician's job, what is done and what you check: see below)".
  - `unit-plan-template.md`, SAFETY: add the line `- who does each step:             [the viewer / a licensed electrician (explained, never instructed): which steps]`.

### R1-M5. "Pick the type from the title" doesn't settle five of the 17 first titles

- **Where:** "The video types": "Pick the type from the title." Example title shapes: How-to "How to [do the job] (the Safe Way)"; Fix "Why Does My X Keep Y?"; Explainer "Why Do Plugs Have Holes?"; Single big mistake "The Dead Outlet Mistake Everyone Makes".
- **Evidence:**
  - A2 "How To Actually Fix Short Wires In An Electrical Box (The Safe Way)" matches the how-to title shape word for word, but its model E09 is filed as fix and its brief is a three-fix ladder.
  - A14 "Why Do LED Bulbs Glow After You Switch Them Off? (WARNING)" matches both "Why Do Plugs Have Holes?" (explainer) and "Why Does My X Keep Y?" (fix); its brief is four causes, one dangerous.
  - C15 "The Dead Outlet Mistake Everyone Makes (It's Not the Breaker)" is the table's own single-big-mistake example, but its brief is a four-cause diagnose ladder.
  - A7 has "How to" and "vs" in one title.
  - C12 "Pull THIS Out… Never Fall Out Again" fits none (it is format P18, borrowed from another persona channel).

  The two shapes give different opening orders (how-to: job → can do → count → …; fix: symptom → wrong guess → real cause → …).
- **Fix (replace "Pick the type from the title." with):**

  > Pick the type from what the title promises, not its first words. "How to fix [a problem]", "The REAL reason your…" and "Why does my X keep Y" are **fix this problem**. "How to [add, install, connect, replace] X" is **how-to** (and a licensed electrician's job is explained, never instructed: see below). "Why do [things everyone owns] have/do X?", with nothing wrong in the house, is **explainer**; a "why does it do that" about a fault the viewer has (the glow, the trip) is **fix**. A title that names one mistake is **single big mistake** even when the brief gives several causes: they become the related slips. A "vs" inside a how-to title is the how-to with one X vs Y block at the step where the choice is made. These are the most common types, not the only ones.

### R1-M6. No rule for two titles on the same topic, and the first batch has three such pairs

- **Where:** nowhere in `SKILL.md` or the unit plan; the rotation log tracks models, stakes, promises and the comment question, not topic or facts.
- **Evidence:** initial-ideas.csv `overlap` column: A2 "Same topic as C2", A12 "Same topic as C12", A31 "Close to B11 (backfeeding)". - **Fix (add after "Using the models", as its own short section):**

  > ## Two titles on one topic
  >

"The mistake" and "pull this out" titles carry the one mistake or the one fix, explored in depth with its related slips. A danger title on a shared topic carries the chain of what happens; a product title carries the part itself and why it's sold. The plan's SIBLING line names the other title and what this script leaves to it. When the brief's facts are mostly the sibling's, the run stops with NOT READY and asks for this title's own material.

  - Rotation log (overrides row for `variety-rotation.md`): add `| topic: [3-5 words]` so a later chat sees the sibling in the last five lines.

### R1-M7. Title promises the rules forbid paying literally: no "title promise" rule, and lesson 46 is missing

- **Where:** "The opening" job 4: "It never takes back the title." "Emotion": "Never, still: … guaranteed outcomes ('this will never happen again')".
