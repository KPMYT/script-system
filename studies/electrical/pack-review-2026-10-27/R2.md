## Part 2. Findings

### BLOCKER

**B1. A fear title makes fear the lead feeling and brings it back every few items. That breaks "fire is the stakes, never the topic" and "able, not scared".**
- **Where:**
  - `unit-plan-template.md`, EMOTION: `- the lead feeling:                [pride and capability, unless the title says otherwise]`
  - SKILL.md rule 8: "**Reaffirm the title's feeling and key word** every few items, in nouns and verbs (the mistake, the real reason, the safe way), not "-ly" adverbs; not every item, never robotic."
- **Evidence:**
  - Of the first 17 titles (`ideation/initial-ideas.csv`), 5 carry a fear word: "Why Do LED Bulbs Glow After You Switch Them Off? (WARNING)"; "10 Breaker Box Mistakes That Make a Panel Dangerous (Is Yours One?)"; "The Generator Mistake That Can Kill the Lineman Down the Street"; "The Worst Electrical Mistake You Can Make - Backfeeding a Generator"; "BEWARE of These 3 Common Wiring Mistakes…". The csv's own emotion column lists "safety fear" first for 4 titles.
  - With "unless the title says otherwise", the plan's lead feeling becomes fear. Rule 8 then brings it back "every few items". Add "every item carries one stake line", and a 10-item panel script repeats "dangerous" about ten times, with fear as its lead.
  - The operator: "fire is the stakes, never the topic" (2026-10-26). CONCLUSIONS §8: "the viewer should leave feeling able, not scared."
  - The model for this exact shape, E32 ("90% Don't Know About This Fatal Generator Connection"), voices the death fear **3 times** in 11:17 (0:06, 1:36, 11:10), with relief at 3:51 and 9:24. Its "whole mistake is one 15-second explanation" (INDEX).
  - Frederick's old fire scripts ran dread words at a median of 2.2 per 1,000; the winners' median is 0.0.
- **Fix (template), replace the line with:**
  `- the lead feeling:                [always pride and capability (the viewer leaves able, not scared). If the title's feeling is fear (WARNING, Dangerous, Kill, Worst, BEWARE), write it here as the title's feeling, carried by the title's noun, never as the lead]`
- **Fix (SKILL.md rule 8), replace with:**
  > 8. **Reaffirm the title's feeling and key word** every few items, in nouns and verbs (the mistake, the real reason, the safe way), not "-ly" adverbs; not every item, never robotic. **When the title's feeling is fear** ("WARNING", "Dangerous", "Can Kill", "Worst", "BEWARE"), what comes back is the title's noun (the mistake, the backfeed, the panel, the lineman), not the danger word. The danger itself is said about three times in the whole video: in the opening, at the item where it's real, and once near the end as the reason the fix matters. Each time, the next line puts control back (rule 10). Fear never becomes the script's lead feeling: pride and capability lead every title.

  > - **"I":** "I/me/my" about 6 to 12 per 1,000. The models that don't demonstrate run 5.1 to 8.7; the higher figures in the rest are demonstration "I", which is banned. Verdict lines ("I'd…", "I won't…") in most items, which comes to about 2 to 4 per 1,000 (models: median 1.7, top quartile above 3.5). No demonstration "I" at all.
