**The item shape.** All five run the 10-31 rules' eight beats: point, the look, why, the elegant woman's choice, the authority beat, the value instruction, "the rule I always give", the result.
- **2 and 3 run them as a fill-in form.** Ten items of about 150 to 230 words leave about 75 seconds each, so every beat gets a sentence or two and every item ends the same way. 2 says "The rule I always give" **ten times**, once per item, as the beat's own label. 3 ends 8 of 10 on "That's the rule". The plumbing review called one ending shape a drumbeat; this is a louder one. The 10-31 rules ask for 250 to 330 words an item in lists of six or more, and 17 of the 20 items here fall short of that.
- **4 and 5 hold the same eight beats but vary the order and the wording,** and their items are long enough (250 to 430 words) for a story and a feeling.

**Item order works against a reason to stay.** The 10-31 rules put the best item first and "one strong item at about two-thirds with the priority flag". All five do this, at 59 to 72%. The effect:
- The flag tells the viewer the best is now.
- The items get shorter after it (1: 371 down to 190 words; 5: 370 down to 248).
- In 1 and 2 the last item is the weakest or an over-full one.

Under session 17's plumbing decision (one promise in the opening that the last tip pays), the strong item would move to the end and the mid-video flag would go. On these scripts, that change would matter most in 1 and 2.

**Items too alike in one video:** 2's items 6 and 7 are "head to toe trends" and "head to toe matching", back to back. By ear, that sounds like one point twice.

**Bridges** link every item in all five, and none is a tease. The subhook formula "[Worried / Afraid / Need] ...? [answer]" appears in 1, 2, 3 and 4:
- "Worried your legs aren't what they were?"
- "Worried your coat dates you?"
- "Worried your clothes look tired?"
- "Need a new winter wardrobe?" / "New summer wardrobe?"

5 alone avoids it ("If your coat swallows you, this returns your shape.").

**Teaching that lands by ear:**
- "a column always looks taller than two blocks" (1)
- "A coat needs a finish, the way a room needs a lamp" (2)
