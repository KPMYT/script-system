# Group A price check (second, independent checker)

Checked 2026-10-27 against `A-research.md` (calling an HVAC tech; furnace parts and repairs). National US figures, for rounded on-screen estimates only. Format copied from `studies/plumbing/price-research-2026-10/A-verify.md`.

## How this check was done

- I only counted publishers the researcher did **not** use for that job. The main ones were **Thumbtack**, **Today's Homeowner**, **Home Depot** (for parts), **Sears Home Services**, **HomeServe**, city and county **permit fee schedules**, and a few one-off pages named in the table.
- **Read live** means I downloaded the page and read the figure off it myself: Thumbtack (furnace repair, HVAC repair, furnace tune-up, furnace maintenance, AC service, blower motor and thermostat pages), Today's Homeowner (HVAC maintenance, AC tune-up, AC repair, furnace ignitor, gas valve, heat exchanger and blower motor pages), Home Depot search pages (prices at the default store), Liberty Home Guard, HVAC.com's limit switch page, latestcost.com, Morgan Hill CA and Clatsop County OR fee schedules. **Via search** means the figure came from the search engine's summary. Treat via-search figures as probably right but not read off the page.
- **Sears Home Services** blocked direct reading (403). Its figures (updated Aug 2026) were read live in a table on **Liberty Home Guard**'s page (published Oct 14 2026), so they are second-hand. Liberty Home Guard sells home warranties.
- **Single contractors.** Sunrise HVAC (Las Vegas) and Efficiency Heating & Cooling (Portland area) publish their own prices. They're shown as local examples, never as national figures.
- **Not counted as independent:**
  - **American Standard's** HVAC repair cost page has the same table as Trane, word for word, and says it was built "with the help of Forbes". American Standard and Trane are one company (Trane Technologies). So Trane's figures probably come from Forbes, and Forbes can't count as a second source next to Trane.
  - **Fixr's** Oct 2026 HVAC article (one of the researcher's main sources) lists its sources at the foot of the page: thefixprice.com, bookingcontractors.com, handvetted.com, hvacpricingguide.com, hvacservicespro.com, royalclassservice.com, plus Thumbtack and Angi. These are small sites, not Fixr's own data. Its flame sensor, igniter and pressure switch figures ($100-$300, $150-$400, $200-$400) are the same as Sears Home Services' figures.
- **Thumbtack's pages** have a 2026 "last updated" header and a headline "according to Thumbtack data" range, but the body text is old: hourly rates of $60-$70, a $65 thermostat install, and "R-22 production will stop in 2020" written in the future tense. I counted the headline ranges. I used the body figures only as support.
- **Searches used: 11 of 12.** Fetches don't count against the limit.

## Out-of-date or easy-to-dispute figures

- **The igniter low end ($100).** Today's Homeowner ($300-$425), Sears ($150-$400) and a Las Vegas contractor ($385) all start higher. A viewer who paid $350 will say "$100" is not real. Use "$150 to $400".
- **Control board top end ($750).** Thumbtack ($600-$1,000) and Sears ($400-$1,000) both run to $1,000.
- **The permit line.** Real fee schedules for a like-for-like furnace swap are mostly $60-$250 (Issaquah $63, Clatsop County $73 with a $119 minimum, Morgan Hill $251). "Usually $100 to a few hundred" overstates a simple swap.
- **Thumbtack's "furnace tune-up" headline ($153-$693, most pay $323).** Don't quote it as a tune-up price. It's the same dataset as Thumbtack's furnace maintenance page and seems to include repairs.
- **Trane plus Forbes.** They aren't two sources. Trane's table is the same as American Standard's, and American Standard credits Forbes.
- **Fixr's Oct 2026 article** stands on small sites. Where it's the only support for a figure, treat the figure as thin.
- **Old sources the researcher cites:** Bob Vila (Jan 2023), Inch Calculator (Sep 2023), Nicor Gas (Dec 2020). Trane's page is from Sep 2024. Today's Homeowner's pages are 2025. The figures mostly agree with newer sources, but the citations are old.
- **Nicor's "a heat exchanger can cost more than a new furnace."** Today's Homeowner puts a new gas furnace at about $3,000 ($2,000-$6,000) and the heat exchanger at up to $3,500, so the two can be close on a cheap furnace. Still, "more than a new furnace" is not safe to say. Agree with the researcher: drop it.
- **Brief title "The $12 Part Techs Charge $300 to Replace."** Sears' flame sensor top is $300 and a Las Vegas contractor charges $285, so $300 is a real top-end price, not the typical one. Keep the researcher's advice: say "up to $300".
