# Group B price check (second, independent checker)

Checked 2026-10-27 against `B-research.md` (AC and heat pump parts and repairs; replacement quotes). National US figures, for rounded on-screen estimates only. Format copied from `studies/plumbing/price-research-2026-10/A-verify.md`.

## How this check was done

- I only counted publishers the researcher did **not** use for that job. The main ones were **Thumbtack**, **Today's Homeowner**, **NerdWallet**, **Consumer Reports**, **NADCA** (the duct cleaners' trade association), **Home Depot** (for parts), plus **HomeGuide**, **Forbes Home**, **ConsumerAffairs**, **AC Direct** and **HomeServe**, which I could only see through search summaries.
- **Read live** means I downloaded the page and read the figure off it myself: Thumbtack, Today's Homeowner, NerdWallet, Consumer Reports, NADCA, Carrier and Home Depot. **Via search** means the figure came from the search engine's summary, because the site blocks downloads (HomeGuide, Forbes, ConsumerAffairs, HomeServe, AC Direct and Lowe's all returned 403). Treat via-search figures as probably right but not read off the page.
- **Some publishers that look independent are not:**
  - **Today's Homeowner** shares a cost table with This Old House (see the plumbing README). Several of its lines are the Angi figures word for word: gas furnace $3,800-$10,000, HVAC system $5,000-$12,500 (average $7,500) and window AC $150-$550. I didn't count those lines. Its own tables (duct cleaning, furnace by efficiency, heat pump by home size, the R-22 charge table) don't match Angi, so I counted those.
  - **NerdWallet's** furnace page quotes "2026 data from ... Angi" ($2,823-$6,888, average $4,800), so it isn't independent for furnaces. Its central-air page names no source, and I counted it.
  - **Carrier's** recharge and R-22 pages footnote Angi for every price ($200-$500 recharge, R-22 $90-$250 a pound), so they count with Angi.
  - **ConsumerAffairs's** furnace figure ($2,800-$6,900, via search) is the same Angi number again, so I didn't count it.
  - **Fixr's** whole-system line ($5,000-$12,500) is identical to Angi's, and Fixr lists angi.com among its sources, so it isn't a second voice for that row.
- **Where I put my searches.** I spent the budget on the rows the scripts will use most: capacitor, refrigerant, furnace 80% and 95%, central AC, heat pump, whole system and duct cleaning. The minor-repair rows I didn't re-check are marked "unverified (not re-checked)". I didn't dispute them, and the researcher's figure stands until someone checks them.
- Web searches used: **11 of the 12 allowed**. Page downloads don't count against the budget.

## Out-of-date or easy-to-dispute figures

- **R-22 "$90-$150 a pound"** is out of date. Angi itself now says $90-$250 (quoted by Carrier's 2026 page). A viewer with an old R-22 system who was quoted $200 a pound will dispute a "$150" ceiling. Also, "a recharge a few hundred dollars" undersells a *full* R-22 charge: Today's Homeowner's table has $930-$1,500 for a 3-ton system.
- **Whole system "$5,000"** as a low end comes from one data source (Angi) repeated by five sites. Independent figures (ConsumerAffairs, Forbes, Modernize) put the typical job at $8,000-$15,000, around $10,000-$11,500. A homeowner who has just been quoted $12,000 will find "$5,000" hard to believe.
- **Thumbtack's low furnace and heat pump averages** ($3,215 and $3,414) mix in electric furnaces, wall units and outdoor-unit-only swaps. Don't quote them as the price of a new gas furnace or ducted heat pump.
- **Thumbtack's heat pump page (Jul 1 2026) still says a $2,000 credit is available "between January 1, 2023, and December 31, 2032"**, citing ENERGY STAR. The researcher's flag 7 says the federal 25C credit ended Dec 31, 2025. Thumbtack's line is probably stale; group D should check this against irs.gov before any script mentions a credit.
- **Pages older than about a year still cited or used:** Today's Homeowner AC (Mar 2025), heat pump (Feb 2025), R-22 (May 2025), duct cleaning (Apr 2025) and furnace (Sep 2025); NerdWallet central air (Oct 2025); Forbes AC and furnace figures (2024). These agree with current sources, but the citations are old.
- **Duct cleaning, a useful fact:** NADCA warns against "whole-house specials for $100 or less" as a sign of a scam. That supports the "$300 and up" low end and is a good line for the script.
