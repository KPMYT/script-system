I **re-read the researcher's own page live** to check the wording and date, and I mark those rows "re-read, same source".
- **Read live** means I downloaded the page and read the wording off it myself. **Fetch summary** means a fetch tool read the page and quoted it back to me. **Via search** means the wording came from the search engine's extract only. Don't quote via-search lines on camera.

## Notes for the guide

1. **Gas leak:** drop "doorbells" unless someone re-reads a live utility page that says it. Use "garage door opener" (Black Hills, Dominion). "Don't try to find the leak yourself" (CenterPoint, Dominion) is a good added line.
2. **CO alarm vs chirp:** Indiana DHS gives the alarm sound ("4 quick beeps and a pause"). That's a clean way to tell viewers the real alarm from a low-battery chirp.
3. **R-410A:** the federal picture is as the researcher reads it. Add a caution that states such as New York (and, per an ACHR headline, Washington) keep stricter rules. That's one more reason not to put an install date in a script.
4. **1% per degree:** the researcher's flag is too strict. The figure is on a live DOE PDF (2021). It can be cited as "about 1% off the heating bill per degree, for an eight-hour setback".
