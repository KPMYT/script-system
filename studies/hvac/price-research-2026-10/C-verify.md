# Group C price check (second, independent checker)

Checked 2026-10-27 against `C-research.md` (the shelf, filters, drafts and the bill, the outage). National US figures, for rounded on-screen estimates only. Format copied from `studies/plumbing/price-research-2026-10/A-verify.md`.

## How this check was done

- I counted only publishers and sellers the researcher did **not** use for that job. Angi and HomeAdvisor count as one company, and Bob Vila's audit page cites Angi, so HomeAdvisor figures were not counted as new for the energy audit.
- I put the effort where the scripts will lean hardest: filters by type, CO alarms, weatherstrip and door sweep, caulk, canned spray foam, the inverter, the furnace transfer switch, the portable generator, the space heater and the energy audit. Blown-in attic insulation, the power station and the interlock got a lighter check. The other rows were **not re-checked** (marked "unverified" below, with the researcher's own safe line carried as the suggested range).
- **Read live** means I opened the page and read the figure off it myself. **Via search** means the figure came only from the search engine's summary. Big-box pages (Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart, Ace, Menards, Meijer, Northern Tool, SupplyHouse, HomeGuide) blocked direct reads (HTTP 403, or the page loaded with no prices). So the parts checks use retailers that did open: **Harbor Freight**, **Filterbuy** (sells direct), **Kellogg Supply**, **Ross Tool**, **Fenwick Hardware**, **Ranch-Home**, **Theisens**, **All American Do it Center**, **Thomas Do it Center**, **Runnings**, **Bees Lighting** and **Electric Bargain Stores**, plus **First Alert's own store** (maker list prices).
- For the cost guides and facts I used **US DOE**, **IRS**, **Consumer Reports**, **Family Handyman**, **Today's Homeowner**, **TechGearLab**, **Linquip** and **Whitney Services** (a Michigan electrician).
- Web searches used: **12 of 12**. WebFetch reads were not counted.
- Retail prices are October 2026 snapshots, and some pages already disagree with their own search snippets (see the spot-checks).

## Out-of-date or easy-to-dispute figures

- **The federal tax credit for an energy audit and for air sealing or insulation has ended.** IRS "Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit", last reviewed Apr 28 2026 (read live): "allowed for qualifying property placed in service on or after Jan. 1, 2023, and before December 31, 2025". It was up to $150 for a home energy audit, and 30% up to $1,200 a year for insulation, air sealing and weatherstripping. Consumer Reports (updated Oct 1 2025, read live) says the same: the credits "now expire at the end of 2025". **No script should say a viewer can get a federal tax credit for an audit, weatherstripping or insulation in 2026.** Utility rebates and discounted audits are a different matter and still exist in many areas, so say "check with your utility" instead.
- **MERV 11-13 "$12-$15 each in a multipack"**: online sellers that sell direct (Filterbuy) are about $8-$10 a filter in a 6-pack. A viewer who buys that way will call $15 high. "$10 to $25" covers both.
- **First Alert CO400 and CO605** are dropping off the maker's list (see the spot-checks). The prices still hold, but don't name the models on screen.
- **Space heater "most $35-$75"** runs a little low. Independent list prices cluster around $50-$100. Use "$30 to $100".
- **Furnace transfer switch "often $200-$500 installed"** (the brief): still not found as a priced job by any publisher. Keep it in words.
- **Inverter prices:** Harbor Freight sells 1,000-1,500 W *modified* sine units for $90-$130. If a script says "about $100 for an inverter" without "pure sine", viewers will find the cheap kind. Furnace boards want pure sine, so say "pure sine" with the price.
- **Old sources still cited by the researcher:** Bob Vila blown-in insulation (Jul 2022); Inch Calculator generators (Aug 2023); HVAC.com Aprilaire filter life (Sep 2023); Fort Collins combustion test (2011) and DOE combustion case study (2013-2016) (the researcher already kept these out of the ranges); Networx chimney balloon prices (undated). The DOE energy-assessment figure I used is from Aug 2021, but it agrees with Family Handyman's 2024 figure.
- **Today's Homeowner's attic page** contradicts itself ($2-$2.50 a sq ft, but $1,500 for 1,500 sq ft). Use the range, not either of its figures on their own.
