# HVAC price research (2026-10-27)

The evidence behind `hvac-scripts/references/price-guide.md`. Collected for the operator's request (2026-10-27): "please doe everything you can still do. get it ready. make the persona profile, the reference files, the price guide and so on." Method copied from `studies/plumbing/price-research-2026-10/README.md`.

## How it was done

1. **Job lists** (`jobs.md`): four groups (A calling a tech and furnace parts; B AC, heat pumps and replacement quotes; C filters, the shelf, drafts and the outage; D facts and safety), drawn from batch 1's 16 briefs, the HVAC study's comments and the pack diagnosis.
2. **First pass** (`A-research.md` to `D-research.md`): one researcher per group, two or more independent publishers or retailers per figure, each source with its URL, figure and date, tagged "read live", "fetched" or "via search". Each also flagged batch-1 brief figures that didn't hold up.
3. **Second pass** (`A-verify.md` to `D-verify.md`): a different researcher per group checked the figures against publishers the first researcher didn't use, with a verdict (confirmed, adjust, disputed, unverified), a suggested script range, live spot-checks and flags for old or easy-to-dispute figures. Each had 12 web searches; page reads didn't count.
4. **The guide** takes each verifier's suggested range. Its Check column maps the verdicts: confirmed → confirmed; adjust → adjusted; unverified (not re-checked, or one source) → thin; a range the verifier said to keep in words → in words. Section 8 (water damage) and three shelf rows are carried over from the plumbing research with their plumbing marks.

## Limits

- HomeGuide, Angi/HomeAdvisor, Forbes, Home Depot (in places), Lowe's, Walmart and some parts sellers block direct page reads, so some figures were seen only in search summaries. They're used as estimates, never as named sources.
- Not independent, so counted as one source: Angi with HomeAdvisor, This Old House and Today's Homeowner's shared lines, Trane with American Standard (whose table credits Forbes), Carrier's and NerdWallet's Angi-footnoted figures, and Fixr's whole-system line.
- Official facts with one publisher (EPA, ENERGY STAR, NFPA, CDC, IRS) were re-read live by the verifier rather than matched to a second source.
- Prices are October 2026 national figures; refrigerant rules and tax credits change. Re-check yearly.

## Brief corrections found

The researchers and verifiers flagged batch-1 brief figures that don't hold up (each research file's last section, and the verify files' "out-of-date" lists). Among them: space heaters "88% of deaths" (now 30% of fires, 73% of deaths, NFPA 2020-2024); MERV 11-13 filters "$20-$35" (about $10-$25); the condensate line "$150-$300" (about $100-$250); "a few gallons a day" from a condensing furnace (up to about 8-10); the furnace transfer switch "$200-$500 installed" (not confirmed). They are to be corrected in niche-research's batch-1 briefs, not here.
