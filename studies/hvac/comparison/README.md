## 1. The eight findings that matter most

1. **The breakdown moment is what HVAC viewers live.** Their comments tell it again and again (counts in `01` §1):
   - about 31 furnace-out-in-the-cold stories, 5 of them on holidays
   - 19 AC-out-in-a-heat-wave stories
   - 22 power-outage stories
   - 14 "the company can't come for days"

   "2:00AM, two days after Christmas, 40 degrees out… You kept my family warm" [322]. Plumbing's strongest opening feeling was the failed attempt; in HVAC, failed attempts are rare (about 13).
2. **HVAC viewers name the trade; plumbing viewers didn't.** About 9% of HVAC comments name the trade, against 8 of 506 in plumbing. That's about 95 distrust comments against about 34 defences of techs. The best-known story is "you need a whole new unit" (about 16), and the quote against the part price (39 comments; capacitors quoted $400-$1,500 against a $13-$35 part). Techs defend themselves hard: "We are not all out here to screw you over" [1.6K]. So plumbing's line "the bill is the pain, not the plumber" doesn't hold as it is. Its rule (aim at the price and the silence, never motives) fits HVAC even better:
   - Ray names a motive in 5 of 6 videos ("the way the pay is built, it rewards the breakdown").
   - HVAC Exposed never softens it, and drew the only "this is a lie" comments (`03` §1).
3. **Real people get to the point; Ray doesn't.** First real value at 0:30 for real people (16 of 25 by 45 s), against 1:26 for the faceless channels (none by 45 s). Ray's openings run 120-260 words. His fastest (0:58) is on his biggest video (`02` §2). Roger the Pipe Guy's own AC drain video names vinegar first at 5:04. The plumbing "point first" rule is right for HVAC.
4. **The winners' list items are long.** 3 of 77 fall inside plumbing's 100-130-word guide; the medians are 303 words (real) and 250 (faceless) (`02` §3). Plumbing's operator already said "fine if it runs a bit longer, as long as it's not fluff".
5. **Safety is where viewers correct the video.** About 62 corrections, 55 of them on hands-on real-person videos (`01` §4):
   - check that the power is really off [1.2K]
   - discharge the capacitor [1.1K]
   - never pressure-wash the coils [1.2K]
   - a respirator for spray foam [2K]

   Nobody in the 36 scripts says "never bypass a limit switch" or "refill the condensate trap". The 2.45M car-as-generator video never mentions exhaust or carbon monoxide (`03` §3). Roger's safety rules (the diagnosis, §3.15) are the gap Ray leaves open.
6. **The comment question works in HVAC.** Ray asks about the viewer's own numbers (state, thermostat setting, bill), and about 44% of his comments answer it, about 12-30 per video. HVAC Exposed's "filter horror story" ask drew none (`03` §6, `01` §8).
7. **Ray's sound is easy to recognise:**
   - "Keep your cool and I'll see you in the next one" (6 of 6)
   - "25 years" inside 50 s (6 of 6)
   - "same companies" (5 of 6; 0 in the other 30 transcripts)
   - "quietly" 10 times (3 in the other 30)
   - "None of this is hidden because it's complicated. It's hidden because…" (4 of 6)
   - a 40-55-word "quick thing… no pressure" plug at 13-21% (4 of 6)
   - "I read every one" (5 of 6)

   Some comments already notice it's an AI voice reused on another channel (`04` §1, `01` §6).
8. **HVAC has feelings plumbing has no word for** (`03` §2):
   - the clock ("It fails in August quietly, and the cold night is only when it tells you")
   - the house that won't reach the number on the dial
   - comfort felt in the body
   - the machine's sounds

## 3. What the study doesn't show

- **Comment depth:** NexLev's comment pages mostly repeat, so each video has about 40-60 comments, not the brief's 80. The biggest comment threads aren't fully read.
- **The voices:** sound and age are not checked (no audio). The voice picks are readings from the transcripts.
- **WINNI** (9.17M, the cold-walls video) has no transcript, so it's out of the script analysis.
- **Auto-captions:** 16 transcripts have no punctuation, so question counts there are minimums. Dollar counts miss amounts said without "$" or "dollars".
- **Very high multiples** (grayfurnaceman 781x, HVAC Exposed 1,056x) come from channels whose recent uploads are off topic or tiny. They show that a video was big for its channel, not more than that.
