20. (34 likes) I've thought of doing this for years and never knew quite how to accomplish it. I tried it once with a 125 volt converted hooked to the cigarette lighter outlet, but it did not produce enough power to start the frig, which has a motor that draws a great deal of current on it's initial startup, and of course running the frig is the first priority to keep food from spoiling. Two points worth noting on your setup. First, the converter you chose will generate just 8 amps at 125 volts, I would be concerned about this providing enough current for both the frig and the furnace. A 2500 watt unit would provide a nice comfortable 20 amp service. Second and perhaps most important, many extension cords use very thin (18 gauge) wire and are rated for under 10 amps. I would encourage folks to be sure to use a higher rated cord, also keeping in mind that long runs of extension cords suffer from voltage loss, so again, use of high gauge wire in the cord minimizes this problem. If you are uncertain, then simple feel the cord with you hands while the furnace or frig is hooked up, and if it is getting warm, then this is a sign of resistance in the wire bleeding off voltage and converting the loss to heat, and a clear sign that a large gauge wire is needed for that application. I would always do this test. Better yet, I have an extension cable made from 12/2 internal house wiring wire,  and so I will never have an issue with it.
