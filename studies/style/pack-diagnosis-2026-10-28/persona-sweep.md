## 4. How Diane's "French way" lens should work

**What the ideation's flip recipe does (steering §2):** "every non-French winner in an intake is filed as a topic and re-made through five proven lens devices": the series key "(The French Way)", the in-group plural, the national contrast pair, the tradition-tenure F2, and the rules listicle. "Every flip keeps the source topic and swaps only the lens; a topic with no documented French practice behind it (contouring, hair extensions, athleisure styling) is **not flipped**." For a script this means the title's topic is the item list, and the French practice is the reason behind each item, as in Werner's "The German object is the reason, not the instruction". The fix is always one the viewer does in her own closet, with American sizes and shops.

**The safe claims: documented practice (truth doctrine, steering §2; `audience.md` §6):**
- the wardrobe: the small edited wardrobe on neutrals with one accent; the personal uniform repeated without apology; buying fewer, better pieces; tailoring as routine
- the pieces: the trench, the Breton striped top, the well-cut blazer, dark straight jeans, the silk or wool scarf, ballet flats and loafers, the structured bag without logos; one statement at a time
- the hair: air-dried, low-maintenance hair; gray worn on purpose and kept glossy
- the face: the bare or near-bare face with a red or berry lip
- the pharmacy culture
- attitude: comfort in one's age

The doctrine itself says "Verify at scripting: any 'every Frenchwoman' wording …, any statistic, any historical origin claim (the Breton stripe, the trench) — real and sourced or dropped."

**How the study's presenters hedged:**
- **Marie-Anne Lecoeur** (a French woman living in France) hedges with frequency words and her own wardrobe: "the jeans that French women wear are usually medium to dark denim"; "And most French women have at least one."; "Of course, I'm showing you what I have in my wardrobe that suit me." (n9ICHZq3_tY).
- **Her "AI Gets French Style WRONG!"** (138K) says AI channels "invariably … get it wrong" but names nothing specific. Its ten pieces are plain staples: white T-shirt, dark denim, the marinière, a shirt, a fine cardigan, navy blazer, navy turtleneck, trench, a Chanel-style jacket, a midi LBD. The corrections are "no skinnies and no rips", and thick cardigans "make people a little frumpy or a lot frumpy". Her pinned comment invites viewers to judge AI channels, and 5 comments are against AI (`03` §6.2). Reading: a Diane script is exposed when it claims what French women do with false certainty or on invented specifics. The staples list matches the truth doctrine, so the doctrine's floor is the safe ground.
- **French Fashion's "The Only 10 Clothes You Need All Winter (The French Way, 50+)"** (449K, 32x, AI presenter) states the lens mostly unhedged ("That's why in France, this is where the money goes."; "Nobody in the history of France has ever lifted a woman's sweater to inspect her waistband."). It hedges once, on the beret ("Yes, the cliché, but there's a reason it became a cliché."). It gives buying tests with numbers (wool percentage, denier) and gives the persona a husband, a granddaughter and boots "I bought when Chirac was president". Its top comment theme was climate pushback ("This might work in France, but in the US we get some serious winters."). Its biggest lesson for us is the item test the viewer can do, not the lived detail, which our claims policy forbids.

**Where our 13 stand (`03` §6.1):** 209 French mentions, 16 hedged (8%). Several are habits stated as fact: "What does a French woman do instead? She buys for her shoulders"; "A French woman does not actually walk the dog bare-faced"; "And French women never really went along with it." Some carry invented proportions or figures: "Half of them skip black in daylight altogether" (akYILT648sU); "French women are known for going longer between cuts, often 10 or 12 weeks" (egJ5B6d879c). One is an unsourced custom: "In France, children are traditionally given a medal at their christening, usually by their godparents, in yellow gold" (41ezk-kRd7Y).

**What the library's rules say:**
- `research-and-sourcing.md` §2: facts are "always verified" (numbers, rules, stories), and "the fact underneath is true". Framing may exaggerate degree, never invent the event.
- `claims-policy.md`: "Nothing false or unsupported is ever stated as fact about a named company or person"; a heritage claim only as the profile's one approved line.
- Werner's precedent ("THE GERMAN WAY"): "Every German claim comes from the brief, hedged the way people talk … Never an invented German law, ban, standard, statistic, product or piece of history"; never "it is illegal in Germany".

**Proposed lens rules for Diane's profile (NEW, from the above):**
1. **Every French claim comes from the brief or the facts guide** (the truth-doctrine list), hedged as people talk: "most French women", "in Paris it's usual", "plenty of women there". No "every", "never" or "all" about French women except in the locked title. Figures and percentages appear only as the brief sources them.
2. **The French practice is the reason, the viewer's closet is the fix.** American sizes, shops and seasons; one sentence of practical caution where climate or budget differs (the French Fashion pushback).
3. **No invented laws, sayings, statistics or customs.** The christening-medal kind of claim needs a source in the brief or goes.
4. **Contrast as habit, not people.** "American women" only as a collective habit in a contrast pair, in the third person, at most once or twice a script (Diane said it in 3 videos). Never the viewer, never mockery; admire the American side where natural (steering: "admires American warmth and generosity").
5. **Her position follows the origin answer** (ASK §3.2). Under (a) she speaks as a borrower ("let's borrow it"); under (b) the one heritage line, fixed; under neither does she place herself in "we Americans".
6. **French words:** at most one or two widely known words per script (chic, voilà), in ASCII or via a scanner allowance after a voice check; never full sentences, never a phonetic accent in text (Werner's "structural, never phonetic").
7. **Topic gate:** if the title's topic has no documented French practice behind it, the script doesn't invent one; it flags the title back (`research-and-sourcing.md` §10).
