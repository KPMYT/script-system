# Style study, comparison 01: the viewers (from their comments)

For the future style niche pack (Scarlet Sterling and Diane de Chambray). It describes and counts what the comments show. It does not suggest rules or pack wording.

**Source.** Every `comments.md` for the 45 study videos in `inputs/videos.csv` (the two DROPPED rows, `SdbAfqmUwJ0` and `X9mj5fEPfVk`, left out), collected 2026-10-28, plus `own-baseline-2026-10/data/*/*/comments.md`. Duplicates across sorts were already merged by the collectors; entries marked "[also in top]" were dropped. 1,651 study comments. Left out: 21 owner comments and the 31 comments marked `[account named like a style channel]` (§6). That leaves **1,603 viewer comments**:
- **Real presenter (R):** 811 comments on 21 videos.
- **Faceless or AI (F):** 634 on 18 videos: 478 faceless, 117 AI presenter (Chic After 60 leggings, Ageless Melanin, French Fashion), 39 "unclear" (Ageless Style 50s & 60s).
- **Copycat pairs (P):** 158 on 6 videos (all faceless/AI or unchecked); counted with F where the text says "faceless/AI", split out in §9.
- **Our channels (O):** 42 viewer comments (Scarlet 38, Diane 4) plus 15 owner link comments.

**How to read this.**
- **(hand)** = I read every comment and coded it; the coded IDs are in my working files, not here. **(kw)** = keyword count by script; blunt, includes false hits. **(reading)** = my interpretation.
- Quotes are verbatim, spelling kept; likes in brackets as the file gives them; then the video ID. No usernames.
- Percentages are of the side's viewer comments (R 811, F 634, P 158).

**Limits.** About 36 comments per video (1 on `UTpC6n7KKi0`, 8 on `ZFx3yIrJqO0`). The top sort favours old comments. Several faceless videos ask a comment question (location, age, favourite colour), which inflates those answers. The channel-named marker was applied by one collector (helper 3, 11 videos) only; on the other 34 videos such comments are in the counts (§6).

## The short version

1. **The audience is mostly 60s and 70s.** 149 viewers give an age (hand): median 70; 51% are 70 or over, 32% in their 60s, 10% in their 50s, 7% under 50. Faceless/AI median 70, pairs 74, real 65 (the real set has "any age" hair videos).
2. **The feared words are rare; practical problems are common.** "Invisible" 3 (two of them welcome it), "frumpy" about herself 6, "given up" 6 (hand). Against: no stylist who can cut 28, no clothes that fit 28, a changed body 18, health or mobility limits 22 (hand).
3. **The models are the biggest complaint:** 78 comments say the bodies shown are too slim, young or perfect (hand): 8.7% of faceless, 7.6% of pair, 1.4% of real comments. Top: [1.1K] `6byOPawCepI`.
4. **"Old lady" is rejected, "granny cut" is used.** On the two "Scream 'Old Lady'" videos, 13 comments push back on the word, most of them among the top-liked; on the "Granny" cut video viewers use the word themselves (hand).
5. **AI remarks: 18 on 6 of 24 faceless/AI videos, 0 on the three AI-presenter videos, 0 calling a real presenter AI** (hand). On our channels, 9 of 42 comments (21%) name AI or a changed accent.
6. **Channel-named accounts:** 31 marked, all on faceless/AI videos; 11 of 43 comments and 28% of the likes on one pair winner. About 36 more read as generated (reading).
7. **Real-presenter audiences comment more and at greater length:** a median of 1.22 comments shown per 1,000 views against 0.76 faceless; 23% against 18% run 40 words or more; the "now I understand why" comment appears 18 times on real videos and 0 on faceless ones (hand).
8. **In the copycat pairs the flops' comments are answers to the end-of-video question** (24 of 29 give a location), while the winners' argue about the models and tell life stories.

## What this study can't show

- **Who didn't comment.** Commenters are a small, self-selected share (about 1 in 1,000 viewers), likely older, more opinionated and more often unhappy than the silent audience. Ages are only the 9% who gave one.
- **The real age split.** Faceless titles say "over 60", real ones often "over 50" or "any age", so the samples differ by design; YouTube's own audience data is not in this study.
- **Whether the comments caused or followed the views.** Top-sorted comments are old and liked by later viewers; a winning video simply has more of them.
- **Seeded comments outside one collector's 11 videos.** Usernames were not stored, so the channel-named count is a floor; the ~36 "reads as generated" are my reading and stay in the counts.
- **Whether viewers did what they say.** "I bought", "I tried" and "will try" are claims.
- **How many viewers notice AI without saying so,** or how the AI remarks affect retention. Silence on the three AI-presenter videos is not proof that viewers think the presenter is real.
- **Our channels:** 42 comments on 38 videos, most from the two or three best-viewed uploads, are too few for rates.
