# Style study, comparison 04: models and voice samples

For Scarlet Sterling (the affluent American, "look younger, polished, expensive") and Diane de Chambray ("the French way"): which video types exist in the niche, which study videos show each type's structure best, how the faceless/AI winners differ from the real creators, what separates the copycat winners from the flops, whose narration could give voice samples, and how real presenters and AI personas sound. **Description only** ("we just study for now"). Nothing here picks a model file; the operator decides later. No library, pack, skill or study-data file was changed.

**Sources.** `notes/` (43 study notes, `notes/INDEX.md`), `data/<channel>/<id>/` (transcripts, meta, comments), `comparison/00-measures.md` and `measures.csv`, `round2/BREAKOUT-MAP.md`, `inputs/study-videos.md`, `own-baseline-2026-10/SUMMARY.md` and its 14 notes, the 38 own transcripts. Views and multiples as collected on 2026-10-28.

**Conventions.**
- **R** = a real person on camera (16 women, 5 male stylists). **F** = faceless, no presenter on screen; the voice is not verified as human or AI unless said (Alice Luxe's about text states AI voiceovers). **A** = an AI presenter (round 2 `ai_avatar`). Vivian Sinclair is F in round 2, but the comments describe an on-screen presenter and call her AI: tagged **F/A**.
- **× normal** = views ÷ the channel's normal. Very high multiples come from tiny normals (AnniePicks' normal is 765).
- Word counts and segment times come from the notes (approximate, spoken words only). "Hook" = the words before the first real item.
- Every quote was checked by script against its `transcript.txt` or `comments.md`. A timestamp is the start of the caption segment where the quote begins. The captions are auto-generated, so the quotes keep their errors (Marie-Anne's "Virtual French fic ladies", Diana GOSS's "Ebola" for voilà).
- Phrase counts are by script over the transcripts, with the sides as in `measures.csv`: real 21, faceless/AI 24 (the 18 by type plus the 6 pair videos), own 38.

### T1. Whole-look mistakes that age you

Examples: MM Personal Styling `gfbWOw00Y-M` (R), Diana GOSS `eKCl-bRx8wg` (R), Marie-Anne Lecoeur `ixlexruRC6c` (R), Ageless Style 50s & 60s `Fs17Qc6l8Ug` (F, Marie-Anne's exact title), Elegance At 60 `vATkBSPjQmc` (F), Chic Over 60 `2IkhtemG-MM` (F).

- **Real:** a 30-175-word hook (Marie-Anne's is 30: "Are you ready for little tough love?" [0:00] (`ixlexruRC6c`)). The list starts at 0:13-0:59. Each item is worn by the presenter: the mistake, the dated version, the swap, the result in age words. Diana: "as you see here I swapped long top for men's shirt tucked it in the skirt" [1:25] (`eKCl-bRx8wg`). Marie-Anne repeats one frame per item: "The next thing that screams old lady is", then "So, what can you do instead?", then "My pro tip is". 5-10 items of 80-800 words. The endings are thin: a next-video pointer, a course ad mid-video, or a subscribe ask at 0:44 (MM).
- **Faceless:** a 32-296-word hook built on a scene or a reversal: "If you want to look older, tired, and invisible, please keep doing these seven things." [0:00] (`Fs17Qc6l8Ug`), or a closet question (`2IkhtemG-MM`). A claimed authority line, a held item ("I've saved it for last"), and sometimes a comment ask before item one. 7-10 even items (170-290 words): a pictured scene ("Imagine...", "Picture..."), why it fails, the swap ("The swap" is said 7 times in `Fs17Qc6l8Ug`), then a keep-or-go line ("then it's earned its exit"). The close: a recap, a reassurance ("This video wasn't meant to shame you. It was meant to wake you up." [10:32] (`Fs17Qc6l8Ug`)), a specific comment ask and a sign-off line.

### T2. Single-item mistakes

Examples: Ever Chic At 50 `kKjLX7Q6wc8` (F), Age with Style `6byOPawCepI` and `K6N1EFo7oec` (F, pair A), Golden Melanin Lifestyle `dn3mNRtYTY4` (F), Elegance At 60 `XByilJlme5Y` (F), Marie-Anne Lecoeur `MWQ6T1wURJU` (R).

- **The opening move is the reveal that the item is the culprit:** "My friends, it's usually not you, it's the blouse" [0:11] (`6byOPawCepI`); "It was your shoes, sis." [0:29] (`dn3mNRtYTY4`); "Ladies, can I be brutally honest?" [0:00] (`kKjLX7Q6wc8`).
- Then the channel welcome and an early subscribe ask (1:04-1:33 in three of the four faceless videos), sometimes a "why this item matters" section (Ever Chic's runs 337 words, so the first mistake lands at 4:35).
- **Each item:** why women do it, "Here's the problem", a picture, "So, what's the solution?", 2-5 fixes, a one-line rule ("Your blouse should frame you, not drown you." [2:54] (`6byOPawCepI`)). Golden Melanin heads off pushback four times ("Now, do not come for me." [2:14]) and holds its last mistake ("Here it is. The one I told you to stay for.").
- **Close:** a recap (184-203 words in the two blouse winners), a comment ask about which mistake.
- **The real version** (Marie-Anne): a 56-word hook, then bad examples pictured and a found "yes" dress for each; items front-loaded (the first and fifth are the longest); "Let me know what you think in the comments."

### T3. The item list (glasses, necklaces, earrings)

- **Ours** follow the same "N good + N that age you" frame, with a "Picture..." outfit scene and a one-line wrap per item ("Simple, warm, and impossible to argue with" [3:04] (`w6w_ktMBHXM`)).

### T4. How to wear X over 60

Examples: Chic After 60 `h6Y1RZzOlzg` (A), Adore Your Wardrobe `vBsFMkGDbhA` (R).

### T11. The gray journey

Examples: Woman in midlife `2e50uMLaCtE` (R, a story), Summer Beauty `xNuoMiG2kVw` (R, the methods). Alice Luxe's gray styles (`aaDZuD5jIfs`) is a T6 haircut list.

[6:25]), then an open question to viewers ("should I put color around my face?"). It has no subscribe ask, and the question was answered by about 24 of 39 comments.
- **The methods:** the skunk line as the villain, a belief reframe ("We have been conditioned to believe that gray hair equals giving up."), a credential, then 3 options, each with who it suits, the time, the cost and the upkeep, with family cases (her mother-in-law, her mother). It ends on "what stage you are in your gray hair journey".

### T12. The makeover story

Examples: MAKEOVERGUY `WgkP3h4QWpg` (R, 2.4M), Rami Jabali `RuyOhWfU-RI` and `qwM0s0Q3Rjw` (R).

- **Rami:** the client's brief, the diagnosis, the plan with a promise ("Stick to me for a year."), the price raised but not said, the cut under music, the first look. No CTA at all.

## What this can't show

- **Why a video won.** Thumbnails, titles' wording, channel size, upload time and the algorithm aren't visible here. The four pairs each carry a confound (section 4), and four pairs are too few to separate structure from luck.
- **How anyone sounds.** Nothing was listened to. The voice candidates, their ages and their accents are read from auto-captions; caption errors were kept. Whether a faceless voice is human or generated is unknown unless the channel says so (only Alice Luxe does).
- **Real against AI.** The tags come from round-2 thumbnail readings and comments, not from checking the videos. Vivian Sinclair is read two ways; Ageless Confidence was never checked.
- **Two types rest on one video each.** T3 (glasses, necklaces, earrings, where 15 of our 38 sit with T6) has only `2q_6Qf69Ywc`; T10 has only `HG2OJ0O3lxg`. Jewelry and makeup have no study video at all.
- **Fresh numbers.** Two picks were under 9 days old at collection (`h6Y1RZzOlzg` 7.5 days, `-eTr9aVSWlM` 8.6). Our channels are about a month old, with normals of 532 and 607, so their multiples swing on a few hundred views.
- **The counts are small and rough.** Hook and item word counts are approximate. Phrase counts depend on caption wording. The comment samples are 1-46 per video and miss the replies.
- **The viewers' ages** come only from what commenters chose to say.
