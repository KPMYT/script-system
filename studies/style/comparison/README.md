# Style study: main findings and suggestions (2026-10-28)

For Scarlet Sterling and Diane de Chambray. The scope is hair and fashion for women over 50; makeup and skincare are left out (the operator, 2026-10-28). The main session wrote this from the six analysis files below and checked their key counts against the sources. **Nothing is applied.** Every suggestion waits for the operator's yes, and any that touches a locked decision goes to `PROPOSALS.md` first.

## 1. The niche

1. **Hair has a higher ceiling than fashion.** Haircut breakouts run a median of about 101K views against 54K for general style, and 33 haircut videos passed 500K (`BREAKOUT-MAP.md`).
2. **"Mistakes that age you" is the main engine**, on real and faceless channels alike: 301 breakouts, 29 at 500K+. "Look expensive / elegant" is mostly a faceless title (146 of 174) and rarely gets big (5 at 500K+).
3. **Real women hold the biggest numbers:** 50 videos at 500K+ against 13 for faceless or AI, and 14 at 1M+ against 3. Faceless channels win by breakouts on tiny channels instead.
4. **The faceless side reposts titles.** Each copied title has about 20 near-identical copies in YouTube's first 20 results, and only one or two break out. Four pairs couldn't show why:
   - one script difference holds in all four (the winner asks for something later in the video), and it is thin
   - in Pair A the title, the thumbnail and the channel are identical, and the views are 1.1M against 7K
   - the one factor outside the script that holds is the channel's own normal, higher for the winner in all three cross-channel pairs

   (`05-copycat-pairs.md`, `05b-pair-thumbnails.md`)
5. **The French angle has demand but a low ceiling so far.** 58 breakouts, and only one near 500K: French Fashion's "(The French Way, 50+)", 449K. A real French stylist has a video calling out AI "French way" channels: Marie-Anne Lecoeur, "AI Gets French Style WRONG!", 138K.
6. **Gray hair is almost all real women** (45 of 60 breakouts), mostly personal journeys. Faceless channels barely touch it.
7. **A Black women over 50 sub-angle is rising** on faceless channels: shoes 881K, colors 438K.

## 2. The viewers (`01-viewers.md`)

8. **They are older than the titles say.** Of 149 viewers who give an age, the median is 70: 51% are 70 or older, 32% are in their 60s and 10% in their 50s.
9. **The problems are practical more than emotional.** The practical ones lead:
   - no stylist who can cut (28)
   - no clothes that fit (28)
   - health or mobility (22)

   The feared words are rare: "invisible" 3 times (two of them welcome it), "frumpy" about herself 6, "given up" 6.
10. **The top complaint on faceless channels is the pictures:** models too young, too slim, or not matching the words. That is 78 comments, 8.7% of faceless comments against 1.4% on real ones.
11. **AI is noticed.**
    - The study: 18 "AI" remarks on 6 of 24 faceless or AI videos. Vivian Sinclair's narrator names herself and claims "40 years beside a fitting room mirror". The video still got 910K, but its top comments call her AI ("Is this lady AI? Uh, yes!", 302 likes) and point at a lawn-product logo in the pictures (514 likes).
    - Our channels: **9 of our 42 comments are about AI or the persona.** Two hear Scarlet's accent change between videos (American, then British), and one asks "Are you Scarlet Sterling, Vivienne Morgan or A.I.?". This is a production point (the voice and the name), not a script one.
12. **What gets answered is a question about the viewer herself.** "Where are you watching from" and her age drew 24 answers in 29 comments and 24 in 39. Our 30 "which one will you try first" asks drew one answer.

## 3. The scripts that win (`02`, `03`, `04`)

13. **There are two templates.**
    - **Real women:** a hook of about 90 words, the first item at about 0:45-0:57, and no age said (3 of 21 hooks say one). They say "I" a lot (23 per 1,000 words), use "but" 6.5 times per 1,000 words and ask about 3.6 questions. They name brands (13-15 of 21), rarely give prices (5 of 21), and give real credentials ("I'm Tracy. I'm 51 years old", "hairstylist for 20 years"). Teases: 1 of 21.
    - **Faceless and AI:** a hook of about 176 words, the first item at about 1:38, the age said often, age slogans ("style has no age limit", "you've earned it": 17 of 24, none of the real), a held finale (8 of 24), specific comment questions, and invented personas with decades of experience and families. None discloses AI in the description.
14. **The winners use hard words and flag them:** "old lady" 10 times, "granny" 9, framed as "harsh but true" or "tough love". Viewers push back ("Waiting for the day when being old isn't an insult", 390 likes). The "it's not you, it's the blouse" reassurance is a faceless habit (12 of 24).

## 4. Our scripts against the winners (`06-own-vs-winners.md`)

Our 38 scripts were written before any style rules existed in the library. The gaps, checked by the main session where marked:

15. **The same packaging in all 38 (checked):**
    - the self-introduction ("I'm Scarlet Sterling and this channel is for women over 60…", "I'm Diane, and I show women over 60…") between 0:25 and 1:16
    - a book pitch twice, between 2:18 and 7:28
    - a fixed sign-off

    31 of the 42 winners introduce no one. **The core already bans this:** the channel intro and the retention teases are locked rules (`HANDOFF.md` §3, `core/retention-mechanics.md`). Our scripts simply never went through the library.
16. **A slow start and teases.** Scarlet reaches item one at a median of 1:39 (275 words), the faceless pace; real women take 0:57. Teases are in 28 of our 38 hooks (the helper's reading; the main session's crude phrase search finds at least 16) against 1 of 21 real.
17. **The title's promise goes missing (checked).** "Instantly" is in 14 of our titles and said in none of those 14 videos. 3 of 8 "younger" titles say "younger". Title words come back in the body at 8.2 per 1,000 words against 13.9-16.7 for the winners.
18. **The emotion gap is plumbing's gap again.**
    - "but" 1.0-1.1 per 1,000 words (36 of 38 below the lowest real video)
    - "you" 34-40 against about 49
    - "I" 4-6 against 23 for real women
    - questions 0.4-1.4 against about 3
    - no credential in any of our 38
19. **A house vocabulary (checked):**
    - "small gold hoops" in 22 of 38 (0 of 45 winners)
    - "reads as" or "reads [adjective]" 112 times
    - "quietly" in 32 videos
    - "from across the room" in 18
    - "Picture…" scenes in 36 of 38 (15 of 42 winners)

    Plausibly part of why viewers call it AI.
20. **Prices, not brands:** prices in 34 of 38, one brand named in 38.
21. **Our best videos beat our worst mostly on the title** ("avoid" or "NEVER" wording), more than on any script measure.

## Suggestions for the operator (none applied)

Answer "1 yes, 2 no". Where a suggestion touches a locked rule, it says so.

1. **Write every style script through the library from now on.** The core's locked rules then remove the self-introduction, the "this channel is for women over 60" line and the teases by themselves (findings 15-16). Nothing new is needed for that.
2. **Say the title's promise early and keep saying it:**
   - the count and the title's key word ("younger", "instantly", "expensive") in the first 20 seconds
   - the key word back in the body
   - a scanner check that every title word like "instantly" or "younger" is said at least twice (finding 17)
3. **Start the style pack from the plumbing pack's emotion rules:** more "but", "you" and questions, the title's feeling repeated, item shapes ending on the win. Our gap is the same one plumbing closed (finding 18).
4. **Give each persona a first-person voice and one soft credential, in line with the ideation skill's persona-claim discipline.** For example, Scarlet "in 40 years of dressing for…", or Diane "the way I was raised in Paris". There would be no invented professions, clients or family facts, and no "40 years beside a fitting room mirror" sort of trade claim. Options: (a) soft backstory only; (b) a fixed tenure in the profile like the trade channels; (c) no credential.
5. **Ban the house vocabulary** in the scanner, with a once-per-script limit or a ban: "small gold hoops", "reads as", "quietly", "from across the room", "Picture…" openers, "gallery", "book club". Rotate the scene openers (finding 19).
6. **Fewer prices; brands allowed as benchmarks.** At most one or two prices per script (always estimates), and brand names in the script where they help, under the claims policy. The ideation skill already allows "the famous expensive brand as the benchmark" (finding 20).
7. **The comment ask is about her:** her age, where she's watching from, what she has kept for 20 years. Not "which one will you try first" (finding 12).
8. **The book CTA:** once mid-video after item three (plumbing's rule) and once at the end, not twice in the first seven minutes. The offer rules apply as they stand.
9. **Hard words:** keep the ideation skill's "never shame the woman" rule. "Old lady" and "granny" only as the feared look, in quotes, and flagged ("harsh but true") at most once. The viewers' pushback (390 likes) is the evidence (finding 14).
10. **The age:** said where the title says it, never as a slogan. Ban "age is just a number" and "style has no age limit" lines, which are an AI-channel habit (17 of 24 faceless, none of the real; finding 13).
11. **Write for a 65-75 viewer:**
    - comfort, fit, mobility, a stylist who can't cut fine hair
    - practical fixes ahead of feelings
    - "invisible" used sparingly (findings 8-9)
12. **Add 6-8 item-list winners to the study before the models are built:** jewelry, glasses, earrings, scarves, bags. 15 of our 38 titles are item or haircut lists, and the study has only one item-list video. About 30 NexLev calls (about $0.60).
13. **Model scripts:** (a) real women only, as HVAC; (b) real women for voice, faceless winners for structure only, as plumbing's "structure only" models; (c) both alike. The study supports (b): the faceless winners are our format's peers, while the real women hold the biggest numbers and the voice.
14. **Production, outside the scripts:**
    - one fixed ElevenLabs voice for Scarlet (viewers hear two accents)
    - "She" in the AI disclosure line (20 of 22 say "He")
    - the channel title "Scarlet Starling"
    - the `scarletesterling.com` link
    - where "Vivienne Morgan" comes from

## What the study can't show

- **What makes one copy of a title break out.** Thumbnails, timing, the channel and YouTube's handling of repeats matter as much as the script, and four pairs can't separate them.
- **Retention.** The study has no viewer-retention data; our channels aren't connected in NexLev.
- **How viewers react to the voice itself.** The voice-sample candidates (Marie-Anne Lecoeur, Tracy Campoli, Woman in midlife, Melissa Murrell, Diana GOSS) need a listen.
- **Counts that are readings.** Hook moves, teases, item boundaries and comment themes are one coder's reading.
