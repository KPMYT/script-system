**What's working:** every hook now opens on the point, puts the viewer in the scene with what they already tried, names the shelf or the trade's silence, and says where the job stops being theirs. Hooks 4 and 5 are the strongest: a two-sentence twist (4) and a picture of a hidden place (5), then the failed try and the villain.

**What's not:**
- **Two rules pull against each other in hooks 1 and 3.** "The first sentence makes the point ... the fix or product itself, named outright" against "Hold the reveal" when the title holds something back. "DO NOT Plunge Your Sink Until You Watch This Video" holds everything back, yet hook 1 opens on item one's fix, and item one then repeats it 45 seconds later ("Number one, block the overflow"). Hook 3 does the same with the empty bowl. Retention can settle this (see section 8).
- **Hook 4 copies the page's own wording.** "You wanted a cheap fix that works tonight. You got one more wasted bottle." is the page's feelings table ("a cheap fix that works tonight", "another wasted jug") said aloud. Its villain line follows the page's example sentence ("Nobody mentions the strip. A visit is a bill in the hundreds."). The writer is copying the page's examples into the script.
- **Hook 2's villain leans towards a motive:** "nobody who clears drains for a living is going to bring it up" suggests they keep quiet because it pays. The page asks for silence as a plain fact, never a motive. A reading, not a clear break.

**Better than 11-05:**
- **Flow:** every script links item to item. Script 2 carries one bowl of water through four jobs, and script 5 "follows the water" from the drum to the drain.
- **Safety where the step is:** the chemical check before the plunger (1 item 4, 2 item 1, 4 mistake 5, 5 step 3), bleach and acid kept apart (3, 4, 5), hot tap water and never boiling (2, 5), the stuck valve left alone (3, 4, 5), unplug and scald (5).
- **Credit the old habit:** "The bottle told you ... and so did your mother. So this isn't on you." (3); "The capful was thrift once and it made sense." (5); "Buying it was the right instinct." (4).
- **Teaching pictures** that land by ear: "Picture a spoon of salt dropped in a bathtub. You'd never taste it." (3); "Porcelain is a skin of glass baked onto clay" (3); "The brown is a sun block." (4).
- **Consistent across videos:** the flange plunger for toilets and the cup plunger for sinks (1 and 4); the toilet call at "$100 to $275" (3 and 4); nothing in the tank (3 and 4).

**Weaker:**
- **The item-ending lines have one shape.** Script 1: "An old rag out of the rag bin doing a service calls job." / "10 seconds with a clamp and the mess stays in the pipe" / "One phone call and the truth about the jug and nobody gets hurt." / "An inch of tap water and that plunger finally has some muscle behind it." Script 5: "Clean filter, drum drains, code gone." Script 4: "Water out, peroxide on the stain, full strength." The page says "never end every step alike". Each line is fine on its own; together they become a drumbeat.
- **Script 2 counts two things at once:** "seven jobs" and "tablet number five". "That's still tablet number two before we get to the faucet." / "That's still tablet number four." Clever, but it's a second number to track by ear across 12 minutes.
- **Script 4's last mistake carries three topics** (mineral ring and citric acid, the clog and the plunger, the main line), and then sums them up.
- **Script 5's step one is a long explanation before the job:** about 70 seconds of what the paste is made of and why it builds, from 0:49 to about 1:50, before "Here's the fix."
- **Number load:** script 5 says 19 dollar figures (the others 4 to 8). Its step 2 alone: "$150 to $300 ... $300 to open a little door". The 2026-10-21 test found number load a weakness ("25 dollar amounts").

## 7. Rule breaks and fact checks

**Clear breaks of the current page:**
1. **Script 1, main line:** "And if two drains back up at the same time, that's the main line. So make that call soon." There's no "stop running water" (page rule 4: "if it's the main, stop running water and call"), and "soon" undersells it. The checker caught it. Scripts 4 and 5 get it right ("Stop running water, every tap in the house and pick up the phone.").
2. **Recaps** in scripts 2 and 4 (section 5).
3. **Script 2, near a stock line:** "If you only try one thing from this list, make it this one." (the page bans "If you take one thing from this video").
4. **Script 1, a 43-word sentence** (rule 8: none over 35): "Some flange plungers let you tuck the sleeve up inside the cup and that'll do in a pinch, but I keep a cup plunger just for the sinks and keep the toilet one far away from the sink where you rinse your dishes." (By caption punctuation; the script may have split it.)

**Readings, not clear breaks:**

- **Script 5's title says "GUARANTEED!!!"; the script rightly never does** ("Most machines come clean in one to three cycles"). That gap is in the title, from ideation, not the script.

## 8. Ranking, and what to look for in the retention graphs

**The script read, best to weakest:**
1. **4, peroxide:** the tightest hook, five clear mistakes, the most "wait, really?" moments. Weak spots: a recap and an over-full last mistake.
2. **3, toilet bowl:** the best teaching and the best book tie, but low on "but" and a little long in the steps.
3. **2, dishwasher tablet:** the fastest start and the best flow. Weak spots: the double count, a recap and a near-stock line.
4. **1, plunge:** strong items, but hook and item one say the same thing, the soft main-line line, and the longest video.
5. **5, washing machine:** a very good hook. Weak spots: a slow step one, the heaviest number load, the flattest voice, and the most glitches.

**Things the scripts suggest checking in stage 2,** written down before opening the retention files:
1. **Hook against hold:** do hooks 1 and 3 (fix given away at 0:00) lose more by 0:30 than hooks 2 and 4 (held back)?
2. **The repeat in script 1:** is there a dip at 0:45, when item one repeats the hook?
3. **The book line:** how big is the drop at each one (4:53, 6:09, 7:58, 7:08, 7:57), and does the 32% spot (1) cost more or less than the 48 to 59% spots?
4. **Script 5's step one** (0:49 to about 1:50, the long explanation): a steeper slope than the other openings?
5. **Recaps:** do 2 (12:11) and 4 (11:57) lose viewers at the recap, before the comment ask?
6. **Number-heavy stretches** (script 5 at 5:06 and 11:51): any dip?
7. **Bridges between items:** are the item changes flat (good), or does each "Number N" or "Step N" show a step down?
