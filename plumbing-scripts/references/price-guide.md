# Plumbing price guide

Typical US prices for the jobs, parts and problems these channels talk about. Researched 2026-10-21 by four researchers (two or more independent publishers or retailers per figure), then re-checked job by job by four other researchers against publishers the first ones didn't use. The full research, every source and every verdict are in `studies/plumbing/price-research-2026-10/`. Re-check the guide once a year: prices drift.

## How to use it

- **The brief comes first.** Use this guide only for a price the brief doesn't give.
- **Always an estimate.** Say a rounded range or "about", never an exact price and never cents ("about $150", not "$149.99"). The figures here are already rounded.
- **Say what moves the price, or the top of it** (`SKILL.md`, "Money framing" rule 2): "anywhere from $200 to $400, depending on where you live", "on the high end, this can run up to $20,000". Every figure here is a national typical range; local prices vary.
- **At most one price per item.** Most items carry their cost in words.
- **"In words" means don't give a figure.** The sources disagree or there's only one, so the script says it in words ("a few hundred dollars", "get a quote"), as the row suggests.
- **Store brand or name brand.** Shelf prices are for the store brand unless a brand is named. If the script names a brand, use that brand's price (peroxide: about $1 store brand, $3 to $4 for Swan).
- **Don't name the source** unless the row says "can cite". Many figures came from cost-guide pages that couldn't be read directly, so they're fine as estimates but not as quotes.
- **Check** column: **confirmed** (the sources agree and the second researcher agreed), **adjusted** (the second researcher moved it; the range shown is theirs), **thin** (usable, but light support), **in words** (no figure).
