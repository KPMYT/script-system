(1) The French bob (le carré): chin-to-jaw length, slightly undone ends, often with a fringe, no stacked or shaved back; on oval, heart and square faces cut just below the jaw with point-cut ends; on a round face take it past the chin with a side part. Ask for a chin-length bob cut to air-dry, soft textured ends, no graduation at the nape. (2) The collarbone lob (le carré long): ends at the collarbone with long face-framing pieces from the cheekbone; the most universal length; lengthens a round face, softens a square jaw, and keeps weight at the ends so fine hair reads fuller. Ask for collarbone length, long layers from the cheekbone down, ends left full, not thinned out. (3) The curtain fringe (la frange rideau) on a shoulder-length cut: parted in the center, longest at the cheekbones, sweeping both ways; it draws the eye to the eyes and cheekbones; shortens a long face, balances a heart-shaped forehead, works on a square face when the ends fall past the jaw. Ask for curtain bangs that start just below the brow at the center and blend into cheekbone layers; trim every four to six weeks (many salons trim a fringe free or cheaply between cuts, check). (4) The soft pixie (la coupe garçonne): 2 to 3 inches on top, scissor-cut sides and nape, a wispy fringe; best on oval and heart faces; on a round face keep height on top and a side sweep; it makes silver look deliberate and lifts fine hair at the crown. Ask for a soft pixie with length on top, no clippers, wispy pieces at the ears and nape. It is the opposite of the short, set, tight-permed 'old lady' cut, flat at the sides and sprayed at the crown. (5) The long layered cut that pins up: shoulder length or a little below, long layers starting at the chin, worn air-dried or twisted into a loose low chignon; suits oval, square and long faces; the chignon lifts the face and shows the earrings. Ask for long layers from the chin, enough length for a low bun, no heavy blunt line.
