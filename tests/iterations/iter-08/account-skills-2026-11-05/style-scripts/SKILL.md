# Style scripts

**Version:** 2026-10-31 (operator QC round 1: the hook formula, the emotion bank, named triggers, inclusion language, storytelling, the 8-beat item, value order, the CTA at the best book match, the personas' approved backstory, endearments and sign-offs, operator wording first, and repeating ourselves allowed. Source: the operator's notes on Scarlett's "7 Style Choices Elegant Women Over 50 Always Make", compared with a competitor (Paris Never Ages) and a real creator (Marie-Anne Lecoeur).)

**Repeating ourselves is allowed** (operator, 2026-10-31): our own facts, stories, figures and lines may repeat across scripts and both channels; only competitors' material is off-limits.

**Operator wording wins.** When a brief, a draft or a note gives specific wording, the script uses it word for word. Claude fills only the parts the operator left open (an insert, a placeholder), fixes spelling only, and puts any suggested rewording in a separate note after the script, never applied.

A style video for a woman over 50 takes one worry about how she looks (older than she feels, frumpy, not put together, a haircut that never looks right) and answers it with practical fixes she can do with her own closet, her own mirror and her own stylist. The winners in this niche are real women and stylists on camera (Marie-Anne Lecoeur, Justin Hickox, Diana GOSS, Tracy Campoli, Summer Beauty and the rest, `references/models/`): they get to item one inside a minute, say the title's promise early, explain why each look ages or works, name a feeling now and then in one plain sentence, and give permission instead of rules. This skill copies that shape for channels with no real person on camera. The operator's line: "practical solutions to emotional problems".

## What this skill covers

**It owns:** the viewer, the villain, the opening, emotion all the way through, "point then promise", the video types and the item shape, CTA placement and shape, the ending, length, voice, proof, money, safety and the style scanner checks. Scripts are clean narration only: no edit notes, cues or directions of any kind.

**It doesn't own:**
- **The persona:** name (never spoken), the approved backstory (age, career, city, years), personal details, the endearment, the sign-off, the French lens (Diane). These live in the channel's persona profile.
- **The book and CTA wording:** also in the persona profile.
- **The claims policy, output format, voiceover copy and audit:** these live in the core rules.

**Authority:** the core claims policy and offer rules come first, then safety and the title's promise, then the persona profile, then this skill, then the core base rules. The core `workflow.md` runs every script (START, the input gate, the output format).

## Inputs

Style scripts are written from the title and the brief alone; there is no research pack. The workflow reads this block at START and won't write until every required input passes.

```inputs
- title | required | the locked title, exactly as approved
- brief | required | the ideation brief for this title; the script's facts, swaps, brands and any prices come from it first
- research-pack | not used | style scripts are written from the brief
- rotation-log | required | the channel's last five ROTATION LOG ENTRY lines (from the earlier scripts in this chat, or pasted), or "none" for a new channel
```

- **The brief carries the style addendum** (style-ideation, "Style brief addendum"): the title's main desire and main fear; every item as a specific fix; the items scored and ordered by value; the book-matched item at number 3 or 4 with its Way or Secret; a sourced authority beat per item where one fits; any operator wording marked as fixed. A brief without specific fixes (see "The item shape") stops the run with NOT READY.
- **The brief is the fact source.** The scanner checks every number in the script against it, and the brief wins on safety ("unless brief says otherwise", operator 2026-10-29).
- **No freshness check,** and no special rule for dated facts on trend titles (operator, 2026-10-29).
- **Where the brief is silent, the script fills in** from well-established styling knowledge, said plainly: proportion, contrast, the neckline and the necklace, the rise of a jean, where a hem falls, what face-framing layers do. Never a price, a product's availability, a brand fact, or a health, scalp or dye-chemistry fact beyond the brief and the safety defaults.
- **A whole-look title may keep one short face paragraph** from the brief (groomed brows, a lip color, skincare before makeup), with no products and no results (operator, 2026-10-29). A makeup-led title waits for the makeup pack.
- **Brief prices on a non-budget title are background,** not lines (see "Money"). The ideation brief spec asks for dollar figures; this pack doesn't say them unless the title is about budget.
- The run stops with NOT READY only when the brief can't carry the title's main promise (too few items, no real swaps). Never for missing prices.

## The viewer

She is older than the titles say. Of 149 viewers who gave an age in the study's comments, the median is 70: 51% are 70 or older, 32% in their 60s, 10% in their 50s. Write for one woman of about 65 to 75, and touch the 50s when the title does (operator, 2026-10-28: "most of the time but we will touch on 50+ also sometimes"). One set of emotions for every age, with a light over-70 lean: comfort, mobility, the older client.

- **Her problems are practical first:** no stylist who can cut fine hair (28 comments), no clothes that fit her body now (28), health or mobility (22). A practical fix for a practical problem is the emotional relief.
- **She wants the why.** "Now I understand why" is the comment real presenters get and faceless channels don't (18 against 0). The explanation is the relief: say why a look ages or works in every item.
- **She wants brands and where to buy** (30 of about 117 requests). Name them where the brief does.
- **She wants her body named, not assumed slim:** a fuller middle, a softer jawline, shorter legs, a bust. Never assume a slim, tall or young body.
- **She pushes back on rules** ("I'll wear what I want", 55 comments). Give the reason and the out: if she loves a piece, she keeps it, and here's how to wear it.
- **She is proud** of a style she has kept for decades and of her age (47 comments together). Never make her past self a joke.
- **Terms explained the first time,** in a few plain words: undertone, contrast, silhouette, proportion, rise, drape, capsule, balayage, lowlights, demarcation line, toner, gloss, face-framing layers, blunt cut, curtain bangs, lob, root lift, diffuser, heat protectant, A-line, midi.
- **The standard kit:** a full-length mirror, a phone camera, her closet, a hairdryer and a round brush. Anything else is named with what it does.

## The villain

**Optional, as in electrical.** Real presenters name no adversary in 11 of 21 videos. Use one only where the title carries it, and aim it at a habit or a default, never at a motive or at people.

- **Allowed:** the advice she was handed years ago ("somewhere along the way, someone told her short was sensible"), the shop's default (sizes cut for a younger body, makers who don't make what she needs), the trend cycle (lightly), the dated version of a look (gently, never her past self as a joke).
- **Never the salon or the stylist** (operator, 2026-10-29: "salon is not the enemy"), not even in tone. When a title names salons ("Cuts Hairdressers Won't Suggest"), pay its promise as practical advice: what to ask for, the words to use in the chair, what to check before the scissors come out. On hair titles, the viewer's real pain is a stylist who didn't listen (12 of 15 hair videos' comments); the fix is how to be heard, never blame.
- **Never** younger women or other women, the viewer, "the fashion industry doesn't want you to know", or any company's motive. Two facts side by side, never joined by "so" or "because" into a motive (as plumbing).

## The opening (the hook): the five-move formula

**The hook pays off the title at once** (operator, 2026-10-31: "we need to pay off the title very fast"). It replaces the old rule of carrying one model's hook move by move; `references/hooks.md` stays as a study bank of what real hooks do, never as the template. Under about 110 words; item one starts by about 40 seconds.

1. **Title payoff, sentence one.** The title's count and promise in the title's own words, plus the main desire the title implies (the emotion bank below): an "elegant" title adds turning heads; a "younger" title adds taking years off. Never a scene, a question or a greeting before it.
2. **The unchangeables cleared.** Two or three things she can't change, named and taken off the table: weight, height, skin, age, budget, "not more beautiful than you, not richer than you". Aim the contrast at habits, never at other women ("the women who struggle" is out).
3. **The gap is information.** The only difference between her and the women she admires is what they know, and she's about to know it.
4. **Within reach.** Cheap or free, quick, mostly from her own closet, doable this week; the result she'll feel or see.
5. **Curiosity into item one.** The best item comes first, flagged as the easiest with the biggest difference (or the one most women get wrong), then straight into it.

Clear and to the point: one idea a sentence, plain words. The title's promise words are said twice, once in the first minute (`title-words`).

**Never in the hook:** a name or channel intro, a greeting (the profiles have none), "at their age" or any sting on age, other women as the losers, a promise the title doesn't carry (a "decades younger" line under an "elegant" title), a tease or held item, a "Picture…" or "Imagine…" opener, a task before item one, a recap of what's coming.

**The audit's Hook line** lists the five moves as written, with the word count and the seconds to item one. The rotation log's `hook` field records the unchangeables cleared and the item-one angle, for reference; repeats are allowed.

## Emotion, all the way through

Built from this niche, not copied from plumbing (operator, 2026-10-28). The evidence (`studies/style/comparison/03`, `07`, `08`):
- **In what viewers write,** the feelings that lead are **permission** ("I'll wear what I want", 113 comments, 10,346 likes) and **relief at being understood** (46), mostly relief at an explanation. The feared feelings are present but rarer: frumpy 25, losing her spark 17, invisible 6.
- **In what real presenters say,** a feeling is named in about **a quarter of items** (31 of 115), in **one sentence of about 12 words**, more often before the fix than after, and often in the presenter's own words. Ours named one in about 1 item in 11.

The rules:
1. **Practical fixes for emotional problems.** Every item solves a practical thing (the cut, the length, the fit, the color, the fabric); about one item in four also names the feeling behind it, in one plain sentence, usually before the fix. The fix is the comfort.
2. **Put her in her own mirror,** her own closet, her own photo, her own chair at the salon. Never an imagined stranger judging her ("the woman at the next table").
3. **The why is the relief.** One or two plain lines in every item on why it ages or why it works: where the eye goes, what light does, proportion, contrast, weight pulling down.
4. **Permission in plain, specific words, never a slogan.** Say what she may stop doing and what she may keep. Slogans ("age is just a number", "style has no age limit", "you've earned it", "you deserve it", "it's never too late") are in 17 of 24 faceless or AI videos and none of the 21 real ones; they are banned.
5. **The blame moves off her, in fresh words each time** (operator, 2026-10-31: "we especially in this niche are always reassuring the viewer that the things they cannot change are not the problem"). The reassurance comes in the hook and comes back through the body, up to about four times a script, never in the same words twice: the cut was wrong for fine hair; the jacket was made for another shoulder; nothing here asks her body to change. The bare formula "it's not you, it's the [garment]" at most once (the scanner counts it).
6. **"But", much more:** real presenters about 6.5 to 8 per 1,000 words; ours ran about 1. Aim for about five or more, joining two sentences where the thought turns.
7. **Ask her:** about four to eight questions a script, about her own hair, closet or mirror. The turn into a fix can be a question ("So what works instead?"), rotated.
8. **Say the title's feeling again** every few items, in the title's own words, and at least once in the second half of the body: a feeling said twice in the hook and then dropped for ten minutes is lost (iter-08: "frumpy" gone for eleven minutes). The scanner's TITLE SPREAD line reports each title word's count, its count in the second half and its longest gap, report only. Real winners say title words 14 to 17 times per 1,000 words; ours about 8. Don't overdo it (operator, 2026-10-29): a natural return now and then, never a refrain. A title that clears the viewer of something ("It's Not Their Weight") is paid as a plain permission line in the script's own fresh words, saying the fixes ask nothing of her body, never with the banned "it's not your weight" formula. A title's "NEVER" about French women is paid by its hedged meaning (rarely, most skip it); a capitalized promise word ("ANY face shape") is said back in the body as well as the hook.
9. **End each item on the result:** the look (lifted, longer, polished, taller), a compliment she'll hear, or now and then how it feels. More often seen than felt.
10. **The hard word, flagged, once at most.** "Old lady" or "granny" only as the feared look, in quotes, announced as harsh ("harsh but true", "I'll say it plainly"), at most once a script; the scanner requires the flag. Never at her: the adjective goes on the garment. Viewers push back ("Waiting for the day when being old isn't an insult", 390 likes).
11. **Her feelings, lightly:** "frumpy" where the title has it; "invisible" sparingly; "losing her spark" as a picture, rarely. The over-70 lean: comfort, feet and balance, a stylist who can't cut fine hair, clothes that close easily.
12. **The "I" is the expert's:** opinions and verdicts ("I'd choose", "my rule", "the rule I always give"), and, inside the profile's approved backstory, experience ("when I was designing, we cut it so…", "I changed my mind about…"). Soft and uncheckable (see "Proof"). About 10 per 1,000 words, reported.
13. **Every title carries one main desire and one main fear** (the bank below), named in the hook and brought back in the subhooks and the item results. The title may not say them; the script does.
14. **Name the trigger outright, on the garment** (operator, 2026-10-31: "paris does them so much better and names them outright"). At least once in every item, say what the piece does in the trigger's own words: adds years, looks older, wider, shorter, heavier, frumpy, dated, cheap; or turns heads, younger, taller, slimmer, expensive, polished. "The belt makes the middle look wider", never "your wide middle"; "a coat that stops at the hip adds years", not only "looks tired".
15. **Include her** (operator, 2026-10-31: "I LOVE how paris does this"): the narrator stands beside her, not above her. "We" and "us" about the habit ("most of us went through a shoulder-pad decade"), a shared worry said gently ("a lot of us stopped wearing anything sleeveless years ago, and that's allowed"), shared history ("we were taught that matching the bag to the shoes was polite"), doing it together ("let's go through your closet together", "now slip on your everyday shoes"), "women like us". The "you" still carries every fix.
16. **The endearment** is the profile's one word (Scarlett "Ladies", Diane "my dear friends"), never another; how often comes from the profile.

**Hair and fashion notes** (`08`: the feelings are mostly the same; three things differ):
- **Hair titles:** the pain is a stylist who didn't listen and the fear of the change itself ("if a short cut scares you…", the patience of growing out gray). The feeling line sits on the nerve before the change; the fix includes what to ask for in the chair. Hard words are rarely needed: hair scripts say "aging" and "older".
- **Fashion titles:** the pain is the body changing and clothes not made for her; the feeling line sits on the body, comfort and confidence. The hard words ("frumpy", "dowdy", "mumsy") live here, still flagged and at most once for "old lady" or "granny".

## Point then promise, and the subhooks

Point first, always: each item opens on its point, then explains. The count line promises the whole list.

**Subhooks are one-liners** (operator, 2026-10-31): a short version of the hook that plays on one emotion and reaffirms, between items or at an item's turn. One feeling plus one reassurance, then the handoff: "This next one costs nothing, and it works on any body." "Your height doesn't matter for this one; the line does." Three or four a script, different from each other; reusing our own lines from earlier scripts is fine.

**The body walk** is a bridge device for whole-look lists: the items run in body order (feet up, or face down) and each bridge names the next stop ("now up to the neckline", "and down to your shoes"). The value order still wins: walk the body only where it doesn't push the best item back.

Also allowed: a myth flag that names the belief it breaks; the priority flag ("if you change one thing") once, on the strong item at about two-thirds. **No recaps.**

### The item shape: eight beats

Every item, after its subhook (operator, 2026-10-31):

1. **Point:** the choice or the mistake, said as the item's name.
2. **The look:** what she sees in her own mirror or closet, with the trigger named (rule 14).
3. **Why:** what it does to her look, through the eye, light, line or proportion, told in longer sentences with a reason behind it.
4. **The elegant woman's choice:** what the women she admires do instead, and why.
5. **Authority beat:** a sourced fashion-history fact or figure, the persona's own experience from the approved backstory, or a change of mind; about 60 to 80 words; it ends on the principle the item teaches. Within her reach (see "Using the models").
6. **Value instruction:** the turn as a question ("So which one do you choose?") and the specific swap, with one or two examples.
7. **The rule I always give:** one memorable action she can repeat (the persona's own rule, said as hers).
8. **Result:** the look she gets, a compliment she'll hear, or how it feels. Vary the endings: some on the result, some on permission, some on a question, some on the bridge.

**Specific, not broad** (operator, 2026-10-31: "specific fixes not broad ones"). Each item carries at least four of: the exact garment or cut; the placement or measurement (the hem just touching the top of the shoe, sleeves to the wrist bone); the fabric; two to four colors; a physical check she can run (stand sideways, turn away and back, walk across the kitchen, sit down in it). The check is given plainly, never announced ("Here's the check" is banned). Broad advice ("wear clothes that fit") goes back to the brief.

**Highest value first.** Score each item 1 to 3 on how visible the change is, cost and effort, how many viewers it fits, and how hard it hits the title's emotion; the highest total opens the list, the second goes second, and one strong item sits at about two-thirds with the priority flag. The brief's order wins when it gives one.

**Length:** about 250 to 330 words an item in lists of six or more; about 300 to 450 in lists of five or fewer. The authority beat is part of that, not extra. In "never these" lists, every "never" is paid by its swap in the same item.

## Using the models: never a copy

The models show how winning videos are built. A script borrows their structure and their devices, never their wording, their stories or their facts, and never one model's structure whole.

1. **Name the models, what they lent and the differences** in the plan and the audit's Originality line; each claim checkable in the script.
2. **No repeats** of a model in the channel's last five scripts (the rotation log's `model` field).
3. **Planning only.** Model files are read at the planning step, never kept open while writing.
4. **M files** (real presenters) lend structure, voice and emotion; **F files** (faceless or AI) and competitor videos lend structure and devices only. The competitor Paris Never Ages (2026-10-31, 8.6K views in 9 hours) lends these devices as patterns: a dated, named story behind each item; the body walk; inclusion language; triggers named outright; the garment given feelings ("the jewelry is doing all the talking"); "X rather than Y" swaps; a long setup and a short verdict. Never its lines, its narrator's backstory or its stories.
5. **The authority beat must be within her reach** (operator, 2026-10-31: "relatable authority"). Any story, person or swap is something a woman of 65 to 75 on an ordinary budget can copy this week. A famous figure is allowed only when her trick works on that budget and the script draws the line down in plain words (the famous pearls were costume, and a good faux strand costs less than lunch out). Prefer what she can see herself in: a woman her age, her own closet, a thrift find, the persona's own experience. Every fact in a story comes from the brief. Never a competitor's story or wording. Our own facts, stories, figures and lines may repeat across scripts and across both channels (operator, 2026-10-31: "we are allowed to repeat and copy ourselves").

The copy guard still flags any run of 8 or more words shared with a model file.

## CTAs

**The book is the only CTA** (operator, 2026-10-29: "no sub cta. just ebook"). Never a subscribe ask, a like ask or a bell.

- **Where** (operator, 2026-10-31: "we are giving value, then value then more value, then once we have a point or fix MOST RELATED to the ebook… that is when we slip in the call to action"): once, after at least two full items, at the end of the item whose fix is closest to the book. The brief places that item at number 3 or 4 so the plug lands about 30 to 50% in, where most viewers still hear it. Never in the opening, never in the middle of an item.
- **The shape, in about 60 to 90 words:**
  1. tied to the fix just given ("the full version of what you just did is in…")
  2. the book by its spoken title (from the profile), with one true fact, and the Way, Secret or chapter that covers this item
  3. one benefit in plain words, from the profile's benefit angles
  4. where to get it: the link in the description and the QR code on screen, in one short sentence
  5. a return line into the next item
- **Rotation:** never the same true fact or benefit angle as any script in the rotation log's `book` field.
- **Never:** a price, "free", a discount, a no-rush close, a "I gathered these for you" provenance line, a long QR walkthrough, the same pitch twice in one video.
- **At the end:** a one-line book reminder in the profile's words, then the comment ask, then the sign-off.

## The ending

After the last item:
1. **The one-line book reminder.**
2. **The comment ask** (the sign-off follows it): a short turn to her, an outright ask to comment ("down in the comments", "leave me a comment"), and **one question about her**, not about the video. The study's evidence: questions about the viewer herself drew answers (where she's watching from: 24 answers in 29 comments), and our "which one will you try first" drew one answer in about 42 comments. Rotate, never the same question two videos running:
   - her age and where she's watching from
   - a piece she has kept for twenty years, or a cut she has worn longest
   - the style rule she was handed at 50 and still follows (or broke)
   - what her mother wore that she swore she never would

   No "I read every one" (0 of 21 real presenters say it; a faceless habit here). Two or three sentences in all.

3. **The profile's fixed sign-off, the last line,** word for word every time (operator, 2026-10-31; Scarlett "Until next time, ladies, stay elegant." Diane "With love from Paris, my dear friends.").

No recap, no tease of a future video, and nothing after the sign-off.

## Length

**At least 12 minutes** of narration at the profile's words per minute (Scarlet 169, Diane 172: about 2,030 to 2,060 words). **12 to 16 minutes is the aim** (operator, 2026-10-29). The value decides the length inside that.

- **Never pad.** Cut repetition: the same check said twice, the promise restated, a "Picture…" scene that adds no fact.
- **If the brief can't fill 12 minutes with real value,** the run stops with NOT READY and asks for a fuller brief.
- **For calibration, not as targets:** the real winners' median is 11.9 minutes, the faceless winners' 15.6, ours 16.4 and 16.9.

## Voice

- **Longer sentences, more storytelling** (operator, 2026-10-31): each piece of advice and each trick has a short story or a reason behind it, told in longer, flowing sentences; short sentences are kept for the verdict that lands it. Plain words still: no poster lines, no three-word wraps, no Wise Narrator.
- **"You" for the fixes, "we" for the habit, "I" for the expert:** "you" and "your" about 45 per 1,000 words (the scanner's YOU line marks a rate under 35); "we" and "us" where the narrator shares the habit or the history (Emotion rule 15); "I" for verdicts and the approved backstory.
- **The endearment** is the profile's, and only the profile's.
- **Candor, warmth, no jokes at her, no swearing.** A gentle laugh at an object is fine ("a handbag carrying half the kitchen drawer"). Blunt about garments, warm about the woman.
- **Brands named** where the brief names them, as benchmarks or examples; an opinion against a brand is said as hers, never as an unsupported fact.
- **Written for the voice** (core `workflow.md` §4): heights in words, sizes as said, "a two-inch heel", "&" in words. Accents and spelling follow the profile (Scarlett is British: British turns of phrase, American garment words where a British one would confuse an American viewer: sweater, not jumper). French words: one or two widely known ones a script at most, no accents, never a full French sentence.
- **No spoken pronunciation guides;** a hint, if needed, goes only in the VO copy.
- **The house vocabulary of our old scripts stays banned** (see "Banned").

## Proof

**Use:**
- the mechanism in one line (why a longer layer lifts, why a cropped hem shortens the leg)
- the check she can run
- the persona's approved backstory and experience inside it, as the profile words it (core claims-policy exception, "One approved backstory")
- a sourced fashion-history fact from the brief
- a tailor's, colorist's or stylist's working rule, credited generally
- the honest limit: if she loves it, here's how to keep it
- a brand's real fact, from the brief

**Never:**
- anything checkable outside the profile: a named house, employer, salon, magazine, client, celebrity friend, relative, address or date
- made-up client stories presented as real people
- **pointing at the screen:** "as you can see", "look at this", "right here"
- **"Studies show"** or "psychologists say"
- **the face or body as proof** on a synthetic presenter ("what I use to look twenty years younger")

## Money

**No price unless the title promises budget, cheap, "without spending" or a figure** (operator, 2026-10-28, suggestion 6: "if the price mentions budget or cheap things"). Then the prices come from the brief, as a rounded range or "about", once or twice a script. Real winners say money figures 0.14 times per 1,000 words; ours said them 1.3 to 1.6 times, in 34 of 38 scripts.

"Look expensive" is delivered through cut, fabric, fit, color and finish, never through a receipt. The famous expensive brand may be named as the benchmark where the brief names it.

## Safety

These defaults apply **unless the brief says otherwise** (operator, 2026-10-29): one short line inside the item, in proportion, never a lecture.

1. **Hair dye at home:** a patch test before every home color, as the box says; any itch, burn or swelling is a stop and a doctor.
2. **Heat tools:** heat protectant, and a lower setting on fine, thinning or gray hair.
3. **Thinning hair:** styling only. Sudden shedding or patches are a question for a doctor or dermatologist. Nothing is said to regrow hair.
4. **Shoes and falls:** heel height, backless shoes and slick soles: the steady choice said plainly, never shamed.
5. **No diet, weight-loss, supplement or exercise plan:** "slimmer" means line and silhouette.
6. **Glasses:** the optician fits the lenses; the script gives frame advice only.

No health, medical, procedure or "reverse aging" claim, ever (claims policy).

## Banned in these scripts

**Never:**
- **A name or channel intro,** a greeting, "today we're going to", "this channel is for…".
- **Teases and held items:** "we'll get to that one", "still ahead", "the one I promised", and the core hard-ban list.
- **A recap** at the end.
- **A subscribe, like or bell ask.**
- **Age slogans:** "age is just a number", "style has no age limit", "no expiration date", "you've earned it", "you deserve", "it's never too late", "elegance is not about age"; and "at their age" / "at your age" as a sting.
- **The salon or the stylist as the villain.**
- **Claims outside the profile,** pointing, "studies show", the face as proof (see "Proof").
- **Shaming the woman:** the adjective goes on the garment. Never "mutton dressed as lamb", "trashy", "fat", "ugly".
- **Announced checks:** "here's the check", "the check is", "your check".
- **The house vocabulary of our old scripts:** "small gold hoops", "reads as" (and "reads expensive / tired / dated"), "from across the room", "gallery", "book club", "whenever it suits you".
- **Edit notes, cues, labels or directions** of any kind.
- **Competitors' catchphrases, lines, stories and personas' names.**
- **Copying 8 or more words in a row** from a reference file.

**At most once per video:** "quietly", a "Picture…" or "Imagine…" opener, "for generations", "camel", "quiet luxury", "on an ordinary Tuesday", "somewhere along the way", the turn question what can you do instead, "if you change one thing", the bare "it's not you, it's the…" formula, "old lady" or "granny" (flagged).

## Before handing over

Check that:
- any operator wording is in the script word for word, and suggestions sit in a separate note
- the hook runs the five moves in order (title payoff with its desire, the unchangeables cleared, the gap is information, within reach, curiosity into the best item), under about 110 words, item one by about 40 seconds, nothing from "Never in the hook"
- the title's main desire and fear are named in the hook and come back in the subhooks and results; each title word is said twice, once in the first minute, and again in the second half (TITLE SPREAD)
- three or four one-line subhooks, each one emotion plus one reassurance, all different
- every item runs the eight beats, carries four of the five specific parts, names its trigger outright on the garment, gives its check plainly, and opens on a signpost the scanner reads
- the items are in value order, with the strong item at about two-thirds
- every authority beat is sourced from the brief or the profile, within her reach, ends on the principle, and isn't a competitor's
- the reassurance comes back up to about four times, in fresh words; the bare formula at most once
- inclusion language is there ("we" about the habit, doing it together); "you" and "your" about 45 per 1,000; "but" about five or more; four to eight questions
- the endearment is the profile's only; British or American voice per the profile
- the villain appears only if the title carries one; never the salon, the stylist, other women or the viewer
- the hard word appears at most once, in quotes, flagged as harsh, on the look
- no price unless the title is a budget title; brands only from the brief
- nothing checkable outside the profile's backstory; no pointing, no "studies show", no face as proof
- the safety defaults appear where the topic touches them
- the French claims follow Diane's lens
- the book CTA comes once, after at least two items, at the best book match, in the five-part shape; its fact and angle differ from the rotation log's; the end runs the book line, the comment ask about her, then the profile's sign-off as the last line
- the plan names its models with at least three differences; no model from the last five scripts
- the script runs at least 12 minutes (12 to 16 is the aim), with nothing padded
