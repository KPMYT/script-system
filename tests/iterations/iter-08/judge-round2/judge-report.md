### Script P

P sounds like one woman talking to you across a table. Each cut is handed to the next with a spoken reason ("Now, if a pixie feels like too big a leap..."), and no two items have the same shape. It is the only one of the three that never sounded like a form being filled in.

**Strengths (quoted):**
- Opening scene: "But a week later someone took a photo at lunch, and you looked at it for a second and quietly turned the phone face down on the table." The viewer is understood before any advice starts.
- "The eye sees a circle sitting on a circle. And because nothing moves, there's no light in it and no ease. Hair that moves looks young. Hair that's been set into place looks, well, set." Easy to hear, easy to remember, and it explains why.
- Transitions are natural speech: "Now let's go back to that flat back of the head, because for a lot of women that's the real culprit, and they don't even know it." / "The sixth cut is the one I'd love more women with a natural wave to try." / "And the seventh cut brings us right back to the front of your face."
- "A soft stack looks like your hair simply has body. A wedge looks like it was engineered."
- Defuses the salon early: "Nobody sets out to get a granny cut. It just creeps up on you, one appointment at a time."

**Where it stops flowing (quoted):**
- The intro is long: 492 words (about 3 minutes of voice) before cut #1. It is good material, but "Now notice what I didn't say. I didn't say short hair is the problem..." restates what was said a paragraph earlier. A viewer who came for the seven cuts may drift here.
- The doctor line interrupts the pixie: "One gentle word while we're here. If your hair is coming out suddenly, or you're finding patches, that isn't a haircut question." It is gracefully put but you can hear that it is a required line. It would sit better after the fine-hair sentence, without the "while we're here" signpost.
- Box-tick clarification: "That's very different from the dome on top of a granny cut. This fullness sits at the back, where your head naturally curves, and it still moves." It explains itself twice.
- The ending is abrupt: cut #7 goes straight into "Every one of these swaps lives in The Hair Hack chapter..." and then the question. There is no line that steps back and lands the video.
- The title promises "look younger". The script mostly says "young" ("reads as young") and uses "younger" once, so the payoff is a little quieter than the title.

### Script Q

Q has the strongest persona: a designer's eye, travel and a confession. Taken one at a time, many lines are lovely. Heard in a row, though, every cut runs through the same six beats: "Number X" → the problem → why → a designing or tailoring story → "So what do you ask for?" → a mirror test → a personal "rule" line plus "instantly". By cut 4 you can predict each beat, and that predictability is the "didn't flow" feeling.

**Strengths (quoted):**
- "Short hair only adds years when it stops moving."
- The confession sounds like a real woman: "For years I believed long hair after a certain age dragged a face down, and I'm afraid I said so to anyone who asked. But I changed my mind once I looked properly..."
- "It works the way a V-neck does on a sweater. A crew neck draws a circle under your chin, and a V-neck draws a line down that lengthens everything above it." A clear image for the ear.
- Good teaser bridges: "And if you've been told you're too old for long hair, this next one is your permission."
- An open loop carried across the book plug: "But what if your face is round or square, or you'd like a little more length?" ... "Now, back to the mirror, ladies, and that extra length."

**Where it stops flowing (quoted):**
- The same closing beat on every cut: "My own rule is to book your next trim..." / "The rule I hold to is an inch of angle and not a fraction more." / "I always say a cut has to work from every side..." / "My rule is to keep your length..." / "With this one, I'd say let the cut do the work..." / "Lighter at the front, always, is how I put it." Most of these end on "instantly" (5 times in total).
- The career story is slotted into nearly every cut: "When I was designing..." (cut 1), "A good tailor will tell you..." (cut 2), "In my years of travel... in Rome or in Tokyo" (cut 3), "When I was designing, we judged every garment from the back" (cut 4), "Over thirty-five years designing in London..." (cut 6). By the fourth, it sounds like a quota.
- The opening restates the title, and the second line is vague: "Seven haircuts make women over 60 look younger instantly, and every one of them helps you avoid..." / "Your age isn't what does it, ladies..." ("does" what?).
- Odd line in the book plug: "The full version of what you've just done for your bob is in my book". The viewer hasn't done anything.
- Rhetorical question template: "So what do you ask for?" / "So which bob do you have now?" / "So how much angle do you want?" / "So what do you say in the chair?" / "So how do you wear it day to day?"
- The ending asks for data, not a story: "tell me how old you are and where in the world you're watching from." It isn't tied to haircuts, and it's flat after 2,600 words.

### Script R

R is quick, plain and kind, and it pays the most attention to the older viewer's body ("If holding a mirror up is a strain on your arms..."). By ear, though, it is the choppiest of the three: 64 short paragraphs, "Number one." said as a bare heading, three of the six joins between cuts with no bridge at all, and many lines that stop to define a word as a glossary would.

**Strengths (quoted):**
- "Keep the length you love. Just give it somewhere to move."
- Empathy: "Maybe you've heard that long hair is a mistake after sixty. That advice can sting, especially when your hair is the part of your look you're proudest of." / "If you've worn one short style for years, changing it can feel like giving up the only thing you knew how to manage."
- Age-aware practical care: "If holding a mirror up is a strain on your arms, a phone photo taken from behind does the same job." / "No round brush, no arms held up through a long blow-dry."
- A clear image: "...so each layer rests on the one below, like shingles on a roof."
- "A picture of the back says what words can't." The salon is treated as a partner.

**Where it stops flowing (quoted):**
- The opening jumps: "Which of the two is in your mirror right now? / And this part surprises most women. The years come from the shape of the cut, so you can keep your length and still lose them." The "And" doesn't follow from the question, and "keep your length" is muddled for a video about a short cut.
- Cut #1 starts by describing the other cut: "Number one. The textured pixie. / The rounded short cut sits on the head as one solid shape..." A listener hears "pixie" and then "rounded short cut" and is briefly lost.
- Glossary asides: "That just means every hair was cut straight across..." / "An oval face is a little longer than it is wide. A heart shape is wider at the forehead and narrower at the chin." / "That's a spray or cream that coats the hair so the heat is gentler on it." / "Those are shorter pieces cut inside the hair..."
- The doctor line stands alone as a box-tick: "A cut can make thin hair look fuller, but sudden shedding, or a new patch where the scalp shows, is a question for a doctor before it's a question for a stylist."
- A backwards bridge: cut 3 is fully explained, and only then comes "Has your face filled out a little at the cheeks over the years? Then this is the length I'd start with." followed by "Number four." with no lead-in. The joins 1→2 and 5→6 are cold too ("Keep the length you love... / Number six.").
- The same tail on every cut: "In the chair, ask for..." / "In the salon, ask for..." and a mirror test (9 "mirror", 12 "ask for").

## 3. Facts: wrong or risky

**P**
- "Every one of these swaps lives in The Hair Hack chapter..." The brief confirms only Ways 197, 198 and 199 (layered bob, textured pixie, length with movement). That all seven cuts are in the chapter is not confirmed. Soften it to "There's more like this in The Hair Hack chapter".
- Book description: "every one gives you the mistake, the swap and the payoff." Q says "the swap, a styling tip and the payoff"; R says "the swap and the payoff". The three disagree. Check against the persona's book text.
- "short, piecey hair hides the scalp far better than long hair that's been stretched thin" and "Fine and gray hair can be more fragile": common stylist advice. Fine as her reading.

**Q**
- "In my years of travel, the women I noticed with round faces, in Rome or in Tokyo, rarely wore their hair cut level at the chin." An invented observation presented as fact about real places. Risky.
- "Stylists have a working rule for bangs... If you can't see any forehead through them, they're too heavy." This presents an invented trade rule; blunt bangs are opaque by design. Reword as her own preference.
- Tailor analogy: "takes bulk out from underneath, in the seams and the lining". A lining adds a layer rather than removing bulk. Muddled.
- Stacked bob: "short, stacked layers that get longer toward the front". This mixes graduation (shorter at the nape, building toward the crown) with an A-line. Loose.
- "Each cut takes one appointment" contradicts the pixie's trim every 4 to 6 weeks in the same script.
- Over-promises: "someone will ask whether you've been away somewhere", "It takes years off instantly", plus 5 "instantly" in total.

**R**
- "It was built for thick hair that holds a set, and fine hair was never going to hold it." Doubtful: roller sets were widely used to give fine hair volume. Risky.
- "Piecey ends weigh almost nothing, so the crown stays up without spray." Overclaim.
- Chin bob: "the bang and the ends of the bob both curve in toward the cheekbones." The ends of a chin-length bob sit at the chin, not the cheekbones. Wrong.
- "The blunt line against the layered one is the very first Way in the hair chapter." This matches the brief (Way 197 opens Ways 197 to 207). Correct.

**Verdict:** P is the only script a listener would hear as one woman talking from start to finish. It is ready after a light edit. Q and R both have good lines, but each repeats a fixed shape on every cut (Q a story-plus-rule template, R a heading, definition and "ask for" pattern). That repetition is what the owner heard as "didn't flow". Neither is publishable without a medium rewrite.
