TITLE: 5 French Haircuts That Flatter ANY Face Shape After 60 (And Take 10 Years Off)
CHANNEL: Diane de Chambray (ch. 2, the French way)
REFERENCE: produced log, French batch 1 #11 · F5 × F3 × age scale (twin of Scarlett #6, F5 × F3 × age scale) · topic: look younger, hair (demand map; face shape ×2 in the sheet) · facts: general styling and stylist advice, US and Paris salon price lists
BRIEF: The 'any face shape' claim rests on one mechanism: a cut flatters through where its length ends, where the volume sits and what the fringe and part do, so each of these five adapts to the face by adjusting those three. Face-shape basics: oval suits almost anything; round wants length past the chin, an off-center part and height at the crown, not width at the cheeks; square wants soft ends and layers around the jaw, not a blunt line exactly at the jaw; long wants a fringe and width at the sides, not long one-length hair; heart wants fullness at chin level and a fringe across the wider forehead. The folk check: hair pulled back, trace the outline on the mirror (hedge). The documented French habit behind all five: a cut made to air-dry and grow out well, never set and sprayed, with color close to the natural shade or gray worn on purpose (hedge to 'most women I know in Paris'). (1) The French bob (le carré): chin-to-jaw length, slightly undone ends, often with a fringe, no stacked or shaved back; on oval, heart and square faces cut just below the jaw with point-cut ends; on a round face take it past the chin with a side part. Ask for a chin-length bob cut to air-dry, soft textured ends, no graduation at the nape. (2) The collarbone lob (le carré long): ends at the collarbone with long face-framing pieces from the cheekbone; the most universal length; lengthens a round face, softens a square jaw, and keeps weight at the ends so fine hair reads fuller. Ask for collarbone length, long layers from the cheekbone down, ends left full, not thinned out. (3) The curtain fringe (la frange rideau) on a shoulder-length cut: parted in the center, longest at the cheekbones, sweeping both ways; it draws the eye to the eyes and cheekbones; shortens a long face, balances a heart-shaped forehead, works on a square face when the ends fall past the jaw. Ask for curtain bangs that start just below the brow at the center and blend into cheekbone layers; trim every four to six weeks (many salons trim a fringe free or cheaply between cuts, check). (4) The soft pixie (la coupe garçonne): 2 to 3 inches on top, scissor-cut sides and nape, a wispy fringe; best on oval and heart faces; on a round face keep height on top and a side sweep; it makes silver look deliberate and lifts fine hair at the crown. Ask for a soft pixie with length on top, no clippers, wispy pieces at the ears and nape. It is the opposite of the short, set, tight-permed 'old lady' cut, flat at the sides and sprayed at the crown. (5) The long layered cut that pins up: shoulder length or a little below, long layers starting at the chin, worn air-dried or twisted into a loose low chignon; suits oval, square and long faces; the chignon lifts the face and shows the earrings. Ask for long layers from the chin, enough length for a low bun, no heavy blunt line. Gray: a sharp cut makes gray read intentional; a clear or silver gloss adds shine (about $40 to $100 in a US salon, check); purple shampoo for brass. Money: a US women's cut averages roughly $50 to $70 (Square data cited about $52); a neighborhood coupe femme in Paris is about €35 to €50, more at high-end salons (check); a cut that grows out well stretches visits to eight to ten weeks (check). Hair safety: a patch test 48 hours before any color, home or salon, as the box instructions require, because dye allergy can be serious; heat protectant with hot tools; thinning is handled as cut and styling only, never a diagnosis or cure, and sudden or patchy shedding is a question for a doctor. Boundaries: hedge every French generalization; no celebrity names; the cut is the problem, never the woman.
