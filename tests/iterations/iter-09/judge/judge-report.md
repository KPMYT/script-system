### Script X

What works:
- The opening is a concrete scene and makes one point: "So you grab a jug of drain cleaner off the store shelf. That's the mistake. If the jug doesn't clear it, you've got a drain full of chemical that burns skin, and now you can't safely plunge it." The rest of the script pays this off.
- It explains each action as you do it, which is what a beginner needs: "Pull it up slowly. Slow is the whole trick. Yank it and the barbs let go of what they grabbed."
- It teaches by feel, which works well with audio only: "Going around the bend, the cable turns and moves along easy. When it gets into the clog, it gets heavy. That's the moment to keep going, not to back off."
- The safety check comes at the right moment, before the plunger: "Before you do anything else, ask yourself one question. Is there chemical drain cleaner already in this drain? If there is, stop."
- It adds a useful detail of its own: the tub overflow is "the round plate on a tub", and the double sink line ends with the result: "you're just pushing water from one bowl into the other."

Where the flow breaks:
- Some stock phrases repeat. "Here's what you do." appears at level one, at level two and at the main line ("So here's what you do."). "That's a call" is said about five times. As a refrain it is clear, but by the end it sounds like a checklist.
- The price line in the opening sits on its own and doesn't lead anywhere: "A plumber charges a hundred to three hundred dollars to clear one sink, tub or shower. After hours or on a weekend, it's one and a half to three times that. A clog comes in three levels..."
- The book break reads like an ad, and its long list is hard for a voice to carry: "A hundred numbered fixes, each with the problem, why it costs money, my first check, the cheap fix, when to call a pro and the money saved." Then "Back to the drain." is an abrupt way back in.
- One line is a clumsy fragment: "Chemical cleaner in the drain and it won't clear on its own, that's a call."
- The script has few emotional beats. Every step has the same shape (tool, price, action, test), and there is little "you just did that" reward. It is steady and plain rather than gripping.

### Script Y

What works:
- It has a strong single takeaway line: "If you take one thing from this whole video, take this. Pull the stopper before you buy anything."
- It gives you sensory detail you can picture: "The first pull usually comes up wearing a gray rope of hair and slime." It also has "Black, slimy and sour smelling."
- It rewards the listener well: "If it spins down clean, you just fixed a slow drain for pocket change, and you'd never done it before."
- It adds useful explanations: the trap "holds a little water to keep sewer gas out of the house". The main-line shutdown includes "the washer and the dishwasher".
- It asks the listener a direct question: "Is the drain pipe under your sink white plastic?"

Where the flow breaks:
- The opening is slow and split up. There are four short paragraphs before level one (the hook, a stand-alone price line, the stopper question, the roadmap), then a description of level one, then the gloves. The price line is there only to tick a box: "A plumber charges $100 to $300 to clear a sink, tub or shower clog, depending on where you are in the country." It leads nowhere.
- The paragraphs are very short throughout, often one or two sentences. Read aloud, it stops and starts.
- A detour about the chemical jug breaks the level-one steps between the strip and the flush: "You've probably seen the jugs on the store shelf that promise to melt all that away. But the strip doesn't promise anything... I'd keep the jug out of it."
- Some lines exist only to stir up feeling against plumbers. They sound forced and cut against the "no shame in calling" message:
  - "Nobody mentions that strip when they quote you for the visit."
  - "Somebody's going to tell you a snake is a plumber's tool. I don't see it that way."
  - "I think that's a disgrace, for a drain you could have cleared on a Tuesday afternoon." This comes right after "there's no shame in that."
- "Here's..." starts a line six times ("Here's how it got there", "Here's why", "Here's how you tell", "Here's the test", "here's the good part", "here's where that leaves you").
- "with my coffee in the other hand": the other hand from what?
- The safety section lands after the plunger and snake steps, with "Now listen close on this next part". By then a beginner following along has already plunged (see section 2).

## 3. Facts and risks

Script X:
- **Level three contradicts itself and is a little risky.** It says "You still start with levels one and two, because the strip and the plunger are the cheapest things to try", and then, two sentences later, "you need to know which one you've got before you touch a thing." If it is the main line, levels one and two mean running hot-water flushes and plunging, which sends more water into a backed-up main. The script's own advice later is "Stop running water." The fix: run the main-line test first, and use the level one and two tools only once you know it's one fixture.
- The book break describes the book's layout ("each with the problem, why it costs money, my first check..."). That is not in the brief, so I can't check it. Confirm it against the persona book text. The script cites no pages, which is safe.
- "A clog isn't one thing that fell down the drain": true of hair and grease, but a beginner can have a dropped object in the trap. This is a minor overstatement.
- Everything else checks against the brief: PVC at about 140 F; washing soda is gentler than a chemical cleaner but still alkaline; cup plunger versus flange plunger; water doesn't compress; 15 to 20 strokes; the snake's feel; trap arm and cleanout; the main-line test; stop running water.

Script Y:
- **The chemical warning comes too late** (see section 2). This is the one real safety risk in either script. Move it to the top of level two, before the plunger.
- "Nobody mentions that strip when they quote you for the visit" breaks the brief's boundary against claiming to know what plumbers do. "I think that's a disgrace" is an attack on plumbers' pricing that the brief doesn't support.
- "you just did a service call's job with a tool that's probably in your closet already": the plunger most people already own is the flange (toilet) plunger the script just told them not to use. This invites the wrong tool, and a toilet plunger in a sink is a hygiene problem.
- "plus a seasonal calendar" (about the book) is not in the brief, so I can't check it. Remove it or confirm it.
- The book citations are accurate to the brief: chapter six covers the stopper, plunger and trap on pages 43 and 44 (Fixes 32 to 34), and the main-line signs are on page 56 (Fix 46). It doesn't put this video's steps in the book's mouth.
- "depending on where you are in the country" softens the price. Harmless.
- Both scripts: neither says that the washing-soda solution from level one may still be in the drain when you plunge at level two. Gloves are on in both, so the risk is low. One line ("flush it clear with hot water before you plunge") would close the gap.

## 4. Verdict

**Publish X, by a clear margin (about 8.0 against 6.2 on the listener scores).** X is the one a beginner could follow safely from the audio alone. Each step comes in the right order, the chemical check comes before the plunger, and the voice is calm and on your side. Y has more personality and better reward moments, but it opens slowly, makes two promises, interrupts its own steps, takes swipes at plumbers, and puts the one dangerous part after the steps it protects you from.

- **X: light edit.**
  - Fix the level-three contradiction: test for the main line before reusing the level one and two tools.
  - Vary "here's what you do" and thin out "that's a call".
  - Trim the book break's feature list and smooth "Back to the drain."
  - Add one or two "you just did that" reward beats.
- **Y: rewrite (structural).**
  - Move the chemical warning to the top of level two.
  - Cut the opening so the first step lands by about 130 words, with one promise.
  - Remove the jug detour, "nobody mentions", "somebody's going to tell you" and "disgrace".
  - Fix the "already in your closet" plunger line and drop or verify "seasonal calendar".
  - Much of its line-level writing (the takeaway line, the sensory detail, the reward beats) is worth keeping.
