## 3. Script by script

### K

Hook: the salon-and-photo scene is the best emotional opening of the three ("quietly turned the phone face down on the table"), and the blame comes off her clearly ("It isn't your age, and it isn't how short your hair is. It's the shape."). But the first sentence is 36 words (check FAIL), the title's promise only arrives at the end of paragraph two, and then the script explains the granny cut for another four paragraphs. Item one starts 492 words (about 3 minutes) in. A viewer who clicked for "7 haircuts" waits too long.

Flow: the best natural talk of the three. Good bridges ("Let's start with the one that surprises people." / "Now let's go back to that flat back of the head..."), and the items do not all run the same pattern.

Best three lines:
1. "But a week later someone took a photo at lunch, and you looked at it for a second and quietly turned the phone face down on the table." Her real fear, shown, not stated.
2. "Does it move? When you turn your head, does it swing, or does it just hang there? If it moves, keep it, and wear it proudly. If it hangs, it doesn't need to be cut off. It needs layers." A simple test plus permission; perfect for the ear.
3. "Nobody sets out to get a granny cut. It just creeps up on you, one appointment at a time." Takes the blame off her and off the stylist at once.

Weakest three lines:
1. "Have you ever asked for something simple at the salon, just a tidy cut that's easy to keep, and then caught your reflection on the way home and thought, when did I start looking like this?" 36 words (check FAIL); too long for TTS as the very first sentence, and it does not say the title.
2. "That swap, a layered bob instead of a blunt one, is exactly the kind of small change I gathered into my book..." Banned "I gathered" book line (check FAIL).
3. "That ease is exactly what reads as young." Banned old house line (check FAIL), and it sounds like a slogan.

Also: "draws a ruler across the one place you'd rather people didn't stare" points at her jaw as something to hide; mildly shaming.

Needs before upload: **light-to-medium edit.** Cut the pre-item section by about two thirds and say the title promise in the first two sentences; split the two long sentences; replace the two banned lines; bring "granny" down to one, flagged as harsh; fix the book over-claim; add the sign-off.

### L

Hook: "These seven haircuts help a woman over sixty look younger instantly, and every one of them avoids the granny cut." The title is paid off in the first sentence (check: every title word present). The fear is named ("seen someone older than you feel"), the blame comes off ("Your age isn't the problem, and neither is the length of your hair"), the change is made small ("a shape can change at your very next appointment"), and the move into item one plays on her fear of the change itself ("the cut most women are afraid to try"). Item one starts at 46 seconds.

Flow: each item ends with a bridge that names who the next cut is for ("But perhaps short hair simply isn't you. Then the next one may be the cut that finally works."; "And if your hair has a natural wave you've been fighting for years, the next cut asks you to stop fighting."). The one repeat: every item opens "Number X is the...", and the "Have you ever... / Do you know that moment..." question device turns up in several items. Small, not a flow break.

Best three lines:
1. "It's a harsh name, I know, and it describes a shape, never the woman wearing it." Handles the title's harsh word the way the rule wants, and protects her.
2. "But perhaps short hair simply isn't you. Then the next one may be the cut that finally works." Her want ("the cut that finally works") used as the bridge.
3. "Not one of them asks you to change your face, your body or your age. They simply stop the shape of your hair from adding years you don't feel, and they let the woman you really are show through." The emotional payoff of the whole video, said as relief.

Weakest three lines:
1. "At the jaw, that means the eye lands right where the skin has begun to soften, and the cut draws a line underneath it." The one line that points at her ageing skin; soften it or cut it.
2. "And here's the relief." It announces the emotion instead of just giving it; a touch scripted.
3. "Fine and gray hair can scorch more easily, and a gentler heat keeps it soft and shiny." "Scorch more easily" is not in the brief (the brief says only heat protectant on a low setting); mild, but it is an added claim.

Also mild: "So you may keep your length, ladies, as long as it moves." "You may" can sound like permission handed down; "you can keep your length" is warmer.

Needs before upload: **light edit** (the three lines above). Nothing blocks upload.

## 6. Verdict

L is the script to upload after a light edit. It is the only one that meets all three of the owner's goals together. The hook pays off the title in its first sentence and names her fear, takes the blame off her and reaches item one in under a minute. The emotions stay kind and relieving through the video. Every item hands on to the next with a line about who the next cut is for. It also passes every hard rule, with exact book Ways, "granny" once and flagged, both safety lines and the full ending.

K has the most human opening scene and the most natural talk. It loses on structure: almost three minutes before item one, "granny" six times, two banned lines, a book over-claim and no sign-off. With an edit it is fixable.

M has some of the sharpest practical lines of the three, but by ear it is the one that "didn't flow". It is dense and fragmented, runs the same pattern every item, is full of transcription errors a TTS voice will stumble on, adds facts that aren't in the brief, and stops mid-sentence. It needs a rewrite.
