These seven haircuts help a woman over sixty look younger instantly, and every one of them avoids the granny cut. It's a harsh name, I know, and it describes a shape, never the woman wearing it. Short, rounded, set or sprayed into place, flat at the back with a little dome on top. If you've ever passed a mirror and seen someone older than you feel, that shape is very often the reason.

Your age isn't the problem, and neither is the length of your hair. It's the shape the cut makes around your face, and a shape can change at your very next appointment. So let's start with the cut most women are afraid to try, because it's the one that surprises them most.

Number one is the textured pixie. Now, I know short hair can feel like the very thing you're trying to avoid. But the difference between a pixie that lifts you and a short cut that ages you is all in the finish. A textured pixie has lift at the crown, piecey ends instead of one smooth curve, a clean nape at the back of the neck, and a soft fringe swept to one side. The rounded cut sits on the head like a cap, one smooth surface, and the eye has nothing to follow but the outline. A textured pixie breaks that outline into little pieces, so the eye moves, and movement is what looks fresh and alive.

It's especially good if your hair has become fine or thinner over the years. Short, piecey layers don't have to carry any weight. Long, fine hair tends to hang and show the scalp, while short, lifted hair looks fuller than it really is. And if what you've noticed is sudden shedding or patches, rather than slow thinning, that's a question for your doctor, not your stylist, and well worth asking first.

One honest word, though. A pixie needs a trim every four to six weeks. Leave it longer and the ends soften and round out, and slowly it grows back into the very bowl you were escaping. So book the next trim before you leave the salon. When you ask for the cut, say texture at the crown, a clean nape and a soft side fringe, and bring a photograph of the shape you like. A picture saves a great deal of guessing on both sides. Get it right, and the first thing anyone notices is your eyes, because a soft side fringe and a lifted crown frame them, instead of a dome sitting above them.

But perhaps short hair simply isn't you. Then the next one may be the cut that finally works.

Number two is the modern layered bob. The bob itself was never the problem. The problem is one heavy, blunt line sitting exactly at the jaw, because wherever a hard line stops, the eye stops with it. At the jaw, that means the eye lands on the jawline itself, and the cut draws a hard line right across it.

A modern bob keeps the length but takes the weight out from inside. Your stylist cuts internal layers, hidden beneath the top, so the hair moves and lifts instead of sitting in a solid block. Then she adds soft pieces around the face, so the front falls in a gentle curve rather than a straight edge. When I was designing, we never finished a garment with a heavy band at its widest point, because it pulls every eye to the very spot you'd rather soften. Hair works exactly the same way.

At jaw length, this bob suits an oval or heart-shaped face especially well, because it adds a little softness just where the face narrows. So ask for internal layers and soft face framing, and tell her you'd like to move away from one blunt line. Do you know that moment when a cut finally sits right and you stop fiddling with it? This is very often that cut. Your jawline looks sharper, and your whole face looks lifted, with nothing changed but the hair.

Number three is the collarbone lob, cut a little longer in the front. A lob is simply a long bob, and this one sits around the collarbone with a gentle forward angle, so the front pieces fall just lower than the back. That angle is doing something rather clever. It draws a line downward along your face, and the eye follows it, so a round or square face looks longer and more oval. It's the same reason a V-neck flatters. The line points down, and the face lengthens with it.

But here's the part to remember. About an inch of angle is plenty. Any more and it turns into a dramatic statement cut, sharp at the front and short at the back, and that's a different look altogether. You want the angle to be felt more than noticed. And if your face is long rather than round, keep the angle even softer, or leave it out, because you don't need the extra length.

Collarbone length is kind in another way, too. The ends move against the collarbone rather than resting on the shoulders, where hair tends to flick out and look untidy by lunchtime. If you've grown your hair to the shoulders and it never seems to do anything, this is a lovely place to land. Ask for a collarbone lob, slightly longer in front, about an inch, no more. You'll see your face look longer and more balanced, and you'll notice your collarbone again, which is one of the prettiest lines a woman has.

Now, if the back of your head looks flat whatever you do, this next cut is for you.

Number four is the soft stacked bob. Most of us find the back of the head gets flatter over time, and it's no fault of yours. Hair changes, and the crown loses some of its natural lift. The rounded, sprayed cuts try to fix that with a dome on top, which only makes the flat back more obvious from the side.

A soft stack fixes it from underneath. Your stylist cuts a gentle graduation at the back, shorter layers at the nape building up to longer ones over the crown. The hair stacks on itself and creates a soft curve where it used to lie flat. From the side, your head has a lovely rounded profile again, and the front can stay at chin length or just below.

The word that matters here is soft. Ask for a soft stack, not a sharp wedge. A sharp wedge has a hard, shelf-like back, and that's exactly the dated look we're stepping away from. A soft stack is blended, with no ledge you could rest a finger on.

Have you ever seen a photograph of yourself from the side and been taken aback? Most women have. With this cut, the side view becomes the one you like best, because the line runs from the nape up over the crown in one smooth lift. It's polished, it's easy to live with, and it holds its shape between appointments.

Now let's talk about long hair, because I'd like to put one myth to rest.

Number five is long hair with soft layers that start at the cheekbone. You may have heard that long hair isn't allowed after sixty. Please don't believe it. Length is perfectly fine. What adds years is long hair cut in one heavy line, all the same length, hanging straight down. All that weight pulls downward, and it drags the face down with it, so the cheeks and jaw look heavier than they are.

Layers that begin at the cheekbone change everything. The first and shortest pieces sit right at the cheekbone, so they lift the eye to the best part of your face, and the rest fall in gentle steps below. The hair moves when you turn your head, and movement is the whole secret to long hair that looks fresh.

Then ask for the ends to be point cut. That means the stylist snips into the ends with the tips of the scissors rather than cutting straight across, so they finish soft and feathered instead of blunt. It's a small detail, but blunt ends make long hair look heavier still, and soft ones let it swing.

So you may keep your length, ladies, as long as it moves. Wear it down and your cheekbones are the first thing anyone sees. Wear it back, and those soft pieces around your face keep the whole look gentle and lifted.

And if your hair has a natural wave you've been fighting for years, the next cut asks you to stop fighting.

Number six is the soft shag with curtain framing. A shag is a cut with layers all the way through it, shorter on top and at the crown, longer underneath. The soft version leaves out the choppy, rock and roll edges. Curtain framing means a fringe parted in the middle that sweeps away to each side, like curtains opening, so it frames your eyes and cheekbones.

This cut is at its very best on wavy hair. The layers let the wave spring up instead of being weighed down, and the whole thing air-dries in minutes. Think how freeing that is. So many of us have spent years blow-drying our waves into a shape they never wanted to hold. With a soft shag, you scrunch in a little product, let it dry, and off you go. The lift at the crown keeps the volume high on the head, which is exactly where you want it. If your hair is straight, a shag can still work, but it asks a little more of you each morning, so be honest about how much time you'd like to spend.

If your hair is fine or gray and you do reach for a dryer or a curling iron to smooth the curtain pieces, that's fine. Use a heat protectant first, and keep the tool on a low setting. Fine and gray hair can scorch more easily, and a gentler heat keeps it soft and shiny. The result is hair that looks relaxed and full of life, as though you've just come back from a week by the sea.

Which brings us to the last one, for the woman who has always loved a fringe.

Number seven is the chin-length bob with a wispy, side-swept bang. Perhaps you've noticed your fringe doesn't love you back the way it used to. A heavy, blunt fringe cut straight across draws a hard line over the brow. It covers the forehead like a shutter and puts all the attention on the lower half of the face.

A wispy bang does the opposite. It's light, with a little air between the pieces, and it sweeps to one side on a diagonal. That diagonal line carries the eye upward and across, so the face looks more open. And because it's so light, it softens the forehead without hiding your eyes.

The chin length keeps the whole cut neat and easy, and it gives the jaw a clean, crisp finish. If you've worn a blunt fringe for years, you don't have to give up a fringe at all. You simply ask for it to be thinned out and swept, and let it grow a little longer on one side. And then something lovely happens. People tell you that you look rested, and they can never quite say why.

That's the quiet secret in all seven of these cuts. Not one of them asks you to change your face, your body or your age. They simply stop the shape of your hair from adding years you don't feel, and they let the woman you really are show through.
