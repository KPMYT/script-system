**Published, why:**
- Fast and pointed opening, with a question that lands: "How many pairs do you own that only ever see the inside of your house?" Rule one starts at 0:32 (about 75 words).
- Some of the best single lines in the pair: "it starts to look like a long shirt that forgot its pants" and "Do you have a shirt dress you stopped wearing because of your legs? This is how it comes back."

**New, why:**

## 2. Diane: "Stop Trying to Dress Young After 50 (The French Way)"

**Published, why:**
- A crisp opening that says the title, then the point, in 73 words: "Stop trying to dress young after 50, because it does the opposite of what you want. The outfit meant to take years off puts them back on."
- Good spoken bridges: "That one's the jeans. The next one you can't even see because it's behind you."
- But the humor lands on her more than once ("After 50, I think that's a lot to ask of two knees."; "It looks like you stopped."), some lines are hard to follow by ear ("the jean was cut for a hip that hasn't arrived yet"), there is a whole makeup item (lashes and liner), and the book line names the wrong Secret for the white shirt (Secret 11, about trends). **Published, why:**
- Names her fear well and moves the blame off her face: "If you've been putting off the color appointment because you're afraid of what a new shade will do to your face, I understand." / "The shade changed that week and your face took the blame for it."
- Gives her words for the chair and makes the colorist a helper: "If your colorist reaches for the whole head of foils, say no, thank you, and say the sentence again."; "Two tones look like hair. One tone looks like a bottle."
- But: a second promise in the hook ("and three that do the opposite") that is never counted out; "your check is" said four times, so items end alike; a 104-word book CTA; unhedged claims ("Iron in the water tints blonde orange, and a simple filter on the shower head takes most of it out"; the cool rinse "closes the surface of the hair"); the patch-test line dropped mid-item in color one. It does say the title's word "instantly" (4 times), which the new one never does.

**New, why:**
- The best bridges of the six scripts; each item hands on to the next like a conversation: "Now, those first two are for the woman who is still coloring. The third is for the woman who has been wondering whether to stop." / "That's the woman going gray from brown. But what about the woman who has been blonde her whole life?"
- Plain, vivid lines that are easy to hear once: "It's like a date stamp on the top of your head." / "You want champagne, not ice." / "People don't look at you and think, her hair is too dark. They think, she looks tired."

- Weak spots: color seven gives the count away, "the seventh color is really a way of wearing any color", which is exactly what the brief asked to avoid (name every one by its tone so all seven are paid as colors). The safety paragraph ("One more thing, my dear friends...") lands between color seven and its own check, so the last item stops, detours, and restarts. "Women say to me, I'm just not ready to look like I've stopped trying" is an invented client quote for a woman who was a fashion designer, not a colorist, and "I hear it all the time" echoes the model ("I hear this one all of the time", Chery 6:36). ### Facts

- **Wrong or risky:** the end book line's "everything we've talked about" (above).
- **Risky, from the brief:** "Silver with no shine turns yellow at the ends" has the cause backwards: yellowing comes from things like heat, minerals and sun, and it dulls the shine; lack of shine doesn't cause the yellow. Safer: "Silver that's gone yellow at the ends loses its shine."
- **Unchecked, small:** face-framing pieces being "gentler on the hair" than a full color (likely true, less processing, but not in the brief).
- **Fixed from the published version:** the iron-in-water claim is now hedged ("your water may be part of the story ... A filter on the shower head may help"); the cool-rinse claim and the minutes-in-the-chair figures are gone; "almost every woman" became "many women".

## Overall: does the one-page brief hold up?

Yes. On three new titles, each written blind from one page plus a brief and a model, all three scripts beat the video we actually published by about one point (8.2 to 8.3 against 7.0 to 7.3), every one landed inside length, opening and book-length limits, every mid-video book line named only confirmed Secrets or Ways (one fixed a wrong match in the published video), and all three endings were in the right order with the exact sign-off. None needs a rewrite; all three need a light edit. Four weaknesses repeat. **First, the openings got longer:** 145 to 161 words against the published 73 to 82, all inside the 170 limit, but none asks her a question and they reach item one about twice as late; the limit is being read as a target. **Second, limits are read as quotas:** all three use "When I was designing" exactly twice and both Dianes use "my dear friends" exactly three times, and in two scripts the second designer line reads inserted ("the shoe sets the rhythm"). The draft could say "up to two, and none is fine". **Third, the end book line can still over-claim** ("Everything we've talked about... goes deeper"). The Scarlett draft's brief said "don't claim everything" and was obeyed; the Diane hair brief didn't, and the writer drifted. That rule belongs in the one-page draft, not only in a brief. **Fourth, models' signature lines leak** ("I bet that's happened at your house, too"; "I hear it all the time") even though no 6-word run was copied. The "never copy her words" rule should name her signature turns of phrase. A lesser point: the hair script dropped the title's word "instantly" altogether, so the draft might add "say the title's sharpest word at least once". One caveat on the test itself: each brief was written from the published video with the audit's flags corrected, so the new scripts started with the fixes in hand; the comparison fairly measures the writing, but not research or brief-making.
