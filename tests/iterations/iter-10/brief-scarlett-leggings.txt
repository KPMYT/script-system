TITLE: How to Wear Leggings Over 60 Without Looking Frumpy
CHANNEL: Scarlett Starling
BRIEF (talking points):
- The feeling: leggings are the easiest thing in the closet until she catches the whole outfit looking frumpy in a shop window; then they go back in the drawer for "house only" and the stiff pants come back. Five rules make leggings look polished after 60, then seven outfits that keep all five, built from the closet she already has.
- Rule 1, cover the hips and the seat: the top reaches mid-thigh or longer (tunic, long sweater, shirt dress, long cardigan over a longer tank). A legging is thin stretch over the fullest part of the body, so a hip-length top leaves every line on show; that's the tug-at-the-sweater feeling. A longer top makes one smooth line from shoulder to thigh. Check: back to a full-length mirror, look over the shoulder; if you can see where the seat ends, the top is too short for leggings (and it moves to the jeans pile, not the bin).
- Rule 2, thick, opaque and matte: ponte (a heavy, smooth double knit that holds its line), heavy stretch cotton, or a brushed fabric with weight. Thin pairs show everything and lose their shape by lunchtime; shine catches the light on thigh and knee, matte keeps the leg one even shape. Check: bend forward in daylight at a window with a mirror behind; if the seat goes sheer, they're for under a long dress only. The fitting-room light is kinder than a parking lot. The thick pair costs a little more and is the best money in the video (in words, no figure).
- Rule 3, a high, wide waistband that doesn't roll or dig: about a hand's width, coming up over the navel, so it starts at the narrowest part of the middle; a thin elastic band rolls when she sits, digs when she stands and leaves a ridge under the top. Check in the store: sit, stand; did the band stay put, can she breathe, is there a line pressed into the skin afterwards?
- Rule 4, ankle length and no bunching: the hem lands at or just above the ankle bone in one clean line; folds over the shoe shorten the leg. Petite: a seven-eighths length (cut to stop a little higher). Leggings can be hemmed by a tailor who works with knits (a stretch hem needs a stretch stitch; not every tailor does it, and don't promise "a few minutes"). Check: a side photo in daylight with shoes on (a mirror lets you stand taller; a photo doesn't).
- Rule 5, dark neutrals, shoe matched to the legging: black, navy, charcoal, chocolate brown. A dark matte leg is the longest line from the waist down; a print on the leg pulls the eye to the fullest part, so a print goes up near the face (a scarf or blouse). Same-color shoe, no break at the ankle. A white sneaker is the one exception, only when everything else is dark. Shoes that work: low block-heel ankle boot, knee boot, loafer, ballet flat, leather sneaker, mule.
- Look 1: black ponte, a straight tunic to just above the knee, black block-heel ankle boots, a long pendant; sleeves pushed to just below the elbow, wrists bare or a watch. A flared tunic hem makes a triangle; straight makes a line.
- Look 2: charcoal leggings, a cable-knit sweater that covers the hips (shoulder seam on the point of the shoulder, not dropped), suede loafers in charcoal or deep brown, or spotless white leather sneakers (the one break that earns its place).
- Look 3: a chambray shirt dress at the knee (chambray: soft washed denim-blue shirting), worn open over black leggings with a dark loafer, bottom two or three buttons undone; easiest for a fuller middle. Keep the dress at the knee, not above.
- Look 4: thick matte ponte, a long silky blouse, a blazer that ends below the hip, pointed flats, a structured bag carried in the hand. Check: step back about three feet; the leggings should pass for slim black pants. A blazer that stops at the hip needs trousers or a skirt instead.
- Look 5, a navy column: navy leggings, navy top, navy flats, a long camel duster (or a long navy cardigan). A column is one color shoulder to shoe; keep the flats navy, not black.
- Look 6: matte faux-leather leggings in a thick stretch fabric, a silky top, a long jacket, block-heel boots, one piece of jewelry (pendant or earrings, not both). Shiny leather reads costume; run the bend test, since faux leather varies most.
- Look 7, polished athleisure: high-waisted leggings, a long dark sweatshirt that covers the hips, a dark quilted vest that ends below the hip, spotless white sneakers, a crossbody bag, no logos anywhere (logos say gym). The vest and sweatshirt are dark so the white-sneaker rule holds.
- Care (one closing action, not a list): wash inside out on cool and hang to dry; skip the dryer, because heat kills the stretch and fades the black. (The published video's four-pair list and the run-back to the looks are cut: the audit counted five actions after the last look.)

- Safety: a block heel over a thin one, about two inches, steadier on a wet sidewalk. No diet, weight or body-shaping claims: "slimmer" means the line, never the body.
