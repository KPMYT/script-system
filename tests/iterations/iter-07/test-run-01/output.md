What's the quickest way to make a good outfit look less expensive, and older, than it really is?
Put the wrong necklace with it.

That length sits near the base of your neck, so it fills the space above a crew neck or a plain round collar without crowding your face.
And one small pendant gives the eye a single point to land on, which looks calm and deliberate.
But the chain itself has to be good, because this is where a lot of the cheap look comes from.
A thin plated chain looks lovely for a few months.
Then the plating wears through where it rubs against your skin and the clasp, and it tarnishes into a brassy, patchy color.
Solid gold keeps its color, and it's worth it on a piece you'll wear every day.
So does vermeil, said ver-may, which is a thick layer of gold over sterling silver.

Gold or silver, then?
As a loose guide, gold often suits warm undertones and silver often suits cool ones.
Your undertone is just the warm or cool cast under your skin, and there are two old folk tests for it.
Look at the veins on the inside of your wrist in daylight, where greenish veins tend to go with warm and bluish ones with cool.

Neither test is perfect, so take them as a hint and let your mirror decide.
Hold the chain against your collarbone at a window and look at your face, not the chain.
If your skin looks brighter and your eyes look clearer, that's your metal.
I'd choose the metal by your face, never by what's in the shop window.
The right chain makes you look polished before you've done anything else.

That short chain works close to your face.
The next one does the opposite job, and it works on your whole frame.

Number two is a long pendant, roughly twenty-four to thirty inches.
A pendant that long draws one straight line down the middle of your body, and the eye follows that line.
So your neck looks longer, and your torso looks longer too.
That makes it a real friend to a fuller bust or a softer middle, because the eye travels down instead of across.
It also loves a V-neck.
The point of the V and the drop of the pendant echo each other, and the whole neckline looks planned.
But the length is where it can go wrong.
If the chain is much shorter than that range, the pendant tends to land right on the bust and sit there, and the line never gets started.
In that range, it hangs clear and keeps going.
Keep the pendant itself simple, too: one disc, one bar, or one stone, rather than a cluster.
Here's the check.

I love a long pendant over a plain knit in the colder months, when the sweater has no shape of its own.
Done right, you look taller and more pulled together, even in the simplest clothes.

So which necklace sends the most light up onto your face?
That one is next.

Number five is one clean statement piece.

Number six is a heavy strand of dark, chunky beads.
Maybe you've looked at a photo of yourself wearing one and thought your face looked tired, and you couldn't say why.

Pearls, or beads in light, polished stones.

Number seven is the tight choker, sitting high on the neck.
A choker that high puts a frame around your neck, and it cuts the line of the neck straight across.
So the eye stops at the band instead of traveling up to your face, and the neck looks shorter than it is.
But the fix is simple, and you already know it from the pearls.
Swap it for a princess-length piece that rests on the collarbone.
That opens up the space between your chin and the necklace, and the whole neck looks longer straight away.
It takes seconds.

If you love your choker, I'd move it down a couple of inches before I'd give it up.
A short extender chain will let it drop to the base of the neck, and an open collar gives it more room.
Check it in the mirror with your shoulders relaxed.
If you can see a clean stretch of neck above the necklace, it's sitting in the right place.
You'll look taller through the neck and shoulders, and more at ease.

Number eight is the matched costume set.
Do you have one of those tucked away in a drawer, still in its box?
Costume jewelry is the inexpensive kind, made to look like fine jewelry, and a matched set is a necklace and earrings, sometimes a bracelet too, all made as one.
