BRIEF: Five styling secrets for a bob after 60: (1) Crown lift, not fullness at the ends: a flat crown with width at the bottom makes a triangle that pulls the face down; mousse or root-lift spray at the roots, a round brush lifting the crown while drying, or two or three Velcro rollers at the crown for ten minutes. (2) Stop curling the ends under: the rounded-under, set-and-sprayed bob is the 'helmet' look; finish the ends straight or bent slightly out with a flat iron, separate them with a little texture cream or spray, and use a flexible-hold spray. (3) Move the part: a deep side or off-center part adds lift and asymmetry, and on fine or thinning hair it can disguise a widening center part (a styling effect, never a cure). (4) Frame the face: tuck one side behind the ear to show the jaw and earrings, let the front pieces fall toward the cheekbones, and a soft side-swept fringe softens the forehead. (5) Make it shine: dull, flat color ages more than the cut; a shine serum on the ends, a salon gloss, color one to two shades lighter than the pre-gray natural shade with warmth rather than flat black, and for a gray bob a purple shampoo about once a week against brassiness. Supporting facts: a bob holds its line with a trim every six to eight weeks; heavy creams flatten fine hair; use a heat protectant and lower heat on fine or color-treated hair. Anchors: a round brush or rollers cost roughly $10 to $30; a US salon cut commonly runs $50 to $100 or more (check). Boundaries: thinning hair is a styling topic only, no diagnosis or treatment; the styling is the problem, never the woman.
