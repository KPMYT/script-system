### Script X

Reads as a numbered do-along: "Step one" to "Step eleven", each ending on a bridge into the next. That makes it the easier of the two to follow with a flashlight in hand. The cost is that it sounds built: almost every step closes on an "I'd ..." line, most carry a rhetorical question, and there are several "You'd think ... but" turns. Three of the eleven "steps" (3, 4 and part of 11) are explanation, not actions.

Strengths (quoted):
- "Yours pulls so much heat out of the exhaust that it cools down and turns to water, a bit like a cold glass sweating in July."
- "But nothing goes in at all until the power's off." (a clean bridge from the bleach warning into shut-down)
- "Dry floor, warm air, and the inducer humming along. That's a furnace with a clear drain, and you didn't pay anybody to tell you so."
- "Can you hear the water running free into that drain? That's what clear sounds like, and it cost you a cup out of the pantry."
- The pump check (Step eight) sits right after the flush and before the trap refill, which is the order you'd actually do it in.

Where it stops flowing (quoted):
- Opening: "When your line clogs, the furnace shuts down, often on the coldest nights. Clearing it can run you $100 to $250, and nothing in your furnace is broken. The cup costs pocket change. It won't open a line that's packed solid ... Eleven steps, start to finish." That's a stack of facts with no scene in it, and "Eleven steps" sounds like a template label.
- "Step three. Know what that slime does once it gets going." It's labelled as a step but there's nothing to do.
- "I'd settle that before you buy a single jug, because there's no sense treating a drain you don't have." Tacked on after the point has already landed.
- "Is that a lot of fuss for a cup of vinegar? Maybe."
- "I'd set a kitchen timer for the half hour, so the flush doesn't slip your mind." / "I'd give it the full cup or two, so the whole line gets a rinse." / "I'd make a note of it once you know." The same closer comes back again and again, and by the ear the pattern shows by step five.
- "I think that's a stiff price for a little plastic box, but it beats a wet floor." An opinion on a price that the brief didn't ask for.

### Script Y

Sounds like one man talking: no step labels, short sentences and plain signposts. The cold open is a real scene. It loses points on order. The condensate pump section (and "while you're flushing, watch the pump") comes after the furnace has already been flushed, refilled and restarted. A carbon monoxide aside also drops in between "fill the trap" and "power back on", the most hands-on moment in the video.

Strengths (quoted):
- "It's the coldest night of the week, and the furnace starts, runs a minute, and quits. Then it tries again."
- "Back the water up, and the pressure switch can't close. No close, no fire."
- "Forty-five years in this trade, and my verdict is plain. Vinegar."
- "Where you pour it is where people go wrong."
- "It also means you can wake up to a cold house with a furnace that has nothing wrong with it. The pump stopped it."

Where it stops flowing (quoted):
- "The store shelf has bottles of stronger stuff. The cup that does this job is already in your cupboard." An orphan two-line paragraph that repeats the opening and breaks the bleach section in half.
- "And while we're on exhaust. If a carbon monoxide alarm ever goes off in your house ..." A full emergency procedure inside the restart sequence. The content is right but the placement is wrong.
- "If there's no floor drain near your furnace, the water goes to a condensate pump." This arrives after "Flip the power switch back on", so "So while you're flushing, watch the pump" comes too late for anyone doing it along with the audio.
- "Then check the furnace. Nothing from the tester, and the door can come off." It doesn't say where on the furnace, and a listener can't do this from the audio.
- The "the trade calls ..." tic shows up three times: "the kind the trade calls a condensing furnace", "The trade word for it is biofilm", "which the trade calls short cycling".
- Mild recap at the close: "So the next time that furnace quits on a cold night, you'll know it may not be dying at all ..."

## 3. Facts and risk

Both scripts:
- Bleach vs vinegar: both keep to the brief. They give bleach its due, attribute the recommendation to Carrier and Lennox, and give Carrier's stated reason ("harsh chemicals can damage system components") without adding a PVC-melting claim. That's correct: PVC itself tolerates dilute bleach fairly well, and the real concerns are other parts, pumps and mixing chemicals. Both include the warning never to mix bleach and vinegar (chlorine gas) and to flush any old bleach first.
- Pressure switch and inducer: both explain them correctly, and both say nothing goes into the pressure-switch hoses or the inducer.
- Trap seal: both say correctly that a dry trap can let exhaust into the house and to refill before start-up.
- Shared small inaccuracy: both say the poured vinegar runs "through the trap" (X: "running down through the trap and the line"; Y: "working through the trap and down the line"). A cleanout or vent tee usually sits downstream of the trap, so vinegar poured there mostly treats the line and not the trap. "Finish with plain water so the trap is full" has the same limit. This comes from the brief, but a light edit should soften it to "down the line".
- Both use warm water, not boiling (X gives the reason: plastic), and both say no garden hose and no compressed air.

X only:
- Step five adds a workaround that isn't in the brief: if the tester can't read through metal-sheathed cable, "turn the thermostat up for a minute. No hum, no click, and the little light on the control board stays dark. Then you can take the door off." This is a reasonable functional check, but it isn't proof of dead wiring (a dark LED can also mean a blown board fuse while line voltage is still present). Low risk, but it should say "if in doubt, kill the breaker too" or be cut. It also leaves the thermostat turned up while the user works.
- "The visit to clear it is still a full service call": a claim the brief doesn't support. Minor.

Y only:
- The tester step ("Then check the furnace") is too vague to carry out. It needs to say where: the power cable at the switch, or the wires going into the cabinet.
- The CO procedure is complete as far as I can judge, but for a drain video it sits in the most hands-on part of the procedure. It would fit better after restart, or as a short closing safety note.
- The pump check is out of order (see above). That isn't a safety risk, but the instruction comes too late to act on.
- Y's early "While the furnace is running, look at the end of it" is a good, safe extra diagnostic step that X lacks.

Neither script contains anything I'd call dangerous.

## 4. Verdict

**Publish Y, by a narrow margin (46 vs 44).** As a listener, Y is the one I'd keep playing. It opens on a scene, sounds like one man, and has the better lines. X is the better instruction sheet but the weaker video: its repeated "I'd ..." closers, rhetorical questions and "Step N" labels make it sound like a template by the midpoint.

- **Y: light edit.** Move the condensate pump section up so it sits between the flush and the trap refill. Move the CO paragraph to after restart, or into a short closing safety note. Cut the orphan "store shelf" paragraph. Say where to hold the tester. Change "through the trap" to "down the line". Trim the recap sentence.
- **X: light edit (heavier than Y).** Rewrite the opening as a scene, keep the one promise and drop "Eleven steps". Cut roughly half the "I'd ..." closers and rhetorical questions. Merge Steps 3 and 4 into explanation between real steps, or renumber to action steps only. Fix or cut the thermostat-up power check. Change "through the trap" to "down the line".
