TITLE: Furnace Keeps Shutting Off? The $12 Part Techs Charge $300 to Replace (Clean It Yourself)

BRIEF: The part is the flame sensor: a thin metal rod in the burner flame that proves to the control board the gas lit. When it's coated with carbon or oxide, the board can't read the flame, so the furnace lights, runs a few seconds, shuts the gas off and retries, sometimes locking out after three tries. A new sensor costs about $10-$30; a service call to clean or replace it typically runs $100-$250, up to about $300 at night or on a weekend. The fix: turn off power at the furnace switch and turn the gas valve off (and let it cool), remove the burner door, take out the one screw holding the sensor, pull it out by its base, polish the rod lightly with a Scotch-Brite pad or very fine emery cloth (not coarse sandpaper; keep grit off the ceramic), wipe it, reinstall, restore power and watch a full cycle. If it still shuts off, other causes: a dirty filter (high limit), a clogged condensate trap or vent pipe (pressure switch), a weak igniter, or a bad sensor wire; that's when to call a tech. Safety: never touch the hot-surface igniter next to it (fragile, skin oils crack it); if you smell gas, stop and call the gas company. Prices as rounded ranges.
