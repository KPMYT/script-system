## Scarlett Starling: "10 Things Elegant Women Over 50 Are NOT Wearing This Winter"

### The hook, in full (157 words)

> There are ten things in your winter closet right now that make an elegant woman look dated, and you'll put at least one of them on this week. Under all those layers, they make you look wider and older than you are.
>
> The best-dressed women over fifty, the ones who look polished in a frozen parking lot in January, have stopped wearing every one. It isn't their age or their budget. They simply know what each piece does to a woman's line. Most of these swaps come from your own closet, and your mirror shows it that same evening.
>
> When a woman looks bulky in winter, the fault is never hers. It's the shape, the length and the fabric. You've stood by the front door, buttoned up, wondering why you looked so much wider than in summer.
>
> So let's start with the one we reach for first when the frost comes, and get wrong most often.

The published hook for comparison (sentence one): "10 things in your winter closet right now are making you look dated and out of touch, and you will wear one of them this week."

**The hook is a little weaker than the published one.**
- Sentence one moves the hit off her: "make **an elegant woman** look dated" against the published "are making **you** look dated and out of touch".
- The second sentence brings back a strong fear ("wider and older than you are").
- The front-door moment is better than the published shop-window one, which the new script saves for item 1.
- **The frame copies the competitor model** (a reading): "There are ten things in your winter closet right now that..." follows H01's "There are 10 things in your wardrobe right now that quietly add years". "When a woman looks bulky in winter, the fault is never hers. It's the shape, the length and the fabric." follows H01's "When a woman looks tired in her clothes, it is never the woman. It's the cut, the length, the color, the fabric." The check's 8-word copy run misses both.

## Diane de Chambray: "Why French Women Get More Beautiful With Age"

### The hook, in full (165 words)

> Plenty of French women grow more beautiful with age. Right now your own closet is doing the opposite to you, and it's adding years you haven't lived.
>
> In Paris you'll often see her: a woman past sixty who looks better than she did at forty.
>
> She has the same lines in her mirror and the same age on her passport. Her budget is no bigger than yours. She simply knows what seven small habits do, built over years, not bought.
>
> Most of them are free. Every one is within reach this week, mostly from your own closet, and your mirror will tell you the first morning.
>
> When a woman looks older in her clothes, the fault lies with the shape and the fit. Never with her. You know the morning. You dressed with care, and the shop window still showed you a woman who looked dated.
>
> So let's start with the habit most women get wrong, because we were all taught to call it kind.

**The hook pulls about as hard as the published one** ("French women seem to grow more beautiful with every decade, and this morning your own closet is adding years to you instead. The reason is not their faces or their skin, it is seven habits, and the first of them is free.").
- **Better:**
  - "adding years you haven't lived" is sharper
  - the hand-off ("because we were all taught to call it kind") is the best reason-to-stay-for-item-one line in either script
- **Worse:**
  - "doing the opposite to you" is clumsy
  - "She simply knows what seven small habits do" bends the page's "what each piece does" into something that doesn't quite mean anything
  - "Most of them are free" lands without its hook (the published "the first of them is free" pulled her into item one)

### What works

- **The signposts now hold the answer.** Published examples: "Number two, they pay for the cut, not the styling"; "Number four, fit is planned and the plan is one fitted piece with one fluid piece"; "Number six. They take one thing off". New:
  - "Number one begins with a gesture you'll know."
  - "Number three is a question for you. When you open your closet, how many colors look back at you?"
  - "Habit number four is about size, or what we were told about it."
  - The published subhook "The French way is mostly taking away. Number six." is gone.
- **The rule labels are gone.** The published "The rule is... The result is..." came four times, plus "the rule I always give".
- **Feeling is the strongest in either pair:**
  - Being understood: "before you've even smiled, your hand goes to your cardigan to pull it closed"
  - Blame off her: "Neither one is your fault."
  - Permission: "You keep your loose blouse. You keep your wide trousers. Nothing has to go to the donation bag."; "Your stylist isn't the problem here."
  - Pride: "You become the woman in the red scarf"
  - The ending: "it will look like you".
- **A real persona turn:** "I used to think a heel was what made an outfit elegant, and I changed my mind. For years, flats felt frumpy to me. But elegance turned out to live in the walk, not the heel."
- **"You" rose from 27.5 to 50 per 1,000 words.**
- **The unsourced 56% walking figure is gone,** and the French phrase too.
- **Design lines dropped to two,** each one doing a job: the shoulder seam, and backstage care.
- **The ending pays off:** the last tip calls back to tip three ("That's where your color from habit three can live, on a day it lives nowhere else") and closes on "it will look like you". The comment question ties to the title and is about her life: "which woman in your own life grew more beautiful with age, and what did she wear?".
