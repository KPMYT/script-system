## 1. Verdict

The new script is clearly better than the original. It drops all of the original's banned openings and its 150-word CTA, and it is safer and more practical. **It would not pass the rules as written**, though. It has 97 curly quotes against the claim "mechanical 0", which fails D3. It also puts dollar figures on 11 of 15 items, uses wording lifted from two library examples, leaves trade terms unexplained, and pays the #7 promise weakly. Every one of these can be fixed in place (FIX-THEN-PASS, no HOLD). But at the claimed "round 3 of 3", a leftover finding means NOT READY.

## 3. Rule failures in the new script

1. **Curly quotes.**
   - The rules: humanizer step 4 says strip "curly quotes", and D3 says mechanical problems "must all be zero". The script has 97 ("didn’t", "would’ve"…), but the evidence says "mechanical 0".
   - The writer's other counts match the main session's scanner ("Most…" 1, reframes 0, filler 0). So the scanner was likely run on another version, or the counts were written by hand. Either way it was not run on the delivered text.
2. **Number load.**
   - The rule: "at most one price per item, and most items carry their cost in words… A list with a dollar figure on every item is too much to follow on one listen."
   - The script prices 11 of 15 items, and the audit lists "every mistake priced" as a strength.
   - The brief asks for "each with its dollar cost". No rule says which wins.
3. **More than one price on an item.**
   - Item 1's three prices are allowed by the three-prices device, but the hook says them again ("a couple hundred bucks… the thousands").
   - Item 2 has "tens of dollars a month" and "$4 to $10".
   - Item 4 has "$500 to $2,000" and "costs a few dollars".
4. **Wording taken from a structure-only line** (excerpts are for "shape and range, never wording").
   - New: "Its whole job is to corrode, so the steel tank doesn't."
   - S04 excerpt: "Its entire job is to corrode so your tank does not."
   - They share 7 words with one swap, under the 8-word guard. SKILL.md's Proof section uses the same line as its example.
5. **The comment line copies the pack's example.**
   - New: "Tell me how old your house is and which of these fifteen…"
   - SKILL.md: "Tell me how old your house is and which drain gives you the most grief."
   - They share 9 words, and SKILL.md isn't on the copy-guard list.
6. **Trade terms left unexplained** ("A trade term gets a short explanation the first time"):
   - "50 to 60 psi"
   - "dug up or relined"
   - "more if it needs jetting"
   - "leave the cheater bar in the toolbox"
   - "open a trap"
7. **The #7 promise is paid weakly.** "the one almost everybody gets backwards" never gets a named belief that is flipped. The item even concedes "it's worked before." Item 9 has the real myth ("Running hot water while you pour doesn't fix it."). This falls under B1 and C5.
8. **Gas steps against the price guide's §9.**
   - Missing: "don't light anything" (and appliances, doorbells, flashlights), "upwind", and "let the gas company… relight the pilots".
   - The rest is correct: out now, no switch, phone or car, 911 plus the gas company, don't assume someone called, some people can't smell it.
9. **Smaller departures.**
   - The priority flag sits at item 3, not "at the first answer".
   - The mid-CTA runs 63 words in three sentences, against "about 20-60 words: one sentence for the ask".
   - The hook's "most of them take a flashlight and five minutes to catch" overclaims (A4). The camera, the anode, the meter (half an hour or more), the gauge and gas don't fit.
10. **Audit evidence.**
    - "item one's answer at about 0:08" is accurate (word 19).
    - "its header at about 0:40" is wrong: it is word 125, about 0:50, past "item one by about 30 seconds".
    - "3,000 words, about 20 minutes" is accurate; it is about 19 minutes at the channel's pace.
    - "6/6 originality" is inflated, and about 4/6 is fair (still a pass). Signal 1's examples come from the brief or are common (the original has the frost-free point). Signal 2's "every mistake priced" breaks the money rule.
    - "reframes 1" misses "doesn't fix it. It just carries the grease…".
    - The Numbers line leaves out "a few dollars".

## 4. Weaknesses that aren't rule breaks

- **Item 1's money sentence** puts six numbers in one breath, with "$300" twice back to back.
- **The first sentence reads as written:** its subject runs 13 words before the verb ("didn't get that way overnight").
- **Item 5's anode job is heavy for a 50+ viewer** ("a big socket and some muscle") and has no stop line. The original's "paying a plumber for an hour still beats buying a whole tank" was safer. There is also no pilot relight after "turn the gas control to off".
- **Likely comment disputes:**
  - Item 4 assumes a frost-free faucet; plain hose bibbs freeze even with the hose off.
  - Item 3 drops outdoor and street-box shutoffs, which slab houses use.
  - "a tank that leaks from the bottom… can't be fixed" will draw "it was the drain valve".
- **Echoes:** "a cheap part" three times; "costs nothing", "for free" and "is free" four times between them; three bridges opening "The next one".
- **Items 12 to 14 read like checklists,** with no picture-phrase. The original's images (wax in a straw, redline) were warmer.
- **Good content dropped from the original:** "same month last year" for the water bill, and the unpermitted work that surfaces at resale.

## 5. Recommended changes

**(a) Library and price-guide fixes**

1. Have the output step straighten quotes, or have the scanner normalise them. Require the evidence to quote the scanner's FIX ON SIGHT total from the final text.
2. Say whether the brief or the money rule wins when a brief asks for "each with its dollar cost". Change the ideation brief spec so it stops asking for a price on every item.
3. Make the cap a number: "dollar figures on at most about half the items". Say whether in-words money counts toward "one price per item".
4. Say that the three-prices device uses up the item's price, and that the hook may not say the same money again.
5. Replace the S04 anode line in SKILL.md Proof with an invented example. Put SKILL.md's example lines (the comment line in particular) under the copy guard, or mark them "shape only".
6. Price guide:
   - Point split frost-free faucets to the sillcock row.
   - Say the foam cover as "under $15", not "a few dollars".
   - Flag brief prices that sit outside the guide, such as the $5,000 to $20,000 "relined".
7. Safety: a gas-leak passage uses §9 in full, including "don't light anything". A water-heater job that needs force gets a stop line, and a gas heater's pilot gets relit per the label.
8. Promise devices: a myth flag names the belief and flips it in the item's first two lines.
9. "Before handing over": item one's header by about 45 seconds (about 110 words). The evidence should compute times from word counts.
10. SKILL.md "Skill level": list terms that are always explained the first time (psi, trap, jetting, relining, cheater bar).

**(b) Keep as is**

- The opening shape: answer by 0:08, the silence villain, tenure once, three named stakes, no intro.
- The 63-word mid-CTA with real pages and the contractor sheet, then the return line.
- The one-line end CTA and the comment line last (P5).
- The price guide and P7: every figure traced, with varied range shapes ("and that's if it's clean water", "a stubborn one costs more").
- Safety placed inside each step: the drain-cleaner splash-back, breaker off before panels, the CPSC numbers.
- Fairness lines: "That's being careful with money, and there's nothing wrong with that."; "Sometimes the low price is the smart move."
- The links between items (roots from number one, toilets from number two).
- The banned list, which removed every problem the original had.
