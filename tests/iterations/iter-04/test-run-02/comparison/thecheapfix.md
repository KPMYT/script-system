## (a) What TheCheapFix does that our script lacks, ranked by likely impact

1. **A payoff line closes every item.** Our script ends 11 of 15 items on an instruction. TCF closes 60-90% on a win ("You just did it for cents."). This is the biggest emotional gap: ours builds fear and rarely releases it in one line.
2. **Bridges and stake headers.** Ours has 9 hard cuts in 14 joins and a 5.7-minute stretch (#7 to #12) with no forward line, past our own five-minute rule. S17 bridges almost every item, and S18 puts the stake in the header.
3. **Sensory and disgust scenes open each item.** Disgust runs about 6.7 per 1k against 18.8. Our items open on mechanism ("Inside every tank water heater, a long metal rod hangs down from the top."). TCF's open on what the viewer sees or smells.
4. **The viewer's failed attempt, named and explained.** "this is why half the people who try this swear it doesn't work". Ours does this well in #1 and #7 only.
5. **A through-line, closed at the end.** S17's film and S18's "living layer". Our thesis ("small problems into big bills") is never closed.
6. **The shelf as a priced villain inside items.** "Disposal pods cost $6 to $10 a pack, and mask it for a few days". Our #7 and #8 have the target but not the price-and-limit line.
7. **A promised honesty beat.** "number 12 is one we got wrong ourselves" and "I'm going to be fair to it" are reasons to stay. We have honest limits but never promise one.
8. **A countdown in a long list.** "Five left". Our 15 items have no halfway marker.
9. **Shorter items.** About 62 seconds against our 71-81.

## (c) Suggestions for our plumbing script rules

These are one line each, with a quoted example of the move (never wording to copy). Any that touch a locked decision go to `PROPOSALS.md`, not straight into the rules.

1. **Close each item on a one-line win.** Make the item's one allowed price the closing contrast between the trade's or the damage's cost and what the viewer just did; this moves the price rather than adding one. Example: "You just did it for cents." (S05 3:34).
2. **Open each item on a scene the viewer can check at home** (sight, smell or sound) before the mechanism. Example: "If you have ever opened your front-loading washer and noticed a black, slimy, musty-smelling ring" (S05 7:00); ours at its best: "A toilet that hisses, trickles, or refills itself in the middle of the night".
3. **Put the item's stake in its header line,** after the number, not only the mistake's name. Example: "Number three, the clog that shuts your whole system down in July." (S18 2:35).
4. **No more than two hard cuts in a row:** every third join at least is a bridge that names the next item's value or asks the question it answers. Example: "If that is what grows in a slot nobody uses, what is growing in the pipe you use every single day?" (S17 2:29).
5. **One disgust picture in each item whose topic has one,** in proportion (it is the item's one picture-phrase). Example: "A solid mat of hair, conditioner residue, and soap scum jammed into the trap." (S05 3:41).
6. **Name the viewer's likely failed attempt, and why it failed, in at least three items of a list.** Example: "this is why half the people who try this swear it doesn't work" (S18 0:32); ours: "the same clog coming back every few months no matter how many times it gets cleared".
7. **Where an item replaces a product, give the shelf one price-and-limit line** (the product, not its maker's motive). Example: "Disposal pods cost $6 to $10 a pack, and mask it for a few days" (S17 10:23).
8. **For "Costing You" titles, say the loss in the present tense in at least three items.** Example: "what a dirty system costs you every single month it runs" (S18 4:02); ours: "That can add tens of dollars a month to the bill."
9. **In lists of 10 or more items, give a countdown line straight after the mid-video ask or near the halfway point.** Example: "Five left, and the last one is the correction." (S17 10:02).
10. **Before the end CTA, one or two sentences close the opening's thesis as a stake, never listing the items** (no recap). This changes the P5 ending order, so it goes to `PROPOSALS.md` first. Example: "Your plumbing does not fail all at once. It closes slowly, one layer at a time" (S17 16:14).
