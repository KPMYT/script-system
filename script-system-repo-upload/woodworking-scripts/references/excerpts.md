# Excerpt library: how the winners say each kind of thing

Short lines from winning BeardedViking (BV) and WhosTheVoss (Voss) scripts, grouped by the job the line does. Each one shows its source as channel · video · timestamp, and the video's views against its channel normal.

**How to use this file:** to learn the range of shapes each kind of line can take, never to copy wording. Every group deliberately holds several different shapes. Pick a different shape from the last script on the same channel, and write it fresh in the persona's voice.

**Transfer notes:**
- **✓ shape works as-is**
- **✎ rewrite needed** (usually a first-person claim turned into an instruction or an opinion)
- **✗ doesn't transfer** (it only works because a real person built the thing on camera)

## 4. Step lines

**Short (a few words):**
- "Flip them around." [BV · sell fast · 8:14]
- "Blow off all that dust." [BV · sell fast · 8:14]
- "45°." [BV · hotcakes · 4:10]

✓ These sit between longer lines and keep the rhythm uneven.

**Medium:**
- "With our miter saw set to 90°, now it's time to make our 18 cuts." [BV · wife · 1:01]
- "Work your way through the grit, starting with 80 grit, working your way to 120, and then ultimately finish at 220." [BV · wife · 3:36]
- "Add glue to the end of your 16-in piece, plop it into place, and screw it together, repeating all the way down the line." [Voss · I still don't understand · 2:36]

✓ All three are instructions with a visible object and action.

**Long or tricky:**
- "My first cut is at 8 in, so I'm going to add 5 in onto my measurement to take into account the distance from the base of my saw to my blade." [Voss · I still don't understand · 0:30]
  ✎ Make it an instruction: "Add five inches to every mark to allow for the distance from the edge of the saw's base to the blade."
- "Where I just made that mark, I take my speed square and put it up against there, but I'm not drawing a line, because that means I'm going to have to erase a line later on." [Voss · I still don't understand · 2:04]
  ✎ Keep the reason ("so there's no line to erase later") and drop the "I" and "where I just made."
- "I'll grab a squeeze clamp, and I'm going to be setting myself up with a stop block right at that 5 and 3/4-in mark we made." [BV · Truck · 1:35]
  ✎ "Clamp a stop block at the five and three-quarter mark."

**Batching (collapsing repeats):**
- "Now, just rinse and repeat for all five of those pieces." [BV · student loan · 6:04]
- "Then you're going to flip your project over and repeat for this other side." [Voss · I still don't understand · 3:07]
- "Now, instead of measuring and marking every single one of these like we did this first one, we can just use this as a template." [Voss · This ONE Project · 6:01]

✓ The template shortcut doubles as a tip.

## 5. Tips and safety inside the step

1. "Anytime that you're using a stop block setup, you want to make sure to either let that blade stop completely before releasing the saw or just hold down that right side. That way, you avoid the kickback factor." [BV · Truck · 1:35 · 39.4x]
   ✓ The safety line lives in the step, with the reason.
2. "Don't forget to blow off all that excess dust. You don't want this dust to interfere with the finish." [BV · wife · 4:06 · 12.7x]
   ✓ A quality tip with the reason.
3. "Now, when you're using spray paint, you definitely want to make sure to wear a mask." [BV · 2 week Vacation · 7:45 · 9.2x]
   ✓ One line, at the moment it applies.
4. "A little painter's tape on the open side of your saw blade really cuts down the amount of sawdust it's kicking back at you." [Voss · This ONE Project · 1:00 · 10.0x]
   ✓ A practical trick.
5. "See, my fingers are at least 3 and 1/2 in away from that blade." [BV · 32 products · 2:08 · 7.1x]
   ✗ It points at footage of his own hands. The instruction survives: "Keep your fingers at least three and a half inches from the blade."

## 7. Money

**Listing proof:**
- "If you look online, most of these go for a range of $25 per pumpkin." [BV · Truck · 9:21 · 39.4x]
  ✎ Keep the range shape, and word it as "listed at" unless there's real sales evidence.
- "I see these online and on Etsy going anywhere from a range of $48 upwards of 70 bucks." [BV · 2 week Vacation · 9:51 · 9.2x]
  ✎ Same fix: "listed between $48 and $70 on Etsy."
- "But I want you to look at what these Etsy sellers are posting their items for and selling them for." [BV · wife · 9:14 · 12.7x]
  ✓ The cue for the listing screenshot.

**The narrator's price:**
- "As far as mine goes, I'll wind up posting mine on my Etsy for about 40." [BV · 2 week Vacation · 10:22]
  ✎ Make it an opinion with no shop claim: "I'd list them around $40."

**Cost:**
- "With the screws, the stain, and some paint, we're going to bring that total to $14.12 spent." [BV · wife · 8:44]
  ✓ Consumables added in.
- "Bringing our grand total to $7.90 in on this project." [BV · student loan · 12:13]
  ✓
- "Cost was $18.31 to build the nice one with the scorch paint and the Mod Podge, the finishes, everything." [Voss · ZERO CUTS · 10:45]
  ✓ An itemized total.
- "This thing cost me right around $13 and some change, which is about the same exact cost of a Quarter Pounder meal from McDonald's." [BV · So many orders · 0:31]
  ✎ The cost compared to an everyday purchase works. Drop "cost me": "about thirteen bucks, roughly a fast-food meal."

**What's left, and scale:**
- "32 time 30, that's $960." [BV · 32 products · 5:14]
  ✎ Make it conditional: "if all 32 sell at $30."
- "That doesn't cover the cost of sitting at the craft booth or getting the craft booth or listing the product." [Voss · ZERO CUTS · 11:47]
  ✓ Honest about the costs the simple math leaves out.

## 8. The doubter

1. "…if you jump in the comments saying, 'No one's going to buy that for $6.' If you don't believe in your product, nobody will believe in it, because you're the one that has to sell it to them." [Voss · This ONE Project · 10:05 · 10.0x]
   ✓ The strongest shape: voice the doubt, then turn it.
2. "Well, Daniel, don't forget about the cost of your drill bits or your drill and your sanders and all that." [BV · Truck · 9:52 · 39.4x]
   ✓ A named imaginary doubter raising a fair cost. Whether to name one is a persona-profile decision.
3. "Nobody's going to buy them for that price. Just save you guys the comment." [Voss · Sawdust · 7:40 · 11.5x]
   ✓ Concede a limit before the comments do.
4. "Before you hit the comment section, I do not have a build video on how to make this … cart thing because I don't need to." [Voss · I still don't understand · 7:12 · 8.3x]
   ✓ **Shape:** pre-empt the obvious comment.
5. "And I say, 'Well, to some this might seem overpriced, but to those who value a personal, handmade, locally built piece of artwork, the price is perfect.'" [Voss · I've never had trouble selling these · 12:48]
   ✎ The customer exchange is his own story. The answer itself transfers as an opinion.
