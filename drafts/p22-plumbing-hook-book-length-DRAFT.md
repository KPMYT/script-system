## 1. Roger's page: the new wording

### The opening (replaces the whole section)

> ## The opening
> The first sentence makes the point: the title's subject and the problem in their house. Then, in the order that suits the title (the model shows one way):
> - **The scene and the mistake:** the problem in their house and what people try first (the jug, the toilet plunger, the kettle). A failed attempt is the strongest feeling these viewers bring.
> - **The villain:** the trade's price or its silence, said as plain fact.
> - **The count:** how many tips, steps or uses the video gives them. Then number each one in the body ("Number four", "Step two").
> - **One reason to stay to the end:** what the last tip does for them, without saying what it is. The last tip must pay it. Never "stick around", "stay to the end" or "bonus".
>
> Start the first real tip **by about 45 seconds**. No tool list (each tool is named once, in the step that uses it), no channel intro, no "today we're going to", no run-down of the other tips. **No plumber in the opening:** no line about where the job stops being theirs, and no "no plumber needed" if a later step names a danger call.
>
> **Hold the reveal.** When the title holds something back ("the secret one-dollar powder", "these 3 powders", "1 cup of this", "the one mistake"), keep calling it what the title calls it ("the powder", "this one cheap powder") until its reveal in the body. Never name it in the opening, and in a list never name even one of them early: each is named only at its own number. Build the curiosity instead: what it does, what it costs them today, that it's cheap and already on a shelf. What the title itself names (the grease, the toilet ring) can be said.
>
> **After the reveal, open the next loop at once.** In a video about one product, the moment it's named, give them the next reason to stay: how many ways they'll use it, and what the last of those does for them. Write it fresh each time, in his voice.

### Hard rule 1, length (replaces it)

> 1. **Length:** at least 2,380 spoken words (14 minutes at his 170 a minute). Fourteen is the floor, never the target. Build out every point in the brief fully and stop when the brief is spent, not when 14 minutes is reached. A thin brief lands about 14 to 16 minutes, a full one 18 to 25 (up to about 4,250 words). Never pad. If the brief can't reach 14 minutes, say so and ask for more points.

### Hard rule 2, the book (replaces it)

> 2. **The book, once, short and general:** two or three sentences, about 25 to 45 words, in his own words, right after the step the book goes deeper on (wherever that fits best, never in the opening and never in the last step). Say that he goes into more of it in the book, name "One Hundred Old-House Fixes That Save You Thousands" ("the book" or "my book"), say who or what it's for in one or two plain benefits, and that it's at the link in the description and the QR code on screen. He may add that he won't bring it up again. Then back into the video.
>    - **Never:** a chapter, fix or page number, a price, "free", "e-book", or this video's steps put in the book's mouth. The book is never mentioned again in the video.
>    - True facts to draw on if one is wanted: 100 fixes for older houses; each gives his first check, the cheap fix and the money saved; the checklists and the seasonal calendar.

### Hard rule 3, the ending (replaces it)

> 3. **The ending,** in this order, then stop: the last tip, with one small thing to do tonight; then a quick, easy comment ask, one short question anyone can answer in a few words, worded fresh each time; and "I read every one." as the last line. No book, recap, subscribe ask, sign-off or next video.

### Hard rule 4, safety (the call lines change; the rest stays)

> 4. **Safety** (the brief wins where it says otherwise), one short line inside its step, with the reason:
>    - hot tap water, never boiling, for drains and plastic pipe; washing soda dissolved first
>    - one cleaner at a time: never bleach with ammonia, vinegar or any acid; never peroxide with vinegar
>    - the chemical check comes before any plunger or snake: a drain with chemical cleaner in it gets nothing added, plunging included
>    - before any water-heater job, the power or the gas goes off
>    - **A call only for real danger,** said once, inside its step, with its trigger:
>      - a gas leak: every step (everyone out, no switches, flames or car, 911 and the gas company from well away, back in only when they say)
>      - live wiring
>      - sewage backing up, the main line: two or more drains backing up at once. When nothing goes down, that test comes first: if it's the main, stop running water and call.
>    - **Everything else is theirs.** Where a job is harder, show them how. Never end a step on "call a plumber". A part that won't move gets the gentle way and a "don't force it", not a phone call.

### The check list after writing (the matching lines change)

> Read it aloud once, run the checking program (the plumbing pack says how), then check by eye:
> - the point first, the count, one reason to stay that the last tip pays, and the first tip by about 45 seconds
> - nothing the title holds back named before its reveal, and the next loop opened right after it
> - no plumber in the opening, and a call only for gas, live wiring or the main line
> - the chemical check before the plunger, and the main-line test first when nothing goes down
> - the book: once, short, general, no numbers
> - the ending: tonight's one thing, the quick comment ask, "I read every one."
> - the "never" list, and no echo of the model
>
> Fix only what fails; never add a line to meet a count.

Unchanged: who he is, the feeling, the flow, money, the voice rules, the "never" list (rule 6 keeps its stock-line bans).

## 3. `plumbing-scripts/check.py` (the checking program)

- **Length:** fail under 14 minutes at the channel's speed (today 12). Report the minutes; nothing fails for being long.
- **Opening:** fail on a call line (a plumber, "that's a call", "pick up the phone") in the first 45 seconds of speech. Report whether a count is said in the opening. The ban on "stick around", "stay to the end", "bonus" and "stay tuned" stays.
- **Book line** (Roger, Mark): fail if the book is pitched more than once, if the book paragraph is over about 60 words, or if it says a page, chapter or fix number. The "about a third" and "after item three" reports go.
- **Ending:** the end-of-video book reminder is no longer required, and fails if there is one. The last line is still "I read every one."
- **Werner:** exactly one marker block, not two.
- **Calls:** report every call line in the body with its sentence, so a needless one is easy to see; fail any that isn't about gas, wiring, sewage or the main line.
