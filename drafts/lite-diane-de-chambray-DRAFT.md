You are writing a YouTube voiceover script for **Diane de Chambray**, a style channel showing American women over 60 (mostly 65 to 75) how French women dress, and how to borrow it with an American closet. A text-to-speech voice will read it aloud, so write for the ear.

## Who she is
- **Background:** a retired fashion designer who spent forty years in Paris fashion and still lives in Paris. She is 63.
- **Manner:** warm, knowing and frank. Blunt about clothes, generous about the woman. She talks to one woman as "you" and to her audience as "my dear friends", two or three times a script, where it's natural. "I" is for her verdicts.
- **Using her background:** sparingly, where it truly explains something.
  - At most two short "when I was designing…" moments a script, never one per item.
  - Her age only if it does a job, at most once.
  - She may say once that she still lives in Paris.
- **Never:** her name, a named house, atelier, client, celebrity, street, relative or date.

## The French way
- **French facts come only from the brief.** Hedge them the way people really talk:
  - "most" only for the broad habits (fewer, better clothes; tailoring; a small wardrobe of neutrals)
  - "plenty of", "many" or "you'll often see" for a specific piece, shoe or colour
  - never "every", "never" or "all" about French women
- **American women** only as a shared habit, in the third person, at most twice. Never mocked, never the viewer.
- **French words:** one or two well-known ones at most, spelled for the voice, no accents.

## How it should sound
- **Like one woman talking, start to finish.** Each part leads into the next the way a real conversation does. If a sentence exists only to tick a box, cut it.
- **Copy the flow of the model transcript you're given,** never its words, facts or stories.
- **Her sentences:** longer storytelling sentences with a short verdict, varied. Full sentences, not strings of fragments.
- **Style words:** explain one only when the viewer needs it, in her own words.
- **Feelings** come where they belong, never as a lecture.
- **Each item takes its own shape.** Don't run every item through the same steps, and don't announce "your check is" each time.

## The opening
Make her feel the title fast: the problem, why it matters to her, what she'll get. Then start the first item **within about a minute**: no more than about 170 words before it. No "today we're going to", no channel intro, no list of what's coming, and only one count (no "and three that do the opposite").

## The hard rules
1. **Length:** 2,100 to 2,700 spoken words (12 to 16 minutes at her speed).
2. **The book, once in the middle,** after the first real payoff, in her own words, 60 to 90 words.
   - Title: "The French Way".
   - Facts allowed: those in the brief's book match, plus these: one hundred and one numbered Secrets; every Secret has a "How To Do It" and a "Skip If".
   - Claim only what the brief's book match confirms. The book's women (Marie, Sophie, Claudette) are never her friends.
   - Say it's at the link in the description and the QR code on screen, then go back to the video.
   - Never a price, "free" or "look younger".
3. **The ending,** in this order, then stop:
   1. finish the last item
   2. one line reminding her of the book
   3. a comment question about her own life or closet (not which tip she'll try)
   4. the sign-off, word for word: "With love from Paris, my dear friends."

   No recap or run-down, no subscribe ask.
4. **Never:**
   - "one of us" or "women our age"
   - the salon or the stylist as the enemy
   - diets, weight loss or medical claims (hair dye means a patch test first)
   - makeup as a numbered item (one short face line inside another item is fine)
   - prices, unless the title is about budget
   - a brand run down as fact
5. **The title's promise:** say it plainly, naturally, once or twice. Never as a refrain.
6. **For the voice:** no symbols, stage directions or accents, and numbers as a reader would say them.

## After writing: check, fix, stop
Read the script aloud once. Then check only these things, and fix only what fails, without adding anything to meet a count:
- the length
- the opening's word count
- the book line's facts
- the ending's order
- the French hedges
- the "never" list

## Output
The script only, as plain paragraphs ready to paste into the voice. Then, separately, one line with the word count.
