# Style niche (women over 50): study plan (DRAFT)

**Status:** plan only, written 2026-10-28 for the operator's yes. No research has run. Nothing in the library changes until each step is approved.

**Channels:** Scarlet Sterling (the affluent American) and Diane de Chambray ("the French way"). Both face-persona channels on one shared topic pool (`style-ideation`, launched 2026-09-14).

**Scope of the study:** hair and fashion for women over 50 first. Makeup and skincare for mature women are scanned as adjacents (labelled `adjacent`) so the map is complete, but they get their own niche skill later, never mixed into the hair-and-fashion pack.

---

## 1. What carries over and what doesn't

**Carries over (the method and the machinery):**
- the order of work: own baseline, competitor discovery, outliers against each channel's own normal, a script and comment study by video type, comparison, diagnosis, then the pack
- the study rules: collect and describe only, never edit data to fit a conclusion, helpers in their own folders, a check round on every collection, a `log.md`
- the core script rules that are not about any niche: START and the input gate, Vidrush prose with no edit notes, every word written for the voice, the claims policy and offer rules, the scanner, the audit, `verify_output.py`, the copy guard, the model library's no-copy rules
- the lessons file from earlier niches (`studies/electrical/lessons-for-the-pack-2026-10-27.md`): every past problem is checked against the new pack

**Doesn't carry over (re-derived from this niche's winners and comments):**
- the villain (the trade, the upsell) and the stakes (money, damage, fire, shock)
- the emotions: here they look like self-image, being seen or invisible, looking "done" or "given up", confidence, belonging, aging gracefully (the study tests these, not assumes them)
- the item shape, the opening by video type, money framing, proof, safety (here: no shame, no medical claims, no diet; already in the ideation skill's hard rules)
- the voice: a woman narrator; voice samples must come from women presenters
- length, CTAs and the ending: tested against this niche's winners, not copied from plumbing

## 2. Steps the earlier niches had that the brief didn't mention

1. **Our own baseline first.** Every niche started with our own uploads: views against the channel's normal, our winners against our flops, the old scripts saved (Frederick's 23, Werner's 14). Both style channels have published since September, so this comes first.
2. **Normal, not raw views.** A video is judged by views divided by its channel's median (15 most recent long-form uploads, 5:00 or longer, at least 7 days old).
3. **Video types before models.** The pack needs 3 or more model scripts per video type, so the discovery sorts the winners into types.
4. **Real versus faceless is a rule, not just a filter.** For HVAC the operator said "we dont want any ai or faceless for reference". Here the faceless AI clone channels (like "S1" in the September intake) are the closest format peers. Question 2 below.
5. **Recency and a views floor.** Electrical used videos from 2022 on and 1M+ views on real channels, 300K+ on faceless.
6. **A facts guide.** Plumbing and HVAC had a price guide, electrical a facts guide. Style probably needs one: the documented French practices (Diane's truth doctrine), what's true about gray hair, fabrics and fits, product claims kept cosmetic. Prices only where clothing or salon costs come up.
7. **Comments as their own analysis** (what the viewers bring, rough counts, "sounds like AI" remarks).
8. **The audience tab and retention.** If our two channels are connected to NexLev, their demographics and retention curves show where viewers leave. Earlier niches didn't use this.
9. **A diagnosis draft before building:** what the new pack keeps, adapts and adds from an existing pack, section by section, with numbered questions.

## 3. The plan, phase by phase

### Phase 0: settle the questions (section 4)

### Phase 1: our own baseline (both channels)
- Every upload with views, date, length; each channel's normal; ratio per video.
- Our top 5 and bottom 5 per channel: transcript, comments, notes.
- Faceless or AI check on our own channels (how YouTube and viewers see them; any "AI" comments).
- Demographics and retention, if the channels are connected.
- Our existing scripts, if the operator points to them (Notion or Drive, read-only).
- Output: `studies/style/own-baseline-2026-10/`.

### Phase 2: the scoping scrape (find the channels)
Sources, all NexLev (none count against the 200-search web cap):
- the niche finder with many meaning-based searches: fashion over 50 / 60, style for older women, look younger, classy and elegant, old money and quiet luxury for mature women, capsule wardrobe over 50, Parisian and French chic over 50, gray hair, haircuts for women over 60, thin and fine hair over 50, hairstyles for mature women; makeup and skincare over 50 as adjacents
- similar channels to our two channels and to the seeds already in the ideation skill (Dominique Sachse, Nikol Johnson, Fabulous50s, the S1 clone channel)
- YouTube search on the proven title shapes ("hairstyles that make you look older", "mistakes that age you", "how French women over 60 dress")
- the faceless outlier and viral-small-channel searches, for new AI and faceless entrants

For every channel found, one row:
- name, handle, country and language
- subscribers, total and monthly views, the normal, upload rate, last upload, channel age
- **type:** real person on camera / faceless narrated / AI avatar / AI clone of a real presenter (NexLev check plus our reading, marked as a reading)
- lane: fashion / hair / both / makeup (adjacent) / skincare (adjacent) / French lens / lifestyle
- top 3 videos with views and ratio
- estimated revenue and RPM (NexLev estimates, marked as such)

Filters: active in the last 12 months; English-language unless question 4 says otherwise. Target: screen 150+ channels, keep every one that qualifies.

Output: a master table (CSV and a readable page), split into real people and faceless, each ranked, plus a short "who is winning" summary.

### Phase 3: the outlier pool and video types
- Outlier videos per kept channel (2022 on, ratio and views floors).
- Sort them into video types (for example: mistakes that age you, haircut lists, colors that flatter, outfit formulas, capsule wardrobes, look expensive, French rules, gray hair, shoes and accessories, "what women over 70 are wearing").
- Map the types to the ideation skill's formats so the scripts can deliver every title it makes.
- Output: an outlier table and a type map for the operator to pick from.

### Phase 4: the script and comment study (the HVAC method)
- About 35-45 videos chosen by type, on the operator's yes to the list.
- For each: meta, full transcript, about 60-80 comments (never usernames), and a notes file.
- Style-specific note sections: the feelings voiced (quoted with timestamps), reassurance and "no shame" lines, the authority line (who she is), how the visuals are described in words, products and brands named, prices, the French lens devices, age tags in speech, what's promised and when it's paid.
- A check round by a second helper against fresh pulls.
- Output: `studies/style/` (data, notes, INDEX, combined transcripts, log).

### Phase 5: analysis
Separate helpers, one file each:
- the viewers (comments)
- openings and numbers (words a minute, "you" and "I" rates, hook length, first value)
- body, emotion and CTAs
- models and voice (model picks by type, women's voice-sample candidates)
- our scripts against the winners: the diagnosis of what's wrong with ours today

### Phase 6: conclusions
Main findings and numbered suggestions with quoted evidence, for "1 yes, 2 no" answers.

### Phase 7: on the operator's call, the build
- a diagnosis draft first (what the style pack keeps or changes from an existing pack)
- then the hair-and-fashion pack, two persona profiles, the facts guide, the model library, excerpts, voice samples, the scanner block, tests, and the two rows in `core/channels.md`
- then test runs with the writer, the VA and the judge kept apart
