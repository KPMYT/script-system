# Reference scripts: complete winning scripts, labelled

Seven complete scripts from the real, human-voiced channels in the plumbing study (Got2Learn, Roger Wakefield, Silver Cymbal and Arrow Pest Service), one for each of seven video types in `SKILL.md`. Each is verbatim and labelled with this pack's skeleton, so the writer can see the structure and pacing of a script that worked from its first line to its last. Our own hits and TheCheapFix (AI-voiced) are left out on purpose: this file shows what real channels do.

**How to use this file:** read it at the planning step, for structure and pacing only: where the point lands, how the promise block is built, how long the items run, where the CTAs sit and how the video ends. **Never copy wording.** Every script here was spoken by a real person, most of them over their own footage, so many lines can't transfer as they are (see each "Doesn't transfer" list). None of them follows all of our rules either: several hold the point back, tease, or end on a recap or a plain subscribe ask. Where a script breaks one of our rules, the label says so, and our rule wins. The scanner's copy guard flags any run of 8 or more words shared with this file.

**Labels** (from the opening jobs, item shapes and bans in `SKILL.md`). Each label sits on its own line above the paragraph where its passage starts, and covers everything up to the next label. Two or more labels on one line mean the passage does each of them, in that order. Labels follow the order things happen, so they repeat and come out of skeleton order. Each paragraph starts with its timestamp: the start of the caption line it begins in, so it can run a second or two early.

- [POINT]: the subject, and the answer or product, said plainly; also the main answer or verdict when it comes later.
- [QUESTION]: a question put to the viewer and answered soon after.
- [VILLAIN]: whoever profits or misleads: the trade's price or silence, a seller, a maker, a shelf product.
- [SCOPE]: honest scope or reassurance: what it won't do, who it's for, when there's no need to act, why it's safe.
- [PROMISE]: a forward promise of later value, or a count of what's coming.
- [ITEM] (1), (2) and on: a numbered tip, fix, cause, step, factor or use. "(2, cont.)" marks an item picked up again after an interruption.
- [MECHANISM]: why it works or why it fails, in plain terms.
- [CHECK]: something the viewer can look at or test to know where they stand.
- [MONEY]: a price, a cost comparison or payback arithmetic.
- [SAFETY]: a risk and how to avoid it.
- [CALL A PRO]: when to hire someone, or what the trade does or charges for the job.
- [FORWARD]: a mid-video line that names the next value, or a callback that pays an earlier promise.
- [ASIDE]: a joke, story, history or digression that the plan doesn't need.
- [CTA]: a like, subscribe, link, other-video, website or sponsor ask.
- [END]: the line that closes the content.
- [COMMENT]: an ask for comments or questions.
- [CLOSE]: the sign-off.
- **Banned by our rules, labelled where they occur:** [INTRO: banned] (a greeting, a name or channel intro, "today we're"); [TEASE: banned] (a vague tease, "stay till the end", a product held back, a tease of future videos); [RECAP: banned] (a summary at the end); [POINTING: banned] ("as you can see", "like this", "right here", "look at", or any line that only makes sense with the footage); [FIRST-PERSON DEMO: banned] (first-person tests and demonstrations, on-camera work, and first-person history or customer stories used as proof).

**Transcript handling:** the words are as captioned, with the captions' mistakes kept. Curly quotes are straightened and sound tags ([clears throat], [laughter], [snorts]) are dropped; ">>" is the captions' mark for a change of speaker. Got2Learn's two auto-captioned scripts (entries 2 and 3) have no punctuation and are left that way. Nothing is shortened: no sponsor read here is long enough to cut.

**Each entry has:** the title; the channel, views against the channel normal (as of 2026-10-20), length, spoken words and video type; one paragraph on why it works; the labelled script; the unit plan it implies (`unit-plan-template.md`, filled from the script, so "the brief" there means what the script itself gives); and what doesn't transfer.

**Selection:** for each type, the human-channel model that the type table in `SKILL.md` names. The pest-control script (entry 7) is the parallel-niche model for the single-product fix. Views/normal compare a video with its own channel only. Got2Learn's three are all-time hits from 2018-2021, so their ratios run far higher than the others'. Sources: `studies/plumbing/data/competitors/<channel>/<videoId>/`, the per-video notes in `studies/plumbing/notes/competitors/`, and `studies/plumbing/comparison/10-opener-teardowns.md` (cards 22, 27, 29, 30, 31 and 35) and `11-reasons-to-stay.md`.
