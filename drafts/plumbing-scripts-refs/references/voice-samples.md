# Voice samples: plumbing

The voice anchor for every plumbing script. These are three stretches of real human narration from winning videos in the plumbing and home-repair study, copied verbatim from transcripts. Only obvious caption errors are fixed, and stage noises are removed. They come only from the real channels (Got2Learn, Silver Cymbal, Roger Wakefield, Arrow Pest Service), never from AI-written uploads. Got2Learn is a faceless voice-over over diagrams, the closest format to ours; Silver Cymbal and Roger Wakefield talk to camera, so take their sentence shapes and leave the camera behind. Sample 2 is about a windshield, not plumbing: it's here for how it talks a viewer through amounts and steps, not for what it says. Test every sentence of a draft against these (core `voice-anchoring.md`). The copy guard applies: never 8 or more words in a row from this file.

**Borrow:** how the sentences are built. That means getting to the point in the first line, "you" for every step, amounts and prices dropped in mid-sentence ("about 6 oz of warm or hot water," "$10 to $20 a month on your gas bill"), the reason given in one plain line ("because they only heat water when you need it"), the honest limit said flat ("you don't really need to address these," "the only problem with them is"), and the short asides ("Nothing boiling.").

**Don't borrow:** the content, the facts, or anything that needs a presenter or a camera. In these samples that means:
- **Pointing at the picture:** "They sell dedicated boxes like this" (Got2Learn) and "the bottle I've got here is 70% concentration" (Silver Cymbal). Our narrator never points at the screen; name the thing instead.
- **The narrator's own video and demo:** "the p-trap I mentioned before and I already explained how it works" (Got2Learn) and "we are going to spread that across our dashboard" (Silver Cymbal, doing the job on camera). In our scripts every step is the viewer's: "you."
- **The trade's motives:** "here's the math that the salesman won't show you" (Roger Wakefield). Banned in these scripts; let the numbers make the point.
- **Hype:** "this is going to really supercharge this cleaning" (Silver Cymbal).
- **Single prices:** "probably around three grand these days" (Roger Wakefield). Our prices are ranges from the brief.
- **Personal life and customers:** none of the three stretches has them, but the videos around them do ("I've lived in my house 25 years," "I got called to a doctor's house one day," both Roger Wakefield). Those are the creators' lives; our narrator never claims a house, a history or a customer, and follows the claims policy.

## Sample 1: Got2Learn, "Here's Why 'S-TRAPS' Are NOT ALLOWED Anymore! (And 3 Ways To Fix It)" (3:10-4:39, 95.0x normal)

Now if you have s-traps on all of your fixtures in your home and you aren't having any problems, you don't really need to address these, but what if you always smell sewer gases, how do you go about fixing it? There's 3 possible ways, a typical p-trap vent, the AAV, the Loop Vent or the Combo vent. So first off, is the p-trap I mentioned before and I already explained how it works but the only thing is that during a renovation for example, these vents aren't always accessible and breaking all the walls is not really an option, which leads me to option number 2. The AAV. The AAV (Air Admittance Valve), which is also known as a Studor vent or Sure-Vent, is by far the easiest way of modifying an s-trap. The only problem with them is that, to be code, they need to be installed 6" above the flood level of the fixture for them to be considered a proper vent and most people don't follow this rule, so they don't pass inspection and some cities prohibit them because of this. They sell dedicated boxes like this to install them in so that you don't just have an AAV on your wall looking weird. Also, since it's a mechanical device, there's chances of it breaking in the open position which would let sewer gases in. However, some municipalities allow them, which is why it's important to inform yourself before planning anything.

## Sample 3: Roger Wakefield, "Don't Buy a New Water Heater Until You Watch This! Tank vs Tankless - The TRUTH" (0:52-2:12, 39.7x normal)

A tankless system for the same house, that's probably 7 to $8,000. Why is there a huge difference?
