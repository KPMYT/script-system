## Part 1. What a Sam script is, in one picture

It's a Hank or Dale script with a history thread running through it:

| Part | Hank / Dale | Sam |
|---|---|---|
| Title | a product that sells | the same product in an old-world style ("The VIKING Lamp That...") |
| Hook (first 15 seconds) | transformation, pain or wish, curiosity | the same three jobs. **The curiosity is the old-world twist** on a thing that already sells. |
| Materials, cut list, steps, finish | basic kit, instructions to "you" | the same, plus the old way as an option on some steps ("if you've got a drawknife, that's how they'd have done it") |
| Asides | opinion, a shop detail | **heritage beats:** short bits of Sam's fascination tied to the step in hand (why they shaped it like this, what it was for, what they'd have used) |
| Money block | listings, cost, fees, what's left | the same. The proof comes from the plain product's listings (see decision 2). |
| CTA and ending | from the profile | from Sam's profile: his book *The Old Ways*, a quiet "this deserves to survive" pitch |

**It stays direct and tutorial-first.** The history never opens the video, never becomes a lecture, and never delays the first material (the locked "first material within about 15 seconds" rule). It lives inside the build, at the pauses. That's where the "little more" in each script comes from.

**A made-up example of a heritage beat** (a shape only, not wording to reuse):
> "While that glue sets, here's the part that gets me. The Norse didn't have a lamp shop down the road. Light was precious, so the thing that held it got carved like it mattered. That's why we're putting the knotwork on the base, and not just because it looks good on Etsy."

## Part 2. Where the ask runs into locked rules: the decisions

Two parts of the ask collide with locked decisions (`HANDOFF.md` section 3). Per `CLAUDE.md` they're written up as proposals P8 and P9 in `PROPOSALS.md`, and nothing changes until the operator decides.

### Decision 1 (P8): how much the history has to be checked

**The locked rules:** "Facts are always verified", and stories count as facts (`core/research-and-sourcing.md` §2, §5). The ideation skill adds "cultural authenticity is a hard floor... no invented dates, 'banned' stories, or fake lineage" for Sam.

**What the operator asked for:** "less built and based on facts and clear sources."

| Option | What it means in a script | My view |
|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | The history is Sam's story and fascination: general, well-known things said plainly ("the Norse burned fish oil and tallow for light"), with a hedge on anything less sure ("the way it's usually told", "most likely"). **No research or sources needed for it.** But no specific dates, named people, numbers, "banned" or "scientists now say" claims unless the brief has them. | Gives the operator the looser feel without the script inventing things that commenters can catch. |
| B | Anything goes: the history may be made up when the script needs it. | Not recommended. About 40% of Dale's and Hank's comments are already "AI" or "fake" remarks (HANDOFF §4). Invented history gives them ammunition, and it breaks the claims policy's "nothing false stated as fact". |
| C | Keep it as now: every historical line verified in the research pack. | Safe, but it's what the operator asked to loosen. |

### Decision 2 (P9): proof when nobody sells the old-world version yet

**The locked rule:** "Proof is always researched online listings, worded honestly" (HANDOFF §3). The research stage flags a title claim it can't support back to ideation.

**What the operator asked for:** "the concept that a lamp works and sells. and we are making the assumption that the viking lamp would also sell the same way."

**Proposal:** the research pack proves the **plain product** (wooden lamps: three to five listings, price range, fees). If listings for the old-world version exist, they're used. If not:
- the narration says what's true: "Wooden lamps like this list between about $40 and $90 on Etsy" (the plain product's range), never "Viking lamps sell for..."
- Sam's price is his opinion inside the plain product's range, as now
- the old-world angle is sold as Sam's opinion ("and I'd argue a lamp with a story like this stands out in that crowd"), never as a sales fact
- a title claim about the old-world version (for example "This VIKING Lamp Sells for $90") counts as supported by the plain product's listings, so the research stage doesn't flag it back

The assumption lives in ideation and the research pack (a new HERITAGE block, Part 4), and the narration never states it as fact. Every other proof rule stays: "listed at" vs "sold for", no shop names, a range rather than one high price.

## Part 3. The woodworking pack: a new section (proposed text)

Goes into `woodworking-scripts/SKILL.md` after "Exaggerated titles". It sits in the pack, not Sam's profile, because Hank and Dale run heritage titles too (if decision 9 keeps them), and the rules mustn't be written twice. Sam's profile then says "every video is a heritage build". The parts marked **[P8]** and **[P9]** go in only on a yes to those decisions.

```markdown
## Heritage builds (old-world and foreign styles)

A heritage build is a build that sells, made in the style of another time or place: a Viking-style lamp, a Shaker-style peg rail, a Japanese-style box. The topic, the skeleton and the money are this lane's; the heritage adds a story thread. Every Sawdust Sam video is one. On other channels, a title with a heritage key makes it one.

- **The skeleton doesn't change.** Promise, materials within about 15 seconds, cut list, steps, finish, money block, end CTA. No history opening and no context paragraph (one sentence at most, as always). The history lives inside the build.
- **The hook's curiosity is the heritage:** the old-world twist on a thing the viewer can already picture selling. The transformation and the pain or wish stay this lane's.
- **Heritage beats.** Short asides of the narrator's fascination, each tied to the step, part or finish in hand: why they shaped it that way, what it was for, what they'd have used instead of the tool in your hand. One to four sentences each, at natural pauses (glue drying, between sections), never inside a tricky instruction. About one per section of the build; together roughly a tenth to a fifth of the script. They count toward the length because each one adds to the build; one that doesn't tie to the build is padding.
- **Style, never replica.** The build is a modern piece in the old style, from the viewer's basic kit. Say "Viking-style" or "the way the Norse shaped theirs", never that it copies a real museum piece unless the pack says so.
- **The old way is an option, never the main step.** The step gives the basic-kit way first; the old tool or method follows as an option where it fits ("If you've got a drawknife, that's how they'd have done it"). Old methods that are unsafe today (open flame inside wood, lead pigments, lye without protection) are never given as options.
- **[P8] What the history may say.** General, well-known history, said plainly, with a hedge on anything less sure ("the way it's usually told", "most likely"). It doesn't need a source. Never a specific date, named person, figure, "banned" story or "scientists now say" unless the brief or research pack carries it with its source. Never mock a culture or its people, and never claim to belong to one.
- **[P9] Proof for a heritage build.** The research pack proves the plain product (a wooden lamp), with the old-world version's own listings used where they exist. When they don't, the money block gives the plain product's listing range and says so honestly ("wooden lamps like this list between..."), the price is the narrator's opinion inside that range, and the old-world angle is opinion ("I'd argue the story makes this one stand out"), never "Viking lamps sell for...". A title claim about the heritage version is supported by the plain product's listings.
```

Two lines added to the pack's **Safety** list:

```markdown
- **Lamps and lights:** a listed lamp kit or LEDs inside wood, never an open flame. A candle holder keeps the flame in a metal or glass cup, clear of the wood.
- **Old-world finishes and pigments:** only today's safe versions (no lead pigments; lye, if used, with gloves and eye protection).
```

One line added to the pack's **scanner** block (heritage claims the history may never make, per P8 option A):

```scanner
ban-regex: \bscientists? (?:now )?(?:say|said|admit|baffled)\b | invented authority claim in heritage history
```
