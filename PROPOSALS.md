## P1. "The winners never introduce themselves" (opened 2026-10-18, iter-00 audit)

**Decision (HANDOFF.md section 3, from the study):** "No persona or channel intro. The winners never introduce themselves."

**Evidence:** the pack's own top reference opens with a self-description. BeardedViking's highest video (39.4x normal) starts: "I've been showing beginner woodworkers how to build simple projects and turn their hobby into extra income without using any kind of fancy tools or expensive equipment. Take a look around my shop…" (`woodworking-scripts/references/voice-samples.md`, sample 1; `woodworking-scripts/references/reference-scripts.md`, entry 1, "two sentences of credibility" before the first material at 0:17). Sample 2 (15.8x) opens "Today I'm going to show you how to build…". Neither one names the channel or says "welcome back".

**Proposal:** keep the rule; correct only the reason given for it. Suggested wording: "The winners never name themselves or the channel, and they reach the first material within about 15 seconds. When there's a credibility line at all, it's about the viewer's goal, not the presenter." Our personas couldn't use BV's credibility line anyway (claims policy: no checkable history).

**Status:** open, for the operator.

## P3. Concrete forward promises in the plumbing pack (opened 2026-10-20)

**Decision (HANDOFF.md section 3, FacelessOS rebuild):** "Rehooks: short-range bridges between segments are allowed and encouraged. Long-range promises are banned." (`core/retention-mechanics.md` §6: "A rehook points at the very next segment, never at 'the end of the video' or 'a bonus later.'")

**Evidence:** the operator's instruction (2026-10-20): "we want to be faster to the point of what we are saying but we want to very quickly give them more reason to stay so we promise more value. watch how competitors do this." In the plumbing study the winners promise named items further ahead than the next segment: TheCheapFix's hits promise a numbered item with its dollar figure in the first minute and pay it 3-8 minutes later (`studies/plumbing/comparison/11-reasons-to-stay.md`, device D1; its titles carry the same bracket, "#4 Saves $300"); winning openers stack a median of about 4 promised payoffs in the first 90 seconds (`10-opener-teardowns.md`, synthesis). The vague teases the core bans ("stay till the end", "bonus at the end") stay banned; the promises in question name the item and its stake.

**Proposal:** the plumbing pack overrides `retention-mechanics.md` §6 for one thing only: a **promise block** right after the first answer may name two or three later items with their concrete stake ("Number seven saves you a two-hundred-dollar call"), and later "next" lines may point further than the next segment, provided every promise names its item and stake, uses no tease phrase from the hard-ban list, and is paid inside the video (the first one within about five minutes). Vague long-range promises stay banned everywhere. The core rule is unchanged for every other niche.

**Status:** approved by the operator on 2026-10-20 ("1. yes"). Applies when the plumbing pack goes into the library; the core rule is unchanged for every other niche.

## P4. Tenure and first-person trials against the claims policy (opened 2026-10-21)

**Decision (HANDOFF.md section 3, FacelessOS rebuild, and `core/claims-policy.md`):** the claims policy sits above everything; packs and profiles may tighten it, never loosen it. Its "never allowed" list includes "years of experience or credentials" and "having personally made, sold, tested, built, lived or collected something".

**Evidence:** two later operator decisions for plumbing (HANDOFF section 3, "Operator decisions, 2026-10-20 (plumbing, after the study)") go past that list:
- **Fixed tenures:** Roger the Pipe Guy 45 years, Mark Silverbridge 40, The Vintage Pipefitter 50, stated in scripts, in the profile's `tenure:` line, with the scanner failing any other figure. The persona-profile template and the scanner already carry `tenure:`, but the claims policy still bans years of experience outright.
- **First-person trials:** "No first-person claims of tests, demonstrations or on-camera actions, unless the title or brief specifically calls for one." The claims policy bans "personally tested" with no such exception (its "Build narration" line does let a niche pack allow first-person demonstration).

The study supports the narrow form of both: a tenure stated the same way every time drew no complaints on Roger Wakefield, while contradicting tenures drew mockery on our channels (`studies/plumbing/comparison/03-own-vs-competitors.md` §8); claimed tests drew "Where is the footage of him doing the tests?" (`07-body-craft.md` §5).

**Proposal:** two narrow exceptions in `core/claims-policy.md`, each set by the layer that owns it:
1. **Years in the trade:** allowed only as the persona profile's fixed `tenure:` figure, worded the same way every time, and never with dates, employers, towns, licences or other credentials.
2. **A first-person trial:** allowed only where a niche pack permits it and the title or brief calls for one. Everything else on the "never" list stays.

**Status:** decided by the operator on 2026-10-21: "the years of experience can be from the title. we can mention in the video but not personally tested." Exception 1 approved and applied to `core/claims-policy.md` ("Fixed years in the trade"). Exception 2 not applied: "personally tested" stays on the never list. Confirmed 2026-10-21: "we dont in the video say I tested. but we say tests and results", so the plumbing exception for titles that call for a trial is gone too. Also 2026-10-21: "title numbers win" (a title's years figure may be used in the script); applied the same day to the claims policy and the scanner (`--title`), on every channel.

## P7. Where the plumbing brief is silent, the script fills in (opened 2026-10-21, first plumbing test)

**Decision (HANDOFF.md section 3 and session 4's "the brief is king"):** "a missing fact stops the run rather than being invented"; `plumbing-scripts/SKILL.md`, "Inputs": "If the brief is missing something the script needs (the trade's typical price, a dose, a wait time), the run stops with NOT READY and names it. It never invents the fact." Core `greenlight-audit.md` D4: a number that traces to nothing is a Hold. Core research rules: facts are always verified.

**Evidence:** the first plumbing test (`tests/iterations/iter-04/test-run-01/`): a 15-item mistakes brief with prices for 2 items was refused, and the chat asked for a price and a "right way" fact on all 15. The operator: "we need to have better briefs so but we dont have to extremely overload scirpt with numbers like the issue we had w hank remember. where the brief doesnt suppliment. the script should fill in here." The Hank tests found the same: too many numbers is hard to follow on one listen (iter-01, W3; core `humanizer.md` H22).

**Proposal (plumbing only), three changes:**
1. **"Inputs", the last bullet, becomes:** "Where the brief is silent, the script fills in. The method, the right setting, how often to check, and what a mistake leads to come from well-established, everyday plumbing knowledge, said plainly. Two limits: no dollar figure the brief doesn't give (say the cost in words instead: "that's a new water heater", "a flooded basement"), and no dose, mix or chemical method beyond the brief and the five safe defaults in "Safety". The run stops with NOT READY only when the brief can't carry the title's main promise."
2. **"Money framing" gains:** "Not every item needs a number. At most one price per item, and most items carry their cost in words. A list with a dollar figure on every item is too much to follow on one listen."
3. **A new row in "Where this pack overrides the core"** (D4): "A non-price number the script fills in (a setting such as 120 degrees, a healthy pressure range, 'once a year') is allowed when it's well-established general knowledge; the evidence block lists each one as 'general knowledge'. Every dollar figure still traces to the brief."

**Open question for the operator:** may the script also add typical price ranges from general knowledge when the brief gives none (option B), or are filled-in costs always said in words (option A, recommended: fewer numbers, and no out-of-date prices for viewers to dispute)?

**Status:** approved by the operator on 2026-10-21: "yes agree for all. and also the cost should always be estimated tho. never to the cents amount." Applied with one change from option A/B: a price the brief doesn't give comes from a researched price guide (`plumbing-scripts/references/price-guide.md`, each figure checked against two or more sources and re-checked), always said as an estimate, never to the cent.

## P9. Proof from the plain product for heritage builds (opened 2026-10-23)

**Decision (HANDOFF.md section 3, from the woodworking study):** "Proof is always researched online listings, worded honestly", and the research stage flags back any title claim it can't support (`core/research-and-sourcing.md` §10).

**Evidence:** the operator (2026-10-23): "we take for example the concept that a lamp works and sells. and we are making the assumption that the viking lamp would also sell the same way." Listings for an old-world version of a product often won't exist.

**Proposal (`drafts/sawdust-sam-DRAFT.md`, Part 2, decision 2):** for a heritage build, the research pack proves the plain product with its listings, and uses the heritage version's own listings where they exist. Where they don't, the narration gives the plain product's range honestly ("wooden lamps like this list between..."), the price is the narrator's opinion inside it, the heritage angle is opinion only, and a title claim about the heritage version counts as supported by the plain product's listings. The assumption is recorded in the pack's HERITAGE block, never stated as fact in narration.

**Status:** approved by the operator on 2026-10-23 ("p9 - yes"). Applied to `woodworking-scripts/SKILL.md` ("Heritage builds", and a pointer in "Proof"), the research pack template's HERITAGE block and `core/research-and-sourcing.md` §2.
