# Voice samples: HVAC

The voice anchor for every HVAC script. Three stretches of real human narration from HVAC winners in the HVAC study (`studies/hvac/`), copied verbatim from the transcripts; only punctuation is added where the auto-captions had none, and three obvious caption slips in sample 3 are fixed ("a vacuum" to "the vacuum" twice, "unclog" to "unclogged"). They come only from real people on camera, never from faceless or AI-voiced channels (operator, 2026-10-27: "we dont want any ai or faceless for reference"). Test every sentence of a draft against these (core `voice-anchoring.md`). The copy guard applies: never 8 or more words in a row from this file.

**Provisional.** The operator will listen and choose the voice samples ("we will decide this ourselves later", 2026-10-27). Until then these three stand. They were picked from the transcripts for a plain, practical tradesman's register; nobody has listened to them yet for age or accent.

**Borrow:** how the sentences are built. That means:
- the point in the first line, and "you" for every step
- the reason in one plain line (the dust the furnace pulls in settles on the rod)
- the cost said as a consequence ("that can cause you a service call")
- the honest odds said flat ("nine times out of ten it's okay")
- the honest limit ("sandpaper is not really recommended")
- the step ordered so the viewer can follow it with their hands busy

**Don't borrow:** the content, the facts, or anything that needs a presenter or a camera. In these samples that means:
- **Pointing at the picture:** "like it is here", "like I'm using here" (Silver Cymbal), "my flame sensor has some buildup on it" and "this front door right here" (Word of Advice TV). Our narrator never points at the screen; name the thing instead.
- **The narrator's own job:** "what I'm gonna show you", "I have a clog further up that pipe" (Silver Cymbal). In our scripts every step is the viewer's: "you".
- **A single bill or product named as the tool:** "a dollar bill or a $5 bill or a $10 bill" (Word of Advice TV) is a joke about the step; keep to the brief's method.
- **Tenure and trade history:** each man's own years ("I've been working on furnaces for 30 years", The Funny Carpenter) are his; Roger's are the profile's 45, said at most once.
- **Safety the sample skips:** none of the three proves the power is off with a tester. Our scripts always do (`SKILL.md`, "Safety").

## Sample 1: The Funny Carpenter, "5 Furnace Filter Mistakes That Will Cost You Money!" (0:44-1:47, 5.81x normal)

Mistake number four, forgetting to turn off the power before changing your filter. If the furnace is running when you're trying to change the filter, first of all, it's going to be really difficult to pull that out with all that pressure against the dirty filter and you can wind up tearing it into pieces and you go to put the new filter in, that's going to be difficult also. And if there is a piece of plastic or a piece of paper stuck in your return air all the way back to new construction. When you pull that furnace filter out, it's going to get sucked into the blower and that can cost you a lot of money. Now, when it comes to turning off the power to the furnace, there should be a switch nearby the furnace, otherwise you go to your breaker. But you don't want to turn off the power to the furnace if it's running. You need to give this furnace time to cool down. You should go to your thermostat, turn the temperature down, wait 60 seconds, then turn off the power. If you don't wait and you turn it off right away, the furnace hasn't had a chance to cool down, it's unsafe for the furnace and it will overheat and that can cause you a service call. Nine times out of 10 it's okay. Once in a while, it's going to cost you money.

## Sample 2: Word of Advice TV, "Furnace Not Working - The Most Common Fix" (3:22-4:27, 140.71x normal)

If you see, my flame sensor has some buildup on it. And this buildup just comes from the carbon inside of the flames and also the front door right here. See how it has all these grates? It's sucking air in for combustion for the flames. And you know, whatever particles are in the air, dust, and whatever other debris also settles on this flame sensor. And with time, when you have enough of a coating on here, it'll prevent the flame sensor from sensing any flame. So, to clean the flame sensor, you can either use a dollar bill or a $5 bill or a $10 bill, depending on how much you have in your pocket, but just grab it and clean it up real good. You can use some fine steel wool or what I like to use, this tends to work the best and last the longest, is a ScotchBrite pad, almost like a dishwashing scrubby. Just grab the flame sensor real firmly, press down hard, no matter what you're using. Um, sandpaper is not really recommended because it tends to be too abrasive. If you scratch this rod, then the stuff will stick to it even faster.

## Sample 3: Silver Cymbal, "How to UNCLOG your AC DRAIN Condensate line FAST - Water stains on Ceiling? or in the Pan" (0:30-1:49, 46.03x normal)

First thing we're gonna start with is a real easy fix. So go outside, find out where your drain line goes. You've probably seen it, and you'll see water dripping out like it is here, but the rate the water's coming out right now is kind of slow. So what that means is that I have a clog further up that pipe in the air conditioning system, and that's why it was leaking in the ceiling. So what I'm gonna show you is a real simple way to try to unplug it, and we're just gonna use a little shop vac. Now you want to use one that's rated for wet or dry, but most of them are, and this just happens to be a smaller cordless one. Connect the vacuum to the end of this pipe, but if you look close you're gonna see that the vacuum cleaner is much larger than the pipe. So a very simple, easy way to fix this is to just take a rag or some tissues like I'm using here, wrap them around the pipe, and what we're trying to do is just kind of close the gap with the vacuum. Now remember, this is not a plumbing system, so we're just trying to make it so that the pipe fits pretty snugly against those rags and we get as airtight of a seal as we can. So now we can go ahead and turn the vacuum cleaner on, and we're gonna suck anything out of that pipe, and you want to let the vacuum run for probably at least a minute. There's nothing you're gonna hurt by pulling this water out of the line, and in fact, if you have a big clog, what's gonna happen is when you disconnect the vacuum you may see a few more gallons come out of this pipe, and that's a good thing because that means you unclogged the pipe.
