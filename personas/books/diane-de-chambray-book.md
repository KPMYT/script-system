# Book index: The French Way

- **Channel:** Diane de Chambray. **Author:** Diane De Chambray (as printed in the running footer of the Secret title pages, "The French Way Diane De Chambray", with a capital D in "De"; the OCR of the title page reads "Diame De Chambray", an OCR slip).
- **Exact printed title** (used in CTA markers): The French Way. The title page adds the subtitle "101 Style Hair and Makeup Secrets / The French Use To Look 20 Years Younger" (two lines, no punctuation). See "Claims to keep out of CTAs" below before any part of the subtitle is spoken.
- **What's in it:** an introduction, ten teaching chapters and a conclusion (the contents numbers the conclusion as "Chapter 11"), and 101 numbered Secrets, one chapter of ten (eleven in the first) after another. Every Secret carries "How To Do It", "Skip If" and "Years Younger"; 56 of them also open with "The Dated Habit" and "The French Secret". Four frameworks run through it: the Capsule Rule, the Color Compass, the Proportion Equation and the Elegance Audit. Three recurring women, Marie, Sophie and Claudette, appear as examples.
- **Source:** OCR of the_french_way_ebook.pdf from the operator's Drive folder, 2026-10-29; image-only PDF, 452 pages, one OCR text file per PDF page.
- **Page numbers:** the pages below are PDF pages. The book does print page numbers (small, at the foot of the page; the OCR caught 70 of them, for example "10" on PDF page 13, "440" on PDF page 443 and "446" on PDF page 449). Printed page = PDF page minus 3 throughout. Quote the PDF page in this file; a reader holding the book will see a number three lower.
- **What this file holds:** the chapter page map, every Secret's title, page and chapter, the facts a CTA may state, the claims to keep out, and the book's own stories and characters (which never go in a script). **The full text is not stored here;** the OCR files are the record.
- **Use:** for the book match (which Secret and chapter cover a video's topic) and the facts a CTA may state. It supplies no facts, steps or numbers to the script itself.

## Facts a CTA may state

- The title, The French Way. An introduction, ten teaching chapters and a conclusion, the chapters named by topic.
- **101 Secrets, numbered 1 to 101.** Checked against the OCR text: every number from 1 to 101 has exactly one "Secret N" title page, in order, with no gaps or repeats (Secrets 1-11 in the first chapter, ten in each chapter after).
- The parts of a Secret: every one of the 101 has "How To Do It" (the step) and "Skip If" (when it doesn't apply to you). 56 of them (1-5, 12-22, 32-51, 72-81, 92-101) also open with "The Dated Habit" and "The French Secret"; the other 45 open with plain text. So "every Secret tells you how to do it and when to skip it" is true; "every Secret names the dated habit" is not. The third label every Secret carries, "Years Younger", is not a fact a CTA may use (see below).
- The four frameworks, by name (introduced on p. 8): the Capsule Rule ("a small set of foundational pieces, no more than five to eight"), the Color Compass ("five to seven colors, identified once by understanding your undertone"), the Proportion Equation ("one fitted piece and one fluid piece belong together in every outfit") and the Elegance Audit ("a short, weekly act of attention").
- The introduction's thesis (p. 7): "elegance after sixty is not about subtraction of age but addition of authority, and it is built through constraint, not through more shopping."
- Who it's for (p. 9): "the woman who has started to suspect that her closet no longer represents her", who "wants a system rather than a mood board"; it is "not for the reader chasing a specific trend cycle, hoping to look thirty again, or looking for a single miracle purchase."
- The conclusion's first week (p. 451): five Secrets to start with, in order: 12 (find your undertone), 22 (one fitted piece with one fluid piece), 60 (cap every outfit at three accessories), 76 (the category audit) and 93 (the fifteen-minute weekly audit).
- Glasses: the accessories chapter has two Secrets on frames (58 and 59), so eyewear may be matched to that chapter.
- Never the page count (the profile's "389 pages" does not match the PDF; see the note at the end), a price, a file format or any bonus.

## Claims to keep out of CTAs

- **"Look 20 Years Younger."** The subtitle (p. 1) reads "101 Style Hair and Makeup Secrets / The French Use To Look 20 Years Younger". The book's own thesis says the opposite: "None of them will tell you to look younger. All of them will tell you how to look finished, deliberate, and unmistakably yours." (p. 7); "Most style advice aimed at women over sixty is, if you look closely, a covert form of apology. It promises to make you look 'younger,' 'instantly'" (p. 6); the book is "not for the reader ... hoping to look thirty again" (p. 9); "You are not here to look like you did at forty." (p. 11). The book contradicts itself again inside the Secrets: the closing label of every one of the 101 Secrets is headed "Years Younger:", and a few of those lines make age claims ("reads roughly a decade younger", Secret 83, p. 370). A CTA never says "younger", never names the "Years Younger" part, and never speaks the subtitle.
- **"Makeup."** The subtitle promises makeup Secrets. Counted from the text: **no Secret is about makeup as its subject.** One Secret has a makeup step: 68, "Shape Your Brows to Frame, Not Announce" (p. 303), whose How To Do It says to "Fill sparse areas with powder or a brow pencil" (p. 305). One more touches it in passing: 84, the date-night formula, changes "the shoe, the accessory count, and the lip color" (p. 374). Every other mention of makeup is a passing remark (pp. 66, 68, 72, 172-173, 304). The beauty Secrets are hair (62-64), skin (65-66), nails (67), brows (68), posture (69) and grooming (70-71). So a CTA never promises makeup lessons, and a makeup video matches only the hair, skin and grooming chapter, by title.
- **"French women" as a fact.** The book's claims about French women are its own generalizations (for example p. 194, p. 361, p. 448); they are not sources for a script.
- **Page count.** The PDF has 452 pages; the printed page numbers run to at least 446 (on PDF p. 449). Neither matches the profile's "389 pages".
