- **Channel:** Scarlett Starling. **Author:** Scarlett Sterling (as printed on the title page and in the running footer, "300 Budget-Friendly Ways to Look Expensive Scarlett Sterling"; the book spells it Sterling, the channel's current profile spells it Starling).

- **What's in it:** an introduction, 28 chapters and a conclusion (the contents numbers the conclusion as "Chapter 29"), and 300 numbered Ways, ten or eleven to a chapter. Every Way carries "The Swap", "Styling Tip", "Body/Skin Note" and "Payoff"; most also carry "The Mistake" (233 labelled) and "Why It Works" (258 labelled), the rest open with a plain paragraph instead. Chapter 24 is the ten no-cost quick wins; the conclusion ends with "The First-Week Fast Track" (five Ways). A friend, Claire, recurs as an example.
- **Source:** OCR of the 300 Budget-Friendly Ways to Look Expensive PDF from the operator's Drive folder, 2026-10-29; image-only PDF, 1,009 pages, one OCR text file per PDF page.
- **Page numbers:** the pages below are PDF pages. The book does print page numbers (small, at the foot of the page; the OCR caught nine of them, for example "163" on PDF page 167 and "986" on PDF page 990). Printed page = PDF page minus 4 throughout. Quote the PDF page in this file; a reader holding the book will see a number four lower.
- **What this file holds:** the chapter page map, every Way's title, page and chapter, the facts a CTA may state, the claims to keep out, and the book's own stories (which never go in a script). **The full text is not stored here;** the OCR files are the record.
- **Use:** for the book match (which Way and chapter cover a video's topic) and the facts a CTA may state. It supplies no facts, steps or numbers to the script itself.

## Facts a CTA may state

- The title, 300 Budget-Friendly Ways to Look Expensive (spoken "Three Hundred Budget-Friendly Ways to Look Expensive"). Twenty-eight chapters between an introduction and a conclusion, named by title.
- **300 Ways, numbered 1 to 300.** Checked against the OCR text: 295 of the 300 have a "Way N" title in order with no repeats; the other five (169, 190, 267, 282 and 295) have their full text, in sequence, but no readable title (see the table). The 300 Payoff lines confirm the count: exactly 300 Ways.
- More than a thousand pages (1,009 PDF pages; the printed page numbers run past 1,000).
- The parts of a Way: every one of the 300 has the swap, a styling tip, a body or skin note and the payoff. The mistake and why it works are labelled in most (233 and 258 of 300) and given as an opening paragraph in the rest. "Every Way gives you the swap and the payoff" is safe; "every Way in six labelled parts" is close but not exact.
- The three frameworks, by name (introduced on p. 13): the Fit-First Rule ("a well-fitted basic will always outread a poorly fitted designer piece"), the Quiet Luxury Palette ("a limited, cohesive set of four to six colors") and the Fabric Touch Test ("identify, by feel alone, which materials absorb and reflect light the way expensive garments do").
- The introduction, "Why Looking Expensive Has Nothing to Do with Your Price Tag", and its thesis (p. 10): "looking expensive is a learnable system of visual signals, not a spending threshold. The signals are fit, fabric, color, grooming, and presence."
- Who it's for (p. 15): "women over sixty who want a practical, daily system", "a reference they return to before they get dressed, before they shop"; it is "not for anyone hoping for a shortcut that involves no attention at all". "Every Way stands on its own."
- The quick wins: the chapter "The Quick-Win Hacks: 10 No-Cost Tricks That Read Expensive Immediately" (pp. 843-871) holds Ways 251-260, moves made "with what is already hanging in the closet" (p. 844).
- The First-Week Fast Track (p. 1007, explained on p. 1008): Way 1 (check the shoulder seam), Way 53 (write your palette on an index card), Way 195 (wear the fifty-dollar blouse like it cost five hundred), Way 261 (build your capsule around eight pieces) and Way 290 (commit to wearing one outfit you love, today).
- No dedicated chapter for glasses, thinning hair or apple shapes (see "Topics with no chapter of their own" below). The profile's line on this is correct.
- Never a price, a discount, "free", a file format or any bonus.

## Claims to keep out of CTAs

- **"Younger."** The title and subtitle make no age claim, and the book's thesis is about looking expensive, not young. A few Payoff lines do claim it: Way 4 "reads as noticeably younger" (p. 31), Way 65 "widely reported by stylists as reading five to ten years younger" (p. 238), Way 98 "Stylists commonly describe this single change as reading five to eight years younger" (p. 350), Way 197 "reads five to ten years younger" (p. 678), and Way 212 "a face reads younger" (p. 728). None of these is a fact a CTA may use; the profile's ban on "look younger" and "anti-aging" stands.
- **"Makeup."** The subtitle's "Makeup" is real: the chapter "The Makeup and Skincare Minimum: What Signals 'Polished' Without Looking Done" (pp. 712-747) holds eleven Ways, 208-218 (primer, sheer coverage, concealer, cream blush, brows, lashes and mascara, lip color, setting powder, a five-minute face, overnight skincare, a dewy finish).
- **Dollar figures.** Many Payoff lines carry prices (for example "$75-120", Way 1, p. 22). They are the book's estimates; a CTA never quotes one.
