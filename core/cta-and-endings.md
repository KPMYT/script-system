# CTAs and endings

**How this file relates to niche packs and persona profiles.** This is a base rule file. Everything about *what* a CTA offers lives in the persona profile or niche pack:
- the offer itself: a book, an email list, an affiliate product, or just a subscribe
- its length
- its wording style
- any placement detail

This file sets the mechanics every CTA follows, whatever it offers. Claims about the offer follow `offer-rules.md`. The format (markers and layout) follows `workflow.md`, section 4.

---

## 1. Why CTAs work, and how they fail

- **The action window.** Viewers act most readily in the moments right after they've received something useful. Ask then, not before.
- **Give before asking.** A CTA before the first real value feels like paying a toll before seeing the road.
- **One ask per moment.** Several asks at once ("like, comment, subscribe, check the link, follow on…") get none of them done.
- **Specific direction.** "First link in the description" works; "support the channel" doesn't.
- **Always promise more value.** Every CTA tells the viewer what they get that this video doesn't give them: more projects, the full measurements, a new build in their inbox. The promise must be true and specific, from the persona profile (`offer-rules.md`).
- **No early thanks.** "Thanks for watching" signals the end, and people leave before the ask lands.

---

## 2. Placement

**The mid-video CTA:**
- **Goes right after the first real payoff,** found by the structure, never by the clock. In a list, that's after the first item or two is delivered in full. In a build or how-to, it's after the first real step or insight. A longer video simply reaches that point later.
- **Never comes before the first delivered value,** and never pushes the main content back. In a single-build video, the build has started before the CTA.
- **Plays against an open question.** The sentence just before the CTA opens or restates a question that's still unanswered, so the pitch plays while the viewer is waiting on something.
- **Is followed by a return line** that gets straight back to the content (`variety-rotation.md`, slot 4).

**The end CTA:**
- **Starts directly after the final content sentence:** the last item, the last step, the last insight, delivered in full. Nothing sits between them.

---

## 3. What every CTA contains

Whatever its length or offer, each CTA has:

1. **What it is,** named plainly and truthfully.
2. **The added-value promise:** what the viewer gets beyond this video, specific and true.
3. **Where to get it:** one clear, simple direction.

Then the CTA closes according to where it sits:
- **The mid-video CTA** ends on the return line back into the content.
- **The end CTA** ends on its ask (subscribe or engagement), if the persona profile includes one.

**Linking the offer to the video.** A clause can connect the offer to this video's problem ("every measurement for this rack is in there too"). A separate recap sentence can't.

**Page references come only from the book match.** When the persona profile names a book text, the plan records which pages cover this video's topic (`workflow.md`, section 3, step 2). A CTA or book mention may name pages only from that record ("more of this is on pages 63 to 65 of my book"). With no match, it gives the book's general value and names no pages. A page that doesn't cover what the script says it covers is a false claim about the offer (`offer-rules.md`).

**Future value stays general.** "So the next build shows up in your feed" is fine. A named or described upcoming topic never is: the script has no idea what the next video will be.

---

## 4. The ending

Get to the point and stop. After the final payoff, the script does exactly one of two things:

1. **Goes into the end CTA,** then the one closing line, then stops.
2. **Goes straight to the one closing line,** if the channel has no end CTA.

The closing line is exactly one sentence: the persona's sign-off, a callback, or a practical next action (`workflow.md`, section 4).

**Never at the end:**
- a recap
- a wind-down ("so that wraps it up," "and there you have it")
- thanks for watching
- a second sign-off
- a promise of a specific next video
- anything after the closing line
- a second closing sentence

---

## 5. Keeping CTA wording fresh

The structure repeats; the words never do. (One exception, operator 2026-10-20: the end CTA's wording may repeat, and it isn't logged.)
- **Rewrite every CTA from scratch each time,** keeping its structure. Change the opening words, the value line, the directions and the close.
- **Never reuse or closely paraphrase** a CTA opening that appears in the pasted rotation log (`variety-rotation.md`, slot 8).
- **Banned echoes,** from old scripts, never used on any channel (close variants included):
  - "before we start counting, you ought to know where this comes from"
  - "take your time" / "there is no rush" / "I'll wait right here" / "I'll be here"
  - "open your camera, the same one you use to take pictures"
  - "square code"
  - "the link is waiting for you"
  - "it deserved to be written down"
