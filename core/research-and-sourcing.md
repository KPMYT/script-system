# Research and sourcing

**How this file relates to niche packs.** This is a base rule file. A niche pack supplies:
- its own research pack template
- its best sources
- which facts count as "live," and how often they're re-checked

Where a niche pack is silent, this file applies.

---

## 1. Where research sits

```
ideation (title + brief)  ->  research (verified research pack)  ->  script (writes only from the pack)
```

- **In:** the locked title and the ideation brief. The brief's facts are leads to check, not finished proof.
- **Out:** the niche's research pack, or the default pack in section 9 when the niche has none.
- **Back to ideation:** when a title's claim can't be supported, even generously (section 10).

The script stage never adds facts the pack doesn't contain.

---

## 2. Facts versus framing

**Facts are always verified.** They include:
- numbers, prices, fees, measurements, specs and dates
- rules, codes and policies
- steps and methods the viewer will follow
- stories and quotes
- anything stated as fact about a real company, person or product

**Framing may be exaggerated, within limits:**
- emotional stakes, and how big a problem feels
- an industry or category cast as the villain ("the big stores would rather you didn't know this")
- the degree a title promises ("EVERY tool," "stupid money")

**The limits on framing:**
- **The fact underneath is true.** Exaggeration changes the degree, never invents the event.
- **A worst case must be a real possibility, said as one.** "That drip can end up costing you a ceiling" is fine. "That drip will flood your house tonight" isn't.
- **Villain framing stays tone.** Nothing false is ever stated as fact about a named company or person.
- **No exaggeration that could lead someone into danger or panic** on health or safety.
- **No guarantees** of money, results or outcomes.
- **Never extreme.** If the framing would make a reasonable viewer feel misled once they saw the facts, it's too far.

---

## 3. The source hierarchy

**Primary beats secondary, and secondary beats aggregator.** The blog post repeating a figure isn't the source; the document it links to is. If a chain of citations dead-ends without reaching a real document, the figure is unverified.

**Numbers need an owner.** Every number was counted by someone: a manufacturer, an agency, a marketplace, a code body. Trace it back to them. A number floating free in an article has no owner and no place in the pack.

---

## 4. Where good material lives

General sources across the portfolio. Each niche pack adds its own best sources.
- **Manufacturer specs, manuals and installation guides.**
- **Retailer and marketplace listings,** including sold listings, for prices and demand.
- **Codes, standards and official rules:** plumbing and building codes, airline and TSA rule pages, platform fee pages.
- **Safety agencies and recalls:** for example, CPSC recall notices.
- **University extension services:** gardens, chickens, food safety.
- **Archives and collections:** museum and textile archives, digitized newspapers.
- **Original texts:** for example, the actual Stoic writings rather than quote sites.
- **First-hand accounts on the record:** published interviews, talks, testimony.

---

## 5. Verify or cut (hard rule)

Every fact either traces to a source that was actually opened, or it doesn't go in the pack.
- **Opened, read, logged.** Not a source another video mentioned. Not a number remembered from somewhere.
- **Log as you verify.** Every item gets its source, link and checked date the moment it's verified. An item without them is pending, not verified.
- **Failed verification means cut,** and nothing later brings it back because the video "needs a number there."
- **Gaps stay visible.** A needed fact that isn't verified yet sits as a marked gap until it's filled or the line that needed it is cut. Placeholder numbers never ship.

---

## 6. Evidence wording

The wording has to match what the evidence actually shows:
- **"Listed at"** for an asking price, and **"sold for"** only with real sales evidence.
- **"A [named] study found,"** never "studies show."
- **Each number said alongside whoever counted it,** wherever it matters. Attribute a number, rule or claim once per source per section, not on every line; steps need no attribution. A script that names a source in every other sentence reads like a citation list.
- **A checked date** on anything that changes, in the pack. Prices are spoken without a date, because the upload outlives the price; a niche pack may add a "prices subject to change" line.

---

## 7. Freshness

- **Live facts can change:** prices, fees, rules and policies, product availability. Each niche pack lists which of its fact types are live, and how old they can get before being re-checked.
- **Timeless facts never need re-checking** once verified: how a joint works, why a drain gurgles, what Seneca wrote.
- **With no niche pack,** live facts are re-checked before every script.

---

## 8. The verification pass (mandatory)

After the pack is built, and before it goes to scripting, a second pass:
1. Reopens every link.
2. Confirms every number, price and spec against the page, as of today.
3. Checks the evidence wording ("listed at" against "sold for," named sources).
4. Checks each title claim against what the pack supports (section 10).
5. Fixes or cuts anything that fails, and records a "verified on" date at the top of the pack.

A pack without a "verified on" date doesn't go to scripting.

---

## 9. The default research pack

Use this when the niche has no template of its own.

```
RESEARCH PACK
Title (locked):
Channel:
Verified on:
Promise in plain words:
Title claims -> support:         [each claim or number in the title -> the line below that supports it]

FACTS                            [one per line]
- fact | source | link | checked date | live or timeless

STEPS OR METHOD                  [if the video teaches something; complete for every fix the title promises:
                                  shut-off, remove, install, test. A fix with no full steps is a blocking gap]
- step | what's visible | mistake, safety, option or quality note

SAFETY POINTS                    [each tied to the step it belongs to]

PROOF FOR THE MAIN PROMISE
- evidence | source | link | date | wording ("listed at", "sold for", "a [named] study found")

DOUBTER
- the likely objection           [from real comments where possible]
- what answers it

ADVERSARY AND FRAMING
- the category cast as adversary, and the framing (tone only; no false facts about named companies)

GAPS
- anything unverified (the script stage stops on any gap that matters)
```

---

## 10. Flagging a title back to ideation

If a title's claim can't be supported, even counting the framing allowed in section 2, the research stage doesn't patch it. It sends a short note back to ideation:
- the claim
- why it can't be supported
- an honest version that can be

The honest wording the pack suggests follows the audit's rounding rule (`greenlight-audit.md`, D4): time and cost round up, results and savings round down. "Within about 15 minutes" isn't honest when the sources say 10 to 25.

The script is never written to support a claim the pack can't.
