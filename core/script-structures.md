# Script structures (fallback)

**How this file relates to niche packs.** These structures are the fallback. Whenever a niche pack defines a structure for the format, the niche pack's version wins completely. Use these for a new channel, or for a format the pack doesn't cover yet.

---

## Rules every structure shares

- **No intro, no teases, no recap.** No persona or channel intro, no long-range promises (`humanizer.md` hard bans), and no summary at the end.
- **The first payoff comes fast.** The line after the hook delivers or escalates (`retention-mechanics.md`).
- **Context is only what's necessary.** Give only the context needed to understand what comes next, in a sentence or two at most, and only when it's actually needed. Never a context block.
- **Length comes from the content.** List the content units first (each item, step, layer or option), give each one what it needs, and the length is the total. No word counts, no timestamps, no fixed section lengths.
- **Anything shown is named.** The video is built from the script, so every visual must be named in the narration.
- **CTAs and the ending** follow `cta-and-endings.md`.

---

## Hook options

Every hook delivers the title's promise. On top of that, any of these can be used where they fit. None is required, and different ones should rotate (`variety-rotation.md`).

- **A stakes sentence:** why this matters to the viewer ("Get one of these wrong and the whole board's firewood").
- **Paying off the title straight away:** deliver a first piece of the promise in the hook itself.
- **Escalating the title's shock:** take the title's claim a step further, as long as it's true and within the framing limits in `research-and-sourcing.md`.

A promise about what's coming later in the video ("the fixes get more useful as we go") is a long-range tease, and it's banned. Short bridges between segments are fine (`retention-mechanics.md`).

---

## Picking a structure (when there's no niche pack)

**The brief comes first.** If the ideation brief names a format, use it, and use the matching structure below for anything the brief leaves open. Use the title's shape only when the brief names no format:

| The title… | Structure |
|---|---|
| counts a list of things | List |
| says how to do or make something | How-to or make-along |
| asks why something happens, or how it works | Why it happens |
| asks why something happens *and* how to fix it ("Why your toilet keeps running, and how to fix it") | Diagnose and fix |
| pits options against each other, or asks which is best | Comparison |
| centers on a lesson or an idea | Essay |
| promises a before and after | Transformation |

---

## 1. List

For tips, mistakes, ideas, things to know.

1. **Hook:** the promise with its number, plus any hook options above.
2. **First item,** straight away.
3. **Each item:**
   - what it is
   - why or how it works
   - the proof (the number, the source, the example)
   - a short bridge to the next item
4. **Last item,** then the ending.

**Order:** second-best first, best near the middle, third-best last (`retention-mechanics.md`). A countdown saves the top item for last. Items are uneven in length: each takes what it needs.

## 2. How-to or make-along

For fixing, making or doing something step by step.

1. **Hook:** the result promised.
2. **What you need:** items, and cost or where to get them if that matters. Batch the trivial things.
3. **The steps:**
   - each one an instruction to the viewer with a visible action
   - extra words only for a common mistake, a safety risk, an alternative, or a quality tip
   - repeats collapse into one line
4. **How you know it worked.**
5. **When to stop and call a pro,** where that's relevant.
6. **The ending.**

## 3. Why it happens / how it works

For explaining a cause, a mechanism or a hidden reason. Build understanding in layers, never all at once.

1. **Hook:** the consequence first, meaning what the viewer sees or suffers.
2. **Layer 1:** the simple version anyone gets: one core idea and one everyday comparison.
3. **Layer 2:** the mechanism, meaning what's actually going on. It builds on Layer 1 and never contradicts it.
4. **Layer 3:** what it means for the viewer's own situation.
5. **What to do about it.**
6. **The ending.**

**Layer 1 and the line after the hook.** The line after the hook never sets up background (`retention-mechanics.md`, section 3). Layer 1 goes straight after the hook only when it's a sentence or two and itself delivers part of the promise. Otherwise fold it into the first cause, where it's needed.

## 3b. Diagnose and fix

For "why it happens and how to fix it": a problem with a few possible causes, each with its own fix.

1. **Hook:** the symptom the viewer sees, in their words, covering every version the title promises (for example, the toilet that refills on its own and the one that never stops), plus the promise: which cause it is, and the fix.
2. **The quick test that sorts the causes,** if there is one, straight away. That's the first payoff.
3. **Each cause,** cheapest and quickest check first:
   - how to tell it's this one
   - why it happens, in a sentence (the only background the cause needs)
   - the fix, as steps the viewer follows, with each safety point at the step it belongs to
   - how you know it worked
4. **When it's beyond a DIY fix,** where that's true.
5. **The ending.**

Say each cause's diagnosis once, where it's used. Don't explain all the causes up front and then again one by one.

## 4. Comparison

For "which one," "A versus B," "the best…"

1. **Hook:** what choosing wrong costs.
2. **The criteria:** what actually matters for this choice.
3. **Each option** (or each round):
   - what it does well
   - where it fails
   - what it costs
4. **The verdict by situation,** which always picks: "for X, get this; for Y, get that."
5. **The ending.**

## 5. Essay

For a lesson, an idea, or a piece of wisdom (for example, a Stoic channel).

1. **Hook:** the idea in plain words, as a claim the viewer can hold onto. No "Most people believe X, but…" template.
2. **Its source:** the real text or person behind it, named.
3. **What it looks like on an ordinary day:** a concrete situation the viewer recognizes.
4. **The fair objection:** where the idea seems wrong or hard, and the honest answer.
5. **What to do with it:** one practical way to use it this week.
6. **The ending.**

## 6. Transformation

For makeovers, flips, restorations, room or outfit changes.

1. **Hook:** the before, and the after it becomes.
2. **The stages,** each with a progress bridge to the next ("it's square now, but still rough").
3. **The after,** named and described so it can be shown.
4. **What it took:** time, cost, materials.
5. **The ending.**
